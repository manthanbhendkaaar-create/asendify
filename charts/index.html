Trend

Price has made a series of higher highs and higher lows, climbing from about 82 to a peak near 108.6. The latest move is a pullback inside that uptrend rather than a clear reversal so far.

Market structure

A sideways range between roughly 94 and 101 broke to the upside, and price then ran to around 108.6. It is now pulling back towards the old range top. The last swing low that keeps the uptrend intact is just under 99.5.

Key levels

Resistance108 – 109Recent swing highWhere the last rally stalled; sellers stepped in here twice.
Support99.5 – 101Old range topFormer resistance that price broke through; often watched for a retest from above.
Support94 – 95Range floorBase of the earlier consolidation.

Patterns

  • Breakout and retest. Price broke out of the 94–101 range and is pulling back towards the breakout area. A retest is not confirmed until price reacts there.

Volume and indicators

Volume rose on the breakout above 101 and has been lighter during the pullback. No other indicators are visible.

Scenarios traders often watch

1

Support holds

If price holds the 99.5–101 zone and forms a higher low, traders often watch for a move back towards the 108–109 swing high.

Level 102.5Invalidation 98.8

A 4H close below 99.5 would break the higher-low structure.

2

Breakout to new highs

If price closes above 109 on the 4H chart, traders often watch whether 109 then acts as support on a retest.

Level 109.4Invalidation 106.8

Falling back below 107 would suggest the breakout failed.

3

Support gives way

If price closes below 99.5, traders often watch the 94–95 range floor as the next area of interest.

Level 99.2Invalidation 101.6

Reclaiming 101 would put price back above the old range top.

Position-size arithmetic

Your rule: risk 1% of £10,000 = £100 per trade.

Pure arithmetic: units = risk amount ÷ distance between the level and its invalidation. It assumes the chart is priced in your account currency and ignores fees, spread, slippage, lot or contract sizes and leverage rules. It is not a recommendation to trade.

What would change the picture

  • A 4H close below 99.5 (uptrend structure broken)
  • A strong close above 109 (new high, trend continuation)
  • Pullback volume rising sharply (sellers more active than on the way up)

FAQ

Questions

Is this financial advice?

No. Chart Reader is educational. It explains what a chart shows and what traders often watch, using conditional wording. It never tells you to buy or sell, there is no guarantee of accuracy, and trading carries a high risk of losing money. Always make your own decisions.

How much does it cost?

100 chart reads for £435: £300 plus a 45% service fee of £135. One-time payment through PayPal. Reads never expire.

What counts as a read?

Only a successful read uses one. If the image is not a chart, or the reader fails, the read is given back automatically.

Which charts work?

Screenshots of candlestick, bar or line charts from platforms such as TradingView, MetaTrader, broker apps or exchanges, for stocks, crypto, forex, indices or commodities. A readable price axis gives the best results. Images are resized in your browser before upload (max 2 MB).

Who reads the chart?

An AI vision model reads your screenshot and writes the explanation to a fixed structure. It can misread a blurry chart or a cut-off price axis, so always check the levels yourself.

What happens to my screenshots?

They are stored privately and deleted automatically after 30 days. The written results stay in your history.

How do I sign in?

With your email. There are no passwords: we email you a sign-in link that works once. You can ask for a new link any time on the sign-in page.

Please see our policies.

Does Chart Reader tell me when to buy or sell?

No. Every read uses conditional wording ("if price does X, traders often watch Y"), and an automatic check rejects any read that tells you to buy or sell.

Can it work out my position size?

Yes, as pure arithmetic. Add your account size and risk % per trade, and each scenario shows the size that matches the distance between its level and its invalidation. It ignores fees, spread, slippage and contract sizes.
