Is this trade worth the risk? Risk:reward calculator

Reward:risk = (target − entry) ÷ (entry − stop). To break even before costs you must win more than 1 ÷ (1 + reward:risk) of trades: 50% at 1:1, 33.3% at 2:1, 25% at 3:1. Size the trade so the stop loses only your chosen % of the account.

Questions

How do I calculate risk to reward?

Risk is the distance from your entry to your stop-loss; reward is the distance from your entry to your target. Divide reward by risk. Entry 100, stop 95 and target 110 risk 5 to make 10, so the reward:risk is 2:1 (often written as a 1:2 risk-to-reward).

What win rate do I need to break even?

Before costs, breakeven win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward:risk). At 1:1 you need to win 50% of trades, at 2:1 about 33.3% and at 3:1 25%. Fees and slippage push the real number a little higher.

How is position size worked out from risk %?

Money at risk = account size × risk %. Position size = money at risk ÷ risk per share or unit, rounded down. A £10,000 account risking 1% has £100 at risk; with a £5 stop distance that is 20 shares.

Is a higher risk:reward always better?

Not on its own. Far targets are hit less often, so a 4:1 setup with a low win rate can do worse than a 1.5:1 setup that wins more often. Expectancy combines both: win rate × reward:risk − loss rate, in units of risk (R).

Does this work for short trades?

Yes. If your stop is above your entry the calculator treats the trade as a short, and the target must be below the entry.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is arithmetic on the prices you enter and does not recommend any trade. Trading carries a high risk of losing money, and most retail traders lose money.
