Is there a free tool that finds my worst YouTube title and rewrites it?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 25 October 2026 · Last updated 4 November 2026

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast reviews your latest public uploads, names your weakest title and rewrites it, with no sign-up. It judges titles the way a stranger would, from public data only, so confirm the weak title with your own YouTube Studio numbers before you change anything.

Key takeaways

  • A free outside tool can flag a weak title quickly, but only Studio shows what viewers actually did.
  • In Studio, judge click-through rate (CTR) alongside impressions, and compare within the same traffic source.
  • Rewrite by naming the specific subject, putting the key words first and keeping the promise honest.
  • YouTube's built-in A/B test for titles and thumbnails picks winners by watch time, not clicks.
  • Change one thing at a time and give it time before judging.

How to spot your weakest title in YouTube Studio

An outside tool sees what a viewer sees. YouTube Studio shows what viewers actually did. To find the title that is underperforming:

  1. Open Analytics, then the Content tab, and look at each recent video's impressions and impressions click-through rate (CTR).
  2. Read CTR alongside impressions. YouTube's impressions and CTR FAQ says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%, and that videos with many impressions, such as those on the Home page, may naturally have lower CTRs. A low CTR on a video with very few impressions tells you little. A low CTR on a video YouTube showed widely is the one worth fixing.
  3. Compare like with like. Check traffic sources (Browse features, Suggested videos, YouTube search) and compare videos within the same source. Search and Browse behave differently.
  4. Look at several videos, not one. One weak upload can be bad luck or a weak topic. A pattern across three or four videos is a packaging problem.
  5. Remember the thumbnail counts too. CTR measures the title and thumbnail together, so a low number does not prove the title alone is at fault.

For the retention side, YouTube's guide to the audience retention report explains the intro figure, which shows the share of viewers still watching after 30 seconds. A high number suggests the opening matched the title and thumbnail.

A simple method for rewriting a weak title

Once you know which title to fix, run it through these checks. They line up with YouTube's own thumbnail and title tips, which say to keep titles accurate, put the most important words first because viewers may see only part of the title, and use capitals and emoji sparingly.

  • Name the specific thing. Replace vague words ("vlog", "update", "stuff") with the actual subject, dish, game, place or result.
  • State the payoff early. What does the viewer get or learn? Put that first, because long titles are cut off.
  • Add one reason to click. Curiosity, a clear outcome, a contrast or a constraint such as time, budget or skill level. One is enough.
  • Match the thumbnail, do not repeat it. The title and thumbnail should tell one story without saying the same words twice.
  • Stay honest. If the video does not deliver what the title promises, people leave early, and that hurts you more than a low CTR.

Worked example: fixing one title

Example: a home-cooking channel has ten recent videos. These numbers are illustrative only.

  • Nine videos have an impressions CTR between 4% and 6% in Browse features.
  • One, "Sunday cooking vlog", had about 20,000 impressions in Browse but a CTR of about 2%.
  • Its intro figure is similar to the channel's other videos, so people who click are staying.

That points to packaging, not content. The creator rewrites the title to "One-pan chicken traybake in 30 minutes (no washing up)" and leaves the thumbnail alone, so she knows which change made the difference. She checks again after two weeks, comparing Browse CTR only. If the video is eligible, she could instead run YouTube's built-in test with both titles and let watch time decide.

Other honest options

A free roast is one view. These are worth using alongside it:

  • YouTube's A/B test for titles and thumbnails. YouTube's help page on A/B testing titles and thumbnails says you can test up to three variants of a title, a thumbnail or both, on desktop Studio with advanced features turned on. The winner is chosen by watch time share, not CTR. It is not available for Shorts or for videos marked Made for Kids, age-restricted or private.
  • An AI chatbot. Paste your title, a one-line summary of the video and who it is for, then ask for ten specific, honest alternatives. Treat the output as drafts, not answers.
  • Peers in your niche. Show two or three creators a few title options next to your thumbnail and ask which they would click and why.
  • Your own back catalogue. Your best-performing titles, by CTR within the same traffic source, are the most reliable guide to what your audience likes.

FAQ

Does the roast use my click-through rate to pick the weakest title?

No. It only sees public information, so it picks the title that looks least clear and clickable to a stranger. Your CTR is in YouTube Studio, and it is worth checking there too.

What is a normal CTR on YouTube?

YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%. Compare yours with your own recent videos in the same traffic source.

Should I change the title of an old video?

It can be worth trying on a video that YouTube is still showing but few people click. Change one thing at a time, give it time, and keep the new title honest.

How does YouTube's title test pick a winner?

By watch time share, not click-through rate. The version that earns the most watch time is shown to all viewers.

Can a tool rewrite all my titles?

Some can draft many versions, but you still need to choose and test. Use the rewrite method above, then check results in Studio.
