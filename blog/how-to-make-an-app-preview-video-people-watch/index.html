How do I make an App Store or Google Play preview video that people watch?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Make the first seconds show your real app doing its most useful thing, with short on-screen text, because both stores play previews silently. Apple allows up to three previews of up to 30 seconds each and plays them muted by default; Google Play may autoplay your YouTube preview muted for up to 30 seconds and wants core features within the first 10 seconds.

Key takeaways

  • Apple: up to three app previews per language, each up to 30 seconds, and they "play with the sound muted" by default.
  • Google Play: one preview video via a YouTube link; "only the first 30 seconds auto-plays", muted, on some devices and surfaces.
  • Both stores want real in-app footage, not people holding phones or long logo intros.
  • Google Play asks for core features "as early as possible within the first 10 seconds" and at least 80% real app experience.
  • Neither store wants prices or time-limited claims in the video, and Google Play bans "download now" style calls to action.

What each store allows

The two stores treat previews differently. Apple hosts the file itself; Google Play embeds a YouTube video. Here are the rules side by side, from Apple's app previews guidance and Google's preview video help page:

Apple's App Store Connect preview specifications add the technical limits: each preview must be 15 to 30 seconds long, at up to 30 frames per second, as .mov, .m4v or .mp4 (H.264) or ProRes 422 HQ, and no larger than 500MB. So a 30-second edit is the ceiling, and a 15-second cut is the floor.

Apple adds that previews "display and autoplay on your product page and within search results". That means many people meet your video while scrolling a list of rival apps. The first frames are doing the same job as a thumbnail and a hook at once.

Why retention matters more here than in a normal ad

A store visitor is already interested in your category. They are comparing you with the next result. They don't need persuading that apps exist; they need to see quickly what yours does and whether it looks easy. Every second spent on a splash screen or a slow logo is a second where they can scroll to a competitor.

Because the video starts silent, voiceover can't carry the message. Apple says to "consider using copy to add additional context to your footage" and to make sure text "remains on the screen long enough for people to read." Google Play makes the same point and also recommends captions.

An edit plan for a 30-second preview

SecondsWhat to showOn-screen text
0 to 3The single most useful screen, already in actionThe outcome in 3 to 5 words
3 to 10The core task done start to finishOne short line per step
10 to 20Two or three supporting featuresOne feature name each
20 to 27A result screen: the finished thingWhat the user gets
27 to 30App name and iconNone needed

A few editing habits help:

  1. Record native screens. Apple recommends "capturing the native UI resolution rather than zooming in on the view." Crisp UI looks trustworthy.
  2. Cut on actions. Cut the moment a tap resolves, not after a pause. Dead time on a static screen is where people leave.
  3. Use touch hotspots, not hands. Apple allows "graphic elements, such as touch hotspots" to show interaction.
  4. Keep transitions simple. Apple suggests dissolves and fades and warns against transitions that imply features the app doesn't have.
  5. Pick a poster frame that sells. If autoplay is off, Apple shows a poster frame instead, so choose a frame that explains the app on its own.
  6. Disclose what costs extra. If features need an in-app purchase, a subscription or a login, Apple says you must disclose it.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches openings for silent autoplay videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and cold opens for £1,000 one-time. Apple's and Google's preview guidelines are free alternatives.

How long can an App Store preview be?

Up to 30 seconds each, and you can add up to three per language.

Does a Google Play preview video have a length limit?

Google gives no maximum, but only the first 30 seconds autoplays, so keep it short.

Can I show someone holding the phone in my preview?

Apple says not to film people interacting with a device. Google Play says the same unless the app's core use happens off the device.

Can I put a price in my app preview?

Apple advises against specific prices because they vary by region, and Google Play doesn't allow pricing or promotional claims.

Do app previews play with sound?

Apple's play muted by default and Google Play's autoplay muted, so use on-screen text.
