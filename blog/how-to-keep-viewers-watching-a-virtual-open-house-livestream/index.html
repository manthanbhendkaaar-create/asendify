How do I keep viewers watching a virtual open house livestream?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Show the property's best feature in the first minute, walk a planned route that saves a strong room for later, bring viewers in every few minutes with a question or a quick answer to theirs, and keep the main tour to roughly 15 to 25 minutes. Have a second person read comments so the presenter never stops moving. End with one clear next step, such as booking an in-person viewing, and then cut the recording into a short highlights video for people who missed it.

Key takeaways

  • Zoom's live event guide says the "first 60–90 seconds" often decide whether attendees stay, and suggests keeping housekeeping under 30 seconds.
  • The same guide recommends an interaction point every 8 to 10 minutes and putting one specific call to action in the final 3 to 5 minutes.
  • Zoom's engagement features include polls, live Q&A, reactions and automatic captions; Instagram, Facebook and YouTube live streams offer comments and pinned messages.
  • A property tour is a natural story: arrival, build-up, best room, details, next step.
  • Every claim on a live stream is still property marketing and must be accurate.

Plan the route like a story

StageTimeWhat to show and say
Hook0 to 1 minThe standout feature (view, kitchen, garden) and the headline facts: price, bedrooms, area
Arrival1 to 3 minStreet, parking, front door, first impression
Build3 to 10 minLiving spaces, in the order a visitor would walk them
Best room10 to 14 minSave one strong room for the middle, and tease it early ("wait until you see the garden")
Practical details14 to 18 minStorage, heating, broadband, any known issues
Q&A and next stepLast 3 to 5 minAnswer remaining questions, then one call to action

The early tease is an open loop. It gives viewers a reason to stay through the hallway and utility room.

Interaction that keeps people watching

  1. Ask a question early. "Are you a first-time buyer or moving up?" Answers tell you what to stress.
  2. Answer questions every few minutes, not only at the end, in line with Zoom's 8 to 10 minute rhythm.
  3. Pin the essentials: price, address area, how to book a viewing.
  4. Invite specific requests. "Want to see inside the wardrobes? Type YES." People who asked will wait to see it.
  5. Name what's coming next as you leave each room.

Get the facts right

A live stream is marketing like any listing. In the UK, National Trading Standards guidance says listings should give material information such as price, council tax band and tenure, plus details like room sizes, utilities and parking. Don't guess on air: "I'll confirm the broadband speed in the comments" is better than a wrong number. Get the seller's permission before streaming, and remove personal items, documents and anything that shows the seller's security setup.

After the stream

Most people won't watch live. Turn the recording into:

Full replayAs streamed, with the dead start trimmedLink for serious buyers
Highlights tour60 to 120 sListing page, social, email

Worked example

Example: a three-bed house with a large garden.

  • 0:00 Live from the garden: "This is the reason this house won't last. Three bedrooms, 120 square metres, and I'll show you the whole thing in 20 minutes."
  • 1:00 Street and front door.
  • 4:00 Living room and kitchen; a colleague reads a question about the boiler's age, answered from the seller's notes.
  • 11:00 Main bedroom: "Someone asked about storage, so here are the wardrobes."
  • 17:00 Back to the garden at sunset for the closing shot and booking link, pinned.

Details are illustrative.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to keep viewers watching a property tour?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Zoom's free live event guide is an alternative.

How long should a virtual open house be?

Around 15 to 25 minutes for the tour itself, plus a few minutes of questions.

Which platform is best for a live property tour?

Use wherever your buyers already follow you: Instagram or Facebook Live for local audiences, YouTube for a searchable replay, or a webinar tool if you want registration.

Do I need two people?

It helps. One presents and films; the other reads comments and relays questions.

What should I do with the recording?

Trim it, then cut a short highlights tour and a few clips for listings and social.
