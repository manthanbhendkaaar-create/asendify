Watch Time

How to Get More Watch Time on YouTube

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Updated 12 October 2026

Short answer: Watch time is the total time viewers spend watching your videos, so you grow it in two ways: get more of the right viewers to start watching, and keep them watching longer. Start with the first 30 seconds and the biggest dips in your retention graph, then use end screens, playlists and chapters to lead viewers to their next video. Padding videos to make them longer usually backfires, because viewers leave.

What is watch time on YouTube?

YouTube defines watch time as "the amount of time viewers have watched your video." In YouTube Studio it is shown in hours. It is different from average view duration, which YouTube describes as the "average minutes watched among those who stayed to watch."

A simple way to think about it: watch time grows when more people watch, when each person watches for longer, or both. Average view duration only measures the second part. Our guide to increasing average view duration covers that part in depth.

Why does watch time matter?

There are two main reasons.

  • Recommendations. YouTube says it added watch time to its recommendation system in 2012, because clicks alone did not show whether viewers found a video worth watching. It also measures "valued watchtime" through surveys that ask viewers to rate what they watched.
  • Monetisation. One route into the YouTube Partner Program is 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months. The other route uses Shorts views. YouTube says watch hours from the Shorts feed do not count towards the 4,000 hours.

The first point matters most. Watch time from viewers who are glad they watched is the kind YouTube says it wants to reward.

Should you make longer videos to get more watch time?

Only if the content needs the extra length. A longer video gives each viewer more to watch, but if the extra minutes are padding, viewers notice and leave. YouTube's retention help notes that videos generally taper off during playback, and that gradual declines mean viewers are losing interest over time. Padding makes that decline steeper.

A useful rule: make the video as long as the topic needs and no longer, then cut anything that repeats. If a 12-minute topic is stretched to 20 minutes, you risk losing viewers before they reach the parts that matter.

How do you keep viewers watching longer?

  1. Fix the opening. YouTube's "intro" figure shows what share of viewers are still watching after 30 seconds. Confirm the title's promise in the first few seconds and move greetings and logos out of the way. See how long a YouTube intro should be.
  2. Keep a question open. Raise the next question before you answer the current one.
  3. Cut dead sections. Pauses, false starts, tangents and repeated points.
  4. Show, don't just tell. Put numbers, charts and examples on screen.
  5. Do not signal the end early. "That's it for today" tells viewers they can leave.

For more ideas, see how to make YouTube videos more engaging.

How do you get viewers to watch a second video?

A viewer who watches two of your videos gives you watch time from both, from a single click. Make the next step easy:

  • End screens. You can add an end screen to the last 5 to 20 seconds of any video at least 25 seconds long, with up to four elements on a standard 16:9 video, including a video or playlist.
  • Say it out loud. Mention the next video in the content and give a reason to watch it, rather than relying only on the end screen.
  • Playlists and series. Group videos that belong together, so the next one is obvious.
  • Chapters. YouTube's chapters break a video into sections, each with its own preview. They can help a viewer who wants one part find it, rather than give up and leave.

YouTube Studio's end screen element click rate report shows how often viewers click each element, so you can see which suggestions work.

How do you find what is costing you watch time?

Open the audience retention report for your most-watched recent videos. YouTube marks:

  • Dips: moments where viewers skipped or stopped watching.
  • Spikes: moments rewatched or shared. A spike can also mean a section was unclear.
  • Top moments: where almost no one dropped off.

Go to the biggest dip, watch the 20 seconds before it, and find the cause. Then compare with typical retention for your 10 latest videos of similar length, to see whether the problem is one video or a habit. Our guide to why viewers leave YouTube videos lists the usual causes.

What should you avoid when chasing watch time?

  • Misleading titles and thumbnails. YouTube warns that if your title does not accurately represent the video, viewers may stop watching, which can impact discoverability.
  • Padding. Long intros, slow recaps and repeated points.
  • Fake cliffhangers. Promising something later and never delivering it breaks trust for the next video.
  • Changing everything at once. You will not know what worked.

Frequently asked questions

Does Shorts watch time count towards the 4,000 hours?

No. YouTube says watch hours from the Shorts feed do not count towards the 4,000 watch hours for the YouTube Partner Program. Shorts have a separate route based on qualified Shorts views.

Is watch time or average view duration more important?

They measure different things. Watch time is the total, and average view duration is how long viewers stay on average. Better editing that raises average view duration usually raises watch time too, because each view counts for more.

Do longer videos get more watch time?

They can, if viewers keep watching. Padding a video to make it longer tends to make viewers leave sooner, which can cancel out the extra length.

Do end screens increase watch time?

They can send viewers to another of your videos, which adds watch time from the same visit. YouTube Studio shows the click rate for each end screen element, so you can test which suggestions work.

What is the fastest way to get more watch time?

Fix the first 30 seconds and the biggest dip in your most-viewed videos. Those are the moments where the most viewers are lost, so improvements there have the biggest effect.
