What is a good structure for a 30-second video ad?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A reliable structure for a 30-second video ad is hook (0 to 3 seconds), problem or promise (3 to 8), proof or demo (8 to 20), offer (20 to 26) and call to action (26 to 30). The hook stops the scroll, the promise tells viewers what they'll get, the proof shows it's real, and the ending tells them exactly what to do. Keep one message throughout, show the brand inside the action from the start, and make every beat work with the sound off.

Key takeaways

  • Google's ABCDs give the shape: Attention, Branding, Connection and Direction (a call to action).
  • Facebook's tips, as reported in 2021: single focus, brand near the start, get to the point and open with a strong hook.
  • TikTok advises introducing your proposition in the first 3 seconds and closing with a clear call to action.
  • On YouTube skippable ads, viewers can skip after 5 seconds, so the hook and promise must land before then.
  • One ad, one message. If you have three benefits, make three ads.

The template

SecondsBeatJobOn screen
0 to 3HookStop the scrollThe result, a face mid-sentence or the product in action, with 3 to 7 words of text
3 to 8PromiseSay what the viewer getsOne clear benefit in plain words
8 to 20ProofShow it's realDemo, before and after, a real number, or the product being used
20 to 26OfferMake it concreteWhat it costs or what they get, only real terms
26 to 30Call to actionSay what to do nextWritten and spoken: "Book a free call", "Shop the range"

This mirrors the playbook's intro structure for YouTube, Promise, Proof, Path, stretched into a full ad.

What the platforms say

Google's ABCDs of effective video ads map onto the template almost beat for beat:

  • Attention: "Jump in" and "get to the heart of the story faster".
  • Branding: "Introduce your brand or product from the start and maintain that presence."
  • Connection: "Avoid doing too much in your ad. Keep messaging and language focused and simple."
  • Direction: "Be intentional and add a CTA to drive a specific objective. Say what you'd like viewers to do."

Search Engine Journal's summary of Facebook's video ad tips (2021) adds "single focus", "highlight your brand" near the start and "keep their interest" with twists and surprises. TikTok's creative best practices say to "introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds" and to guide viewers "on what to think, feel, or do with a strong CTA".

Why the order matters

Many people only watch part of an ad. If the benefit arrives at second 18, everyone who left at second 5 never hears it. Putting the promise early means a partial view still does some work, and putting proof in the middle gives people who stayed a reason to believe. On YouTube, Google's ad formats guide confirms skippable in-stream ads can be skipped after five seconds, so the hook and promise must both land before then.

Keeping the middle alive

Seconds 8 to 20 are where most ads lose people. Three ways to hold them:

  1. Change the picture every few seconds. A new angle, a close-up, a text label.
  2. Open a small question and answer it. "Here's the part most people get wrong..." then show it.
  3. Cut repetition. If the voiceover says what the picture already shows, cut one of them.

Worked example: a 30-second ad for a language app

Illustrative script:

SecondsPictureText / voice
0 to 3A traveller ordering confidently in a Lisbon café"Ordering in Portuguese after 3 weeks"
3 to 8Phone screen, app open, logo visible"10-minute lessons built around real conversations"
8 to 20Three quick cuts: a lesson, a speaking exercise, the café again"Practise the phrases you'll actually use"
20 to 26Price card"First week free, then £X a month" (real terms only)
26 to 30App store button"Download and start today"

The brand appears at second 3 inside the product shot rather than as a separate logo card, and the claim "after 3 weeks" should only be used if it's true for that person and typical results are made clear. The UK CAP Code requires evidence for objective claims and says ads must not mislead by exaggerating performance.

Adapting the template

The 30-second template flexes for different goals and placements.

Goal or placementHow to adapt
Awareness, new brandLonger promise and proof; softer call to action
Direct responseOffer earlier, around second 15; repeat the call to action
Retargeting people who watched beforeSkip the problem; open on the offer or new proof
Vertical feedsText higher in the frame; faster first second
Skippable YouTubePromise before second 5, when the skip button appears

Check each beat against your data. After launch, compare the template's timings with where viewers drop off. If many leave just before second 8, the promise beat may be too slow; if they leave around second 20, the offer may arrive before enough proof. See where viewers drop off in a video ad.

Keep one message. The template only works if each beat serves the same promise. If you're tempted to add a second benefit, make a second ad instead.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches video ad structure and pacing?

The Retention Editing Playbook covers structure and pacing at £1,000 one-time. Google's ABCDs and TikTok's creative guide are free alternatives.

Should the call to action come only at the end?

Put the main call to action at the end, but a short mention or on-screen offer earlier helps people who leave early.

Can I use the same structure for a 15-second ad?

Yes, compressed: hook 3 seconds, promise and proof 8, call to action 4.

Where should my logo go?

Inside the action early, such as on the product or packaging, rather than as a separate intro.
