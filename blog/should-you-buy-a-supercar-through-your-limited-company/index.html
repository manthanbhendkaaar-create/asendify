Should you buy a supercar through your limited company?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 18 November 2026 · Last updated 18 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Usually not, if the main reason is tax. In the UK, a petrol supercar emitting over 50g/km of CO2 only qualifies for 6% capital allowances a year, and if you use it privately, including commuting, you pay income tax on it as a company benefit while the company pays National Insurance. The sums rarely beat buying it personally from taxed income.

This page is education only, not tax or financial advice. Speak to an accountant before you buy any car through a company.

Key takeaways

  • Cars do not qualify for the annual investment allowance or full expensing, so the company cannot write off a supercar in year one.
  • New zero-emission cars get a 100% first-year allowance; cars at 50g/km or less get the main rate (14% a year from April 2026); cars over 50g/km get just 6% a year.
  • Any private use by you or your family, including commuting, makes the car a taxable benefit for you, based on its value and CO2 emissions.
  • Your employer (your company) pays National Insurance on benefits instead of you.
  • For most directors, a high-emission supercar is cheaper overall bought personally; an electric performance car is the main exception worth modelling.

How capital allowances work for cars

GOV.UK's page on capital allowances for business cars sets out the rules for cars bought from April 2021:

CarAllowanceWhat it means
New and unused, zero emissions (e.g. electric)100% first-year allowanceFull cost deducted from profits in year one
Second-hand electricMain rate14% a year on a reducing balance (18% before April 2026)
New or second-hand, 50g/km or lessMain rate14% a year on a reducing balance
New or second-hand, over 50g/kmSpecial rate6% a year on a reducing balance

The same page says cars do not qualify for the annual investment allowance, the super-deduction, full expensing or the 40% first-year allowance. Almost every petrol supercar is well above 50g/km, so it falls into the slowest band.

The benefit-in-kind problem

GOV.UK's guide to tax on company cars says you pay tax on the value to you of a company car if you or your family use it privately, including for commuting. The taxable value depends on what the car cost and its fuel type, and lower CO2 emissions reduce it. If the company pays for fuel for personal journeys, that fuel is taxed separately. HMRC has an online calculator for the exact figure.

On top of that, GOV.UK's page on National Insurance on company benefits explains that you usually do not pay National Insurance on job benefits; your employer pays it instead. When you own the company, that cost still comes out of your business.

For an expensive, high-emission car, the benefit is large. You would be paying personal tax every year on a big share of the car's value, while the company claims only 6% a year in allowances.

When a company car can make sense

  • New electric performance cars. The 100% first-year allowance and lower taxable value for low-CO2 cars change the maths. Model it with your accountant.
  • Genuinely business-only vehicles. If a car is never used privately, there is no benefit charge. In practice this is very hard to show for a supercar kept at home.
  • Sole traders and partnerships. GOV.UK says you work out the claim based on the proportion of business use; private use reduces the allowance.

Worked example

Example: a director's company has £300,000 of taxable profit and buys a used petrol supercar for £120,000 with CO2 well over 50g/km. The director drives it at weekends and to the office.

  • Capital allowance, year 1: 6% x £120,000 = £7,200. At the 25% main corporation tax rate, that saves the company £1,800.
  • Year 2: 6% x (£120,000 - £7,200) = £6,768, saving about £1,692.
  • Benefit in kind: because the car is used privately, the director pays income tax every year on its taxable value, and the company pays National Insurance on it. For a car of this price and emissions, HMRC's calculator will show an annual taxable value far larger than the allowance.

So the company saves under £2,000 a year in corporation tax, while the director takes on a much larger annual personal tax bill. Buying the car personally avoids the benefit charge altogether.

Questions to ask your accountant

Take these to the meeting, with the car's price, CO2 figure and your expected mileage:

  1. What would the annual taxable benefit be on this exact car? Ask them to run it through HMRC's company car calculator.
  2. Which capital allowance pool does it go into? Check whether it is main rate (50g/km or less) or special rate (over 50g/km).
  3. What happens when the company sells the car? Selling for more or less than its written-down value can create a balancing charge or allowance.
  4. How would I extract the money to buy it personally instead? Salary or dividends both carry personal tax, so compare the two routes like for like.
  5. Does an electric alternative change the answer? Ask for the same sums on a new zero-emission car.

A clear answer to these five usually settles the question in one meeting.

Costs that do not change with who owns it

Whoever holds the title, the car still costs the same to run: insurance, servicing, tyres, road tax and depreciation. Those running costs, not the purchase structure, decide whether a supercar is affordable. Work them out first, then ask your accountant whether a company purchase changes anything.

FAQ

Is there a planner that ranks supercars by what they cost per year at my mileage?

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner ranks cars that fit your budget by yearly cost at your mileage, with editable running costs. Your own spreadsheet is the free alternative.

Can my company write off a supercar in one year?

No. Cars do not qualify for the annual investment allowance or full expensing. Only new zero-emission cars get a 100% first-year allowance.

What capital allowance does a petrol supercar get?

Usually the special rate of 6% a year, because almost all petrol supercars emit over 50g/km of CO2.

Is commuting private use?

Yes. GOV.UK says commuting counts as private use for company car tax.

Is an electric supercar better through a company?

It can be, because new zero-emission cars get a 100% first-year allowance and low CO2 reduces the taxable benefit. Ask an accountant to model it.
