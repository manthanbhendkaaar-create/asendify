How do I make a hotel or B&B room tour video that guests watch to the end?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open on the moment a guest would remember (the view from the window, the made-up bed as the door opens), then walk the room in the order a guest experiences it: door, bed, window, bathroom, the little extras. Show the details people ask about before booking (bed size, shower, desk, storage, noise, step-free access), keep each room tour to roughly 20 to 60 seconds, and film it exactly as guests will find it. End with the room name and how to book. Room tours lose viewers when they open on the logo, rush past the bathroom or look nothing like the room people actually get.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia's 2026 data reports that videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate.
  • Google's Business Profile help allows videos "up to 30 seconds long" and says images "should represent reality".
  • Meta's Reels best practices recommend vertical 9:16 video, natural light and a clear payoff such as "a big reveal".
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "jump in" and "feature people".
  • Honesty is the retention strategy that matters most: a room that disappoints on arrival costs you reviews.

What guests want to see

QuestionShot that answers it
"How big is the room?"Wide shot from the doorway, then a slow walk to the window
"How big is the bed?"Bed with a person sitting on it, or a caption "King, 150 x 200 cm"
"What's the bathroom like?"Shower running, towels, toiletries
"Can I work here?"Desk, sockets, Wi-Fi speed on screen if known
"Is it quiet?"Window closed, honest note about street-facing rooms
"Is it accessible?"Step-free route, grab rails, door widths if relevant

Filming tips

  1. Shoot in daylight with all lamps on; open curtains for the view.
  2. Stabilise. A gimbal or a slow, steady walk; shaky footage makes small rooms feel smaller.
  3. Don't use ultra-wide lenses that distort size. Show the room as it is.
  4. Include a person where it helps scale: someone sitting on the bed or opening the wardrobe, with their consent.
  5. Capture natural sound (curtains, kettle, shower) and caption any speech.
  6. Make one video per room type, so guests see the room they're booking.
  7. Film vertical and horizontal: vertical for Reels and Shorts, horizontal for your website and booking pages.

Keep it honest

Show the room guests will actually get, at its normal standard and furnishing, not a one-off staged version. If the view is from some rooms only, say so. If the bathroom is shared, show it. Accurate videos set expectations and protect your reviews; misleading ones invite complaints and can breach advertising rules.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to open a short tour video?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and reveals for £1,720 one-time. Meta's Reels best practices are free.

How long should a hotel room tour be?

About 20 to 60 seconds per room type; under 30 seconds for Google Business Profile.

Should I use a wide-angle lens?

Avoid ultra-wide lenses that make rooms look bigger than they are.

Should I show the bathroom?

Yes. It's one of the things guests most want to see before booking.

Do I need a separate video for each room?

Ideally one per room type, so guests see the room they are booking.
