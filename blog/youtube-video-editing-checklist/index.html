The Pre-Upload Editing Checklist for Higher Retention

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Updated 11 October 2026

Short answer: Before you upload, check five things: the first 30 seconds deliver what the title and thumbnail promised, nothing in the video could be skipped, there is always a question left open, the visuals change before attention drops, and the video ends on its payoff. The checklist below turns those five into simple yes/no checks you can run on every export.

How should you use this checklist?

Run it on the final export, not the timeline. Watch the video once from start to finish at normal speed, the way a viewer would, ideally on your phone. Every "no" is a fix to make before you publish. If you edit for someone else, send the checklist with the draft so you both judge the video the same way.

Does the opening keep its promise?

  • Does the first frame show something interesting, not a logo or a blank wall?
  • Within the first few seconds, does the viewer know they are in the right place?
  • Does the opening match the title and thumbnail exactly?
  • Is the payoff of the video clear (what the viewer will get by staying)?
  • Have you removed long greetings, channel intros and "before we start" segments?
  • Is the first cut early? A long, static opening shot loses people.

The opening matters most because YouTube reports it separately: the "Intro" figure in your retention report shows what share of viewers are still watching after 30 seconds. More on this in our guide to hooks in the first 30 seconds.

Is there anything a viewer could skip?

  • Have you cut pauses, ums, false starts and retakes?
  • Is any point made twice? Keep the better version.
  • Is any setup longer than it needs to be?
  • Would the video still make sense without this section? If yes, cut it or shorten it.
  • Does every tangent earn its place?

A simple test: if you would be tempted to skip ahead while watching, a viewer will too.

Is there always a reason to keep watching?

  • Is at least one question open at every point in the video?
  • When you close one question, do you open the next?
  • Is there a fresh hook around the middle, where most videos sag?
  • Do you deliver everything you teased? Teasing and not paying off feels like clickbait.

Our guide to open loops in video editing shows how to build these in.

Do the visuals change before attention drops?

  • Is there any stretch where the frame does not change for a long time? Add an angle change, B-roll, a zoom or a graphic.
  • Are numbers, steps and key terms shown on screen?
  • Do on-screen visuals match what is being said at that moment?
  • Is on-screen text big enough to read on a phone?
  • Are effects helping the viewer understand, or just decorating?

Is the audio clean and consistent?

  • Is the voice clear and at a steady level from start to finish?
  • Is the music quiet enough under speech?
  • Are sound effects used with purpose, not on every cut?
  • Have you checked for clicks, background hum and sudden loud moments?
  • Do you have the rights to all music and sound effects used?

Bad audio makes people leave faster than average visuals do.

Does the ending pay off?

  • Does the video deliver the main payoff clearly?
  • Does it end soon after the payoff, instead of winding down for a minute?
  • Have you avoided announcing the ending early ("that's it for today")?
  • Does the final screen point to one next video, not five?

Are the title, thumbnail and video aligned?

  • Does the thumbnail show something that actually appears in the video?
  • Does the title make a promise the first 30 seconds confirm?
  • Are captions or subtitles accurate, especially names and numbers?
  • Are chapters (if you use them) clear, and do they avoid giving away the payoff too early?

For a step-by-step method, see how to write a hook that matches your title and thumbnail. Editing ads or shorts? The same checks apply, with a few changes covered in our guides to editing video ads and short-form retention editing.

What should you check after the video is live?

The checklist catches problems before upload. Retention data shows the ones you missed. A day or two after publishing (YouTube says key moments data typically takes one to two days to process), open the video's retention graph and look for:

  • The Intro figure: how many people stayed past 30 seconds.
  • Dips: sections viewers skipped or left during. Rewatch those exact seconds.
  • Spikes: sections people rewatched or shared. Either strong interest or something unclear.
  • Typical retention: how this video compares with your recent videos of similar length.

Add a line to your checklist for every new mistake you find. Our guide to reading your retention graph explains each shape in detail. Editing in Premiere? Our Premiere retention workflow shows where each check fits.

Copy-paste version

  1. First frame is interesting, no logo or long greeting.
  2. Opening matches the title and thumbnail.
  3. Payoff is clear in the first 30 seconds.
  4. No pauses, filler or repeated points.
  5. Every section earns its place.
  6. A question is open at every point.
  7. Fresh hook around the middle.
  8. Every tease is paid off.
  9. Visual change whenever a section drags.
  10. Numbers and key terms on screen, readable on a phone.
  11. Voice clear and level; music under speech.
  12. Music and sound effects cleared for use.
  13. Ends soon after the payoff.
  14. One clear next video on the end screen.
  15. Captions and chapters checked.
  16. Retention graph reviewed one to two days after upload.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check before uploading a YouTube video?

Check that the opening matches the title and thumbnail, nothing could be skipped, a question is always open, the visuals change before attention drops, the audio is clean, and the video ends soon after its payoff. Then check captions, chapters and the end screen.

How long should a pre-upload check take?

Long enough to watch the full export once at normal speed, as a viewer would. Watching on a phone helps you catch small text and audio problems.

How do I know which part of my video is losing viewers?

Open the video's retention graph in YouTube Studio a day or two after upload. Dips show where viewers skipped or left; rewatch those exact seconds and add the cause to your checklist.

Should I use a checklist for YouTube Shorts too?

Yes, with a shorter version. The opening matters even more, so check the first second, cut every pause, keep one question open and end right on the payoff.

Can an editing checklist replace a good idea or script?

No. A checklist stops a good video being let down by its edit. It cannot rescue an idea people do not want or a title the video does not deliver.
