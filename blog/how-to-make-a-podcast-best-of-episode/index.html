How do you make a best-of or highlights episode of a podcast that people watch?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 7 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Rizzle suggests "1-3 minute segments", roughly "3-5 highlights per episode", and that each should "stand alone" without the full episode context (Rizzle).
  • Spotify for Creators quotes an editor: "every clip that you use should be there for a reason", and advises not to be afraid to "utilize silence to help with your pacing" (Spotify for Creators).
  • The Podcast Host says montage-style episodes take more work than a regular episode, so they suit milestones rather than weekly publishing (The Podcast Host).
  • YouTube's retention report says "dips mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video" (YouTube Help). Each segment boundary in a best-of is a place to check.

What a best-of episode is for

A best-of or highlights episode gathers the strongest moments of your show into one new piece. It has three jobs. It gives new viewers a quick way into your back catalogue. It gives regular viewers a reason to come back at a milestone, such as an anniversary or a season end. And it gives you a second use of material you have already recorded.

It is not a pile of clips. A viewer who clicks a highlights episode expects a shaped piece with a reason to watch to the end. The Podcast Host notes that a montage-style episode takes more effort than a normal one, which is why it suggests them for milestones rather than weekly publishing. That article describes episodes built from listener or guest contributions, which is a different format, but the point about effort applies here too.

Choosing moments

Rizzle's guide lists the kinds of moments to look for: key insights or quotes, funny or emotional segments, important takeaways and answers to common listener questions. Add your own measure: your retention graph. YouTube's report shows spikes where viewers are "watching, rewatching, or sharing" a part, which is a sensible place to look for candidates in your own back catalogue.

Pick more than you need, then cut. Choose moments that make sense without the rest of the episode, because a viewer of the highlights episode has not heard the original. If a moment needs a long lead-in, either trim the start or leave it out.

Step-by-step: build the episode

  1. Decide the theme or question in one line. "Our best advice on first videos" is better than "highlights".
  2. Make a longlist. Go through retention graphs, comments and your own notes for strong moments. Aim for about twice what you need.
  3. Cut it to a shortlist. Rizzle suggests 3 to 5 highlights per episode, each 1 to 3 minutes. For a longer special you might use more, but check each earns its place.
  4. Check each moment stands alone. Read the first sentence cold. If it needs context, trim it or add a text line. (See our page on how much context a podcast clip needs.)
  5. Order for the viewer. Open with the strongest moment, not the first in time. Put a lighter one before a heavy one, and end on something with a clear close.
  6. Write the bridges. Use a short line, in voice or on screen, to link one moment to the next: why this one comes next, and where it came from. Keep each to one or two sentences.
  7. Label every source. Add a small caption with the episode name or number so viewers can find the full version.
  8. Make the transitions smooth. Rizzle advises smooth transitions to avoid abrupt cuts. A short beat of silence can help; Spotify's guide quotes an editor on using silence to help pacing.
  9. Keep audio levels consistent. Different episodes were recorded on different days, and level jumps push viewers away.
  10. Add a short opening and closing. Say what the episode is and who it is for, then point to the full episodes at the end.
  11. Write the title and description honestly. Say it is a best-of. Spotify's video podcast guidance (Spotify for Creators) warns against "any promises or claims you don't deliver on" in thumbnails; the same applies to titles.
  12. Check the graph at each boundary after publishing and adjust the order next time.

Where to publish it

YouTube's podcast help says to "avoid mixing clips, different shows, or uploads from other channels" in a podcast playlist, and to keep each show's playlist to its own full-length episodes (YouTube Help). A best-of is not a full episode, so think about whether it belongs in the main episode playlist or in a separate playlist for highlights. Google's podcast guidance suggests treating each long episode as an anchor and cutting clips for Shorts and social (Google), so you could also cut individual moments from the highlights episode as clips.

Length

There is no fixed length. A theme cut of five moments of two minutes each is about ten minutes plus bridges. A season recap with ten episodes may run longer, but each segment should be short. A good check is to ask whether the viewer would miss any segment if it were gone. If not, cut it.

Example: a best-of for a 50-episode show

Example: a business podcast reaches its 50th episode. The host decides on one theme: "the best advice on getting your first customer". The longlist has 14 moments, picked from retention graphs and listener comments. The shortlist has five, each between 70 and 150 seconds. The strongest, a guest's story about calling 30 people in a week, goes first. Each of the other four has a one-sentence bridge and an episode label. The host adds a 15-second opening and a 10-second closing with a pointer to the full episodes. The total is a little over ten minutes. After publishing, the host checks the graph at each boundary and notes which segment lost most viewers. This scenario uses invented numbers and is not a measured result.

Permissions and fairness

If guests appear, check that your agreement with them covers reuse of their recording in a compilation. Do not rearrange words so a guest appears to say something different. If a moment has changed in meaning since you recorded it, such as advice that is now out of date, say so in the bridge or leave it out.

What not to do

  • Do not stitch together clips with no bridges and call it an episode.
  • Do not include moments that need the full episode to make sense.
  • Do not use a misleading title to imply new material.
  • Do not make every segment the same length; vary the pace.
  • Do not forget to link to the full episodes.
