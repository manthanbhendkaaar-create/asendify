How do I edit a wedding or festival highlight reel so attendees keep watching?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open on the strongest moment with people in it, give the reel a simple arc (build, peak, close), keep it short, and vary shot size and sound so it never settles into one rhythm. Attendees watch for themselves and their friends, so faces and reactions matter more than wide scenic shots. Most highlight reels work best at around two to three minutes; a longer film can sit beside it for people who want the full day.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia reports "videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate" and that "engagement naturally declines after the five-minute mark" (Wistia). A short reel is easier to finish than a long one.
  • YouTube's retention report says spikes appear "when more viewers are watching, rewatching, or sharing those parts of your video" (YouTube Help). Attendees rewatch moments with themselves in them.
  • Mayer's coherence principle says "learning is more effective when extraneous information and content is excluded, rather than included" (Dartmouth). It is about learning, but cutting what does not serve the story is the same discipline.
  • The W3C says captions "not only include dialogue, but identify who is speaking and include non-speech information conveyed through sound" (W3C). Captions help guests watching without sound.

What attendees want from a reel

A client or organiser wants a reel that sells the event. Attendees want something different: to see the day as they felt it, to find themselves and their friends, and to share it. A reel that does both is built from people and emotion first, and scenery second.

Wedding reels and festival reels share a structure but differ in energy. A wedding reel moves between quiet and loud; a festival reel stays loud but needs breaks so it does not become noise. Both lose viewers when shots repeat, when the music has one level, or when the reel runs past the point where it has said everything.

Step by step: build the reel

  1. Decide the audience. Is this for the couple and guests, or for a client's marketing? That sets tone and length.
  2. Pick the best ten moments. Watch all footage and list the ones with faces, reactions, movement or sound. Aim for variety of people.
  3. Choose the opening. Start on a strong people moment within the first few seconds, not on a title card or drone shot.
  4. Sketch the arc. Build (arrivals, preparation, set-up), peak (ceremony or headline act), close (last dance or last song).
  5. Rough cut by moment, not by time. Do not follow the day in order if a different order tells it better.
  6. Cut on movement and emotion. Use a laugh, a turn or a step as the cut point.
  7. Vary shot size. Mix close, medium and wide; a run of the same size feels flat.
  8. Vary pace. Add a slower beat after a fast stretch so the next peak lands.
  9. Use music with a plan. Pick a track with a clear change, and check you have the licence for where it will be posted.
  10. Keep real sound. Laughter, vows, applause or a crowd roar adds truth.
  11. Add captions. Short captions help viewers on silent autoplay.
  12. Trim. Remove any shot that repeats a point; keep the reel under three minutes unless the client asks otherwise.
  13. End clean. A clear last image and a short end line; do not fade out slowly.
  14. Test on a phone. Most guests will watch on one.
  15. Check the graph. After posting, look for dips and spikes and note which moments were rewatched.

Example: a 3-minute festival reel

Example: an editor has six hours of footage from a one-day music festival. The first cut is 6 minutes, starts with a drone shot and a title and uses the same fast track throughout. The second cut opens on a crowd singing along, uses a 2-minute 40-second track with a drop at 1:20 and gives the headline act the biggest peak. The editor removes 14 repeated crowd shots, adds a quiet 6-second moment of friends on the grass before the final peak, captions the opening line, and ends on the main stage lights with the date of next year's event. They post the reel and note which moments get rewatched. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result.

Consent, music and privacy

Check what the couple or organiser has agreed about sharing, especially for children and private moments. Use music you are licensed to use on the platforms you post to; YouTube may block or claim a video otherwise. This is editing guidance, not legal advice.

What to avoid

  • Opening on scenery. People first.
  • One tempo throughout. Add a quiet beat.
  • Too long. Say it and stop.
  • Too many angles of the same moment. One or two is enough.
  • Hiding the real sound. Keep some laughter or applause.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on pacing event reels?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. It is not event-specific.

How long should a highlight reel be?

Two to three minutes suits most; make a separate longer film for people who want more.

Should I follow the day in order?

Not necessarily; follow the story, as long as it still makes sense.

Do I need captions?

They help people watching on mute, which is common on social feeds.

Can I use any song?

Only music you are licensed to use where you post it.
