What frame rate should you use for YouTube videos?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use the frame rate you recorded at. YouTube's own guidance is that "content should be encoded and uploaded using the same frame rate that was used during recording", and it accepts common rates of 24, 25, 30, 48, 50 and 60 fps. For most talking-head and vlog videos, 24, 25 or 30 fps is fine; choose 50 or 60 fps for gaming, fast action or footage you will slow down. In the UK, 25 or 50 fps avoids flicker from mains lighting.

Key takeaways

  • Match your timeline and export to your recording frame rate; converting between rates can cause stutter.
  • YouTube recommends 8 Mbps for 1080p SDR uploads at 24 to 30 fps and 12 Mbps at 48 to 60 fps.
  • 24 fps gives the familiar "cinematic" look, 30 fps suits TV-style content, and 60 fps makes motion smoother.
  • Set your shutter speed to about double the frame rate (for example 1/50 at 25 fps) for natural motion blur.
  • In 50 Hz countries such as the UK, lights pulse 100 times a second, so 25 or 50 fps with a 1/50 or 1/100 shutter avoids flicker.

What YouTube recommends

YouTube's recommended upload encoding settings are short and clear on frame rate:

  • "Content should be encoded and uploaded using the same frame rate that was used during recording."
  • "Common frame rates include: 24, 25, 30, 48, 50 and 60 frames per second (other frame rates are also acceptable)."
  • "Interlaced content should be deinterlaced before uploading", for example "1080i60 content should be deinterlaced to 1080p30."

The same page gives recommended bitrates for standard dynamic range (SDR) uploads, which rise with frame rate:

Resolution24, 25 or 30 fps48, 50 or 60 fps
1080p8 Mbps12 Mbps
4K (2160p)35 to 45 Mbps53 to 68 Mbps

YouTube does not reward one frame rate over another. What matters is that the footage looks right and plays smoothly.

Which frame rate suits which video?

Adobe's guide to frame rate describes 24 fps as giving "that classic cinematic look", 30 fps as typical of live TV, sport and news, and 60 fps as a rate that "increases the smoothness of action", popular for 4K and video game recording. It notes that 120 fps and above "produces slow-motion video".

The shutter speed rule

Adobe gives the common rule of thumb: "the shutter speed needs to be twice the frame rate" for realistic movement, which is the same idea as the 180-degree shutter. At 25 fps that means 1/50 of a second; at 30 fps, 1/60; at 60 fps, 1/120. Adobe also notes that higher frame rates need more light, because each frame is exposed for less time.

Why your lights flicker on camera

RED Digital Cinema's guide to flicker-free video explains that many lights "illuminate using high frequency pulses of light", and that "many types of artificial lighting flicker at twice the rate of the power source." Mains power is "50 Hz (in Europe and most of Asia) and 60 Hz (in North America), which cause 100 and 120 pulses of light each second, respectively."

Its rule is to choose a frame rate, and a shutter speed, equal to the pulse rate divided by a whole number. In the UK that means:

  • Frame rates: 25 fps (100 / 4) or 50 fps (100 / 2).
  • Shutter speeds: 1/50 (100 / 2) or 1/100 (the pulse rate itself).

RED notes that in North America "the standard 24 and 30 fps settings work with most shutter speeds (since 30x4 and 24x5 = 120)." That is why UK creators who copy American settings, 30 fps with a 1/60 shutter, sometimes see banding or flicker under office or LED lights. RED also warns that LEDs and faulty bulbs can flicker regardless, so always shoot a test clip.

Editing and exporting without stutter

  1. Set the project to your camera's frame rate before you add footage.
  2. Keep mixed footage to a minimum. If you combine 60 fps gameplay with a 25 fps face cam, decide which rate the final video uses and test playback carefully.
  3. Export at the same rate. Don't let the export preset change it.
  4. Use the right bitrate. YouTube's table above is a sensible floor for SDR uploads.

Worked example: a UK creator's settings

Example: Amir films a weekly talking-head tech review in his London flat under LED ceiling lights, plus short clips of phones being unboxed that he sometimes slows down.

  • Main camera: 4K at 25 fps, shutter 1/50. The 25 fps rate divides evenly into the 100 light pulses per second, so the ceiling lights shouldn't flicker, and 1/50 gives natural motion.
  • Slow-motion clips: recorded at 100 fps with a 1/100 shutter, then placed on the 25 fps timeline at 25%, giving four times slow motion. The double-the-frame-rate rule would suggest 1/200, but that is not a whole-number division of the 100 light pulses, so he keeps 1/100 to avoid flicker and adds a lamp because each frame gets less light.
  • Timeline and export: 4K, 25 fps, matching the main camera.
  • Bitrate: at least 35 to 45 Mbps, YouTube's SDR recommendation for 4K at 24 to 30 fps.
  • Before: he had copied a US tutorial and shot at 30 fps with a 1/60 shutter, and viewers commented on faint banding across the walls. After switching, the banding disappears in his test clip.

FAQ

Is there a self-paced course that teaches retention editing to creators who edit their own videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Accelerator is a self-paced course with assignments on your own footage. Free editing-software training is a fair alternative.

Is 24 or 30 fps better for YouTube?

Neither is better for the algorithm. 24 fps looks more cinematic and 30 fps looks more like TV. In the UK, 25 fps is often the safer choice under mains lighting.

Should I upload in 60 fps?

Only if you recorded at 60 fps and the content benefits from smooth motion, such as gaming or sport. Talking-head videos rarely need it.

Does YouTube support 25 fps?

Yes. YouTube lists 24, 25, 30, 48, 50 and 60 fps as common frame rates and accepts others too.

Why does my footage stutter after export?

Usually because the timeline or export frame rate doesn't match the recording. Set the project to the camera's rate and export at the same rate.

What shutter speed should I use at 25 fps?

About 1/50 of a second, double the frame rate. In the UK, 1/50 or 1/100 also avoids flicker from mains lighting.
