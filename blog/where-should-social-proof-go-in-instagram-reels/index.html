Where should social proof go in Instagram Reels to keep people watching?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

In an Instagram Reel, social proof usually works best straight after the hook, as a two-to-three-second proof that backs up the claim, and once more just before the call to action. Leading with it can waste the first seconds, which decide whether people keep watching. Use real reviews, results or customer clips, never invented ones: fake and hidden-incentive reviews are banned in the UK.

Key takeaways

  • In January 2025 Instagram added a View Rate insight showing "what percentage of their followers and non-followers continue to watch their video after the first three seconds", as reported by TechCrunch. The opening seconds are where most decisions happen.
  • Research on product pages by Northwestern's Spiegel Research Center found purchase likelihood for a product with five reviews was "270% greater" than for one with none, and that ratings between 4.0 and 4.7 tended to perform best. That's product pages, not videos, but it shows why proof matters.
  • The UK competition regulator's fake reviews guidance (CMA208, April 2025) explains that fake reviews, concealed incentivised reviews and misleading presentation of reviews are prohibited.
  • A Tuck School of Business working paper on Facebook video ads found text-heavy informational segments generally increased abandonment; a slow wall of review text can lose viewers.
  • Put proof where it answers the doubt the viewer has at that moment.

These are working placements used by editors and marketers, not measured rules. Test them on your account.

Why "right after the hook" is often the best spot

The hook earns the next seconds; proof keeps them. A viewer who stays past the first seconds is thinking "is this true?" A quick review, before-and-after or customer clip answers that.

It avoids a slow start. Opening with "Rated 4.8 by our customers" is common, but unless the number is remarkable, it rarely stops the scroll by itself.

It matches how people judge products. Spiegel's research on online reviews suggests that a handful of real reviews changes purchase decisions a lot, with smaller gains after the first five. You don't need a long montage.

Types of social proof for Reels

  • Customer clip. A real customer reacting or explaining, with permission.
  • Review screenshot. Large text, one review at a time, readable on a phone.
  • Star rating or review count. Only if accurate and current.
  • Before and after. Real results, honestly shown.
  • Press or award mention. Only if real and checkable.
  • User-generated content. With permission and correct disclosure if it was paid or gifted.

What the UK rules mean for your Reels

The CMA's guidance explains that businesses must not publish fake reviews, must not hide that a review was incentivised, and must not present reviews in a misleading way, such as only showing the good ones while suggesting they're typical. For Reels, that means:

  1. Use reviews from real customers who used the product.
  2. If you gave something in return for a review or video, disclose it.
  3. Don't edit a review to change its meaning.
  4. Don't imply a cherry-picked review represents everyone's experience.

This is general information, not legal advice; check the CMA guidance for your situation.

Worked example

Example: a skincare brand makes a 25-second Reel for a vitamin C serum. The first version opens on a 4-second slide of five review quotes, then shows the product. Instagram's insights show a low View Rate, meaning many people leave in the first three seconds.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on hooks and structure for Instagram Reels?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops, pacing and pattern interrupts with templates and a 30-day plan. Instagram's own insights are a free way to test.

Should I start a Reel with reviews?

Only if the proof itself is striking. Usually a hook first, then proof straight after, holds more viewers.

How many reviews should I show in a Reel?

Often one or two strong ones. Research on product pages found gains slow after the first few reviews.

Can I use a customer's video in my Reel?

Yes, with their permission. If they were paid or given something, disclose it.

Can I edit a review to make it shorter?

You can shorten it, but don't change its meaning. Misleading presentation of reviews is prohibited in the UK.
