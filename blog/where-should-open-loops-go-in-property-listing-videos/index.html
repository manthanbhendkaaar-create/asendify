Where should open loops go in a property listing video?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Tease the home's standout feature in the first 3 to 5 seconds without showing all of it (the garden glimpsed through a doorway, the view behind a closed blind) and reveal it fully in the last third of the tour. A smaller loop in the middle ("and there's one room the photos don't show") carries viewers past the hallway and utility shots. Meta's own creator guidance says popular short videos often build to a payoff such as a reveal, and a property tour has a natural one.

Key takeaways

  • The main loop is a teased "wow" feature: a view, a garden, a kitchen, a loft room.
  • Open it in the first seconds; reveal it after the less exciting rooms.
  • A loop must never hide a problem. In the UK, listings must include material information; National Trading Standards guidance lists items such as council tax band, price, tenure, utilities, parking and flood risk.
  • Short social versions should pay off by about 70% of their runtime; full tours can hold the reveal longer.
  • Check the YouTube retention graph: spikes show moments that were rewatched or shared. A spike on the reveal means the loop worked.

Why listing videos lose viewers

Most tours start at the front door and walk room by room. Buyers learn early that a three-bed semi has a hall, a lounge and a kitchen, and the video stops giving them reasons to watch. The best feature, often the garden or the view, comes last, after many viewers have gone.

An open loop solves this without changing the honest order of the tour: you show a hint of the best feature at the start, then let the tour build to it.

Honesty rules for property loops

Teasing a feature is fine. Hiding a fact is not.

  • Show key facts on screen early or at the end: price, tenure, council tax band and other material information your listing needs.
  • Don't use the loop to delay bad news. If the garden is shared, say so when you reveal it.
  • Don't use ultra-wide lenses that make rooms look bigger than they are.
  • Don't stage a view that isn't from the property.
  • Film the property as it will be sold, not with furniture or features that won't be included, unless clearly stated.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops for property videos?

The Retention Editing Playbook teaches open loops and reveals for £1,720 one-time; it is YouTube-first and applies to tours published there.

Should a property video start at the front door?

Start with a glimpse of the best feature, then go to the front door and tour in order.

Is teasing a feature misleading?

Not if the feature is real and shown in full later. Never use a loop to hide a problem or material fact.

Where should the price go?

On screen early or with the key facts at the end, alongside tenure and council tax band.

How long should a listing video be?

20 to 40 seconds for social, 60 to 120 seconds for portals and your website.
