Should you upload HDR video to YouTube?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Upload HDR to YouTube only if you actually shoot and grade in HDR and can check it on an HDR display. YouTube supports it, but the file must meet a strict spec: PQ or HLG transfer, Rec. 2020 colour, 10-bit or 12-bit depth and HDR metadata. YouTube then serves HDR to compatible phones and TVs and "will automatically convert HDR video to SDR videos when necessary" for everyone else. For most creators, a well-made SDR video is simpler and looks consistent everywhere.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's HDR spec: PQ or HLG (Rec. 2100), Rec. 2020 primaries and matrix, "10 bits or 12 bits", 720p to 2160p, 23.976 to 60fps.
  • "HDR videos must have HDR metadata in the codec or container to be played back properly on YouTube."
  • Recommended codecs are VP9 profile 2, AV1 and HEVC/H.265; ProRes, DNxHR and 10-bit H.264 work but need very high bitrates.
  • Viewers without HDR screens get an automatic SDR version; you can supply a .cube LUT to guide that conversion.
  • Free DaVinci Resolve includes HDR grading but works with 8-bit formats; Blackmagic lists 10-bit video under the paid Studio version.

YouTube's HDR requirements

YouTube's help page on uploading HDR videos lists the technical requirements:

RequirementYouTube's spec
Transfer function (EOTF)PQ or HLG (Rec. 2100)
Colour primaries and matrixRec. 2020; matrix Rec. 2020 non-constant luminance
Bit depth10 bits or 12 bits
Resolution720p, 1080p, 1440p, 2160p (UHD widths preferred over DCI)
Frame rate23.976 to 60fps
MetadataRequired; PQ should include SMPTE ST 2086 mastering data and MaxFALL/MaxCLL
Containers testedMOV/QuickTime, MP4, MKV
Recommended codecsVP9 profile 2, AV1, HEVC/H.265

If PQ mastering metadata is missing, the page says YouTube assumes values for a Sony BVM-X300 mastering display. HDR10+ dynamic metadata is optional.

How viewers see HDR on YouTube

HDR plays on compatible mobile devices and HDR TVs, and the page notes a Chromecast Ultra can stream HDR to an HDR TV. Everyone else gets an SDR version: YouTube "will automatically convert HDR video to SDR videos when necessary." You can supply a .cube LUT to control how that conversion looks. That matters because many viewers will see the SDR conversion, so you need to check both versions.

HDR vs SDR for a typical creator

QuestionSDRHDR
Camera neededAnyOne that records 10-bit HDR or log footage
Editing and gradingStandardHDR grading with an HDR-capable monitor to judge it
ExportYouTube's standard settings, for example H.264 MP410- or 12-bit, Rec. 2020, PQ/HLG, with metadata
What viewers seeThe same everywhereHDR on compatible screens; auto-converted SDR elsewhere
RiskLowBad metadata or grading can look washed out or too dark

YouTube's standard upload encoding settings for SDR are simpler: H.264 High Profile, 4:2:0, in an MP4, at 8 Mbps for 1080p at 24 to 30fps or 35 to 45 Mbps for 4K. Our page on what frame rate to use for YouTube covers the frame rate side.

Software: what you need

Blackmagic's DaVinci Resolve page says the free version includes HDR grading, while listing support for "10-bit video up to 120 frames per second" and advanced HDR grading under the paid Studio version. Since YouTube's HDR spec requires 10-bit or 12-bit, check that your software and export path really produce a 10-bit file. Our page on whether free DaVinci Resolve is good enough for YouTube compares the versions.

Common HDR mistakes

  1. Grading HDR on an SDR monitor. You can't judge highlights you can't see.
  2. Missing metadata. YouTube says HDR videos need metadata to play back properly.
  3. Exporting 8-bit. YouTube's spec calls for 10 or 12 bits.
  4. Ignoring the SDR version. Many viewers will watch the automatic conversion.
  5. Mixing HDR and SDR clips without care. Inserted SDR graphics or footage can look dull next to HDR shots.

Worked example: a travel creator

Example: Elena shoots travel videos on a phone that records 10-bit HLG HDR. Her last upload looked great on her phone but flat on her laptop.

Her laptop showed the SDR conversion, which explains the flat look. If preparing the LUT and checking both versions takes too long for her weekly schedule, switching to SDR delivery would be a reasonable trade-off: consistent results for everyone with less work.

FAQ

Is there a self-paced course that teaches retention editing to creators who edit their own videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Accelerator is a self-paced course with assignments on your own footage. YouTube's free help pages are a fair alternative for HDR settings.

Does YouTube support HDR?

Yes, for videos that use PQ or HLG with Rec. 2020 colour, 10-bit or 12-bit depth and HDR metadata.

What happens to HDR videos on non-HDR screens?

YouTube automatically creates an SDR version. You can provide a .cube LUT to guide the conversion.

Can I upload HDR in H.264?

YouTube says 10-bit H.264 works but needs very high bitrates; VP9 profile 2, AV1 and HEVC are recommended.

Why does my HDR video look washed out?

Common causes are missing HDR metadata, an 8-bit export or viewing the automatic SDR conversion.

Is HDR worth it for YouTube?

Only if you shoot in HDR, can grade on an HDR display and will check the SDR version. Otherwise SDR is simpler.
