Is there a free YouTube channel review that gives me 3 fixes without signing up?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 15 November 2026 · Last updated 15 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast reviews any public channel from its link in about 30 seconds and gives you a score out of 100 and three fixes you can do this week, with no sign-up, no card and no access to your account. It suits creators who want a quick outside view before spending money on a coach or agency. YouTube Studio's own analytics, free audit tools from other companies, and feedback threads in creator communities are fair alternatives.

Key takeaways

  • A no-sign-up review can only use public data: titles, thumbnails, upload dates and view counts.
  • Three specific fixes are more useful than a long report you never act on.
  • Your private numbers (click-through rate, retention, traffic sources) live in YouTube Studio and should confirm any fix before you commit to it.
  • If you want a person to review your analytics, use YouTube's channel permissions rather than sharing your password.
  • Re-check after 30 days of uploads, not the next day.

Is there a free channel review with 3 fixes and no sign-up?

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast asks only for your channel link. It reads your public channel page, your latest 15 public uploads and the thumbnails of your 8 most recent videos, then returns an overall score, separate title, thumbnail and consistency scores, a one-line verdict, five short roasts and three fixes to start this week. It also shows your best and worst title and rewrites the worst one. It is free, allows three roasts per person per day and needs at least three public uploads.

It does not check spoken hooks, audio, editing, private analytics or private and unlisted videos. For those you need YouTube Studio or a reviewer with access.

Why three fixes beat a 40-point checklist

Long audit reports feel thorough, but most creators act on two or three items and forget the rest. Three fixes, ranked, force a choice about what matters most this week. They also make it easy to see whether a change worked, because you aren't changing ten things at once.

Good fixes are specific and testable. "Improve your thumbnails" is not a fix. "Cut thumbnail text to two words so it reads on a phone" is. "Upload more" is not a fix. "You've gone 19 days without uploading; set a weekly day you can keep" is.

Check each fix against your own data

A public review can point at suspects. Your YouTube Studio data decides. Three checks cover most fixes:

  1. Packaging fixes (titles, thumbnails). Look at impressions click-through rate. YouTube's impressions and CTR FAQ says half of channels and videos sit between 2% and 10%, that CTR is lower when a video gets many Home page impressions, and that small changes are normal. Compare with your own recent videos from the same sources.
  2. Promise fixes (does the video deliver the title?). Look at the share still watching at 30 seconds. YouTube's audience retention guide calls this the intro figure and says a high one may mean the opening matched the title and thumbnail.
  3. Consistency fixes. Look at your upload dates and returning viewers over the next month.

If a fix improves clicks but the 30-second figure drops, the new packaging is overpromising. Roll it back or change the opening.

If you want a human to look too

Some problems only show inside your analytics: a traffic source drying up, one country dominating, or a video that holds viewers but never gets impressions. If you ask an editor, friend or coach to look, invite them with YouTube's channel permissions. The Viewer (Limited) role can see channel details and analytics but can't access revenue data or change anything. You can remove them when the review is done. Never share your Google password for a review.

Worked example

Example: Leo runs a 1,100-subscriber board game channel. He pastes his link into the roast and gets 61/100 with these fixes:

3. Keep a weekly upload dayGaps of 6 to 22 daysReturning viewers drop after long gaps

Leo applies fix 1 to his next three videos and fix 2 to two of them, and uploads every Thursday. After a month he re-runs the roast and compares click-through rate and 30-second retention with his previous month. He keeps the changes that moved his own numbers and drops the rest.

What to do after the review

  • Pick one fix to apply to your very next upload.
  • Note today's numbers (CTR, 30-second hold, views per video) so you can compare.
  • Give it at least three or four uploads before judging.
  • Re-run the review after a month and see if the score and your Studio numbers moved together.

When a free review isn't enough

If your packaging scores well but views still stall, the issue is usually inside the video: the opening, pacing or topic. A public review can't see that. At that point, read your retention graphs closely or get help from someone who can see them.
