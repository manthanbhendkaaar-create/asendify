What is a 6-second focused view on TikTok ads, and should I optimise for it?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A 6-second focused view is TikTok's paid view for its Video Views objective: it counts when someone watches your ad for at least 6 seconds, watches a shorter ad to the end, or interacts positively (a like, share, follow or click) within the first 6 seconds. You bid and pay per focused view. Optimise for it when the job of the ad is to be watched and remembered, such as a launch teaser or a brand clip of 6 seconds or less. If you want sales, leads or app installs, optimise for those instead and use focused views only as a check on whether your opening holds people.

Key takeaways

  • TikTok's Video Views objective is optimised for a metric called focused view, and you bid on cost per focused view.
  • There are three options: 6s focused view (for 1 to 6 second creatives), 15s focused view (7 to 15 seconds) and 15s focused view with the video completion rate toggle (16 to 60 seconds).
  • A focused view counts at the first qualifying event: the watch time, a full watch of a shorter ad, or a positive interaction. Interactions in the first second are not counted and not billed.
  • Focused view ad groups are capped at 7 views per person per 7 days.
  • A focused view is not the same as the ordinary "6-second video views" metric in reports, so don't compare them as if they were.

What TikTok counts as a focused view

TikTok's help page on the Video Views objective and its page on focused view optimisation updates set out the rules:

OptionRecommended creative lengthCounts as a focused view whenBest for (TikTok's wording, summarised)
6s focused view1 to 6 secondsWatched 6s, watched to the end, or a positive interaction in the first 6sEfficient, scalable reach and brand recall
15s focused view7 to 15 secondsWatched 15s, watched to the end, or a positive interaction in the first 15sLonger views and higher completion
15s with completion toggle16 to 60 secondsOptimises for full completionsCompleted views of the whole video

TikTok notes that turning on the completion toggle may make each focused view slightly more expensive, because it chases full watches. The first second is excluded: if someone taps like the instant the ad appears, that is not a focused view.

Focused views vs the other view numbers

TikTok's video play metrics page lists several numbers that look alike:

MetricWhat it counts
Video viewsTimes your video started to play (replays excluded)
2-second video viewsPlays of at least 2 seconds in an impression session
6-second video viewsPlays of at least 6 seconds, full plays of shorter videos, or an engagement in the first 6 seconds
6-second focused viewsViews of at least 6 seconds (or the full ad if shorter), or an interaction in the first 6 seconds; only for focused view products
6-second focused views (paid views)The watch-time part only
6-second focused views (paid interactions)Interactions that happened without a 6-second view
Focused view 6-second view rateShare of focused view impressions that were watched for 6 seconds, or in full

The split between paid views and paid interactions matters. If a lot of your focused views come from interactions rather than watching, the ad may be prompting quick taps without holding attention, which is worth knowing before you judge the edit.

When to optimise for it

Use it when:

  • The ad is a short brand or launch clip and being seen is the goal.
  • You want cheap, wide reach for a message people can take in within 6 seconds.
  • You plan to retarget viewers later with a longer, more detailed ad.

Don't use it when:

  • You need purchases, leads or installs. Optimising for views finds people who watch, not people who buy.
  • Your ad needs 20 seconds to make its point. Pick the 15s option with the completion toggle, or a conversion objective.

How to edit a creative for a 6-second focused view

  1. Make the whole message fit in 6 seconds. TikTok recommends 1 to 6 second creatives for this option. One idea, one product, one line.
  2. Open on the payoff. Show the product, result or face in the first frame. There is no time to build up.
  3. Put the brand on screen early. If brand recall is the goal, the logo or product should be visible before second 3, not only in an end card.
  4. Write one line of on-screen text. Short enough to read in a couple of seconds.
  5. Use sound that suits TikTok, but make sure the clip still makes sense with the sound off.
  6. Loop cleanly. A 6-second clip that loops smoothly may get watched more than once. Note that TikTok excludes replays from most view counts, but includes replay time in average play time.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks for very short video ads?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. TikTok's own creative guides are free alternatives.

Do I pay if someone watches 6 seconds and then likes the ad?

You are billed once, at whichever qualifying event comes first, according to TikTok.

Does a like in the first second count?

No. TikTok excludes interactions in the first second from focused views and from billing.

Is there a 2-second focused view?

No. TikTok reports 2-second video views, but the focused view options are 6 seconds, 15 seconds and 15 seconds with the completion toggle.

Can I set my own frequency cap?

TikTok's frequency cap setting supports Video Views campaigns using 6-second or 15-second focused view optimisation, in the ad group settings.
