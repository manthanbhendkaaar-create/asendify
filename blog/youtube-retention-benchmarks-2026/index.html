YouTube Retention Benchmarks 2026: Real Data from 900+ Shorts

By Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 24 November 2026

Short answer: Across 213 of our own Shorts with 30+ views, the median Short kept 42.6% of its length, and 20 to 29 second Shorts kept more (45.0%) than 30 to 39 second Shorts (39.1%). The retention curve mostly held through the first 5 seconds and fell below half the audience at about 9.9 seconds (median, 176 Shorts).

Our own channels' data. Every number on this page comes from YouTube Analytics and public view counts for Asendify's own YouTube channels only. We don't use client data and we name no outside channel. Sample sizes and date ranges sit under every table. Where our data was too thin to support a claim, we left the claim out. Data pulled 24 Nov 2026. Download the aggregate data (CSV).

Key numbers

  • Shorts keep about 43% of their length. That is the median average percentage viewed across 213 of our Shorts, each with 30+ views.
  • Shorter Shorts keep a bigger share. 20 to 29 second Shorts: median 45.0%. 30 to 39 second Shorts: median 39.1%.
  • Half the audience is gone by about the 10 second mark. That is the median point where a Short's retention curve first drops below 50% (176 Shorts).

Method

  • Source: YouTube Analytics API v2 (average view percentage, average view duration and audience watch ratio by elapsed video time) for the 7 Asendify channels that have Analytics access. Public view counts come from our daily YouTube Data API snapshots, which cover all 24 of our Shorts channels.
  • Window: views from 1 Sep 2026 to 24 Nov 2026, on videos published 2 Oct to 22 Nov 2026. Data was pulled on 24 Nov 2026.
  • Included: only videos our own pipeline made, matched to our upload log. We left out older personal uploads, a gaming back-catalogue on one channel, and videos uploaded to the wrong channel.
  • Threshold: a video needs 30+ views to count in the retention stats, because percentages on a handful of views are noise. 213 of the 941 Shorts met it.
  • Statistic: we report the median with its 25th to 75th percentile range. Means are in the CSV but get skewed by Shorts that loop past 100%.
  • Limits: these are small channels in a handful of niches (self-improvement, trading, AI and business, YouTube editing, books). Treat the numbers as a reference point, not a universal standard. YouTube's API doesn't expose the Shorts "viewed vs swiped away" figure, so we can't report it.
  • Left out as too thin: long-form retention (only 3 of our 55 long videos reached 30 views) and any long-form "still watching at 30 seconds" figure.

FAQ

What is a good average percentage viewed for YouTube Shorts?

In our own channels' data, the median Short kept 42.6% of its length (middle half: 35.4% to 51.7%; 213 Shorts with 30+ views, views from 1 Sep to 24 Nov 2026). 20 to 29 second Shorts kept a median 45.0% and 30 to 39 second Shorts 39.1%. These are small channels in a handful of niches, so treat the numbers as a reference point, not a universal standard.

When do viewers leave a YouTube Short?

Across 183 of our Shorts with a retention curve, the median curve was at 1.05 at 3 seconds (rewatches push it above 1.0) and 0.93 at 5 seconds, then fell to 0.49 at 10 seconds. The median Short dropped below half its audience at 9.9 seconds (176 Shorts). The biggest loss came between seconds 5 and 10, not in the first 3 seconds.

Are shorter YouTube Shorts better for retention?

They kept a bigger share of their length in our data: 45.0% median for 20 to 29 seconds against 39.1% for 30 to 39 seconds. But the longer Shorts were watched for about 1 second longer (12 s against 11 s median average view duration). We had only 8 Shorts of 40 to 59 seconds, too few to draw a conclusion.
