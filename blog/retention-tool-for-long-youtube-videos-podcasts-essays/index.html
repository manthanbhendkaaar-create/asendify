Key takeaways

  • Average percentage viewed naturally falls as videos get longer, so compare long videos with long videos, not with your Shorts or 8-minute uploads.
  • YouTube's Analytics API includes relativeRetentionPerformance, which compares retention "in comparison to all YouTube videos of similar length".
  • YouTube Studio's typical retention view compares a video with your 10 latest videos of similar length.
  • Chapters help viewers navigate long videos; YouTube requires at least three, starting at 00:00, each at least 10 seconds long.
  • In long videos, the actionable drops are usually topic transitions, long tangents and sponsor reads.

Why long videos need a different reading

On a 10-minute video, losing half the audience by the end is common. On a 60-minute podcast, most viewers will not watch the whole thing, and some arrive for one segment. So a low overall average does not mean the episode failed. YouTube's Analytics API metrics recognise this: relativeRetentionPerformance measures how well a video retains viewers "in comparison to all YouTube videos of similar length", from 0 (worse) to 1 (better), and averageViewPercentage is "the average percentage of a video watched during a video playback".

YouTube's help on key moments for audience retention adds that gradual declines are typical as playback continues, while dips mark moments viewers skipped or stopped watching, and that typical retention compares your 10 latest videos of similar length. For long videos, the dips are what to act on.

Chapters: helpful, but read them with care

Chapters let viewers jump to the part they want. YouTube's guide to video chapters says the first timestamp must start at 00:00, you need at least three in ascending order, and each must be at least 10 seconds long; automatic chapters are on by default for new uploads, and your own timestamps override them. Chapters can create spikes at chapter starts and dips where viewers skip ahead, so a dip after a chapter marker may be navigation rather than boredom. Keep that in mind before cutting a section.

FAQ

Is there a retention tool that lists every drop-off timestamp in long videos, with fixes?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor emails each upload's drop-off timestamps with a fix for each, using read-only access. YouTube Studio's graph is the free alternative.

What is a good retention rate for a long YouTube video?

There is no single figure. Compare with your own videos of similar length, as YouTube's typical retention view does, rather than with shorter uploads.

Do chapters hurt retention?

They change how viewers move through a video. Dips after chapter markers can be people skipping ahead, so read them carefully before cutting.

Should I split a long podcast into shorter videos?

Many creators publish the full episode plus clips. Spikes in your graph show which moments are worth clipping.
