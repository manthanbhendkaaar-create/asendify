Key takeaways

  • Facebook's own tips, reported by Search Engine Journal, say video ads "should be able to be viewed, understood, and enjoyed without sound".
  • The same tips note that "movement or fast edits at the start of an ad can capture consumers' attention".
  • TikTok recommends "5-10 words per second" at most for on-screen text, and captions or overlays "to provide context to your ad".
  • Captions must be accurate. The W3C warns that "automatically-generated captions do not meet user needs or accessibility requirements" unless they are checked.
  • Sound-off design is a retention skill: the picture and text must carry the hook and the message.

What a sound-off guide needs to cover

A sound-off ad has to work for someone scrolling in a quiet office or on a train. That means:

  • A visual hook in the first frames: movement, a face, a product in use, or a surprising image.
  • Text that carries the message, short enough to read at a glance.
  • Accurate captions for any speech, including words that change meaning.
  • A story that reads in pictures: problem, product, result.
  • A visual call to action, since a spoken "link below" won't be heard.

A method for sound-off video ads

  1. Watch your ad muted. If you can't tell what it's selling within a few seconds, start there.
  2. Open on movement or a face. Avoid a static logo frame.
  3. Write the hook as text. One short line that raises a question or names a problem.
  4. Show, don't tell. Replace spoken explanation with the product doing its job.
  5. Caption every spoken line. Check auto-captions word by word.
  6. Keep text in a safe area. Away from platform buttons and captions.
  7. Put the call to action on screen. A clear line and, if allowed, the product or offer.
  8. Test muted and unmuted. Ask someone unfamiliar with the ad to watch silently and tell you what it said.

Example: a small brand sells a reusable water bottle and runs a 20-second Reels ad.

The brand runs both versions to similar audiences and compares how long people watch on average. If the sound-off version holds viewers longer, the visual hook is doing its job; if not, they test a different opening image. Results depend on the product and audience; there's no guaranteed outcome.

Common mistakes

  • Relying on a voiceover hook. If the hook is only in the audio, most silent viewers miss it.
  • Walls of text. If viewers can't read it in time, they skip.
  • Unchecked auto-captions. A single wrong word can change the claim.
  • Text over the product. Make sure overlays don't hide what you're selling.
  • A spoken-only call to action. Put it on screen.

FAQ

Is there a guide that teaches visual hooks for sound-off viewing?

Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches visual hooks and pacing as part of a full retention method for £1,720 one-time. Facebook's and TikTok's free creative guides cover sound-off basics.

Do all video ads need captions?

Any ad with speech should have accurate captions, both for sound-off viewers and for accessibility.

Are auto-generated captions good enough?

Only if you check them. The W3C says automatic captions alone don't meet accessibility needs.

How much text should a sound-off ad have?

Little enough to read easily. TikTok suggests no more than 5-10 words per second.

Should I still use music in a sound-off ad?

Yes, for people who do turn the sound on, but the ad must make sense without it.
