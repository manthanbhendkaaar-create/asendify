How do I find where viewers drop off in my video ad, and why?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Ad platforms don't show a second-by-second retention graph for ads the way YouTube Studio does for your own videos, but they do show checkpoints: how many viewers reached 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of the ad, plus early markers such as 2-second, 3-second or 6-second views. Find the biggest fall between two checkpoints, convert those percentages to seconds in your edit, then watch that stretch of the ad and ask what happens there: a slow explanation, a scene change, a repeated line, or the moment the promise is delivered too late.

Key takeaways

  • Google Ads: "video played to" metrics count the share of people who watched 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% of the video.
  • TikTok reports 2-second and 6-second views and plays at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%.
  • On Meta, 3-second plays and ThruPlays (15 seconds by default) give early and mid checkpoints.
  • Convert quartiles to seconds: in a 20-second ad, 25% is second 5 and 50% is second 10.
  • Always expect some decline. Look for the steepest fall relative to the others, not any fall.

Google explains each "video played to" percentage as viewers still watching at that point divided by the number of times the player started. TikTok defines "video views at 25%" as the number of times a video "was played at least 25% of its length". On Meta, a ThruPlay is 15 seconds or near-completion by default, according to MarTech's report, and PPC Land reported in November 2026 that a 6-second option is being added.

A weekly drop-off review routine

Finding drop-offs once is useful; checking them every week is how ads keep improving. A 20-minute routine:

  1. Export or note the checkpoints for each active ad: impressions, early views (2, 3 or 6 seconds), quartiles and the main result.
  2. Convert to seconds using the ad's length, and write the timeline beside them.
  3. Find the steepest fall after the opening for each ad.
  4. Watch that stretch with the sound on and off.
  5. Write one hypothesis per ad, such as "the price card at second 12 feels like a hard sell", and one change to test.
  6. Launch one new version with only that change, against the current version.
What you seeCommon causeTypical test
Fall just before 25% on a short adPromise not clear yetState the benefit in the first 3 seconds
Fall around a long explanationTalking without showingReplace with a demo or on-screen steps
Fall at a scene changeChange feels like the ad is endingUse a bridging line or keep the same person on screen
Fall at the price or offerOffer arrives before value is clearShow more proof first, or move the offer later
Little fall but few resultsRetention isn't the problemTest the offer, call to action or landing page

Compare like with like. Quartiles mean different things on a 6-second bumper and a 60-second ad. Compare an ad with earlier versions of itself, or with ads of similar length and placement.

Check sound-off. Many drop-offs happen where the message relies on audio. Watch the drop-off stretch muted; if nothing on screen explains what's happening, add text or a clearer visual. See how to make a video ad work with the sound off.

Keep a log. Record each change and its effect. After a few months you'll have your own rules about what holds your audience, which is more useful than any general benchmark. For keeping the middle moving, see pattern interrupts in video ads.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to fix drop-off points in video?

The Retention Editing Playbook covers reading and fixing drop-offs at £1,000 one-time. Platform help centres are free alternatives.

Can I see a second-by-second retention graph for my ads?

Ad platforms mainly give checkpoints such as quartiles and 2, 3, 6 or 15-second views. Use those to locate the problem.

Is a big drop at the start normal?

Yes, especially on skippable or scrolling placements. Compare with your other ads.

How do I know which scene caused the drop?

Convert the checkpoint to seconds and watch what happens just before it.
