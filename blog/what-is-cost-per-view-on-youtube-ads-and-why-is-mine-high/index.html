What is cost per view (CPV) on YouTube ads, and why is mine so high?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Cost per view (CPV) is what you pay, on average, each time someone views your YouTube video ad. Google only counts a view when the person watches a set amount or interacts: 30 seconds of a skippable in-stream ad (or the whole ad if it's shorter), 10 seconds of an in-feed or Shorts ad, or a click on the ad, its thumbnail or its call to action. So a high CPV usually means one of three things: the auction for your audience is expensive, your bid target is out of line with Google's estimate, or too few people watch long enough to count as a view. Only the last one is an editing problem, and it is the one most advertisers can fix fastest.

Key takeaways

  • With target CPV (tCPV) bidding you set the average amount you're willing to pay per view; some views cost more and some less.
  • What you actually pay is reported as actual CPV.
  • In-stream views need 30 seconds (or the full ad if shorter) or an interaction; in-feed needs a thumbnail click or 10 seconds of autoplay; Shorts needs 10 seconds or a click on the CTA, Like, Comments or Share.
  • Google warns that a bid that's too low can stop your campaign competing in the auction.
  • Better openings and pacing mean more of the people you reach turn into counted views.

How Google counts a view

From Google's pages on cost-per-view bidding and video views campaigns:

Ad formatCounts as a view when the person...
Skippable in-streamWatches 30 seconds, or to the end if the ad is shorter, or interacts
In-feedClicks the thumbnail, or watches the autoplay for at least 10 seconds (or to the end if shorter)
ShortsWatches 10 seconds, or clicks the CTA, Like, Comments or Share

Google also says video views campaigns are moving to multi-format ads with target CPV as the bid strategy.

The three reasons CPV runs high

CauseHow to spot itWhat to do
Expensive audienceCPV high across every ad in the campaign, even strong onesBroaden targeting, test other audiences or schedules
Bid out of lineLow impressions, campaign struggling to spendSet the target near Google's estimate shown during setup
Weak watch-throughPlenty of impressions, low view rateFix the edit: hook, pace, length

Start with the third. If the same campaign has one ad with a much lower CPV than the others, the audience and bid are the same, so the difference is in the video.

Why the edit drives CPV

Your CPV is shaped by how many impressions you need to buy before someone watches far enough to count. If one in ten people who see your in-stream ad watch 30 seconds, you are effectively paying for ten impressions to get one view. Raise that to two in ten and, other things equal, each view needs fewer impressions. Google's view rate is that share: views divided by impressions.

On skippable ads, the first 5 seconds decide most of it, because that is when the skip button appears. On Shorts and in-feed, the first second has to stop the swipe or earn the click.

Edits that raise view rate

  1. Start with the most interesting shot. Not a logo, not a slow pan. The product working, a face mid-sentence, or the result.
  2. Say who it's for in the first 5 seconds. People who recognise themselves are more likely to stay past the skip button.
  3. Promise something worth staying for. "Here's the one setting that fixed it" gives a reason to keep watching.
  4. Cut dead time. Every pause before second 30 costs you views.
  5. Match the length to the format. A 20-second in-stream ad counts as a view when watched to the end, so a tight, shorter cut can earn views a 60-second cut misses.
  6. For Shorts, edit vertically and natively. Text inside the safe area, sound designed for the feed.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches YouTube hooks that stop skips?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCD guidance is a free alternative.

What is a good CPV on YouTube?

It depends heavily on country, audience and format, and Google doesn't publish one figure. Compare against your own ads in the same campaign.

Do I pay if someone skips at 5 seconds?

On CPV bidding, a skip before 30 seconds (on an ad longer than 30 seconds) without an interaction is not a view.

What's the difference between target CPV and actual CPV?

Target CPV is the average you set; actual CPV is what you paid on average.

Will a shorter ad lower my CPV?

It can, because a full watch of an ad under 30 seconds counts as a view. Test it rather than assume.
