What should a client include in a video ad brief to an agency?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A client's video ad brief to an agency should cover the business goal and how success will be measured, who the ad is for, the single message, the proof you can stand behind, mandatory brand and legal points, the budget and placements, who approves the work, and the key dates. Keep it short and specific. Agencies say vague briefs are one of the main reasons ads miss, and that shows up later as slow openings and unclear offers that lose viewers.

Key takeaways

  • In a BetterBriefs/IPA study, 80% of marketers said they write good briefs; only 10% of agencies agreed.
  • Only 30% of marketers had clear criteria for judging work against the brief, according to the IPA-backed best-practice guide.
  • Say how you'll measure success before work starts: views, watch time, clicks or sales.
  • One message per ad. Extra messages belong in extra ads.
  • Name one person who gives final approval, so feedback doesn't arrive from five directions.

Why the client brief matters for retention

Most retention problems in finished ads trace back to the brief. If the brief lists five benefits, the editor squeezes them into 20 seconds and the offer arrives late. If nobody says who the ad is for, the opening speaks to everyone and stops no one.

The research on briefing is blunt. The BetterBriefs study published by the IPA in 2021 surveyed over 1,700 marketers and agency staff in more than 70 countries. Marketers estimated that 33% of marketing budget goes to waste through poor briefs and misdirected work. 78% of marketers said their briefs gave clear strategic direction; 5% of agencies agreed. And 89% of marketers said it is hard to produce good creative work without a good brief.

A later UK survey for the guide The best way for a client to brief an agency, written by BetterBriefs with Mark Ritson in partnership with the IPA, found 73% of agencies said the briefs they receive aren't good enough, and only 30% of marketers had clear evaluation criteria.

Goal and success measure

Say what the ad is for and how you'll judge it. If the goal is sales, say so; an ad built for awareness looks different. If you plan to run a test, say which metric decides it. Google Ads video experiments, for example, ask you to pick one success metric, such as brand lift, conversions or view rate, and Google recommends starting with a hypothesis.

Audience and message

Describe the viewer as a person with a problem, not a demographic band. Then write the single message. Google's ABCDs of effective video ads recommend keeping messaging "simple and focused" and including a deliberate call to action. If you can't fit the message in one sentence, the ad will struggle to hold attention.

Proof

List the facts you can stand behind: results, awards, demos, customer stories. If you supply testimonials, UK rules require the advertiser to hold evidence they are genuine and to have permission to use them, as the ASA explains in its testimonials guidance.

Placements and formats

List the platforms. Each needs different shapes and openings; TikTok's creative best practices, for instance, recommend vertical 9:16 and putting the proposition in the first 3 seconds. Knowing placements up front lets the agency plan the shoot and edit for every version.

Approvals

Name one person who gives final approval and agree the number of review rounds. In the BetterIdeas Project, reported by Marketing Week, only 30% of agencies said they receive clear, constructive feedback, compared with 52% of marketers who believed they give it. A named approver and consolidated notes close that gap.

What to leave out

  • Long company histories (link them instead).
  • Lists of every product feature.
  • Vague goals like "make it pop" or "go viral".
  • Creative solutions disguised as briefs ("we want a 3-second logo intro"); describe the problem and let the agency propose.

Worked example

Example: a regional gym chain sends an agency a brief that says "Promote our January offer across social. Show our facilities, classes, café, app, personal training and new opening hours."

The agency asks for a rewrite. The new brief says:

  • Goal: 400 trial sign-ups in January.
  • Success measure: cost per trial sign-up; test two openings in week one.
  • Audience: people who quit a gym before and feel intimidated going back.
  • Single message: "Your first two weeks are free, with a coach to show you round."
  • Proof: photos and quotes from three real members (consent on file).
  • Placements: Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube in-stream; 15 and 30 seconds.
  • Approvals: marketing manager, two rounds.

The café, app and opening hours are saved for later ads. The edit now has one clear promise to open on.

FAQ

Is there a template for briefing an agency on a video ad?

Yes: the nine-section table above. The IPA-backed guide The best way for a client to brief an agency goes deeper on strategy.

How long should a client brief be?

One to two pages is usually enough. Link supporting documents rather than pasting them in.

Should the client suggest creative ideas?

Share references you like, but describe the problem rather than prescribing the solution.

What's the most common brief mistake?

Too many messages. One message per ad gives the edit a clear opening and a clear ending.

Should the brief include a budget?

Yes. Budget affects how many versions, lengths and tests are realistic.
