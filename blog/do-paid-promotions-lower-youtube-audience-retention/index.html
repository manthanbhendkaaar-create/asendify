Do paid promotions lower my YouTube audience retention, and how do I see organic retention only?

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's audience retention report separates organic views ("the direct result of user intention") from paid views ("the result of paid placement").
  • Paid views often reach new audiences, who can retain worse than subscribers, so a blended graph can make a good video look weak.
  • YouTube says running a video as an ad has no impact on its organic performance; the two systems are separate.
  • Judge the edit on the organic segment. Judge the targeting on the paid segment.
  • Compare like with like: organic retention against your typical retention, not against a graph full of ad views.

Why ad views can pull the graph down

YouTube's help page on key moments for audience retention describes two kinds of traffic in the report. Organic views are "the direct result of user intention": someone searched, clicked a suggestion, or opened your channel. Paid views "result from paid placement", such as an in-stream or in-feed ad.

Those two groups behave differently. Someone who chose your video already wanted the topic. Someone who saw it as an ad was in the middle of doing something else. Search Engine Journal's report on YouTube separating organic and paid metrics (November 2025) makes the same point: advertising often reaches new audiences who may engage less than existing subscribers, which can lower overall retention and click-through figures when everything is blended.

How to see organic retention only

  1. Open YouTube Studio and go to Content.
  2. Click the video, then Analytics and the Engagement tab.
  3. Open the audience retention report.
  4. Switch the view to organic views only. YouTube's help page lists organic and paid views as separate traffic types in this report, and the Segments drop-down adds new vs returning viewers and subscribers vs non-subscribers.

YouTube notes that "you might need to enable Advanced Mode to access certain data". Advanced mode also lets you compare videos or groups with Add comparison, which is useful for putting a promoted video next to an unpromoted one of similar length.

Does promoting a video hurt it organically?

YouTube's position, as reported by Search Engine Journal, is that organic and paid systems work independently, and whether a video also runs as an ad "has no impact" on its organic performance. So a weak blended graph is a reporting problem, not a penalty. The risk is a decision problem: re-editing a video that is fine, or blaming the edit when the targeting was the issue.

The Intro key moment, which YouTube defines as "what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds", is the quickest single number to compare between segments.

Worked example

Example: a cooking channel spends £150 promoting a 12-minute recipe video. The blended graph shows 38% of viewers still watching at the two-minute mark, against a typical retention band that suggests the channel usually holds closer to half.

The creator switches the segment to organic only. Organic viewers are at 52% at two minutes, slightly above typical. The paid segment is at 24%. The edit isn't the problem; the ad audience is colder. Instead of re-cutting the video, the creator:

  • Leaves the main video unchanged.
  • Makes a 45-second ad cut that opens with the finished dish, not the ingredients list.
  • Narrows targeting to people interested in home cooking.

A month later they compare the two segments again, the same way, before spending more. The figures are illustrative, but the method is the one YouTube's segments are designed for.

Common mistakes

  • Re-editing a video because of a blended graph. Check the organic segment first.
  • Comparing a promoted video's retention with your unpromoted videos. The audiences aren't the same.
  • Ignoring the paid graph. It is free feedback on how a cold viewer reacts to your first seconds.
  • Reading key moments without enough data. YouTube only shows key moments for videos of at least 60 seconds with at least 100 views.

Do YouTube ads lower audience retention?
