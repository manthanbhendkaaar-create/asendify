Is there a ready-made retention checklist for podcast editors?

Key takeaways

  • Spotify tells podcasters that "Episode drop-off data is the key to knowing which episodes and segments held your audience's attention".
  • Early drop-off is the biggest risk. Spotify says listeners "have a tendency to drop off early".
  • Apple counts an "engaged listener" as someone who played at least 20 minutes or 40% of an episode, a useful target for editors.
  • Dips at ad breaks often recover because listeners skip ahead, so don't over-read them.

What a podcast editor can actually control

The host controls the topic and the guest. The editor controls order, length and flow. That is a lot. An editor can move the best story to the cold open, cut a ten-minute warm-up chat, tighten a guest's long answer and place ads where they hurt least. The checklist below sticks to those levers.

The checklist

SectionCheckFix if it fails
Cold openDoes the episode open on a strong moment from later?Pull a 20-60 second clip that raises a question
IntroIs the theme and housekeeping under about a minute?Trim; move sponsor reads and calls to subscribe later
First topicDoes the conversation reach the main subject quickly?Cut small talk; start at the first real question
Long answersAny answer that circles back to the same point?Keep the clearest version
SignpostsDo listeners know what's coming next?Add a short host line: "Next, why the deal nearly fell apart"
Ad breaksDo breaks sit at natural pauses, not mid-story?Move to the end of a segment
AudioDo levels match between host and guest?Level, de-noise and check loudness
Video (if any)Does the shot change often enough?Cut between angles at speaker changes
EndingDoes it end soon after the last strong point?Short wrap and one clear next step

How to use it with real data

  1. Check past episodes. Apple's listener analytics and Spotify's episode charts show how much people play. Apple even prompts: "Are listeners skipping over your episode intro or your second mid-roll?"
  2. Match dips to timestamps. Spotify suggests matching retention dips to segment timestamps to see which part is causing them.
  3. Look for steep, repeated drops. Spotify says "Steep and sudden changes in retention are the best evidence". A gentle slope is normal.
  4. Apply the checklist to the next edit, focusing on the sections where past episodes lost people.
  5. Compare like with like. Spotify recommends comparing episodes with similar themes and structure so you can tell what changed.

Worked example

Example: an editor works on a weekly 55-minute interview show. Spotify data from the last six episodes shows a steep drop in the first 4 minutes and a smaller one around 30 minutes.

ProblemCause foundEdit
Steep drop 0:00-4:0090-second theme, sponsor read, then host catch-upAdd a 40-second cold open from minute 38; cut catch-up; move sponsor to minute 12
Drop around 30:00Guest answers a vague question for 6 minutesTrim to the 2 best minutes; add host signpost before next topic
Small dip at 22:00Mid-roll adLeave it; many listeners skip ads and come back
Ending runs 5 minutesLong thank-yousCut to 60 seconds

The new episode runs 47 minutes. The editor compares its early curve with the last six. If the early drop is smaller, the cold open stays in the template. That result belongs to this show; it is a test, not a promise.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Cutting for length alone. A shorter episode isn't better if you remove the best story.
  • Cold opens that give away the answer. Raise the question; keep the answer for later.
  • Ads in the middle of a story. Listeners lose the thread and may not come back.
  • Over-editing natural speech. Removing every pause can make speech sound robotic.

Adapting the checklist for audio-only shows

Audio-only podcasts can't use visual changes to reset attention, so editors lean on sound and words instead. Useful tools include a short music sting between segments, a host signpost line ("Up next: the deal that nearly collapsed"), changes of voice (bringing in a clip, a listener question or a second host) and tighter answers. The cold open and intro rules apply exactly as they do for video. Check loudness consistency across segments too: listeners on headphones notice sudden jumps, and an uncomfortable volume change is a common reason to stop listening.

FAQ

Is there a ready-made retention checklist for podcast editors?

Yes. The table on this page is free to copy, and Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook adds a fuller checklist with hook and open-loop templates.

How long should a podcast intro be?

Short enough that the listener reaches real content quickly; many editors aim for under a minute before the first substantive point.

Should every episode have a cold open?

Not always, but it is a good default for interview shows, where the best moment is often deep in the recording.

Are ad-break dips a problem?

Not necessarily. Spotify notes many listeners skip ahead through ads rather than leaving.

What counts as a good listen-through?

There's no universal number. Apple's "engaged listener" bar of 20 minutes or 40% is a useful reference point.

Should the host or the editor choose the cold open?

The editor usually proposes two or three options with timestamps, and the host picks. Hosts know which moments their regular listeners will value most.
