What is a match cut in video editing, and how do you use one in a YouTube video?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A match cut is a transition that links two different shots through something they share: a shape, a movement or a sound. The classic example is 2001: A Space Odyssey, where a bone thrown in the air cuts to a spacecraft. Instead of feeling like a jump, the cut feels like a connection. On YouTube, a well-planned match cut can carry viewers across a scene change so it feels deliberate rather than abrupt.

Key takeaways

  • Adobe: a match cut "uses similar visual or auditory elements from the previous scene" to move the audience into the next one.
  • Three main types: graphic (shape or composition), match on action (movement) and audio (a sound carried across).
  • The best ones are planned while scripting or shooting, not rescued in the edit (Boris FX).
  • Line up the shared element in the same part of the frame in both shots, or the match won't read.
  • Use them where they add meaning or speed up a transition; a match cut with no real link loses its effect.

When a match cut is worth it

StudioBinder lists several uses, which translate well to YouTube:

  • Showing time passing. Same framing, same action, different day: a quick way to compress a build, a trip or a habit.
  • Linking two ideas. Cut from the problem object to the solution object in the same position in frame.
  • Adding energy to action. Matching a movement across shots keeps momentum.
  • Bridging with sound. Carry a voice-over or sound effect across a location change so the viewer doesn't feel the jump.

It's less useful in a plain talking-head section, where a simple cut or a jump cut usually does the job.

How to plan and cut one

  1. Script the link. Write the shared element into the plan: "close-up of hand turning key, match to hand turning dial."
  2. Shoot both halves the same way. Same lens if possible, same framing, the object in the same part of the frame. Mark the frame position on your monitor or take a reference still.
  3. Match the speed. For match on action, the movement should continue at the same pace across the cut.
  4. Cut on the movement. Place the edit mid-action, a few frames into the motion, so the eye follows it across.
  5. Check it at full speed. If you notice the trick, adjust by a few frames or scale one shot to line up.
  6. Use sound to sell it. A shared whoosh or impact covers small mismatches.

Boris FX notes an unplanned match cut "can be hard, but not impossible": scrub your footage for shapes or movements that happen to line up. Keep the 180-degree shutter rule consistent across both shots so motion blur matches too.

Worked example

Example: a travel creator wants to move from packing in London to arriving in Lisbon. She films herself zipping a suitcase shut, framed tight with the zip running left to right across the centre. In Lisbon she films unzipping the same case with the same framing and direction. In the edit she cuts mid-zip, adds a single zip sound across the join, and the 10-second "travel day" disappears into one second. Details are illustrative.

Is there a course on editing for retention with assignments?

What is the most famous match cut?

The bone-to-spacecraft cut in 2001: A Space Odyssey is usually named first.

What is the difference between a match cut and a jump cut?

A match cut links two different shots through a shared element. A jump cut skips forward within the same shot, creating a visible jump.

Is a match on action the same as a match cut?

It's one type of match cut, where a movement continues across the edit.

Can you make a match cut in Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve?

Yes. It's a normal cut; the skill is in shooting and lining up the two shots.

Do match cuts help YouTube retention?

They can smooth transitions, but retention depends more on the content and pacing overall.
