What should I look for when hiring an editor for a brand film?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Look for story judgement first and software skills second. A good brand-film editor shows, in their reel and a short paid test, that they can find the strongest moment in messy footage, pace a film so it never drags, and cut versions for different placements. Then check the practical side: clear communication, a defined revision process, file handover and who owns the final project files.

Key takeaways

  • The UK National Careers Service lists the core skills of a video editor as attention to detail, working well with others, accepting criticism and working under pressure, alongside confident use of editing software. Typical salaries run from about £24,000 for starters to £48,000 for experienced editors.
  • Prospects notes that editors "decide what's usable" and build a rough cut before the final, and that applicants usually show their work through an up-to-date showreel. For freelance day rates it points to checking with companies or BECTU, so quotes vary widely.
  • Google's ABCD guidance describes what effective video ads do: engaging pacing, tight framing, branding from the start, people, and a clear call to action. It is a useful lens for judging an editor's commercial work.
  • A Meta-commissioned Realeyes study found attention patterns differ between feed, short form and stream. An editor who can re-pace one film for each placement is worth more than one who only trims.
  • Once a film is live, YouTube's retention report shows where viewers leave, which gives you a fair way to review an editor's work together.

Questions to ask in the interview

  1. "Talk me through a film where the client's first idea did not work. What did you change?"
  2. "How do you decide what goes in the first five seconds?"
  3. "How many rounds of revisions are included, and what counts as a round?"
  4. "How do you hand over files, and do I get the project files?"
  5. "How would you re-cut this film for a vertical feed placement?"
  6. "What do you need from us in the brief?"

Listen for specific answers. Vague replies about "making it pop" tell you little.

Run a short paid test

A paid test on your own footage is the most reliable way to judge fit. Keep it small and fair:

ElementSuggestion
TaskA 30-60 second cut-down from existing footage
BriefOne page: audience, message, placement, must-have shots
TimeA few days, not hours
PaymentPay a fair fee; unpaid tests put off experienced editors
What you judgeOpening, pacing, clarity of message, how they handled feedback

Contracts and practical points

  • Ownership. Agree in writing who owns the final film, the project files and any music licences.
  • Music. Check that any track is licensed for the placements you plan, including paid ads.
  • Revisions. Define a round (one consolidated set of notes) and the fee for extra rounds.
  • Deadlines. Agree dates for rough cut, fine cut and final delivery.
  • Deliverables. List every version, length and aspect ratio you need.

FAQ

Is there a playbook that helps when briefing a brand film editor?

Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and pattern interrupts with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. It helps you brief and review; it does not replace hiring an editor.

How much does a brand film editor cost?

It varies widely by experience, location and scope. UK employed editors typically earn about £24,000-£48,000 a year; for freelance rates, ask several editors for quotes.

Should I pay for a test edit?

Yes. A small paid test respects the editor's time and gives you a fair comparison.

What should a brand film editor's reel show?

Strong openings, clear stories and commercial work similar to yours. Ask to see full pieces too.

Do I need the project files?

If you may re-edit later or switch editors, yes. Agree this in the contract before work starts.
