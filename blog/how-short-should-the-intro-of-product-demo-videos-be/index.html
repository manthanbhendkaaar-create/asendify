How many seconds should a product demo video spend before showing the product?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Keep a product demo intro to a few seconds, often five to ten, and make those seconds show the product doing something useful. Viewers clicked to see how it works, so name the problem in one line and show the result, then explain. Logo sequences, long welcomes and company history belong later, or nowhere. Wistia's data shows early drop-off grows as videos get longer.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia's guide to audience retention calls the first 2% of a video the "nose" and reports an average nose drop of 4.9% for 1-2 minute videos and 17.3% for 5-10 minute videos. Its advice: "Abbreviate the introduction to the material. Get to the point, already!"
  • The same guide suggests "removing intro graphics and logo sequences" and showing and proving your point "rather than just stating it".
  • In its video length analysis of over 13 million videos, Wistia reports product videos under a minute average 50% engagement and advises short videos to get "to the point fast".
  • YouTube's key moments report shows what percentage of viewers "still watched your video after the first 30 seconds", which is the simplest check on your intro.
  • The right length depends on the demo's purpose: a 60-second ad demo needs a near-instant start; a 20-minute walkthrough can afford a short agenda after the first result.

These targets are working ranges used by editors and marketers, not platform rules. Your own retention data should set the final length.

What to cut from demo intros

  1. Logo stings and animated titles. Put the logo in a corner or at the end.
  2. "Hi, I'm... and today we're going to..." Replace with the result: "This is how you send an invoice in eight seconds."
  3. Company background. Viewers watching a demo already know who you are, or do not care yet.
  4. Login screens and loading time. Start already logged in, with demo data ready.
  5. Agenda slides for short demos. Use chapters instead for long ones.

Intros for demos shown in ads

When a demo runs as a paid ad, the intro is effectively the first one to three seconds, because viewers have not chosen to watch. The opening frame should already show the product in use or the result it creates. A spoken line can name the problem at the same time. Anything that looks like a title card or a slow build gives viewers a reason to scroll past before they see why the product matters.

When a longer intro is fine

Long, technical walkthroughs for people who are already customers can take a little longer to set up, because those viewers are committed. Even then, show the finished outcome first and tell them what they will be able to do, then move. Use chapters so returning viewers can skip to the step they need.

FAQ

Is there a guide on writing hooks for product demo videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, openings and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan. Wistia's free retention guides are a good starting point.

Should a product demo start with the logo?

Usually not. Wistia suggests experimenting with removing intro graphics and logo sequences; show the product first and put the logo at the end.

How long should a product demo video be overall?

It depends on where it is shown. Social demos are often under a minute; full walkthroughs can run longer with chapters.

Should the founder introduce the demo?

A founder can work well, but after the first result, not before. One line is usually enough.

How do I know if my intro is too long?

Check how many viewers are still watching after 30 seconds and look for a steep early drop on your engagement graph.
