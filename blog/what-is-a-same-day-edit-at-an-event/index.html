What is a same-day edit at an event, and how do you make one people watch?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A same-day edit is a short video filmed and edited during the event itself, then shown on the screens before the day ends or posted to social that evening. To make one people watch, plan the shots in advance, have an editor working on site, open on the best moment of the day, and keep it short, usually well under 90 seconds.

Key takeaways

  • A same-day edit trades polish for speed: it catches the energy while people are still in the room or talking about it online.
  • It only works with a plan: a shot list, a fixed edit deadline and someone on site flagging big moments.
  • Canon's recap guide notes "few recap videos are more than a minute or so long"; for a same-day edit, shorter is safer.
  • For social, cut vertical; TikTok recommends 9:16 and at least 720p, with the hook in the first seconds.
  • Audio is still the weak point; capture clean sound for any speech you plan to use.

How a same-day edit works on the day

  1. Before the event: agree the length, format and deadline (for example, "60 seconds, shown before the closing keynote at 16:30"). Prepare a project template with titles, logo, music and end card.
  2. Morning: the crew films arrivals, the opening, the room filling, early sessions. Footage goes to the editor every hour or two.
  3. Midday: the editor builds a rough cut while filming continues.
  4. Afternoon: add the best moments from afternoon sessions, lock the cut 30-60 minutes before the deadline, test it on the venue screen.
  5. Show and post: play it in the room, then post the vertical version.

Cvent's event video guide recommends assigning someone onsite to flag standout moments and coordinate approvals. For a same-day edit, that person is essential: the editor can't watch every room.

Edit for attention, not completeness

A same-day edit is not a record of everything. Open on the single best moment of the day: the biggest laugh, a standing ovation, a striking visual. Canon's five steps for recap videos advises editors to "trim the fat" and notes that few recap videos run more than about a minute. Cut on action and music, and leave out anything that needs explanation.

Formats for social

For Reels, TikTok and Stories, cut a vertical version. TikTok's creative best practices recommend vertical 9:16 at 720p or higher and say to "prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds", with captions or text overlays for context. Keep key text away from the edges where app buttons sit.

Example: a same-day edit at a 600-person summit

Example: a technology summit wants a 60-second film for the closing session at 17:00.

  • Crew: one camera operator, one phone shooter for vertical, one editor with a laptop in a side room.
  • Template prepared the week before: title card, music track licensed for event use, end card with next year's date.
  • 09:00-13:00: arrivals, opening keynote, workshops. Footage handed over at 11:00 and 13:00.
  • 14:30: rough cut done; opens on the keynote speaker's big reveal and the room's reaction.
  • 16:00: final shots from the afternoon panel added; cut locked at 16:15; tested on the main screen at 16:30.
  • 17:00: shown before the closing remarks. 18:00: vertical version posted with captions.

The room sees itself on screen, and attendees share the vertical clip that evening.

Kit and permissions checklist

  • A laptop able to edit the camera's footage, plus fast card readers and spare storage.
  • A licensed music track chosen in advance.
  • A plan for consent: signage or ticket terms that tell attendees filming is happening, and a way to opt out.
  • Captions or on-screen text, because venue audio and social viewing are both often silent.
  • A backup export on a USB stick for the venue's AV team.

Approvals without delays

Speed creates a risk: a same-day edit can go out with a speaker's slide that wasn't meant to be public, or someone who asked not to be filmed. Agree in advance who signs off (one person, not a committee), what they check (people who opted out, confidential slides, sponsor names spelt right) and how long they have. A five-minute sign-off window before the locked deadline keeps the edit on time.

Common mistakes

  • No agreed deadline, so the edit runs late.
  • Trying to include every session.
  • Leaving music choice to the last minute.
  • No one flagging big moments to the editor.
  • Never testing the file on the venue screen.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches cold opens and fast pacing for short edits?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches cold opens and pacing for YouTube, which carries over to same-day edits. Cvent's and Canon's free guides cover the on-site side.

How long should a same-day edit be?

Usually 30-90 seconds. Short enough to finish on time and to hold a room or a feed.

Where should a same-day edit be shown?

On the main venue screens before a closing session or during a drinks reception, then as a vertical post on social that evening. Agree the screen slot with the AV team in advance.

Do I need a separate editor?

For anything beyond a very short clip, yes. The camera operator can't film and edit at the same time.

Can I make a same-day edit on a phone?

Yes, for short vertical clips. Phone editing apps are fine for social; a laptop is easier for a longer screen version.

Should the same-day edit replace the full recap?

No. It catches the moment; a fuller recap later can tell the story and serve sales and next year's promotion.
