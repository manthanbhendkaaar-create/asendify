How do you use the Instagram Reels retention graph to decide what to cut next time?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 25 November 2026 · Last updated 25 November 2026

Check skip rate first, because it tells you whether the first 3 seconds failed. Then look for the steepest drops on the retention chart, watch the 2 seconds before each one, and change what you find there next time. The line always slopes down, so judge your Reel by where it falls fast, not by the fact that it falls.

Key takeaways

  • Instagram says of the retention chart: "With Retention, you'll always see a downward slope, but the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is" (Social Media Today).
  • The chart "shows you the moments in your Reel that keep or lose your viewers' interest" (Social Media Today).
  • Skip rate replaced view rate and is defined as "the percentage of views from people who decided to skip your reel during those first 3 seconds" (Social Media Today).
  • A related tool on YouTube is the retention report, where spikes and dips show which parts people watch, rewatch or leave (YouTube Help).

What the two numbers tell you

Skip rate answers one question: did the opening work? If many viewers skipped in the first 3 seconds, the problem is the hook, the first frame or the match between what the Reel promised and what it showed. The retention chart answers a second question: of those who stayed, where did they leave? The line will fall because every video loses viewers over time. What you look for is a cliff, a sudden drop, or a flat stretch that shows a part that worked.

Neither number tells you why. It is your job to watch the Reel at the point of the drop and form a guess, change one thing and compare the next one.

Reading the chart

What you seeLikely meaningWhat to try next
High skip rateThe first 3 seconds did not earn a stayNew first frame or opening line; show the payoff earlier
Cliff in the first few seconds after a good startThe hook promised something the video did not give quicklyShorten the setup; deliver sooner
Steady, gentle slopePacing is workingKeep the structure; test a stronger opening
Sudden drop mid-ReelA slow, confusing or off-topic momentCut or tighten that section
Flat or rising stretchA moment viewers rewatched or stayed forDo more of this; consider using it as the hook
Drop at the endThe ending is flat or the loop breaksShorten the ending; cut the outro

Step-by-step: turn the chart into edits

  1. Open Insights for the Reel. Look at skip rate and the retention chart together.
  2. Check skip rate first. If it is high, work on the opening before anything else.
  3. Find the two or three steepest drops. Ignore the smooth slope.
  4. Watch the two seconds before each drop. Note what changed: a cut, a line, a graphic, a pause.
  5. Write one guess per drop. For example, "The explanation started before the point."
  6. Find the best stretch. Where is the line flattest? What was happening on screen?
  7. Change one thing next time. If you change five things, you will not know which mattered.
  8. Keep a simple log. Reel, change made, skip rate, where the biggest drop was.
  9. Compare like with like. Compare Reels of similar length and topic, not a 7-second joke with a 60-second tutorial.
  10. Give it time. Look again after the Reel has had enough views to be meaningful.
  11. Try the best moment as the hook. If a section held attention, move a short version to the start.
  12. Review after five Reels. Look for patterns across drops, not a single result.

Common causes of drops

Drops often come from: a slow introduction before the point, a talking-head stretch with no visual change, a transition that tells the viewer the interesting part is over, text that is hard to read, or sound that jars. You can often hear or see these on a re-watch, especially on a phone with sound on and then off.

Do not copy another creator's graph. Different accounts have different audiences, and a drop that looks bad for you may be normal for a longer format.

Example: a 30-second recipe Reel

Example: a creator's Reel shows a high skip rate and a sharp fall at about 8 seconds. Watching the clip, the creator sees a 5-second shot of ingredients being laid out, followed by a title. The next Reel opens on the finished dish for 2 seconds, then shows the first step, with the ingredients as an on-screen list rather than a separate shot. The creator notes skip rate and the biggest drop on both and compares them. This scenario uses invented numbers and is not a measured result.

What the graph cannot tell you

The chart does not say who left, why, or whether they would have bought. It does not replace comments, saves or shares. A Reel with lower retention may still do its job, for example by reaching new people. Treat it as one signal.

FAQ

What is skip rate on Reels?

The percentage of views from people who skipped the Reel in the first 3 seconds, as Social Media Today reports Instagram's definition.

Why does the retention line always go down?

Instagram says you will always see a downward slope; a flatter line means a more engaged audience.

What should I change first?

The opening, if skip rate is high. Otherwise the steepest drop.

Should I change several things at once?

No. Change one thing so you can learn from the comparison.
