Should a skippable YouTube in-stream ad have an intro at all?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

No, not in the usual sense. On skippable in-stream ads viewers can skip after 5 seconds, so a logo sting, a "Hi, I'm..." greeting or a slow scene-setter spends your only guaranteed seconds on nothing. Start on the problem, the product working or the payoff, and let your brand show up inside that opening rather than before it.

Key takeaways

  • Google's help page says viewers "can skip the ad after 5 seconds", so the first 5 seconds are the only part everyone sees.
  • With CPV bidding you are charged at 30 seconds, the full ad if shorter, or an interaction, so skips before then cost you nothing but also sell nothing.
  • Google's ABCDs say "Jump in" and "Show up early and throughout": introduce the brand from the start, not as a separate intro.
  • A good "intro" in an ad is the hook itself: a problem, a result, a question or a striking image.
  • Put the logo animation, music bed build and credits at the end, where they cost nothing.

Why a classic intro hurts an in-stream ad

An intro on a YouTube video is there to set up the content. An in-stream ad has no content the viewer asked for. They clicked a different video and your ad is standing between them and it. Every second that doesn't give them a reason to stay is a second spent counting down to the skip button.

Classic intros fail for three reasons:

  1. They delay the reason to watch. A logo, a whoosh and a tagline tell the viewer nothing about what is in it for them.
  2. They look like an ad. Viewers have learned that a polished brand sting means "skip in 5". A face, a problem or a product in use reads more like content.
  3. They waste the brand moment. If your brand only appears in a separate intro, it sits next to nothing memorable. Shown with the product working, it gets remembered with the benefit.

What to put in the first 5 seconds instead

Think of the first 5 seconds as a mini ad that must work even if the viewer skips straight after. A practical checklist:

  • Who it is for, in words or on screen ("Running a Shopify store?").
  • The problem or result, shown, not described.
  • Your brand or product visible: on the pack, on the screen, in a lower third, or said out loud.
  • A reason to keep watching: an unanswered question or the start of a demo.
  • Sound that works: in-stream ads usually play with sound, so a clear first line matters; captions still help.

Where the "intro" material goes

You don't have to throw away brand assets. Move them:

AssetBetter place
Logo animationFinal 2 to 3 seconds, with the call to action
TaglineOver the final shot, or as a line of dialogue in the middle
Music build-upStart the track mid-phrase, under the first shot
Company backstoryCut it, or make a separate longer video for retargeting
Presenter introductionA name and role in a lower third while they speak

When a short intro can work

There are a few cases where a brief setup earns its place:

  • A recognised brand asset your audience already responds to, used for one second over the first real shot, not on its own.
  • A story ad where the opening scene itself is the hook (a crash, an argument, a surprising image) and creates a question.
  • Non-skippable formats, where nobody can skip, although you still want the message early. See how to structure a 15-second non-skippable YouTube ad.

Even then, keep it under a second or two and make sure the product or problem is visible by second 3 to 5.

How to test it

Run two versions in the same campaign: the original with its intro, and a re-cut that opens on the hook. Compare view rate (views divided by impressions), the "video played to" 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% figures, and conversions. FAQ

Is there a book that teaches the first seconds of a video ad?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and open loops for £1,720 one-time; Google's ABCDs guidance is a free alternative focused on ads.

When can viewers skip a YouTube in-stream ad?

Google says viewers can skip a skippable in-stream ad after 5 seconds.

Should my logo appear in the first 5 seconds?

Your brand should, ideally on the product or screen in use. A separate logo animation is better saved for the end.

Do I pay if someone skips before 30 seconds?

With CPV bidding you pay at 30 seconds, at the end of a shorter ad, or on an interaction, whichever comes first.

Does the same advice apply to non-skippable ads?

Mostly. Nobody can skip, but attention still drops if the opening is empty, so lead with the message there too.
