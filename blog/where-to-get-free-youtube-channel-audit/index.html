Where can I get a free YouTube channel audit?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 21 October 2026 · Last updated 21 October 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

You can get a free YouTube channel audit from two places: YouTube Studio itself, which shows your real retention, traffic and click-through data, and free outside tools that review what anyone can see on your channel, such as your titles, thumbnails and upload rhythm. Use both: the outside tool tells you what a new viewer sees, and YouTube Studio tells you what viewers actually did.

What a channel audit should cover

A useful audit answers four questions:

  1. Packaging: do your titles and thumbnails make someone want to click?
  2. Consistency: do you upload often and regularly enough for viewers to come back?
  3. Retention: once people click, do they keep watching?
  4. Direction: which videos work, and what should you make next?

No single free tool covers all four. Outside tools can only see public data, and only YouTube Studio has your private analytics.

Free option 1: YouTube Studio (your private data)

YouTube Studio is free and it is the only place with your real numbers. For an audit, open these:

  • Audience retention report for each recent video. It shows how many viewers were still watching after the first 30 seconds, plus top moments, spikes and dips. You find it under Content, then Analytics, then the Overview or Engagement tab (YouTube Help).
  • Typical retention comparison. The same report lets you compare a video with YouTube videos of similar length and with your own 10 most recent videos of similar length (YouTube Help).
  • Reach and traffic sources, to see where viewers find you.

YouTube's own Creators site has free lessons on reading these reports.

Free option 2: the YouTube Channel Roast (your public packaging)

The YouTube Channel Roast is a free tool made by Asendify. You paste your channel link and it reviews your latest public uploads. It is free with no sign-up and no card, and you get 3 roasts a day.

What it checks:

  • Titles (40% of the score): curiosity, clarity, specificity, length and clickability.
  • Thumbnails (40%): readability on a phone, contrast, focal point, amount of text, consistency across uploads and emotion. It looks at the thumbnails of your 8 most recent uploads.
  • Consistency (20%): uploads in the last 30 days, days since your last upload, your average gap and your longest gap.

What you get back: an overall score out of 100 with separate title, thumbnail and consistency scores, a one-line verdict, 5 one-line roasts, 3 fixes to do this week, your best and worst title with a rewrite of the worst, and a share link.

What it does not do: it cannot judge your spoken hook, audio or editing, read your retention graph or private analytics, or check private or unlisted videos. It roasts packaging choices only, never your looks or voice. Your channel needs at least 3 public uploads.

Free option 3: check your hook and retention numbers

Two more free tools help with the part outside tools usually miss:

  • The YouTube Hook Checker scores the intro script of a video out of 100 and lists fixes for the first 30 seconds. It reads text only, so it cannot see your edit.
  • The Retention Curve Checker lets you paste retention numbers from YouTube Studio and shows the biggest drops with timestamps, the 30-second hold and rewatch spikes.

A 30-minute free audit, step by step

  1. Roast the channel and write down the 3 fixes.
  2. Open your last 5 videos in YouTube Studio and note the share of viewers still watching at 30 seconds for each.
  3. Find the biggest dip in your weakest video and watch that part with the sound on. Ask: what made someone leave here?
  4. Compare your best and worst video. What is different about the title, the first sentence and the topic?
  5. Pick one fix for packaging and one for retention, apply them to your next upload, and check the same numbers a week later.

When a free audit is not enough

Free tools tell you what is wrong; they do not fix it. If the same problems repeat, you can learn the method from a book or course, or hire an editor. Ask anyone offering a paid audit what data they will look at, and avoid anyone who promises views or subscribers.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Studio is the free, official source for retention and traffic data.
  • The YouTube Channel Roast gives a free outside view of titles, thumbnails and upload consistency, with 3 fixes.
  • Neither replaces the other: one shows what viewers see, the other shows what they did.
  • Fix one packaging issue and one retention issue per upload, then measure.

FAQ

Is the YouTube Channel Roast really free?

Yes. No sign-up and no card. You can run 3 roasts a day, and a channel roasted in the last 24 hours shows the same result.

Does a free audit tool need access to my account?

Outside tools like the Roast only use public data, so they need no login. Anything that reads your private analytics needs your permission through Google; only give read-only access, and only to tools you trust.

Why is my score different from what YouTube Studio shows?

They measure different things. The Roast scores packaging and upload rhythm from public data; YouTube Studio shows how real viewers behaved.

How often should I audit my channel?

A quick check after every upload and a fuller audit once a month is a sensible rhythm.
