What is the cheapest supercar to buy and run?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The cheapest supercar-style car to buy in the UK is usually an early Aston Martin V8 Vantage, which What Car says starts from about £20,000 used. For a mid-engined supercar, an early Audi R8 V10 from about £40,000 is among the cheapest, according to evo. The cheapest to run is whichever car has the lowest yearly cost once depreciation, insurance, servicing, tyres, fuel and tax are added, so compare totals, not prices.

This page is for education only, not financial advice. All running-cost figures in the example are planning assumptions, not quotes.

Key takeaways

  • Used prices for some supercars now overlap with new family cars: What Car lists models from about £20,000.
  • A cheap purchase price does not mean cheap running costs. A car that cost £100,000 new still has the maintenance costs of a £100,000 car.
  • UK cars registered from April 2017 with a list price over £40,000 pay an extra £440 a year in vehicle tax for five years.
  • Older, mostly depreciated cars lose less value each year, but repairs and servicing can be higher.
  • Rank cars by total yearly cost, using real insurance and service quotes for your situation.

Why cheap to buy is not cheap to run

The biggest trap with a cheap supercar is assuming the running costs fell with the price. Auto Express puts it plainly in its cheap supercars guide: "A £100k car picked up for £30k still has the maintenance costs of a £100k vehicle." It advises seeing evidence of careful maintenance and checking the service history before agreeing a deal.

What Car makes a similar point. Its list notes that while these cars are unlikely to depreciate much further, they will not be cheap to fuel, and servicing, maintenance and storage all add to the cost.

So the cheapest supercar to own is a balance:

  • Older cars have done most of their depreciating, so they lose less each year. But they are more likely to need expensive repairs.
  • Newer cars are more reliable and may have warranty left, but they lose more value each year.

UK vehicle tax: the expensive car supplement

Tax is one cost you can check exactly. The GOV.UK vehicle tax rate tables show that for cars registered on or after 1 April 2017:

ItemAmount
Standard rate from the second payment (single 12-month payment)£200 a year
Extra charge if list price is over £40,000 (petrol or diesel)£440 a year
Total with the supplement£640 a year
How long the supplement applies5 years from the second time the car is taxed

The supplement is based on the car's original list price, not what you pay for it used. A 2018 car that cost £150,000 new and now sells for £60,000 still pays it until five years have passed. Cars registered between 1 March 2001 and 31 March 2017 are taxed on CO2 emissions instead. GOV.UK's table for those cars puts band M (over 255g/km) at £790 a year for a single 12-month payment, the highest band. Check the CO2 figure on the car's V5C before you budget.

The MOT is a small fixed cost by comparison. GOV.UK sets the maximum MOT fee for a car at £54.85. Repairs needed to pass are extra.

How to find the cheapest supercar to run for you

  1. Shortlist three or four cars in your price range from current listings.
  2. Get insurance quotes on each exact car with your real age, postcode and history. This can change the ranking more than anything else.
  3. Ask a specialist for the cost of a typical annual service and any big jobs due soon.
  4. Price a set of tyres for each car and estimate how often you will replace them.
  5. Work out fuel from your yearly mileage and the car's economy.
  6. Check the tax on GOV.UK using the registration date and list price.
  7. Estimate depreciation by comparing prices for the same model one and three years older.
  8. Add a repair fund, especially for cars out of warranty.
  9. Add it all up and rank by total yearly cost.

FAQ

What is the cheapest supercar you can buy?

In the UK, What Car says used Aston Martin V8 Vantages start from about £20,000, making it the cheapest car on its 2026 list. Whether you call it a supercar or a sports car is a matter of taste.

What is the cheapest mid-engined supercar?

An early Audi R8 V10 is among the cheapest, with evo quoting prices from about £40,000 in October 2026.

Are cheap supercars expensive to maintain?

Often, yes. A car that was expensive new keeps the servicing and parts costs of an expensive car, however little you paid for it.

Do older supercars pay the expensive car tax?

The £440 supplement applies to cars registered on or after 1 April 2017 with a list price over £40,000, for five years from the second tax payment. Older cars are taxed on CO2 emissions instead.

How do I work out which supercar is cheapest to run?

Add depreciation, insurance, servicing, tyres, fuel, tax and a repair fund for each car on your shortlist, using real quotes, then compare the yearly totals.
