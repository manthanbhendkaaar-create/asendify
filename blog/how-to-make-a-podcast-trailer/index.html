How do you make a podcast trailer that gets people to follow?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A podcast trailer that gets follows is short, opens with your best moment rather than an introduction, shows what an episode actually sounds like, ends on an unanswered question, and finishes with one clear call to follow. Spotify recommends keeping trailers under one minute; Apple suggests two minutes or less. Use real highlights from episodes instead of a scripted pitch wherever you can.

Key takeaways

  • Spotify: "Keep your trailer under one minute." Apple: "keep your trailers to two minutes or less."
  • Apple suggests building "tension or curiosity" near the end, for example "an unanswered question by a guest".
  • Use real highlights: favourite quotes and moments that define what listening feels like.
  • End with one call to action: follow, and when new episodes arrive.
  • Make versions for each place: audio for the feed, vertical or square video for social.

What the platforms recommend

Spotify keeps it brief: "Keep your trailer under one minute", and try "to capture the message of your show as concisely as possible". It suggests four elements: introduce yourself, introduce the show, sell your strengths, and include a call to action such as following the show (Spotify for Creators).

Apple suggests you "keep your trailers to two minutes or less", outline first, and include highlights (for interviews, "your favorite quotes"), then "build some tension or curiosity" near the end and finish by telling listeners when new episodes arrive and reminding them to follow (Apple Podcasts for Creators).

Apple also describes three kinds of trailer:

TypePurposeWhere it plays
Coming soon trailerTease a new show, season or series before launchFeed, social, website
Show trailerA highlight reel of your best content, sound bites and quotesFeed, social, website

A 60-second trailer structure

TimeBeatExample
0 to 10 sStrongest moment, no introA guest: "I sold the company for one pound. On purpose."
10 to 20 sWho you are and what the show is"I'm Sam. Every week I ask founders about the decision that nearly broke them."
20 to 40 sTwo or three quick highlightsShort clips: a laugh, a sharp opinion, a surprising fact
40 to 50 sThe open question"Why would anyone sell for a pound? That's episode one."
50 to 60 sOne call to action"New episodes every Tuesday. Follow so you don't miss it."

The first ten seconds work like a cold open: they decide whether someone keeps listening. Starting with "Hi, welcome to the trailer for..." spends them on nothing.

Why ending on a question works

A trailer's job is not to explain the show; it's to make someone want episode one. Carnegie Mellon's George Loewenstein argued that curiosity arises "when attention becomes focused on a gap in one's knowledge" (quoted in PBS NOVA). Ending on an unanswered question creates that gap and points straight at the answer: the first episode.

How to make it, step by step

  1. List your best 10 moments from recorded episodes (or a pilot): quotes, laughs, reveals, strong opinions.
  2. Pick three that show range: one emotional, one useful, one surprising.
  3. Write only the joining lines: who you are, what the show is, the question, the call to action. Keep each to one sentence.
  4. Cut tight. Remove breaths and pauses between clips; the trailer should feel faster than an episode.
  5. Add your show's sound. Apple suggests recognisable sounds, such as recurring music or a catchphrase, to help listeners connect.
  6. Export versions. Audio for the feed; Apple notes a square video may suit an Instagram feed, and Stories need vertical video.
  7. Pin it. Set it as the trailer episode in your host so new visitors hear it first.

Example: a trailer for a new interview show

Example (a hypothetical show, to show the method): a new podcast about people who changed careers after 40.

  • 0:00 Guest clip: "I was a dentist for 22 years. Now I build boats." (5 s)
  • 0:05 Host: "I'm Priya. Every Thursday, someone tells me how they started again, and what it really cost." (8 s)
  • 0:13 Three clips: a guest laughing about their first failed boat, a sharp line about money, a quiet moment about telling family. (25 s)
  • 0:38 Host: "Why would a dentist risk everything at 47? That's episode one." (6 s)
  • 0:44 "New episodes every Thursday. Follow now so it's waiting for you." (6 s)

Total: 50 seconds, under Spotify's one-minute guide.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Long introductions before anything interesting happens.
  • Explaining instead of showing. "We talk about interesting topics" says nothing; a real clip says everything.
  • Too many calls to action. Follow, rate, review, share and visit the website is five asks. Pick one.
  • Promising what episodes don't deliver. A dramatic trailer for a calm show loses people after episode one.
  • Never updating it. Refresh the trailer as you record stronger moments.

FAQ

Is there a book on writing hooks for podcast trailers?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and a bank of openers, and applies them to podcasts.

How long should a podcast trailer be?

Spotify says under one minute; Apple says two minutes or less. Shorter is usually stronger.

Should a trailer be scripted?

Script the joining lines; use real episode clips for the rest.

Can I make a trailer before I've recorded any episodes?

Yes: a "coming soon" trailer. Record a short pilot conversation so you have real moments to use.

Should I make a video version?

Yes, if you promote on social. Use square for feeds and vertical for Stories and Shorts.

Where should the trailer appear?

As the trailer episode in your feed, pinned on social profiles and on your website.
