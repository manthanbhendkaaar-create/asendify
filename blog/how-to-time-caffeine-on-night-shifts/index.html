How should shift workers time caffeine on night shifts?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 28 October 2026 · Last updated 4 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Take small caffeine doses early in the night shift and just before the hours when you usually feel worst, then stop in the last several hours so you can sleep when you get home. Treat caffeine as a tool for the first half of the night. It helps alertness, but it does not replace sleep and does not make it safe to drive tired.

Key takeaways

  • Use caffeine in the first half of a night shift, not all the way through.
  • Keep each serving at or below 200 mg and the 24-hour total at or below 400 mg.
  • Set your cut-off from your post-shift bedtime, not a normal night-time one.
  • Protect your daytime sleep with a dark, quiet, cool room.
  • If you feel too sleepy to drive, do not drive; caffeine is not a reliable fix.

This page is education for healthy adults aged 18 and over. It is general information, not medical advice. If you have ongoing sleep problems on shifts, speak to your GP or occupational health team.

What the evidence and guidance say

A Cochrane systematic review of caffeine in shift workers (Ker and colleagues, 2010) found that caffeine improved performance and reduced errors compared with no caffeine or placebo. The authors rated the evidence as low quality, and the included studies did not measure injuries directly. So caffeine is a reasonable aid for night-shift alertness, but it should sit alongside proper sleep, breaks and your workplace's fatigue rules.

The UK Health and Safety Executive's tips for shift workers are more cautious still. They advise using caffeine only occasionally, not relying on it to stay awake, and avoiding it for a few hours before bed.

The main risk with night-shift caffeine is not the shift itself. It is the sleep afterwards. Caffeine stays in the body for hours, and the US FDA notes that how fast people clear it varies widely. Coffee at 5am can easily still be working when you try to sleep at 9am.

Step 1: Fix your sleep window first

Decide when you will sleep after the shift and protect it. HSE suggests heavy curtains, blackout blinds or eye shades; earplugs or background noise to mask sound; a cool room; and asking family and neighbours to keep noise down. Then count backwards to set your caffeine cut-off. Your "bedtime" is when you get into bed after the shift, not 11pm.

Step 2: Use the first half of the shift

  1. Start of shift: a moderate dose, such as a regular mug of coffee or a tea.
  2. Before the low point: many people feel worst in the early hours of the morning. A second small dose shortly before that point can help.
  3. Keep each serving at or below 200 mg. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) says single doses up to 200 mg and daily intakes up to 400 mg from all sources do not raise safety concerns for healthy adults. Count the whole 24 hours, including anything before your shift.

Step 3: Stop in the last part of the shift

A 2023 meta-analysis of 24 studies by Gardiner and colleagues in Sleep Medicine Reviews (open-access copy, Leeds Beckett University) estimated that a normal coffee (about 107 mg) should be taken at least 8.8 hours before bed, and a typical pre-workout serve at least 13.2 hours before. Those studies mostly involved night-time sleep, so treat the figures as a rough guide for daytime sleep.

The shorter the gap between finishing and sleeping, the earlier you have to stop. If you must have something in the early hours, a small tea is gentler than coffee, but it still counts.

After the cut-off, rely on other alertness tools: water, a short walk, brighter light at your workstation, talking to colleagues and changing tasks.

Step 4: Consider a short nap

HSE suggests a short sleep before your first night shift. If your workplace allows it, a brief nap on a break can also reduce sleepiness. Keep break naps short, around 10 to 20 minutes, so you do not wake up groggy.

Step 5: Get home safely

Drowsy driving after a night shift is a real hazard. HSE suggests considering public transport or a taxi, sharing the driving, and if you feel drowsy, stopping and taking a short nap if it is safe to do so, rather than relying on caffeine to keep going. Keep work-time caffeine aimed at doing the job well, not at "staying awake to drive home".

Step 6: Plan your days off

On days off, many shift workers swing back to a daytime schedule. HSE suggests that when coming off nights you sleep briefly and then go to bed earlier that night. Keep caffeine moderate and in the morning, and avoid large doses to "catch up" after poor sleep.

Who should avoid caffeine or ask first

Some people should avoid or limit caffeine and check with a GP, occupational health team or pharmacist first: anyone pregnant or breastfeeding (the NHS advises no more than 200 mg a day in pregnancy), anyone under 18, people with heart conditions or high blood pressure, people with anxiety or panic disorders, people with insomnia or other sleep disorders, and anyone taking medicines that may interact with caffeine.

Never use caffeine powders or highly concentrated liquid caffeine, and never mix caffeine with alcohol, including an "unwinding" drink after a shift.

FAQ

Is it better to have one big coffee or several small ones on a night shift?

Several small ones in the first half of the shift usually give steadier alertness than one big dose and are easier to keep inside the 200 mg single-dose level.

Can energy drinks replace coffee on night shifts?

They can contain similar or larger amounts of caffeine, often with a lot of sugar, and large cans make it easy to overshoot. Read the label and count them in your total.

What if I can't sleep after my shift even without late caffeine?

Daytime sleep is hard for many shift workers. If poor sleep continues, talk to your GP or occupational health team, as there may be other causes.

Should I drink caffeine on rotating shifts?

The same rules apply, but recalculate your cut-off each time your sleep time changes.

Can caffeine make it safe to drive home tired?

No. HSE advises against relying on caffeine to keep going. If you feel drowsy, use other transport or stop and nap if it is safe.
