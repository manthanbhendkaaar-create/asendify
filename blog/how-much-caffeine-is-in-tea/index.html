How much caffeine is in tea?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A typical cup of black tea contains roughly 40 to 75 mg of caffeine and green tea roughly 30 to 40 mg, depending on the source and how strong it is brewed. That is around half the caffeine of a cup of coffee. True herbal teas, such as peppermint or rooibos, contain no caffeine.

Key takeaways

  • The NHS uses about 75 mg for a cup of tea; the Mayo Clinic lists 48 mg for an 8 oz cup of black tea and 29 mg for green.
  • Different figures come from different cup sizes and brew strengths, not from one source being wrong.
  • Brewing time, the amount of leaf and the size of the mug change the caffeine more than the brand.
  • Decaf tea still has a little caffeine, about 2 mg a cup on the Mayo Clinic chart.
  • Five or six strong mugs of tea a day can add up to as much caffeine as three or four coffees.

This page is general information, not medical advice. If you are pregnant, have a heart condition or take medication, ask a doctor or GP how much caffeine is right for you.

The Mayo Clinic notes that the caffeine in tea and coffee varies with growing conditions and brewing time, so treat every number here as an estimate.

Why the numbers vary so much

You will see tea quoted anywhere from about 30 to 75 mg a cup. Several things explain that.

  1. Cup size. A UK mug often holds 300 ml or more, while many US figures use 8 oz (237 ml). A bigger mug of the same tea has more caffeine.
  2. Brewing time. The longer the bag or leaves sit in the water, the more caffeine comes out. A quick dunk gives a weaker cup than a five-minute brew.
  3. How much tea you use. Two bags in a pot for one person, or a heaped spoon of loose leaf, will give more.
  4. Type of tea. On the official charts, black tea usually comes out higher than green per cup. Individual teas vary, though.
  5. Ready-made drinks. Bottled and iced teas are often weaker per serving than a hot cup, but the bottles can be large.

So if you drink strong builder's tea from a big mug, the NHS figure of about 75 mg is a sensible working number. If you drink weak green tea from a small cup, it may be under 30 mg.

Is tea a good lower-caffeine option?

For many people, yes. Swapping some coffees for tea is one of the easiest ways to cut caffeine without losing a hot drink habit. A cup of tea typically has somewhere between a third and three-quarters of the caffeine of a cup of coffee, depending on the figures you use.

Both the FDA and the European Food Safety Authority say up to 400 mg of caffeine a day does not raise safety concerns for healthy adults. EFSA adds that up to 200 mg a day is fine in pregnancy, and the NHS gives the same 200 mg pregnancy limit. EFSA also notes that 100 mg close to bedtime may affect sleep in some adults, which is worth remembering for an evening cuppa.

If you are comparing tea and coffee for concentration rather than caffeine, see our guide on whether tea or coffee is better for focus.

Which teas have no caffeine?

Tea made from the tea plant, Camellia sinensis, always contains some caffeine. That includes black, green, white and oolong tea. Drinks that are called "tea" but are made from other plants are usually caffeine-free:

  • peppermint
  • chamomile
  • rooibos (redbush)
  • ginger
  • fruit infusions

Check the label, though. Some blends add green tea, guarana or yerba mate, which do contain caffeine.

Example: adding up a tea drinker's day

Example: Gareth drinks six mugs of strong black tea a day: two at breakfast, two mid-morning, one after lunch and one at 8 pm. He also has a can of cola with lunch.

Using the NHS working figures:

DrinkNumberCaffeine eachTotal
Mug of tea6about 75 mgabout 450 mg
Can of cola1about 40 mgabout 40 mg
Day totalabout 490 mg

Gareth thought of himself as a light caffeine user because he does not drink coffee. In fact he is above the 400 mg daily figure for healthy adults, and his 8 pm mug is likely to be close enough to bedtime to affect his sleep.

If he swaps the 8 pm mug for rooibos and the mid-morning pair for one regular tea and one decaf, he cuts about 150 mg. His new total is about 340 mg, under 400 mg, with nothing in the evening. He keeps the habit and the number of hot drinks.

How to cut down on tea caffeine without giving up tea

  • Brew for less time. A shorter brew makes a lighter cup with less caffeine.
  • Use a smaller cup or one bag per pot. Less tea in, less caffeine out.
  • Switch to decaf after lunch. Decaf black tea is about 2 mg a cup on the Mayo Clinic chart.
  • Go herbal in the evening. Rooibos looks and tastes closer to black tea than most herbal options.
  • Cut down gradually. Dropping several mugs at once can bring withdrawal headaches. Our guide to how much caffeine is in a cup of coffee helps if you are swapping between the two, and decaf coffee is another low-caffeine option.

FAQ

Does green tea have more caffeine than black tea?

Usually not. On the FDA and Mayo Clinic charts, black tea has more caffeine per serving than green, though individual teas and brewing methods vary.

How much caffeine is in a cup of tea compared with coffee?

Roughly half or less. The Mayo Clinic lists 48 mg for an 8 oz black tea and 96 mg for an 8 oz brewed coffee; the NHS uses 75 mg for tea and 100 mg for instant coffee.

How many cups of tea a day is too much caffeine?

At about 75 mg a mug, five mugs is about 375 mg, close to the 400 mg daily figure for healthy adults. In pregnancy, the NHS limit of 200 mg is about two to three mugs.

Does decaf tea have caffeine?

A little. The Mayo Clinic chart lists about 2 mg in an 8 oz cup of decaf black tea, which is very low but not zero.

Does milk reduce the caffeine in tea?

No. Adding milk does not remove caffeine. A splash of milk makes very little difference to how much caffeine is in the cup.
