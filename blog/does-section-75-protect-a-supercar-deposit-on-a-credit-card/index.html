Does Section 75 protect a supercar deposit paid on a credit card?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Usually not. Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 makes a card provider jointly liable with the seller for misrepresentation or breach of contract, but it excludes any single item with a cash price over £30,000. Most supercars cost more than that, so a deposit on a credit card normally gets no Section 75 protection. Dealer-arranged finance can give a different protection under section 75A, and card chargeback may still help.

This page is general information, not financial or legal advice. For your own case, ask your card provider, Citizens Advice or a solicitor.

Key takeaways

  • Section 75 covers purchases where a single item's cash price is over £100 and not more than £30,000.
  • The limit is the item's cash price, not the amount you put on the card, so a £1,000 deposit on a £70,000 car is not covered.
  • Section 75A gives a claim against the lender under a linked credit agreement (such as dealer-arranged finance) where the goods' cash value is over £30,000 and the credit is no more than £60,260.
  • Under section 75A you usually have to try the seller first, unless they cannot be traced, do not respond or are insolvent.
  • Chargeback is a card scheme process, not a law, and may still help with a deposit, but it has time limits and no guarantee.

What Section 75 does

Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 says that if you pay a supplier using a credit agreement that finances the purchase (such as a credit card) and you have a claim against the supplier for misrepresentation or breach of contract, you have a "like claim" against the credit provider. The two are "jointly and severally liable", so you can pursue either.

The key limit is in section 75(3): the protection does not apply to a claim relating to a single item with a cash price of £100 or less, or more than £30,000.

Citizens Advice's guide to buying a used car sets out the same payment choices: cash has little protection, a debit card may offer chargeback, a credit card can give Section 75 protection on goods costing £100 to £30,000, and with trader-arranged finance you can take action against the finance company as well as the trader. If you arrange your own loan, the lender has no responsibility if the car is faulty.

Section 75A for cars over £30,000

Section 75A fills part of the gap. It applies to a "linked credit agreement": a regulated consumer credit agreement that finances specific goods only, where the lender used the seller to arrange the credit or the goods are named in the agreement. Dealer-arranged personal loans for a specific car can fit this.

You can bring a breach-of-contract claim against the lender if:

  • the seller cannot be traced, or
  • you contacted the seller and they did not respond, or
  • the seller is insolvent, or
  • you took reasonable steps against the seller without getting satisfaction (court action is not required).

It does not apply where the cash value is £30,000 or less (that is Section 75's territory), where the credit is more than £60,260, where the credit was mainly for business, or where it is secured on land.

Option B shows how easy it is to miss a limit: a loan above £60,260 falls outside section 75A. A £55,000 loan for the same car would be inside it. Our pages on PCP vs HP vs lease vs cash for a supercar and your rights if a used supercar is faulty explain the finance and fault rules in more detail.

Practical steps

  • Do the checks before paying anything, including a private history check for outstanding finance; see how to check a used supercar's history.
  • Keep deposits small and get a written receipt saying whether the deposit is refundable.
  • Use a well-known dealer with a trade association or Motor Ombudsman code membership, which Citizens Advice recommends.
  • Ask the card provider about chargeback quickly if something goes wrong; time limits apply.

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner ranks cars that fit your budget by yearly cost of ownership with editable running costs. A spreadsheet of your own quotes is the free alternative.

Does Section 75 cover a car over £30,000?

No. Section 75 excludes single items with a cash price over £30,000, however much you paid on the card.

Is a deposit on a credit card protected?

Only under Section 75 if the car's cash price is £30,000 or less. For dearer cars, ask your card provider about chargeback.

What is section 75A?

A protection for linked credit agreements, such as dealer-arranged loans for specific goods, where the cash value is over £30,000 and the credit is no more than £60,260.

Does HP give me protection?

Under hire purchase, the finance company is usually the legal seller, so claims for faults or misdescription can go to it.

Is chargeback the same as Section 75?

No. Chargeback is a card scheme process run by your card provider, with time limits and no legal guarantee.
