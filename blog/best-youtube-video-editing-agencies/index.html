What are the best YouTube video editing agencies?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 October 2026

The best YouTube video editing agency for you depends on how many videos you publish, your budget, and whether you need plain editing or editing built around audience retention. Subscription services such as Vidpros and Vidchops suit steady monthly volume, freelance marketplaces suit tight budgets and one-off jobs, and retention-focused agencies suit channels where watch time is the problem.

Key takeaways

  • There is no single best agency; match the service to your volume, budget and main problem.
  • Check your audience retention report before hiring, so you know whether you need tidy cuts or retention work.
  • Ask every option for a dedicated editor, clear turnaround times and real before-and-after examples.
  • For low volume and simple formats, a good freelancer is often better value than any agency.
  • Be wary of anyone who promises views or subscribers.

Vidpros: best for a predictable monthly editor

Vidpros sells editing as monthly subscription plans. As listed on 4 November 2026, its plans are Starter, Growth and Scale, with a dedicated editor on Growth and Scale. Its YouTube page says packages cover assembly, story cut, B-roll, motion graphics, captions, audio mix and colour, with Shorts repurposing, and that it offers white-label work for agencies (Vidpros).

Choose it if you upload often and want predictable monthly capacity.

Vidchops: best for credit-based plans and Shorts

Vidchops runs subscription plans based on monthly credits, where one credit is one long-form video or four short-form videos, and credits do not roll over. Its plans are Vidchops Weekly, Vidchops Pro and Full Time Editor, and it lists a 14-day money-back guarantee.

Choose it if you publish a fixed number of long videos a month and want Shorts cut from them.

Freelance marketplaces: best for small budgets and one-off videos

Marketplaces such as Upwork and Fiverr let you hire an individual editor per project or per hour. Quality varies widely, so this route needs more work from you: write a clear brief, ask for a paid test edit, and check samples in your niche.

Choose it if you upload occasionally, your format is simple, or you want to try outsourcing before committing to a monthly plan.

How to choose between them

  1. How many videos a month? One or two: a freelancer. Four or more: a subscription or agency.
  2. What is the real problem? If viewers leave in the first 30 seconds, you need retention work, not just tidy cuts. YouTube Studio's audience retention report shows the share still watching after 30 seconds, and the dips where people leave.
  3. Who edits each week? Ask whether you get the same editor every time.
  4. How fast are revisions? Ask for turnaround on first cuts and on revisions.
  5. Can they show results? Ask for real before-and-after examples.

It also helps to know what YouTube rewards. YouTube says its recommendations learn from clicks, watch time, survey responses, shares and likes, so an editor who only makes videos look polished may not fix the problem you have. Read YouTube's own guidance on the YouTube Creators site so you can brief an editor clearly.

Questions to ask any editor or agency

  • Will the same editor work on my videos every week?
  • Do you ask for my retention graphs before you start?
  • What is your turnaround on first cuts and on revisions?
  • Can I see a before-and-after retention graph from a real client, with permission?
  • What happens if I want to pause or cancel?

Write the answers in one table, so you compare like with like.

FAQ

Is an agency better than a freelance editor?

Not always. A freelancer is often better value for simple formats and low volume. An agency helps when you need steady capacity, cover when an editor is away, or a wider set of skills.

How much should I budget for YouTube editing?

It ranges from per-video freelance fees to monthly retainers. Volume, length, motion graphics and turnaround speed drive the cost, so get written quotes for your actual format.

Which agencies focus on audience retention?

Look for ones that ask for your retention graphs before they start and talk about specific drop-off moments. Asendify builds its process around retention, but it is currently waitlist only.

Can I learn retention editing instead of hiring?

Yes. Start with your own retention reports, fix one leak per upload, and study openings of videos that hold your attention.

Should I trust an agency that promises more views?

No. Results depend on topic, packaging and audience as well as editing. A good editor promises a process you can check, not numbers.
