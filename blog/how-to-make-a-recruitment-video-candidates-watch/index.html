How do I make a recruitment video that job candidates actually watch to the end?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Keep a job or recruitment video to about one to two minutes, open with the role and what the work actually looks like (not the company's founding story), let real team members describe it in their own words, caption everything, and end with how to apply. LinkedIn's advice on video job descriptions is to "aim for 1-2 minutes" and start with "a compelling introduction". And because a recruitment video is a job advert, the same equality rules apply to what people say and show: describe the job, not the ideal applicant.

Key takeaways

  • One role (or one team) per video; aim for one to two minutes.
  • First seconds: the job title, the team and one vivid detail of the actual work.
  • Real employees beat a CEO speech; unscripted beats scripted.
  • Be honest about the hard parts. A candidate who drops out after watching is a good result.
  • Captions and a transcript are essential: many people watch muted, and accessibility matters to candidates.
  • Avoid wording that implies age, sex or nationality preferences; Acas says to "define the job not the applicant".

What the guidance says

SourceAdvice
LinkedIn: video job descriptions"Aim for 1-2 minutes to ensure viewers stay engaged"; "Start your video job description with a compelling introduction"; show day-to-day work and team dynamics; add captions and a transcript
Acas: advertising a job"Use inclusive language that defines the job not the applicant"; phrases like "recent graduate" or "highly experienced" can discriminate on age; advertise "in at least 2 different ways or places"
Wistia on video lengthEngagement "naturally declines after the five-minute mark"; videos under a minute average about half watched

LinkedIn's page also quotes figures on views and applications from vendor studies; treat those as marketing claims rather than independent research.

Why candidates stop watching

  • The corporate opener: drone over headquarters, logo animation, mission statement.
  • Generic praise: "We're like a family", "fast-paced environment", "passionate people", which every company says.
  • No actual job: two minutes of culture and perks, and the viewer still doesn't know what they'd do on a Tuesday.
  • Scripted employees reading lines in front of a branded wall.
  • Too long: a five-minute "careers film" covering every department.

A 90-second structure

SecondsWhat to showExample
0 to 8Role, team, one concrete moment of the workA support engineer on a call fixing a customer's outage: "This is what my Tuesday looks like."
8 to 35What the job involves day to dayTwo or three real tasks, shown as well as described
35 to 55Who you'd work withThe hiring manager and a peer, each saying one honest thing
55 to 75The hard part and the good part"On-call weeks are busy. We rotate them and you get a day off after."
75 to 90Practical facts and how to applyLocation or remote, salary range if you publish it, closing date, link

Example lines are illustrative. If you show pay, benefits or flexible working, make sure the advert text says the same.

Getting good footage from employees

  • Interview, don't script. Ask open questions ("What did you do yesterday?", "What surprised you when you joined?", "What's hard about this job?") and cut the best answers together.
  • Film people doing the work, then use their interview audio over it.
  • Ask for permission in writing before using anyone's face or voice in a public advert, and agree what happens to the video if they leave.
  • Show the team as it is. Staged diversity is noticeable; leaving people out is too.
  • Keep it short per person: one strong answer each beats long speeches.

Keeping it legal and fair (UK)

A video job advert counts as an advert, so the same rules apply as for text:

Risky in a videoSafer alternative
"We want young, energetic people""The role is hands-on and involves being on your feet for most of a shift"
"Perfect for a recent graduate""No previous experience needed; full training given"
"Looking for a native English speaker""You'll need fluent written and spoken English"
A team only described as "lads"Describe the work and the team neutrally

Acas notes that you "must not ask for or exclude anyone with a certain protected characteristic", except in specific circumstances. If you're unsure, check their guidance.

Also caption the video, provide a transcript, and post the advert in more than one place, since Acas warns that social-only adverts can miss applicants.

Example: cutting a 4-minute careers film into role videos

Example: a logistics company has a 4-minute careers film covering warehouse, drivers and office teams. It opens with the CEO's welcome for 50 seconds.

They re-cut it into three 75-second videos, one per role. The warehouse version opens on a team lead scanning the first pallet of the morning: "Six a.m., this is where it starts." A picker explains the shift pattern and the break schedule, the manager explains training, and it ends on pay, location and the apply link. The CEO's welcome moves to the careers page. Company and details are illustrative.

Where to use recruitment videos

  • At the top of the job advert or careers page.
  • As a short vertical cut for LinkedIn, Instagram or TikTok, with captions burned in.
  • In messages from recruiters to shortlisted candidates.
  • At careers fairs on a loop (silent, with captions).

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to keep viewers watching to the end?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. LinkedIn's and Acas's free guidance covers job-advert basics.

How long should a recruitment video be?

About one to two minutes per role, which matches LinkedIn's advice.

Should the CEO appear in a recruitment video?

Briefly at most. Candidates usually want to hear from the people they'd work with.

Do recruitment videos need captions?

Yes. Many people watch muted, and captions and a transcript make the advert accessible.

Can I say "young team" in a job video?

It's risky. Acas warns that age-related wording can discriminate; describe the job and the culture instead.
