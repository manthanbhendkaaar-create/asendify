How should I frame a podcast set so clips crop cleanly to vertical for Shorts, Reels and TikTok?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Frame each person in their own close shot with their face near the centre of the frame, a little headroom, and nothing important at the far left or right edges. Record in 4K if you can, so a 9:16 crop still gives a full 1080 × 1920 picture. Keep microphones, logos and guests' hands where the vertical crop and the app's on-screen buttons won't hide them.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube counts square or vertical videos up to three minutes as Shorts. Your clips need a vertical version; your full episode should stay 16:9.
  • Meta's Reels ads guidance says to "Keep the bottom 35% of your 9:16 creative free of text, logos, and other key elements", because the app's interface covers it.
  • TikTok's video ad specs list vertical 9:16 as recommended and say "The safe zone size is determined by the dimension", with downloadable safe-zone files.
  • Spotify's video podcast set-up guide advises leaving "empty space above your heads (headroom) and some breathing room on the sides."
  • YouTube's podcasting guide favours "a close up that works well with a mobile-first world", which is also the shot that crops best.

The crop maths, in one table

A 9:16 crop from a 16:9 frame keeps the full height and about a third of the width.

Recording resolutionWidth of a 9:16 cropExport at 1080 × 1920Quality
3840 × 2160 (4K UHD)about 1,215 pxSlight downscaleSharp
2560 × 1440 (1440p)810 pxAbout 1.3× upscaleUsually fine
1920 × 1080 (1080p)about 608 pxAbout 1.8× upscaleSoft, especially faces
Wide two-shot, 1080pabout 608 px, one person only1.8× upscaleOften badly framed

The numbers come straight from the ratio: crop width = frame height × 9 ÷ 16. If you only record 1080p, close single shots matter even more.

Where to put people and objects

Think of each close shot as holding a hidden vertical rectangle in the middle third of the frame.

ElementWhere it should sitWhy
FaceCentre third, eyes about a third from the topSurvives the crop; clears top-of-screen captions and titles
MicrophoneLow and to one side, not across the mouthDoesn't block the face in a tight crop
Hands and propsInside the centre third when shownEdges get cut off
Logo or show nameBehind the speaker, high in the centreVisible in both 16:9 and 9:16
Lower thirds and captionsAdded in the edit, above the bottom 35%App buttons and captions cover the bottom
Second personTheir own camera, not the edge of this oneClean single shots for each speaker

Set design for both shapes

A set that looks good in 16:9 can feel empty or cluttered in 9:16. Three habits help:

  1. Bring the background closer to centre. Plants, lamps and shelves at the far sides vanish in vertical. Put the interesting detail directly behind each person.
  2. Light from the front-side, not the far side. A light at the edge of the frame lights a part of the scene the vertical crop never shows.
  3. Keep the table edge low. In a tall crop, a big desk across the bottom eats space the app already covers with buttons.

Example: re-framing a two-host set

Example: a tech podcast films two hosts at a desk with one 1080p camera showing both. Their Shorts look soft and one host is often half out of frame.

ChangeBeforeAfter
CamerasOne wide 1080p cameraWide plus a close 4K camera on each host
Face positionLeft and right thirds of the wideCentre of each close shot, headroom above
MicrophonesBoom arms in front of mouthsArms from below and to the side
LogoBottom-right corner of the wideNeon sign behind and between hosts, visible in both shapes
EditCrops from the wideCrops from close shots; captions placed above the bottom 35%

The editor checks "stayed to watch" on the next ten Shorts against the last ten. This is an illustrative plan, not a reported result; a clearer picture removes one reason to swipe, but the moment chosen still matters most.

Test before you record

  1. Put a 9:16 guide over the camera's monitor or recording app. Many cameras and phone apps let you add frame guides.
  2. Ask each person to lean back, lean in and gesture as they normally do. Check they stay inside the guide.
  3. Record 30 seconds, crop it in your editor, and watch it on a phone with the app interface in mind.
  4. Place a sticky dot on the floor or desk so people sit in the same place each week.

MistakeFix

Mic covering the mouth in close-upMount from below or the side

FAQ

Is there a playbook on making vertical podcast clips people watch?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. The free platform spec pages cover safe zones.

Should I film a separate vertical camera?

It can help on a busy set, but a close 4K shot of each person usually gives a clean crop without extra gear.

Is 1080p enough for vertical clips?

It works for close single shots, but a 9:16 crop from 1080p is upscaled about 1.8 times, so faces can look soft.

Where should captions go in Shorts and Reels?

Above the bottom third. Meta says to keep the bottom 35% of a 9:16 video free of key elements.

Does framing affect the full episode?

Close, centred shots also look good in 16:9, and YouTube's guide favours close-ups for mobile viewers.
