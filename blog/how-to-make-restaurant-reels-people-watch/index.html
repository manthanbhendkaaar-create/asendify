How do I make restaurant Reels that people watch to the end?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open on the most appetising moment (the cheese pull, the pour, the sizzle) or a clear promise of one, then build to a reveal: the finished plate, the first bite, the full table. Film vertical (9:16) in natural light, keep most Reels short enough that the payoff arrives before attention drifts, and end on one reason to visit, such as the dish name and when it's available. Restaurant Reels lose people when they open on the sign outside or a slow pan across empty tables.

Key takeaways

  • Meta's Reels best practices say Reels work best with a clear beginning, middle and end or a strong hook, and give "a big reveal, punchline or plot twist" as examples of a payoff.
  • Meta recommends vertical 9:16 ("helps ensure your reels are immersive"), natural light, and avoiding watermarks and content that looks recycled from other apps.
  • Meta says to use "one or two hashtags that are trending and/or relevant"; tags like #Reels "will not help you get featured".
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "jump in" and to "feature people" to tell a better story.
  • Food is a natural reveal: process first, finished dish or first bite last.

A 15-second structure

TimeShotPurpose
0 to 2 sThe most visual moment, or a promise of itStops the scroll
2 to 10 sThree or four fast process shotsBuilds anticipation
10 to 13 sThe reveal: finished dish or first bitePayoff
13 to 15 sDish name, when it's on, how to bookReason to visit

Cut to the rhythm of the sound you use, change shot every one to two seconds during the build, and hold the reveal a little longer so it lands.

Filming tips for food

  1. Light from the side by a window. Meta suggests natural light and avoiding light directly above or behind you; side light shows texture.
  2. Get close. Steam, crumbs and drips matter more than the whole room.
  3. Capture sound. Sizzles, pours and crunches help even if many people watch muted; add captions for any speech.
  4. Shoot vertical natively, not cropped from landscape.
  5. Show people, with permission: the chef's hands, a server, a happy table.
  6. Film more than you need, then cut the slow parts.
  7. Don't repost TikToks with the watermark. Meta advises against third-party watermarks and recycled-looking content.

Keep it accurate

Show dishes as customers will receive them: the same portion, the same garnish. If a Reel names a price or offer, make sure it's current. If you mention allergens or dietary claims ("gluten-free", "vegan"), they must be true for that dish as served.

How to tell what's working

Check each Reel's insights for how many people watched to the end and where they dropped. If many leave in the first seconds, the opening shot isn't strong enough; if they leave in the middle, the build is too long. Try two openings for the same dish on different days and keep the style that holds people longer.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to hook viewers in short food videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, reveals and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Meta's Reels best practices are free.

How long should a restaurant Reel be?

Short enough that the reveal arrives before attention drifts; many work at 10 to 30 seconds.

What should a restaurant post on Reels?

Dish builds, behind-the-pass clips, "what to order" picks, prep footage and real reactions.

Should I use trending audio?

It can help, but the food has to be the hook. Use audio you're allowed to use for a business account.

How many hashtags should I use?

Meta suggests one or two that are relevant or trending.
