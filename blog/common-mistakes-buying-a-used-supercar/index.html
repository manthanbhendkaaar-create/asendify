What are the most common mistakes people make when buying a used supercar?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The most common mistakes when buying a used supercar are skipping an independent specialist inspection, budgeting for the price but not the running costs, not checking the MOT history, log book and finance status, falling for a car with gaps in its service history, and buying privately without realising you have fewer legal rights than with a dealer. With cars where one repair can cost thousands, the checks before you pay matter more than the haggle.

Key takeaways

  • Citizens Advice says "Never buy a car without the log book", and that a private history check (up to about £20) can show if a car is stolen, still on finance or was written off and returned to the road.
  • The free GOV.UK MOT history check shows each test result, the mileage recorded and what failed, for cars tested since 2005.
  • Devon, Somerset and Torbay Trading Standards says buying from a trader gives "better legal protection" than buying privately, and strongly recommends an inspection by a qualified engineer if you can't assess a car yourself.
  • Clocking (winding back the odometer) is a known risk: compare the mileage with MOT and service records.
  • Running costs (insurance, servicing, tyres, depreciation) often matter more than a few thousand pounds off the price.

Before you view

  1. Set an all-in budget, including insurance quotes for the exact model and your postcode.
  2. Research common faults for that model and year on owners' forums and specialist sites.
  3. Check the MOT history online using the registration from the advert.
  4. Ask for the service history before travelling.

At the viewing

  • See the original V5C, and check the name and address match the seller.
  • Check the VIN on the car matches the V5C.
  • Look for wear that doesn't match the mileage: pedals, steering wheel, seat bolsters.
  • Test drive from cold if you can.
  • Don't hand over money on the day if anything feels rushed.

Before you pay

  • Run a vehicle history check for finance, theft and write-off markers.
  • Book a specialist pre-purchase inspection.
  • Get any promises (new tyres, a service) in writing.
  • If buying from a dealer, know your rights if the car turns out to be faulty.

Questions to ask the seller

  • How long have you owned it, and why are you selling?
  • Who has serviced it, and can I call them to confirm the work?
  • When were the last major service, clutch, tyres and brakes done?
  • Has it ever been tracked, modified, remapped or repaired after accident damage?
  • Are there any warning lights, known faults or advisories from the last MOT?
  • Is there any finance on the car, and will you settle it before handing it over?
  • Can I take it to a specialist of my choice for an inspection before paying?

Write the answers down. If a later check contradicts what you were told, that tells you as much as the fault itself. A seller who answers clearly and welcomes an inspection is usually a better bet than a cheaper car with vague answers.

FAQ

Is there a planner that ranks supercars by yearly cost of ownership?

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner ranks cars your budget can buy by yearly cost at your mileage. Your own spreadsheet of quotes is a free alternative.

Should I get a used supercar inspected?

Yes. A specialist pre-purchase inspection is one of the most useful things you can pay for. Be wary of sellers who refuse.

How do I check if a supercar has outstanding finance?

A paid vehicle history check can show whether money is still owed on the car.

Is it safer to buy from a dealer than privately?

Trading Standards says buying from a trader gives better legal protection than a private sale.

How can I spot clocking?

Compare the odometer with MOT and service records, and look for wear that seems too heavy for the mileage.
