How do you edit a YouTube livestream replay so people actually watch it?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Cut the "are we live?" opening so the replay starts at the first real moment, remove long dead sections, add chapters so viewers can jump to what they want, and post the best parts as separate highlight clips. YouTube Studio's trim tool can do the cuts without re-uploading, keeping the same URL, views and comments, on videos shorter than six hours.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help on archiving says streams under 12 hours can be archived automatically; longer ones may not be captured, so keep a local recording.
  • YouTube Studio's trim tool removes the start, end or middle sections of a video "without re-uploading", and keeps the URL, views and comments. It works on videos shorter than six hours.
  • Highlights are shorter edited clips you can make during or after a stream, helped by stream markers.
  • A replay viewer is not a live viewer. They don't care about the chat banter or the countdown; they want the content.
  • Chapters turn a two-hour replay into something people can navigate instead of abandon.

Live viewers vs replay viewers

Live viewerReplay viewer
Why they're thereThe event, the chat, being part of itThe content, usually one part of it
Tolerance for waitingHigh: they expect a slow startLow: dead air feels like a broken video
What they needInteractionNavigation and pace
What to give themReal-time chat, shout-outsA trimmed start, chapters, highlight clips

The same footage serves two audiences. Editing the replay is about turning an event into a video.

Step 1: Trim the start

Most streams open with a holding screen, sound checks and "we'll wait a couple of minutes for people to arrive". That's fine live. In a replay it's the worst possible intro, because the opening is where most viewers decide whether to stay. Our guide to key moments in YouTube Studio explains why the first 30 seconds matter so much.

In YouTube Studio, open the replay, choose Editor, then Trim and cut. YouTube Help says you drag the blue box over the part you want to keep, and "anything not in the box will be removed from the video". Start the box at the first moment with real content.

Step 2: Cut dead sections in the middle

Look for technical problems, long breaks, and stretches where you were reading chat with nothing happening on screen. Studio lets you remove middle sections with New cut: drag the red box over the section to remove.

A few limits from YouTube Help to know first:

  • The tool works on videos shorter than six hours.
  • Unedited videos with more than 100,000 views may not save changes (this doesn't apply to YouTube Partner Programme channels).
  • Since June 2025, saved Studio editor changes can't be undone with "revert to original", so keep your local recording.

Step 3: Add chapters

A two-hour replay without chapters asks viewers to scrub blind. YouTube's chapters help page says manual chapters need at least three timestamps in ascending order, the first must be 00:00, and each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long. Our guide on how to add chapters to YouTube videos walks through it.

Name chapters after what happens, not when ("Q&A: pricing questions", not "Part 4").

Step 4: Make highlights

YouTube Help describes highlights as "a shorter, edited version of the live stream". Drop stream markers live when something good happens, then use Create highlight to trim each moment into its own video. Highlights do two jobs: they reach people who would never watch two hours, and they point back to the full replay.

Step 5: Check the replay's retention

After a few days, open the replay's audience retention report. Dips show where viewers left or skipped. In a long replay, expect a steep early drop and then people jumping to chapters. Spikes often mark the moments worth turning into highlights.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches re-hooks for long videos, with a 30-day plan?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops, re-hooks and pattern interrupts, with a Bonus Pack including a 30-day plan. YouTube's free help pages on trimming and highlights are a good companion.

Will trimming my replay reset its views?

No. YouTube Help says the Studio trim tool keeps the video's URL, views and comments.

Can I trim a stream longer than six hours?

Not with the Studio editor, which works on videos shorter than six hours. Edit the local recording instead and upload it as a new video.

Should I re-upload the replay as a new video?

Only if the edit is major. A trimmed replay keeps its views and comments; a new upload starts from zero. Our page on video length and retention can help you decide how long the edit should be.

What if my stream was longer than 12 hours?

YouTube Help says streams over 12 hours may not be archived, so always keep a local recording.

Are highlights better than the full replay?

They serve different people. Highlights reach casual viewers; the replay serves people who want everything. Make both and link them together.
