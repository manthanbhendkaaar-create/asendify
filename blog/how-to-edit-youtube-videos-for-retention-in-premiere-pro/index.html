How to Edit YouTube Videos for Retention in Premiere Pro

By Manthan Bhendkar · 7 min read · Updated 12 October 2026

Short answer: Edit for retention in passes: cut pauses, repeats and false starts from a transcript, rebuild the first 30 seconds so they confirm the title and thumbnail, then add visuals wherever attention could drop and finish with clean sound and captions. Premiere's Text-Based Editing, ripple delete, markers and auto ducking make each pass faster. The decisions behind them are the same in any editor.

A quick note on the name: Adobe dropped "Pro" with Premiere 26, so the app is now called Adobe Premiere. Most people still search for Premiere Pro, and everything below applies to both. Feature names come from Adobe's own help pages, listed in the sources at the end.

What does editing for retention mean in Premiere?

Retention editing means making every edit decision around one question: will the viewer keep watching? Premiere does not answer that for you. It gives you tools to cut faster, so you have more time for the decisions that matter.

The workflow below has five passes. Doing them in order stops you polishing a section you later delete.

  1. Set up: write down the promise.
  2. Make a rough cut from the transcript.
  3. Rebuild the opening.
  4. Add visuals and pattern interrupts.
  5. Finish sound and captions, then check the data after upload.

What should you do before you start cutting?

Write the video's title and thumbnail idea at the top of your notes, or in a marker at the start of the sequence. Premiere's markers let you name a point on the timeline and add comments, which makes them a simple way to leave notes for yourself. Then add a marker where the video delivers its main payoff, and one at each point where the topic changes.

This takes a few minutes and shapes every later decision. If you know what the title promises, you know which footage serves it and which footage is only there because it was filmed. Our guide to writing a hook that matches the title and thumbnail covers the promise in more detail.

How do you make a fast rough cut in Premiere?

For talking videos, start from the transcript rather than the waveform.

  • Transcribe the footage. Premiere's Speech to Text creates a transcript of your clips.
  • Cut from the text. Text-Based Editing lets you create and edit sequences using the transcript. Adobe also documents Paper Edit, for building a sequence from transcript selections.
  • Remove pauses and fillers. Adobe's help explains how to detect and delete pauses, fillers, text or speakers in transcripts.
  • Close the gaps. Use ripple delete when you remove clips on the timeline, so the clips after them move up and no gaps are left.

Then do the part no tool can do: remove what repeats. Most first takes make the same point twice, or explain something the viewer already understands. Read the transcript and ask of each paragraph, "If this disappeared, would the viewer miss it?" If not, cut it.

Do not cut every breath. Automatic pause removal can make speech sound rushed. Play the rough cut back at normal speed and restore any pause that gives a point room to land.

How do you rebuild the first 30 seconds?

YouTube's retention report has an "intro" figure: the percentage of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds. YouTube says a strong intro can mean the opening matched what viewers expected from the title and thumbnail. So edit the opening last, once you know what the video actually delivers.

  • Move the line that confirms the title as close to the start as you can.
  • Cut greetings, channel recaps and "before we start" sections from the opening.
  • If the payoff is strong, show a glimpse of it early and promise the rest.
  • Keep any logo animation short, or move it after the hook.

We cover this in more depth in how long a YouTube intro should be.

Where should B-roll, graphics and pattern interrupts go?

Add visuals where attention is most likely to drop, not at fixed intervals. Good places are:

  • When the topic changes (your markers from the setup pass).
  • When the speaker explains a number, a process or a comparison that is easier to see than hear.
  • During any long stretch of the same shot.
  • When the speaker mentions something the viewer cannot see.

Each visual should do a job: show the thing being described, put a key number on screen, or reset attention with a new angle or a punch-in. A transition or effect that does none of those is decoration. On finance and education channels in particular, clear on-screen numbers tend to help more than flashy motion.

How do you finish sound and captions?

Viewers forgive plain visuals more easily than bad audio. Before export:

  • Balance music under speech. Premiere's Essential Sound panel can automatically lower background music when dialogue plays (auto ducking).
  • Keep levels consistent between sections, and between the speaker and any clips you insert.
  • Add captions if they help. Speech to Text can turn the transcript you already made into captions. Check names and numbers by hand.

Then run through our pre-upload editing checklist before you export.

How do you check the edit after upload?

Open the video's audience retention report in YouTube Studio once it has collected some views. YouTube describes dips as moments viewers skipped or where they stopped watching, and spikes as moments that were rewatched or shared. Note the timestamp of the biggest dip, go to that point in your Premiere project, and look at what happens just before it. Our guide to why viewers leave YouTube videos lists the usual causes.

Save your sequence settings, caption style and graphics as templates, so the next video starts from what worked.

Does this workflow work in DaVinci Resolve or CapCut?

Yes. The five passes are about decisions, not buttons. DaVinci Resolve, CapCut and Final Cut Pro all let you cut, close gaps, add B-roll, mix audio and add captions, though the feature names, and what is included in free versions, differ. Check each tool's official documentation for the exact steps.

Frequently asked questions

Is Premiere Pro good for YouTube retention editing?

Yes. It has the tools most retention workflows need: transcript-based editing, pause removal, ripple delete, markers, auto ducking and captions. But retention comes from the decisions, not the software, so the same results are possible in other editors.

What is the fastest way to remove pauses in Premiere?

Transcribe the footage, then use Text-Based Editing to detect and delete pauses and fillers in the transcript. Play the result back afterwards and restore any pause that makes the speech sound rushed.

Is Premiere Pro now called Adobe Premiere?

Yes. Adobe dropped "Pro" from the name with Premiere 26. It is still the same editing app, and the workflow in this guide applies under either name.

Should I edit the hook first or last?

Last, or at least revisit it last. Once the rough cut is done you know what the video really delivers, and you can make the first 30 seconds confirm that promise.

Do I need plugins to edit for retention?

No. Built-in tools cover every pass in this guide. Plugins can save time on specific tasks, but they do not replace deciding what to cut and where to add visuals.
