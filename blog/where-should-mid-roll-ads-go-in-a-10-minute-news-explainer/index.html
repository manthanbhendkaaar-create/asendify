Where should mid-roll ads go in a 10-minute news explainer?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 26 November 2026 · Last updated 26 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Put mid-roll ads at natural breaks between sections of the story, such as after the context and before the analysis, and never mid-sentence. YouTube says manual mid-rolls need a monetised video of 8 minutes or longer, so a 10-minute explainer qualifies, and slots at natural pauses are more likely to serve ads.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help on mid-roll ads says monetised videos of 8 minutes or longer can have ads during the video, and that automatic slots are on by default.
  • It says slots at "natural breakpoints, such as a pause in audio or transition in visual" are more likely to serve ads, and that cutting mid-sentence or mid-action makes slots less likely to serve.
  • Manual slots are edited in YouTube Studio on desktop; red-flagged slots in Studio can be moved.
  • YouTube's retention report help says dips show where viewers abandon or skip, so check your graph before choosing slots.
  • An ad slot does not guarantee an ad, and ads can cost viewers, so test one or two slots, not six.

Where the breaks usually fall

Point in a 10-minute explainerWhy it worksRisk
After the hook and context (about 2:30 to 3:30)Viewers have the question and the backgroundPlacing before the hook payoff loses people
Between "what happened" and "why it matters" (about 5:00 to 6:00)A clean topic changeIf it comes too late, fewer see it
Before the conclusion (about 8:00 to 8:30)Viewers who reach it are committedFew viewers remain, so lower reach
Mid-sentence or mid-demoNeverYouTube says these are less likely to serve ads and they irritate viewers

These times are examples, not YouTube rules. Use your own structure.

How to choose

  1. Mark the section changes in your edit: context, development, analysis, conclusion.
  2. Check the retention graph from a similar video. Avoid putting a break on a spot where viewers already drop.
  3. Insert slots manually in YouTube Studio on desktop at those pauses.
  4. Leave a beat of silence or a visual change before the break so it feels natural.
  5. Keep the automatic slots off or review them, since they are on by default.
  6. Compare average view duration and revenue between two versions over several videos.

Why a news explainer needs care

A news explainer relies on trust. An ad that lands in the middle of a sentence about an event feels careless. Cut so each section ends on a clear line, add a half-second pause, then let the break fall there. Do not place a break where you promise to reveal something; hold the reveal until after the break only if the wait is short.

Example: a 10-minute explainer on a rail strike

Example: the video runs 10:00. Hook 0:00 to 0:25, background to 2:40, the dispute to 5:30, the effect on passengers to 8:00, what happens next to 10:00. The editor sets two manual slots: one at 2:40, after the background, and one at 5:30, between the dispute and the effect. The automatic slots are turned off. After four videos the creator compares retention at those points with an earlier video that had automatic slots, and keeps the manual positions if viewers stay. How long must a video be for mid-roll ads?

YouTube says monetised videos of 8 minutes or longer can have mid-roll ads.

Should I use automatic or manual slots?

Automatic slots are on by default. Manual slots let you choose natural breaks; you can use both or turn automatic off.

Does every slot show an ad?

No. YouTube says slots do not guarantee an ad.

Can I move a slot after publishing?

Yes, in YouTube Studio on desktop.
