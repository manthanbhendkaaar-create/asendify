Do YouTube Shorts hurt long-form videos on the same channel?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

No, according to YouTube. It says it has built separate recommendation systems for Shorts and long-form videos and has "not seen evidence of Shorts negatively impacting long-form performance". What can happen is that Shorts viewers don't go on to watch your long videos, so judge Shorts by whether they bring the right people, not by raw views.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's myth-busting post on Shorts says Shorts and long-form have separate recommendation systems, each focused mainly on that format's viewing patterns.
  • In a 2023 Q&A reported by TechCrunch, YouTube said it does not see Shorts as "cannibalizing long-form", and that the Shorts feed favours variety because viewers swipe without choosing.
  • The real risk is mismatch: a Short on a different topic brings subscribers who never watch your long videos.
  • Measure it: look at returning viewers and how many long-form viewers came from Shorts, not just Shorts views.
  • Link each Short to a matching long video so interested viewers have a direct path.

These are YouTube's statements, not independent studies. They tell you the system isn't designed to punish a channel for posting both formats. They don't promise that Shorts will grow your long-form audience.

Why some creators feel Shorts hurt them

The audiences are different

The TechCrunch report quotes YouTube's explanation: long-form viewers actively choose a video, while Shorts viewers swipe without knowing what's next. A person who enjoyed a 20-second clip in the feed has not chosen your channel in the same way.

Mismatched topics dilute the audience

If a cooking channel posts viral pranks as Shorts, it can gain thousands of subscribers who never want a 15-minute recipe. Those subscribers then see long videos in their feeds and ignore them. That isn't the algorithm punishing you; it's an audience that never wanted the long video.

Different metrics confuse the picture

Shorts views and long-form views mean different things. YouTube's engaged views explainer says a Short now counts as a view "the moment it starts to play", while "engaged views" (the older, stricter count) still drive analytics such as retention and average view duration. Comparing a Short's view count with a long video's view count tells you little.

How to check your own channel

  1. Returning viewers on long videos. In YouTube Studio, compare returning viewers on long videos before and after you started posting Shorts. Our page on why new and returning viewers retain differently explains why returning viewers usually hold better.
  2. Retention on long videos. Look at the average retention curve on long videos over the same period. A Shorts habit shouldn't change how a long video holds the people who click it.
  3. Traffic from Shorts. Check how many long-form views come from your Shorts (via related-video links and channel visits).
  4. Topic match. List your last 20 Shorts. Mark each as "same topic as long videos" or "different". If most are different, that's the likely problem.

How to make Shorts help long videos

  • Same topic, smaller bite. Cut Shorts from your long videos or on the same subject. Our guide to picking the best moment of a long video for a Short covers how.
  • Add a related video link. YouTube lets you link a Short to a long video. See how to add a related video link to Shorts.
  • Leave a question open. End the Short with the question the long video answers. That's an open loop across formats.
  • Don't delete flops. YouTube advised against deleting and reposting Shorts.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops that carry viewers from a Short into a long video?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops, re-hooks and pattern interrupts, with a Bonus Pack including a 30-day plan. YouTube's own blog posts on Shorts are a free companion.

Should I start a separate channel for Shorts?

YouTube's position is that both formats can live on one channel. A separate channel makes sense mainly when the Shorts are on a completely different topic from your long videos.

Do Shorts subscribers watch long videos?

Some do and some don't. Subscribers from on-topic Shorts are more likely to watch, because they came for the same subject.

Does the Shorts algorithm use my long-form data?

YouTube says the systems understand cross-format viewing but focus mainly on each format's own viewing patterns.

Should I delete Shorts that flopped?

YouTube advised against deleting and reposting Shorts to revive them, because it may treat that as spam.

How many Shorts should I post?

YouTube doesn't set a number. Post as many on-topic Shorts as you can make well; volume of off-topic clips is what tends to dilute an audience.
