How does stock B-roll affect viewer retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Stock B-roll helps retention when it shows exactly what the speaker is saying, adds a change of picture and hides a cut. It hurts when it is generic filler, such as handshakes, people typing and city time-lapses, because it adds nothing and research on learning shows interesting but irrelevant material distracts. Match every stock clip to a specific line, or leave it out.

Key takeaways

  • Richard Mayer's coherence principle states "people learn more deeply from a multimedia message when extraneous material is excluded rather than included", with positive results in 18 of 19 tests.
  • A meta-analysis of 58 studies and over 7,500 students, reported by Washington State University, found "interesting but irrelevant" extras lowered learning scores.
  • Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014) found videos that intersperse a talking head with slides were more engaging than slides alone, so changing the picture can help when it supports the content.
  • These studies measure learning and engagement in educational video, not YouTube retention. They point the same way as editors' experience: relevance beats decoration.
  • Check YouTube's key moments report for dips where stock-heavy sections run.

Why generic stock can hurt

Viewers are always deciding whether the next minute is worth it. When the picture says the same thing as a hundred other videos, it signals low effort. When it contradicts or ignores the words, it splits attention. That is the coherence and seductive-details problem in practice: the brain spends effort on material that does not help it follow the point.

Stock footage also has a recognition problem. Popular free clips appear in many videos, and regular viewers notice. That can make your video feel templated, even if the script is good.

How to choose stock B-roll that holds attention

  1. Write B-roll notes in the script. Next to each line, write what the viewer should see. If you cannot think of something specific, keep the speaker on screen.
  2. Search for the noun, not the mood. "Shipping containers at night", not "business success".
  3. Prefer footage that adds information: a real place, a product, a chart, archive material, a screen recording.
  4. Keep clips short enough that they change with the idea, and cut back to the speaker for key claims.
  5. Match the look. Grade stock to sit with your main footage so it does not jump out.
  6. Check the licence. Use footage you are allowed to use for your purpose, especially in ads.
  7. Review retention after publishing and compare stock-heavy sections with speaker-led ones.

Stock, AI or your own footage?

Your own footage is the most specific and trusted, but takes time. Stock is fast and affordable. AI-generated B-roll can fill gaps but needs care to avoid a misleading or artificial look. Many teams mix all three: own footage for the core, stock for context, and graphics for numbers.

FAQ

Is there a guide on choosing B-roll for retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers visuals, pacing and hooks with templates and a 30-day plan. Mayer's free multimedia research summaries cover the principles behind it.

Does stock footage make a video look cheap?

Generic or overused clips can. Specific, well-graded stock that shows what you are saying usually does not.

How much B-roll should a YouTube video have?

There is no fixed ratio. Use B-roll where it shows something specific, and keep the speaker on screen for key claims.

Is free stock footage safe to use in ads?

Check each licence. Some free licences allow commercial use, but restrictions on people, logos and trademarks can still apply.

Can B-roll hide jump cuts?

Yes. Placing a relevant clip over a cut is a common way to smooth an edit without slowing it down.
