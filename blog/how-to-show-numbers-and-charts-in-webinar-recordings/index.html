How do I show numbers or charts in webinar recordings without losing attention?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Show one number or one simple chart at a time, highlight the part that matters, and say the takeaway out loud as it appears. Build complex charts step by step rather than revealing everything at once, put labels on the chart instead of in a legend, and cut slides that only repeat what you say. In the recording, zoom into the chart and trim the time spent reading slides aloud. Research on learning from video supports each of these: less clutter, clear signals and words that match the picture.

Key takeaways

  • Richard Mayer's research summary (UNH PDF) found people learn more when "extraneous material is excluded" (coherence, 18 of 19 tests), when cues highlight the organisation (signalling, 26 of 28 tests), and when graphics and narration are presented at the same time (temporal contiguity, 8 of 8 tests).
  • The same summary found no gain from adding on-screen text to narrated graphics (redundancy, 8 of 12 tests). Don't put your script on the slide.
  • In 6.9 million edX sessions, Khan-style tablet drawing tutorials held students 1.5 to 2 times as long as PowerPoint slides or code screencasts (Guo, Kim and Rubin, 2014). Drawing or building a chart live beats a static slide.
  • For longer videos, Wistia says "the 1st, 2nd, and 4th quarters are prime spots for a lead generation form", which is useful if your webinar replay asks for sign-ups.
  • YouTube's retention help says dips show where viewers abandon or skip, so check whether your data-heavy sections cause them.

Before the webinar: prepare the slides

  1. Write the takeaway first, then design the chart to show it.
  2. Cut every chart that doesn't support a decision or a point you're making.
  3. Split complex charts into two or three simpler ones.
  4. Use large fonts. Many replays are watched on phones.
  5. Plan a reveal order with animation steps, so each element appears as you mention it.

Quick checklist for a data-heavy webinar

  • Every chart has a takeaway title.
  • No slide shows more than one main idea.
  • The key bar, line or number is highlighted; everything else is muted.
  • Labels sit on the chart, not in a legend.
  • Fonts are large enough to read on a phone.
  • Complex charts are split or revealed in steps.
  • In the replay, charts are zoomed in and long pauses on static slides are cut.
  • The full data is in a handout for people who want detail.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches pacing and pattern interrupts for long recordings?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers pacing for £1,720 one-time. Mayer's multimedia principles are free.

Should I share the slides separately?

Yes. Offer the deck or a one-page summary for people who want the detail. The video only needs the point.

Are tables ever better than charts?

For exact values people need to look up, yes, but put them in the handout, not on screen.

How long should a data slide stay on screen?

As long as it takes to make the point, then move on. Most need well under a minute.

Should I animate charts?

Simple build-ups help. Flashy transitions don't.

What if my audience needs the detailed numbers?

Give them the detail in a handout, a downloadable deck or a link to the full report, and use the video to explain what the numbers mean. Viewers who want to check the figures can study them at their own pace, while everyone else keeps following the story. Mention the handout early so detail-focused viewers don't feel they need to pause and squint at the screen.
