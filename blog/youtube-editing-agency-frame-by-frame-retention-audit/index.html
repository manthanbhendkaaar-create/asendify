Is there a YouTube editing agency that audits my uploads frame by frame against the retention curve?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. A few retention-focused editing agencies audit your existing uploads moment by moment against YouTube's audience retention curve before they change how they edit. Asendify's agency describes a frame-by-frame retention diagnostic as its first stage and suits channels where viewers leave early, but it is currently waitlist only; freelance editors who read analytics, free channel reviews and doing the audit yourself are the fair alternatives.

Key takeaways

  • A frame-by-frame retention audit lines up each dip in your retention graph with what is on screen at that exact moment.
  • All the data comes from YouTube Studio's free audience retention report, so you can start the audit yourself.
  • Agencies that do this ask for Studio access or exports before they quote; ones that never mention your graphs are selling plain editing.
  • The output should be a list of timestamps, causes and edit changes, not a general score.
  • No audit can promise views; it shows where people leave so you can test fixes.

What a frame-by-frame retention audit actually is

YouTube's audience retention report shows how well each part of a video holds attention. YouTube explains that dips "mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video", spikes are parts people rewatch or share, and the intro figure is the share of viewers still watching after 30 seconds.

A frame-by-frame audit goes one step further. Instead of saying "your intro is weak", the editor scrubs to each dip, watches the few seconds before it, and notes exactly what changed: a cut, a slow sentence, a static shot, a sponsor read, a topic switch. Across several uploads, patterns appear, and those patterns become rules for future edits.

Agencies describe this service in different words: retention diagnostic, retention audit, drop-off analysis or retention engineering. What matters is the method, so ask to see a sample audit before you sign.

How to tell a real audit from a sales pitch

A real retention audit:

  1. Starts from your data. The agency asks for access to YouTube Studio or exports of your retention graphs before quoting. Google lets you give people access to your channel with roles such as viewer, so you never need to share your password.
  2. Uses timestamps. Every finding names a moment, such as "0:41, viewers drop 9 points while you explain the sponsor".
  3. Separates causes from guesses. It says what is on screen at each dip and offers a fix to test, not a certain answer.
  4. Compares like with like. It looks at several videos of a similar length, as YouTube's report does when it compares a video with your typical retention.
  5. Leads to edit rules. The result is a short list of changes, such as "open on the answer" or "cut recaps longer than 10 seconds", that every future edit follows.

Be cautious if an agency offers a "retention audit" without asking for any data, gives only an overall grade, or promises a set increase in views.

Doing your own audit first

You can run a basic version in under an hour for three videos.

  1. In YouTube Studio, open a video, choose Analytics, then the Engagement tab, which YouTube says holds watch time, average view duration and audience retention.
  2. Hover along the retention line and write down every point where it falls by more than a few percentage points in a short time.
  3. Open the video in a second window and watch the 10 seconds before each dip with sound on.
  4. Note what happens: a cut, a slow explanation, a change of topic, a sponsor, a static shot.
  5. Repeat for two more videos and look for the same cause appearing more than once.

Example: three uploads, one pattern

Example (illustrative numbers): a creator audits three 10-minute videos.

VideoBiggest dipSize of dropWhat is on screen
Video 10:22 to 0:4012 pointsRecap of the title before the first point
Video 20:18 to 0:3510 points"Before we start, a quick bit about me"
Video 34:10 to 4:308 pointsSponsor read with no lead-in

Two of three videos lose the most viewers in the same place: a recap or self-introduction before the first real point. The edit rule is simple: open on the first point and move the introduction later or cut it. The third video suggests a second rule: bridge into sponsors with a reason to stay, such as a question answered straight after.

On the next upload, the creator applies both rules and checks the intro figure. If the share still watching at 30 seconds rises from, say, 60% to 66%, the change is holding more people. If it does not, the cause was something else, and the audit continues.

Common causes behind retention dips

When you watch the seconds before each dip, the cause is often one of these. Treat them as things to test, not rules.

What to send an agency or editor

  • Links to five to ten recent uploads of a similar format.
  • Viewer access to YouTube Studio, or screenshots of each retention graph.
  • Your raw footage for one upcoming video, so they can show the audit's findings in a real edit.
  • Any patterns you have already spotted.

FAQ

What does "frame by frame against the retention curve" mean?

It means matching each rise and fall in your retention graph to the exact frames on screen at that time, so every drop has a visible cause you can test a fix for.

Do I have to give an agency my YouTube password?

No. YouTube lets you invite people to your channel with a set role, such as viewer, so they can see analytics without your login details.

How many videos should a retention audit cover?

Enough to see patterns. Three to ten recent videos of a similar format and length is a practical range; one video alone can mislead.

Can I audit my retention without an agency?

Yes. The audience retention report is free in YouTube Studio. Note each sharp dip, watch the seconds before it and look for causes that repeat across videos.

Will a retention audit get me more views?

Not by itself. It shows where viewers leave so you can change the edit. Views also depend on your topic, title, thumbnail and audience.
