Yes. Key takeaways

  • ASCEND's first three episodes cover sleep, skin and posture, the three basics many glow-up guides start with.
  • NHS sleep advice centres on regular sleep and wake times, no screens for an hour before bed, and a quiet, dark, cool room.
  • NHS sun advice is sunscreen of at least SPF 30 with at least 4-star UVA protection, reapplied every 2 hours outdoors.
  • An NHS physiotherapy leaflet says there is no single "perfect" posture; changing position regularly matters more.
  • Add one routine at a time. Research suggests a new daily habit takes about 66 days on average to feel automatic.

Sleep: what the guidance says

The NHS page on how to fall asleep faster and sleep better recommends:

Go to bed and get up at similar times, including days offHelps your body know when it's time to sleep

A "sleep reset" in any form usually starts with the first row: a fixed wake time, every day.

Skin: keep it simple

Many three-step routines come down to cleanse, moisturise and protect. The protect step is the one with the clearest official guidance. The NHS page on sunscreen and sun safety advises sunscreen with an SPF of at least 30 and at least 4-star UVA protection, applied generously, reapplied every 2 hours when outdoors and after swimming or sweating. It also notes the sun is strongest between 11am and 3pm from March to October in the UK, and that you can burn even on cloudy days.

If you have acne, eczema or another skin condition, a pharmacist or GP can advise on products that suit it.

Posture: move more, worry less

Posture advice online often promises that standing straighter will fix everything. The NHS is more measured. A Cambridge University Hospitals physiotherapy leaflet, myth busting about posture, says "There is no such thing as 'perfect' posture", that there is no clear evidence bad posture causes back pain, and that the key is time spent in one position. Its message: "There is no 'best' posture, but change it regularly."

So a sensible posture reset is less about holding a stiff pose and more about breaking up long sitting, supporting your back when you do sit, and standing with relaxed confidence when it counts. ASCEND frames it as presence: how you carry yourself when you walk into a room.

Why one at a time

A University College London study led by Phillippa Lally, reported by UCL in 2009, found it took about 66 days on average for a new daily behaviour to feel automatic, with wide variation. Missing one day didn't significantly harm progress, but very inconsistent people didn't form habits. Starting three routines on the same Monday makes it more likely you'll drop all three.

Common mistakes

  • Starting everything at once. Pick one.
  • Buying ten products. Three basics are enough to start.
  • Lie-ins at the weekend. They undo a fixed wake time.
  • Forcing a rigid posture. Movement beats stiffness.
  • Ignoring real symptoms. Long-term insomnia, painful skin or ongoing pain need a professional.
