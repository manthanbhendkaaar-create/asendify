Where should open loops go in an explainer video?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Put the main open loop in the first few seconds, as the question the explainer answers ("Why does your energy bill go up when you use less?"), and then end each step with a small loop into the next one. Close the main question just before the call to action. Explainers work best when that question is concrete; research on educational video recommends signalling, segmenting and cutting extraneous material, and loops are a natural way to signal what comes next.

Key takeaways

  • An explainer's open loop is its central question, stated in the viewer's words.
  • Brame's review describes signalling as cues that help direct learner attention. A spoken "but there's a catch" is a signal and a loop at once.
  • Break the explanation into steps and bridge each step with a small question.
  • Wistia says viewers usually watch over 50% of educational videos of 1 to 5 minutes and advises putting the most important information in the first half; answer part of the main question early.
  • Check the retention graph: YouTube says a flat line means viewers are watching that part from start to finish, while dips show where they leave.

Why explainers need loops

Explainers often go in a logical order: background, definitions, how it works, benefits, call to action. Logical isn't the same as gripping. If the viewer doesn't know why they need the background, they skip it or leave. A loop turns "here's some background" into "to see why your bill goes up, you need to know one thing about how meters work".

Mistakes to avoid

  • A question no one asked. "What is blockchain?" is weaker than "Why did my bank refuse a crypto transfer?"
  • Answering the main question in the first 10 seconds, then explaining for two minutes with no reason to stay.
  • Never answering it at all. "Find out more on our website" instead of a payoff damages trust.
  • Too many loops at once. One main question and one bridge at a time is enough.
  • Animation without signalling. Pretty motion graphics don't hold attention unless they show the step you are on.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops for explainers?

The Retention Editing Playbook teaches open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time; it is YouTube-first. Brame's CBE Life Sciences Education review is a free alternative on learning design.

What should the first line of an explainer be?

The question the video answers, framed as a real situation the viewer recognises.

How many open loops should an explainer have?

One main question, plus a small bridge loop between each step.

When should the main question be answered?

Partly early, so viewers trust the video, and fully just before the call to action.

Do loops work in animated explainers?

Yes. Show the question visually and use on-screen text to signal each step.
