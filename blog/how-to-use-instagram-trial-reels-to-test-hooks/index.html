How do I use Instagram trial reels to test hooks before my followers see them?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Make two versions of the same reel that differ only in the first 3 seconds, turn on the "Trial" toggle for each before sharing, and wait at least 24 hours. Trial reels go to non-followers first and stay off your grid, so you can compare views, likes, comments and shares, then share the stronger version with everyone and archive the other.

Key takeaways

  • Trial reels are shown to non-followers first and do not appear on your grid or Reels tab unless you choose to share them with everyone.
  • Engagement figures (views, likes, comments, shares) appear about 24 hours after you post a trial.
  • You can set a trial to share itself with followers if it performs well on views in its first 72 hours.
  • Trial reels launched for professional accounts in December 2024; check Instagram's Help Center if you cannot see the toggle.
  • A fair test changes one thing only, usually the hook, and keeps the rest of the edit identical.

How trial reels work

Instagram announced trial reels on 10 December 2024 on its creator blog and in Meta's newsroom. You create a reel as normal, switch on "Trial" before sharing, and the reel is shown to people who do not follow you. Followers will not see it in Feed or the Reels tab, though Instagram says some may still come across it, for example if it is sent to them or appears on a page for the same audio. TechCrunch's launch report says the feature was rolling out to everyone with a professional account.

Why test hooks rather than whole reels

Instagram's ranking explainer says one of its most important Reels predictions is how likely someone is to watch a reel all the way through. Nobody watches to the end if they leave in the first seconds, so the opening is where small edits make the biggest difference. Testing whole reels against each other tells you which topic people preferred; testing two openings of the same reel tells you what kind of hook works for you.

A simple hook test, step by step

  1. Pick one reel you believe in. Choose a topic that has worked before, so a weak result points at the hook, not the idea.
  2. Write two openings. For example, A opens on the result; B opens on a question. Keep the same length, music and ending.
  3. Export both cleanly. No watermark from another app, full resolution.
  4. Post both as trials within a short gap. Same day, similar time, similar caption.
  5. Wait at least 24 hours. Check views, likes, comments and shares for each, plus the comparison with earlier trials.
  6. Share the winner. Tap "share with everyone" on the stronger version. Archive the other.
  7. Write down what won. After five or six tests you have a pattern, not a guess.

Common mistakes

  • Changing the hook, the music and the length at once, then not knowing which change mattered.
  • Declaring a winner after a few hours, before the 24-hour figures arrive.
  • Using a hook that the rest of the reel does not deliver on.
  • Running trials on every reel. Save them for ideas you want to learn from.

FAQ

Is there a book with hook formulas to test on Reels?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) includes hooks, a cheat sheet and templates for £1,720 one-time. Instagram's creator blog is a free alternative.

Who can use trial reels?

Instagram rolled them out to professional accounts from December 2024. If you cannot see the Trial toggle, check that your account is professional and see Instagram's Help Center.

Will my followers see a trial reel?

Not in Feed or the Reels tab, but Instagram says some may still come across it, for example if someone sends it to them.

How long should I wait before judging a trial?

At least 24 hours, which is when Instagram shows the engagement figures. The auto-share option looks at the first 72 hours.

Can I share a trial reel to my grid later?

Yes. Tap "share with everyone" and it appears on your profile and can reach your followers.
