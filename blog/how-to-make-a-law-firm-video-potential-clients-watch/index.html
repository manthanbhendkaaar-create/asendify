How do I make a law firm video that potential clients watch to the end?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Start with the client's problem in their own words, not the firm's history. Someone watching a law firm video is usually worried about a specific situation (a divorce, a dismissal, a house purchase, a will), so open with that situation, let one lawyer explain plainly how the firm helps with it and what the first conversation looks like, keep it to roughly 60 seconds to two minutes, and end with one clear next step.

This page is about video marketing for law firms. It isn't legal advice or regulatory advice; check your regulator's rules before publishing.

Key takeaways

  • One practice area per video. A "we do everything" firm video holds nobody.
  • Clients choose a person, so a real lawyer on camera beats stock footage of gavels and bookshelves.
  • Explain the process (first call, what to bring, what happens next) rather than reciting credentials.
  • In England and Wales, the SRA Code requires that publicity is "accurate and not misleading", including anything you say about charges.
  • Plain language keeps people watching; jargon makes them leave.

A 90-second structure for a practice-area video

SecondsContent
0 to 8The situation in the client's words: "If your employer has just told you your job is at risk..."
8 to 25Who the lawyer is and the kind of people they help, in one or two sentences
25 to 60What happens when you get in touch: first call, what to have ready, how fees are explained
60 to 80One reassurance about how the firm works (clear updates, plain explanations), not a promised result
80 to 90The next step on screen: call, form or booking link

Filming and editing tips

  1. Interview format. Ask questions off camera and cut the natural answers together; it sounds less rehearsed.
  2. Eye level, soft light, quiet room. Clients should feel they're sitting across the desk.
  3. Two cameras (or a second angle) so you can tighten answers without jump cuts.
  4. B-roll of real moments: a meeting room, the lawyer reading a document, the reception. Avoid staged handshakes.
  5. Captions and a lower-third with name and role.
  6. Cut to the point. If a sentence doesn't help the viewer decide, remove it.

What to avoid saying

The SRA's guidance on marketing to the public warns against misstating "the prospects of success of any claim" or "the level of any award likely to be made", and says "no win, no fee" should be clearly explained. So avoid "we'll win your case", invented success rates or vague "no cost to you" lines. Outside England and Wales, follow your own bar or regulator's advertising rules.

Example: an employment law video

Example: a firm's homepage video is three minutes of partners introducing themselves. The new employment video opens with "Been told you're at risk of redundancy and not sure what it means?" A solicitor explains, in plain words, what the first free call covers, what documents to have, and how fees will be explained before any work starts, then "Book a call on our website." FAQ

Is there a book on making professional-service videos people watch?

The Retention Editing Playbook (£1,720 one-time) teaches hooks and structure for YouTube; it isn't law-specific.

How long should a law firm video be?

Roughly 60 seconds to two minutes for a practice-area page; shorter cuts for social.

Should partners or junior lawyers appear?

Whoever the client is likely to speak to and whoever explains things most clearly on camera.

Can we mention fees in the video?

If you do, they must be accurate and not misleading. Many firms point to the fees page where full details sit.

Can we use client testimonials?

Only genuine ones, with permission, and without misleading viewers about likely outcomes.
