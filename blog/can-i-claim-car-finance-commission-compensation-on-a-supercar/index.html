Can I claim car finance commission compensation on a supercar deal?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Possibly. The FCA's car finance compensation scheme covers car, van and motorbike finance, including PCP and hire purchase, taken out between 6 April 2007 and 1 November 2024 where the lender and broker had an undisclosed commission arrangement. But high-value loans above yearly thresholds and leases are excluded, which can rule out many supercar deals. As of November 2026, parts of the scheme are suspended pending a court challenge.

This page is general information, not financial or legal advice. Check the FCA's car finance pages for the latest position.

Key takeaways

  • Eligible agreements: car, motorbike, van or campervan finance from 6 April 2007 to 1 November 2024, including HP and PCP, with an undisclosed commission arrangement.
  • Covered arrangements include discretionary commission, high commission (at least 39% of the total cost of credit and 10% of the loan) and contractual ties between broker and lender.
  • Excluded: personal contract hire (leases), business agreements, high-value loans above the yearly thresholds, and cases already decided.
  • The FCA estimates about 12.1 million agreements (37%) are covered, with average compensation of around £830 per agreement.
  • A legal challenge is due to be heard from 16 February to 3 March 2027; until it ends, lenders do not have to calculate or pay compensation.

Who is covered

The FCA's page on car finance compensation (updated 3 November 2026) sets out the scheme:

TestCoveredNot covered
DateAgreements from 6 April 2007 to 1 November 2024Outside those dates
Type of financeHire purchase and PCP on cars, motorbikes, vans and campervansPersonal contract hire (leases); business agreements
CommissionUndisclosed discretionary commission, high commission or contractual tiesArrangements that were properly disclosed
Loan sizeWithin the yearly thresholdsHigh-value loans above the thresholds; agreements over £25,000 made before April 2008
Past outcomesNot yet resolvedAlready decided by the ombudsman or a court, or compensation already accepted

The high-value exclusion matters for supercar buyers. If your finance was above the threshold for that year, the scheme does not cover it, though you may still be able to complain to your lender in the usual way. Check the thresholds on the FCA's page before assuming you qualify.

The three kinds of hidden arrangement

  • Discretionary commission arrangement (DCA): the broker or dealer could set your interest rate, and earned more commission the higher it set it.
  • High commission: commission was at least 39% of the total cost of credit and 10% of the loan.
  • Contractual ties: the broker only used one lender, or gave it first refusal, without telling you.

How much compensation and when

The FCA says people receiving compensation get "an average of around £830 per agreement", though amounts vary, compensation is generally capped, and in about 1 in 3 cases the final amount is limited. The FCA estimates 37% of agreements, about 12.1 million, are covered.

The timing is uncertain. The scheme has been legally challenged and parts are suspended, with the hearing due between 16 February and 3 March 2027 and a judgment expected in the months after. If the scheme is upheld and not appealed, payments are expected to begin in 2027. Some lenders may make settlement offers in the meantime.

The legal background

The scheme followed years of complaints and court cases. The UK Supreme Court gave judgment on 1 August 2025 in Hopcraft v Close Brothers, heard with Johnson v FirstRand and Wrench v FirstRand. One question was whether Mr Johnson's relationship with his lender was unfair under the Consumer Credit Act. Section 140A of the Act lets a court find a credit relationship unfair because of the agreement's terms, how the lender used its rights, or anything done or not done by or on behalf of the lender, before or after the agreement. A finding can be made even after the agreement has ended.

Raj does not need a claims company. The FCA notes that claims management companies can charge up to 36% of compensation including VAT; on the £830 average that would be about £299. Our page on PCP vs HP vs lease vs cash for a supercar explains how dealer-arranged finance works, and voluntary termination covers your right to end an HP or PCP deal early.

How to complain

  • Find your lender from old bank statements, the dealer or your credit file.
  • Complain to the lender first, for free. The FCA lists lenders' contact details and template letters.
  • Watch the deadlines in the lender's reply; if unhappy, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service.
  • Beware of scams. The FCA will never ask you to transfer money or share PINs or passwords, and you should not be asked for a fee.

Does the car finance compensation scheme cover PCP?

Yes, PCP and hire purchase taken out between 6 April 2007 and 1 November 2024, if there was an undisclosed commission arrangement.

Are leases included?

No. Personal contract hire is excluded.

Are expensive cars excluded?

High-value loans above the yearly thresholds are excluded. Check the FCA's page for the threshold that applied to your agreement.

How much could I get?

The FCA estimates an average of around £830 per agreement, with amounts varying and generally capped.

Do I need a claims company?

No. You can complain to your lender for free. Claims companies can charge up to 36% of compensation including VAT.
