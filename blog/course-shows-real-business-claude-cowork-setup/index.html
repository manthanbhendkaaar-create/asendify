Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. The Cowork Operator is an independent online course by Asendify that documents, step by step, how Asendify runs its own online shop with Claude Cowork: its operator rules, connected apps, scheduled tasks, reporting and safeguards, with prompts, templates and ready-made skills across 8 modules and 33 lessons. It suits business owners who learn best from a working example. Anthropic's own free guides explain the features but not one business's full setup.

Key takeaways

  • Feature guides tell you what Cowork can do. A real setup shows the rules, order and safeguards that make it usable day to day.
  • Anthropic describes Cowork as delegating outcomes: you describe the goal, the outcome and the cadence, and Claude plans and does the work.
  • Scheduled tasks can run in the cloud even when your device is off; tasks that need local files require the desktop app open.
  • Approval modes range from asking before every action to skipping checks; Anthropic says skip mode is only for tasks you fully trust.
  • Any real-world setup should be adapted, not copied. Your tools, risks and customers differ.

Why a worked setup helps

Most people who try Cowork hit the same wall: they understand the features but not how to put them together. Which tasks first? What rules does Claude need? What should always need a human yes? How do you know a scheduled task actually ran?

Anthropic's Cowork product guide frames the core idea simply: you describe the goal, desired outcome and cadence, and Claude plans the steps and does the work, with deliverables that cite the files and messages they came from. The hard part is deciding those goals, outcomes and limits for your own business.

Worked example

Example: Tariq runs a small print-on-demand shop on his own. He has used Claude for writing but never set up Cowork properly. He decides to copy the structure of a documented setup, not its exact content.

By week four he has three scheduled tasks and two skills. He spends about 30 minutes a day reviewing instead of two hours doing. The step he says mattered most was the first: writing the rules before connecting anything.

What he did not copy: the specific apps and products in the example, which did not match his shop. A real setup is a pattern to adapt.

How to use any example setup safely

  1. Start with rules, not tasks. Decide what always needs your approval.
  2. Connect the fewest apps needed for the first task.
  3. Begin with read-only or draft-only work.
  4. Add one scheduled task at a time and confirm it runs.
  5. Review weekly and remove anything you no longer check.
  6. Audit any skill you did not write before using it.
