How do I run a business with Claude Cowork?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 7 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

You run a business with Claude Cowork by treating it as an assistant inside rules you set: you decide what to sell and approve anything risky, while Cowork does repeatable work such as research, reports, documents and file admin. Start with one recurring job, connect only the apps it needs, and review every output before it reaches a customer.

Key takeaways

  • Cowork is good at multi-step knowledge work that ends in a file or summary. It is not a replacement for your judgement about customers, money or strategy.
  • Write your rules down first: tone, what Claude may do alone, and what always needs your approval.
  • Start with one recurring task, get it right by hand, then schedule it.
  • Keep high-stakes actions, such as payments and customer messages, on manual approval.
  • Measure whether each task actually saves you time. Drop the ones that do not.

Step 1: understand what you are working with

Anthropic's get started with Claude Cowork guide describes Cowork as bringing Claude Code's agentic abilities to knowledge work, with no terminal. You describe an outcome, and Claude plans the steps, breaks the work into subtasks and delivers finished outputs, such as spreadsheets with working formulas, presentations and formatted documents.

A few facts shape how you can use it in a business:

  • It needs a paid plan: Pro, Max, Ultra, Team or Enterprise.
  • Sessions run in the cloud on Anthropic's servers and keep going if you close your laptop.
  • Local files, your browser and computer use need the Claude Desktop app open.
  • Sessions cannot currently be shared with other people, though you can share individual artifacts made in a session.
  • On Team and Enterprise plans, admins control some settings, such as whether automatic approval is available.

Step 2: write your operating rules

Before you connect anything, write a short set of rules Claude will follow in every session. Cowork's global instructions apply to every session, and folder instructions add context for a specific folder. Projects let you keep the files, instructions and memory for one area of the business together.

Your rules should cover:

  1. What the business is: products, customers, tone of voice.
  2. What Claude may do alone: for example, draft, research, organise and summarise.
  3. What always needs your approval: sending messages, publishing, spending, deleting, changing prices or accounts.
  4. How to report: a short summary of what was done, what failed and what needs a decision.
  5. What to do when unsure: stop and ask rather than guess.

Step 3: connect only what each job needs

Cowork can use apps you connect and folders you grant access to. Anthropic's guide to using Cowork safely explains that risk comes from two things together: what Claude can read and what it is allowed to do. Content from outside your control, such as customer emails or web pages, can contain hidden instructions designed to manipulate it. This is called prompt injection.

For a business, that means:

  • keep financial documents and other sensitive files out of folders Claude can reach unless a task truly needs them;
  • be careful with websites where you are signed in or that handle money;
  • use trusted connectors only;
  • keep write actions, such as sending email, on approval.

Cowork's permission modes help. Manual asks before acting. Auto lets Claude proceed while each action is checked for safety. Skip removes the checks entirely. Anthropic recommends staying close and reviewing what Claude does for work involving money, messages sent as you or important files.

Step 4: start with one recurring job

Pick a task you already do every week, that has a clear output and low risk if it goes wrong. Good first candidates are a weekly sales summary, a competitor round-up or turning raw notes into a tidy document.

Run it by hand three or four times. Each time, tighten the instructions where the output fell short. When it reliably produces what you need, schedule it. Anthropic's page on scheduled tasks in Cowork lists daily briefings, weekly reports, recurring research and file organisation as common uses. Scheduled tasks run remotely on the cadence you choose, even when your computer is asleep, and each run is its own session you can review.

Step 5: build a review habit

The point of Cowork is that you spend your time checking and deciding rather than producing. That only works if you actually check. A simple routine:

  • read the summary Claude leaves at the end of each task;
  • open the file and spot-check two or three figures against the source;
  • approve, edit or reject anything customer-facing before it goes out;
  • note recurring errors and fix the instructions, not just the output.

Step 6: keep an eye on usage

Multi-step tasks use more of your plan's usage than quick questions, because every step uses tokens. The Cowork guide suggests grouping related work into one task and starting a new conversation for unrelated work. If you hit limits often, Anthropic's usage limit best practices page explains the options.

Mistakes that cost small businesses time

  • Automating a broken process. Fix the manual version first.
  • Giving broad access on day one. Expand only when a task needs it.
  • Skipping review because it worked last week. Inputs change; outputs drift.
  • Treating drafts as final. Anything that reaches a customer should pass through you.
  • Expecting it to choose your business. Cowork can research demand, but deciding what to sell is your call.

FAQ

Can Claude Cowork run my business on its own?

No. It can do a lot of the repeatable work, but decisions about products, customers and money need a person. Anthropic itself advises staying close and reviewing actions that involve money, messages sent as you or important files.

What is the first task a business should give Cowork?

Choose something you do weekly, with a clear output and low risk, such as a sales summary or competitor round-up. Run it by hand a few times before scheduling it.

Do I need Team or Enterprise to use Cowork for business?

No. Cowork is available on Pro, Max and Ultra as well as Team and Enterprise. Team and Enterprise add admin controls, which help if several people use it.

Is it safe to connect my email and files?

It can be, with care. Connect only what a task needs, keep sensitive financial files out of reach, use trusted connectors, and keep sending and deleting on approval.

Will using Cowork make my business more money?

No tool can promise that. Cowork can save time on repeatable work; whether that turns into more revenue depends on your product, market and decisions.
