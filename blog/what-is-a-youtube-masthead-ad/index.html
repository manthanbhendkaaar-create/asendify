What is a YouTube Masthead ad, and how do you edit a video for it?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A YouTube Masthead is a premium video ad at the top of the YouTube Home feed, bought by reservation through a Google sales representative rather than in a normal self-serve campaign. On desktop, the featured video auto-plays without sound for up to 30 seconds; on mobile and TV it auto-plays for the full video length. So the edit has to grab attention and tell the story silently in its opening seconds, then reward anyone who turns the sound on or clicks.

Key takeaways

  • Google: "Masthead ads are only available on a reservation basis through a Google sales representative."
  • On desktop, the video "auto-plays without the sound for up to 30 seconds at the top of the YouTube Home feed."
  • Masthead can be bought on cost-per-thousand-impressions (CPM) or cost-per-hour terms.
  • Specs: 16:9 at 1920 x 1080, any length (Google recommends over 10 seconds), headline up to 40 characters, description up to 60 and CTA text up to 15.
  • Because it starts muted, the first seconds must work as pictures and text alone.

Editing for a muted autoplay

A Masthead viewer has opened YouTube to watch something else. They'll glance at the top of the page for a second or two. Your job is to make that glance count.

  1. Open on the single most striking image. No fades from black, no logo animation.
  2. Tell the story in pictures. If someone saw only the first 10 silent seconds, would they understand the offer?
  3. Use big, short text. Three to six words on screen at a time, high contrast.
  4. Show the brand early and keep it there, as Google's ABCDs recommend: "Introduce your brand or product from the start and maintain that presence."
  5. Design a payoff for sound. When someone unmutes, they should hear something worth it: a voice, a punchline, the real product sound.
  6. Keep the headline and CTA aligned with the video's promise; the CTA text has only 15 characters.

A structure for the first 30 seconds

SecondsSilent viewer seesWith sound on
0 to 3Hero image of the product or event, brand visibleA strong opening line or sound
3 to 10The key benefit shown, with three-to-six-word textVoice explains the benefit
10 to 20Proof: a demonstration, a real person, a date or a priceDetail and emotion
20 to 30Clear next step on screenSpoken call to action

After 30 seconds on desktop, assume most people have moved on. Anything essential belongs in that window.

MistakeWhy it hurtsBetter

Using a vertical-only editThe Masthead takes 16:9 onlyDeliver a proper landscape cut

Because a Masthead is expensive and usually tied to a single launch, there's little room to test on the day. Test the opening beforehand as a normal in-stream or in-feed ad, see which version holds attention, and use that edit for the Masthead.

Worked example

Example: a streaming service books a Masthead for the launch day of a new series.

ElementChoice
First frameThe lead character's face in close-up, series title on screen
Seconds 3 to 10Three quick action shots, text: "New series. Out today."
Seconds 10 to 20One dramatic scene that makes sense without sound
Seconds 20 to 30"Watch now" on screen, logo held
Headline (40 max)"The new thriller everyone's talking about"
CTA (15 max)"Watch now"

A viewer who never unmutes still learns the title, the genre and that it's out today. A viewer who does unmute hears the series' key line of dialogue. Figures and choices are illustrative.

Is a Masthead right for a small advertiser?

Usually not. It's sold by reservation through Google's sales team and suits big launches where reach on a single day matters. For most small businesses and creators, skippable in-stream, in-feed and Shorts ads in a self-serve campaign give more control over budget and testing. The editing lessons still transfer: a silent, striking first few seconds help in every muted feed.
