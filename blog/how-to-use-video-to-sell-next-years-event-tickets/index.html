How do I use video to sell next year's event tickets?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Plan next year's ticket videos while this year's event is happening. Capture a 60 to 90-second highlight video, short attendee and speaker soundbites, and moments that show what people actually get (the talks, the room, the networking). Then release them in stages: the highlight video to past attendees for early-bird sales, short vertical clips for each audience on social, and speaker announcements as they're confirmed. Keep any urgency, such as early-bird deadlines, true.

Key takeaways

  • Highlight videos sell the feeling. Cvent says "highlight videos aren't about explaining, they're about evoking", and frames them as your event summed up "in 90 seconds".
  • Plan footage for next year now. Cvent suggests "recording speaker soundbites for next year's promos" and describes 60+ testimonial clips captured onsite that became part of promo campaigns.
  • Match length to channel: Cvent advises keeping LinkedIn videos under a minute and says "20–30 seconds of value usually performs best" in email.
  • Urgency must be real. The UK's Competition and Markets Authority says businesses must "have evidence to substantiate the urgency claims" they make, and that timed offers must actually end when stated.
  • Open every video on the best moment, not the logo. Google's ABCDs advise you to "jump in" and "feature people".

Editing tips that keep people watching

  1. Open on the peak. The biggest laugh, the loudest applause or the line people quoted afterwards, in the first two seconds.
  2. Let the music carry the highlight video, but add two or three short spoken lines from speakers or attendees so it isn't just a montage.
  3. Caption and add text. Many people watch muted. Add the event name, dates and one benefit on screen.
  4. Cut vertical versions for Reels, TikTok and Shorts, and a square or horizontal one for LinkedIn, email and the website.
  5. End with one action: "Early-bird tickets: link below", with the real deadline if there is one.

Keep the urgency honest

Ticket marketing leans on urgency: early-bird deadlines, limited seats, price rises. These can be effective and legitimate, but only when they're true. The CMA warns that low stock claims "are likely to be misleading" when they aren't true, and that countdown timers mislead where "there is in fact no limit to the time". If your early-bird ends on 31 March, say so and end it then. If a workshop really has 40 seats, you can say that. For the detail, see whether you can say tickets are limited in an event promo video.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting months to release the highlight video, when the feeling has faded.
  • A five-minute recap nobody finishes; make a short version first.
  • Opening on the logo and a drone shot of the venue.
  • Using attendee faces and quotes without permission.
  • Fake scarcity or deadlines that are extended quietly.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks and pacing for event promo videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Cvent's and Google's free guidance are alternatives.

When should I release the highlight video?

Soon after the event, ideally within a week or two, while attendees still remember it and early-bird interest is highest.

How long should a ticket sales video be?

Short: 60 to 90 seconds for the highlight video, under a minute for most social clips, and 20 to 30 seconds in email.

Can I use attendee clips in ads?

Only with clear permission, and the quotes must be genuine.

Can I say tickets are selling fast?

Only if it's true and you can show the evidence. False urgency can break consumer protection rules.
