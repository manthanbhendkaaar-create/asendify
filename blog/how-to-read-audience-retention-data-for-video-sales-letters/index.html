How do I read the audience retention data for a video sales letter?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Read a video sales letter's retention data at three points: how many viewers survive the hook, how many are still there when the offer or price appears, and how many reach the call to action. Then match every steep drop to the exact script line on screen at that moment. Average engagement alone hides where a VSL loses buyers.

Key takeaways

  • Many VSLs are hosted on video platforms built for marketers. Wistia's engagement graph, for example, shows where viewers stop watching, plus an orange area for "how many times that section of the media was replayed".
  • Wistia defines average engagement simply: if viewers average 10 minutes of a 20-minute video, "average engagement is 50%". The graph is aggregate, so check individual viewers for detail.
  • Wistia heatmaps colour each viewer's session: white means the viewer "did not play that section", and hotter colours mean rewatching.
  • If your VSL is on YouTube, YouTube's key moments report shows the share still watching at 30 seconds and "dips" where viewers abandon or skip.
  • A Tuck School of Business working paper on Facebook video ads found text-heavy, informational segments generally increased abandonment; long text slides in VSLs are worth checking first.

These are common readings used by marketers and editors, not fixed rules. Context decides.

How to read the graph step by step

  1. Export or note key timestamps. Mark the hook end, main proof, offer, price, guarantee or terms, and call to action in your script.
  2. Overlay them on the graph. Look at the slope between each pair of markers.
  3. Find the steepest drops. A gentle downward slope is normal. Sudden cliffs are what to fix.
  4. Check rewatches. Wistia shows replays as an orange area. Rewatches at the price can mean interest, or confusion about what's included.
  5. Look at individual heatmaps. For viewers who bought, see what they watched and skipped. For those who didn't, see where they stopped.
  6. Compare traffic sources. Cold ad traffic and warm email traffic often behave very differently. Segment where your platform allows.
  7. Change one thing at a time. Test a new hook, or a new offer section, not both at once.

Common VSL retention patterns

  • Cliff in the first 10 seconds. Usually a slow start, a generic opening, or a mismatch with the ad that sent people.
  • Steady slide through the middle. Too much backstory, repetition or text slides.
  • Cliff at the price. Value not built well enough, or a mismatch between audience and price.
  • Spike just before the CTA. People going back to rewatch the offer details.
  • Lots of skipping. Viewers using the scrub bar to find the price. Some VSL pages hide the bar for this reason, which can frustrate viewers; test it carefully.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on hooks and pacing for video sales letters?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan. Wistia's and YouTube's analytics help pages are free starting points.

What is a good retention rate for a VSL?

There's no reliable public benchmark; it depends on length, price and traffic. Compare against your own previous versions instead.

Should I hide the scrub bar on my VSL?

Some marketers do to stop people skipping to the price. It can annoy viewers, so test it and watch both retention and sales.

Why do people drop at the price?

Often because value wasn't built clearly enough first, or the traffic isn't the right audience for the price. Look at what comes right before the price.

How long should I wait before changing a VSL?

Until enough people have watched to show a clear pattern. Small samples can mislead.
