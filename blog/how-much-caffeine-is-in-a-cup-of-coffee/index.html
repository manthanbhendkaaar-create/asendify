How much caffeine is in a cup of coffee?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 31 October 2026 · Last updated 31 October 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A typical cup of brewed coffee contains roughly 80 to 100 mg of caffeine, according to the US Food and Drug Administration, but the real amount varies a lot with the beans, the brewing method and, above all, the size of the cup. The NHS puts a mug of instant coffee at about 100 mg and a mug of filter coffee at about 140 mg. For healthy adults, 400 mg a day is the commonly cited upper limit, which is about three to four cups depending on how strong they are.

Caffeine in common coffees

Published figures differ between sources because coffee itself differs. These are the figures from public health bodies, which are a sensible starting point:

DrinkApproximate caffeineSource
Cup of brewed coffee (8 oz / 240 ml)80 to 100 mgFDA
Mug of instant coffeeabout 100 mgNHS
Mug of filter coffeeabout 140 mgNHS
Mug of teaabout 75 mgNHS
Can of colaabout 40 mgNHS
Can of energy drink (250 ml)about 80 mgNHS

Café drinks can be much stronger than these averages, because cups are bigger and many contain more than one shot of espresso. If a café publishes caffeine figures for its drinks, use those.

Why the same "cup" can differ so much

  • Cup size. A "cup" in research is often 8 oz (about 240 ml). A large café coffee can be two or more times that.
  • Number of espresso shots. Lattes, flat whites and cappuccinos get their caffeine from espresso, so a double shot roughly doubles it.
  • Bean type. Robusta beans generally contain more caffeine than Arabica beans.
  • Brewing method and strength. How much coffee you use, how finely it's ground and how long it brews all change the result.
  • Decaf isn't zero. Decaffeinated coffee still contains a small amount of caffeine.

How much is too much?

The FDA says that for healthy adults, 400 mg a day is an amount not generally associated with dangerous, negative effects. The European Food Safety Authority reached a similar view: up to 400 mg a day, and single doses of up to 200 mg, do not raise safety concerns for healthy adults (EFSA).

These limits are lower for some people. The NHS advises a limit of 200 mg a day during pregnancy, and people with certain health conditions or who take some medicines may need less. People also vary in how sensitive they are: some feel jittery or sleep badly on far less. If you have a health condition, are pregnant or take medication, check with a doctor or pharmacist.

A worked example

Say your day looks like this: a large filter coffee at 8 am (call it 140 to 200 mg), a latte with a double shot at 11 am (roughly 120 to 150 mg), and a can of cola at 3 pm (about 40 mg). That could add up to 300 to 390 mg, close to the 400 mg daily limit, and the morning coffee alone may be near the 200 mg single-dose level. The afternoon cola also matters for sleep, because caffeine stays in your system for hours. Swapping the latte for a single shot, or the cola for a caffeine-free drink, keeps you comfortably under both limits.

Getting more from the caffeine you already drink

How much you drink is only half of it; when you drink it matters too. Timing your coffee around your sleep, avoiding big single doses and noticing your own tolerance all change how useful caffeine feels. Key takeaways

  • A standard cup of brewed coffee has roughly 80 to 100 mg of caffeine (FDA); a mug of filter coffee about 140 mg (NHS).
  • Cup size and the number of espresso shots change the total more than anything else.
  • Healthy adults: up to 400 mg a day and 200 mg in one go are the commonly cited limits (FDA, EFSA).
  • Pregnancy: the NHS advises no more than 200 mg a day.
  • Count every source: tea, cola, energy drinks and chocolate add up.

FAQ

How much caffeine is in a shot of espresso?

A single shot of espresso usually contains somewhere around 60 to 80 mg of caffeine, but it varies with the beans and the café. A double shot is roughly twice that.

Does a darker roast have more caffeine?

Not meaningfully. Roast level changes the flavour much more than the caffeine. Cup size, bean type and the amount of coffee used matter more.

How many cups of coffee a day is safe?

For most healthy adults, up to about three to four standard cups a day stays within the 400 mg guideline. Large café coffees count as more than one cup.

How much caffeine is in decaf coffee?

Decaf still contains a small amount of caffeine, usually a few milligrams per cup, so it isn't completely caffeine-free.

How long does the caffeine from a cup of coffee last?

Caffeine stays in your body for several hours, which is why an afternoon coffee can still affect your sleep at night. The calculator above helps you find a cut-off time.
