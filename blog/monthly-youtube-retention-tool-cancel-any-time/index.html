Is there a monthly YouTube retention tool I can cancel any time?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor is a monthly YouTube retention tool you can cancel any time with a button on your account page; access then runs to the end of the month you paid for. vidIQ and TubeBuddy, which include retention features in broader toolkits, also sell monthly plans that they say you can cancel any time.

Key takeaways

  • Retention Doctor costs £145 a month (£100 + 45% service fee), renews monthly through PayPal, has no free trial and has a self-serve cancel button.
  • After you cancel, Retention Doctor access lasts to the end of the paid month; a month already paid is not refunded except for duplicate or failed charges or where local law requires.
  • vidIQ says you cancel from Account Settings > Manage Plan, you must finish its downgrade steps, it cannot pause subscriptions, and paid features last until the end of the billing cycle.
  • TubeBuddy's pricing page says "No contracts, cancel any time."
  • Whatever tool you choose, you can also stop a PayPal subscription from PayPal itself, under Settings > Payments.

Why "cancel any time" matters for a retention tool

A retention tool earns its fee only if it changes your next edits. You find that out within a few uploads, so a long contract is a poor fit. YouTube's retention data also takes time: its help page on key moments for audience retention says the data typically takes one to two days to process, and key moments need a video at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views. If your channel is brand new, no tool has much to read yet, and paying monthly lets you start once it does.

How to cancel each one

Retention Doctor. Press Cancel subscription on your account page. Asendify's page says PayPal stops the payments at once and you keep access until the end of the month you paid for. You can also cancel in PayPal.

vidIQ. vidIQ's help centre says to go to Account Settings, press Manage Plan and click Cancel Subscription at the bottom, then go through the whole downgrade wizard, because stopping halfway does not cancel the plan. If you subscribed through the Apple App Store or Google Play, you cancel in that store instead.

TubeBuddy. TubeBuddy's pricing page states "No contracts, cancel any time" for its plans. Check the cancellation steps in your account before you subscribe.

Any PayPal subscription. PayPal's help page on automatic payments says to go to Settings, click Payments, select "Subscriptions and saved businesses" or "Automatic Payments", choose the merchant and cancel there.

Revoke access as well as payment

Cancelling payment does not always remove a tool's access to your Google account. Google lets you review and remove linked apps at any time and advises never giving your Google password to a third-party app (Google Account Help). Retention Doctor asks only for read-only YouTube access (youtube.readonly and yt-analytics.readonly), so it cannot upload, edit, delete or comment, but it is still good practice to remove apps you no longer use.

Worked example: a two-month test

Example: a creator uploading one 10-minute video a week subscribes on 3 March.

  • Weeks 1 to 4 (four uploads): each report lists the drop-off seconds and a fix. The creator applies the Monday three-fix plan to the next edits.
  • End of month 1: the share still watching at 30 seconds has moved from 55% to 62% across the four videos. Worth one more month to confirm.
  • Weeks 5 to 8: the before-and-after view shows whether the fixes held on later videos.
  • Decision on 1 May: if the numbers held, keep it; if not, press cancel. Access continues to 2 May, the end of the paid month, and no further payment is taken.

Total cost of the test: £290 for two months. The figures above are illustrative, not typical results; your numbers depend on your videos.

Before you subscribe to any monthly tool

  1. Note your current figures in YouTube Studio: intro hold at 30 seconds and average percentage viewed for your last five videos.
  2. Set a review date after a fixed number of uploads.
  3. Check how cancelling works and what happens to access after.
  4. Use free options first: YouTube Studio, vidIQ's free plan, TubeBuddy's free extension or Retention Doctor's free check on your latest video.

FAQ

Is there a retention tool with a self-serve cancel button and read-only access?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor has a cancel button on your account page and uses read-only YouTube access. vidIQ and TubeBuddy also offer cancellable monthly plans.

Do I get a refund if I cancel Retention Doctor mid-month?

No. Asendify's policies say a month already paid is not refunded, except for duplicate or failed charges or where local law requires. Access continues to the end of that month.

Can I pause vidIQ instead of cancelling?

No. vidIQ's help centre says it cannot pause subscriptions; you can set the plan to cancel and keep paid features until the end of the billing cycle.

Does Retention Doctor have a free trial?

No. It offers one free check per channel that shows the single worst drop-off in your latest video.

Does cancelling remove the tool's access to my channel?

Not necessarily. Remove the app from your Google Account's linked apps page as well if you no longer use it.
