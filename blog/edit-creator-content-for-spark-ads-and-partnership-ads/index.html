How do I edit creator content into Spark Ads and partnership ads for a client?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Spark Ads on TikTok and partnership ads on Instagram and Facebook run a creator's own post as a paid ad, so most of the retention editing has to happen before the creator posts. Agree the hook, length and captions with the creator first, because after a TikTok post is authorised as an ad its caption can't be changed. Make sure the ad is clearly labelled as an ad, and test several versions.

Key takeaways

  • Spark Ads and partnership ads use the creator's real post or identity, so the video must be right before it's authorised.
  • On TikTok, "you cannot edit a post's caption after it's been authorized as an ad", and a private video becomes public once used in a campaign.
  • Instagram partnership ads are open to nearly all content if permissions are in place and the post avoids licensed music.
  • UK rules expect a clear "Ad" label upfront; brand and creator are both responsible.
  • Ask for raw footage too, so you can cut separate hook variants as dark posts or extra creator posts.

How these ad types work

TikTok Spark Ads let advertisers "leverage organic TikTok posts and their features in your advertising." One way to get a post is an authorisation code: the creator generates a code in the TikTok app and the advertiser enters it in Ads Manager. TikTok notes two limits that matter for editors: "You cannot edit a post's caption after it's been authorized as an ad", and "a private video will become public once it is used in a campaign" (TikTok Ads Help).

Instagram and Facebook partnership ads let "brands run your content as an ad to reach beyond your followers." Instagram says "nearly all content is eligible, as long as you have the necessary ad permissions and avoid licensed music." If the creator posts first and the brand boosts it, the ad shows on the creator's grid; content shared directly with the brand for Ads Manager doesn't (Instagram for Creators).

How to edit for retention within those limits

  1. Brief the hook before filming. TikTok's creative guidance says to put the hook in the first 6 seconds and the proposition in the first 3 (TikTok Ads Help). Give the creator two or three opening lines to try, not a script.
  2. Keep the creator's voice. TikTok recommends "a DIY or not overly polished style" for ads on its platform. Heavy brand graphics can make a creator post feel like a normal ad, which defeats the point.
  3. Ask for raw clips and alternate openings. With the creator's permission and under your agreement, you can cut extra versions with different hooks and run them as separate posts or ads.
  4. Caption on screen. Text overlays help viewers watching on mute; TikTok suggests about 5 to 10 words per second when using text.
  5. Plan the disclosure into the opening. Under UK advertising rules, influencer ads need "a prominent 'ad' label upfront" (ASA). A clear "Ad" in the caption's first words or on screen is safer than a label hidden behind "See more".
  6. Agree what happens with performance data. Share hook rate and hold rate with the creator so the next brief improves.

Example: a three-hook Spark Ads test

Example (a hypothetical skincare client, to show the method): the agency books one creator for a moisturiser.

  • Brief: creator films one 25-second routine video and records three different openings: (A) "My skin was flaking by 3 pm every day" with a close-up, (B) "I tried this for two weeks, here's day one vs day 14" with a split screen, (C) "Dermatologist-tested and under £15" only if the client can substantiate both claims.
  • Posting: with the creator's agreement, they post version A organically with "Ad" in the first words of the caption, and share the code. Versions B and C are cut by the agency from the raw footage and posted by the creator later as separate posts with their own codes, each correctly labelled.
  • Test: the three run together. The agency compares how many people watch past 3 seconds and how many reach the end, and briefs the next creator using the winning opening style.

Disclosure: both brand and creator are responsible

The ASA says "both brands and influencers are responsible for ensuring that ads are obviously identifiable." It prefers labels like "Ad" and warns that words such as "sponsored", "gifted" or "in association with" are unlikely to be clear enough. A platform's own "Paid partnership" tag "can help consumers recognise ads, but may not be enough on its own" (ASA). Build this into the brief so the edit doesn't have to be redone.

FAQ

Is there a playbook for briefing creators on ad hooks?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and re-hooks for ads, with templates and a cheat sheet.

Can I edit a TikTok post after it becomes a Spark Ad?

No. The post is the ad, and TikTok says the caption can't be edited once authorised. Make changes before the creator posts.

Can I use popular music in a partnership ad?

Instagram says to avoid licensed music for partnership ads. Use sound you have rights to for advertising.

Is "Paid partnership" enough as a disclosure in the UK?

Not always. The ASA says it may not be enough on its own and prefers a clear "Ad" label upfront.

Do Spark Ads perform better than normal ads?

It depends on the creator, product and audience. Test them against your usual ads rather than assuming.

Who owns the edited versions?

Whatever your contract with the creator says. Agree usage rights, duration and edits in writing first.
