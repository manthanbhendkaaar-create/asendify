Why did my YouTube Test & compare result come back inconclusive?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A Test & compare result usually comes back inconclusive for one of three reasons: the versions were too similar for viewers to respond differently, the video did not get enough impressions during the test, or the test was stopped or disrupted. When that happens, YouTube keeps the first title or thumbnail you uploaded, so nothing is lost, and you can run a bolder test.

For another kind of check, the Asendify AI site has a free test you can take without paying.

Key takeaways

  • "Inconclusive" means YouTube could not find a clear watch-time difference between your versions. It does not mean the test failed.
  • Similar designs and low traffic are the two most common causes.
  • Editing the title or thumbnail during a test stops it.
  • Low-resolution images, below 1280 x 720, cause all test thumbnails to be shown at 480p.
  • The fix is usually a bolder test on a video with steady impressions.

What the three results mean

YouTube's help page on A/B testing titles and thumbnails explains that Test & compare can test up to three titles, three thumbnails or three combinations, and that the version with the highest watch time is shown to all viewers. Results come back in one of three labels:

ResultWhat it meansWhat YouTube does
WinnerOne version clearly earned more watch timeApplies the winner for all viewers
Performed sameVersions performed about the sameKeeps your first option
InconclusiveNot enough evidence to call a winnerKeeps your first option

Because the first option you upload is the default when there is no clear winner, put the version you would choose anyway first.

The main causes of an inconclusive result

1. The versions were too similar

YouTube's own tips say that very similar variants make a clear winner less likely. A thumbnail with slightly brighter colours, or a title that swaps "easy" for "simple", gives viewers no real reason to behave differently. Watch time ends up nearly identical, and the test cannot separate them.

2. Not enough impressions

A test needs viewers. YouTube notes that low impression counts make a clear winner less likely, and that how fast a test finishes depends on impressions and how recently the video was published. A video getting a trickle of traffic may run for the full two weeks and still not reach a verdict.

YouTube's page on impressions and click-through rate also notes that not every time a viewer sees a thumbnail counts as an impression, and that impressions on external websites or end screens are not included. So your counted impressions may be lower than you expect.

3. The test was disrupted

Changing the title or thumbnail while a test runs stops it, and you have to start again. A small control group sees only your default version, and YouTube leaves its data out of the results, which slightly reduces the traffic that counts.

4. Image quality issues

If any thumbnail is below 1280 x 720, YouTube downscales all the test thumbnails to 480p. Blurry images can flatten the difference between versions.

How to design a test that reaches a result

Change one big thing per version. Good examples:

  • Thumbnail: your face versus the object or result; text versus no text; wide shot versus tight close-up.
  • Title: result angle ("How I cut my bills by £600") versus method angle ("The 4 bills I renegotiate every year").
  • Combination: a full packaging idea against another, such as "transformation" versus "mistake".

Then pick a video that will get traffic: a fresh upload you expect to be recommended, or an older video that still gets steady views.

A quick way to judge whether two thumbnails are different enough: shrink both to the size they appear on a phone and look at them side by side for two seconds. If you cannot instantly tell them apart, your viewers will not either.

Why watch time, not clicks, decides

It can feel odd that a thumbnail with more clicks did not win. YouTube explains in its post on the recommendation system that it began using watch time because clicks alone did not show whether viewers actually watched and were satisfied. Its discovery guide says average view duration and average percentage viewed are ranking signals. Test & compare follows the same logic. If one version gets more clicks but those viewers leave sooner, the two can end up with similar watch time, and the result is inconclusive.

Example: from inconclusive to a clear winner

Example: Kemi tests two thumbnails on her bread-baking video, which gets about 400 impressions a day.

TestVersion AVersion BResult after 14 days
FirstLoaf on a board, warm lightSame loaf, slightly brighter lightInconclusive
Second (new upload, about 2,500 impressions a day)Loaf on a boardClose-up of the crumb, cut in half, no textWinner: B

The first test failed on both counts: near-identical images and low traffic. For the second, she used a clearly different design on a video getting about six times the impressions. These figures are illustrative; the method is what matters.

What to do with an inconclusive result

  1. Do not read too much into it. It is not proof that packaging does not matter.
  2. Keep the default. YouTube already did.
  3. Note what you tested. Even a non-result tells you that small differences do not move your audience.
  4. Retest with a bigger contrast on a video with more traffic.
  5. Look elsewhere if needed. If viewers click but leave early, the opening may matter more than the thumbnail.

Building a testing habit

One test teaches you little. A simple log teaches you a lot. Keep a note for every test: the video, what you changed, the impressions per day, and the result. After five or six tests, patterns appear, such as "close-ups beat faces" or "method titles beat result titles". Those patterns are worth more than any single winner, because you can use them on every future upload.

FAQ

Does an inconclusive result hurt my video?

No. YouTube keeps your first option as the default, so the video carries on as before.

How long does a Test & compare test run?

YouTube says tests should be completed within two weeks. Videos with plenty of impressions can finish in a few days.

Can I stop a test early?

Changing the title or thumbnail stops the test. If you do that, you will need to start a new test to get a result.

Why did my version with more clicks not win?

YouTube chooses the winner on watch time. If the extra clickers left quickly, total watch time may have ended up similar, or lower.

Can I test thumbnails on Shorts?

No. YouTube's help page says Shorts cannot be tested with Test & compare.

Should I retest the same video?

You can, if it still gets steady impressions. Use clearly different versions, otherwise you are likely to get the same result.
