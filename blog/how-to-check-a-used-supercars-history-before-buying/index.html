How do I check a used supercar's history before buying?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Start with the free GOV.UK checks: the DVLA vehicle enquiry confirms the car's details and tax status, and the MOT history service shows every test result and recorded mileage since 2005. Then pay for a private history check, which Citizens Advice says costs up to about £20 and shows outstanding finance, write-offs and theft. Always see the original V5C log book, and on an expensive car, pay for an independent inspection.

This page is general information, not financial or legal advice.

Key takeaways

  • The DVLA vehicle enquiry is free and shows tax and SORN status, MOT expiry, date first registered, last V5C issue date, engine size, fuel type and emissions.
  • The MOT history service is free and shows pass or fail results, the mileage at each test and, for Great Britain tests, which parts failed or had minor problems; car records go back to 2005.
  • A private history check (up to about £20) shows whether the car is recorded as stolen, has outstanding finance, has been written off or has a mileage discrepancy.
  • Citizens Advice says never buy a car without the original log book, and an independent inspection costs roughly £120 to £250.
  • Paying by credit card can give Section 75 protection on purchases of £100 to £30,000, even if only part was paid on the card.

The DVLA check is your first test of the seller's story. Citizens Advice's guide to buying a used car suggests confirming that the registration number, MOT test number, mileage, make and model match what the seller tells you. Small mismatches may be errors; suspected fake details are a reason to walk away.

The MOT history is especially useful on a supercar. Low annual mileage is normal for these cars, so look for regular tests rather than high miles. A gap can be explained by the car being declared off the road (SORN), but ask about it. Mileage should only go up from test to test, and repeated notes about tyres, brakes or corrosion tell you what the next owner may have to pay for.

The paid history check

Citizens Advice says a private history check costs up to about £20 and shows whether the car:

  • has been reported stolen
  • has outstanding finance
  • has had a serious accident or been written off and returned to the road
  • has a genuine mileage

Outstanding finance matters most. If a car still has finance on it, the finance company may own it. On a car worth tens or hundreds of thousands of pounds, £20 is a small price for that answer. If the check shows a write-off category, our page on whether to buy a Cat S or Cat N supercar explains what the categories mean for value and insurance.

Paperwork and inspection

Citizens Advice's warning is blunt: "Never buy a car without the log book." Ask for the original V5C, not a copy, and check it matches DVLA records. If it does not, report the seller to the police.

For a supercar, also ask for:

  • the full service history, ideally from a main dealer or recognised specialist
  • invoices for major work, such as clutch, brakes, tyres and any warranty repairs
  • any remaining manufacturer or approved-used warranty details

Inspect in daylight and dry weather, test drive for at least 15 minutes on different roads if you are insured to, and pay for an independent report if you are unsure. Citizens Advice puts the cost at roughly £120 to £250. A specialist who knows the model may cost more but can spot model-specific problems.

Paying safely

Cash gives little protection if something goes wrong. A debit card may offer chargeback. A credit card can give Section 75 protection on goods costing £100 to £30,000, even if only part was paid on the card. Finance arranged by a dealer lets you take action against the finance company as well as the dealer. After buying, tax the car yourself straight away; the seller cannot transfer tax already paid. If the car is on finance, see whether you need GAP insurance on a supercar.

Is the GOV.UK MOT history check free?

Yes. It shows each MOT result, the mileage recorded and, for tests in Great Britain, which parts failed or had minor problems.

Does the DVLA check show outstanding finance?

No. You need a private history check, which Citizens Advice says costs up to about £20.

How do I spot clocked mileage?

Compare mileage across MOT tests, service records and invoices. It should only go up. A private history check also flags mileage discrepancies.

Should I get an inspection on a supercar?

It is wise on any expensive car. Citizens Advice puts independent reports at roughly £120 to £250; a model specialist may charge more.

Can I buy a supercar without a V5C?

Citizens Advice says never buy a car without the original log book.
