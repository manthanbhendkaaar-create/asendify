Do sponsor reads hurt YouTube audience retention, and where should they go?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

A sponsor read often shows up as a dip on the retention graph, because some viewers skip ahead or leave when the video stops delivering what they came for. The cost is usually biggest when the read comes before the first payoff. You can measure it exactly by reading the graph at the second the sponsor starts and ends, and you can usually reduce it by moving the read after an early payoff, keeping it short and relevant, while still disclosing it clearly.

Key takeaways

  • On YouTube's retention graph, a dip means viewers skipped or stopped watching that part.
  • Measure the sponsor's cost as the percentage points lost between the read's start and end, compared with the same stretch in videos without a read.
  • Early reads, before viewers get any of what the title promised, tend to cost the most.
  • Moving or shortening a read is fine; hiding it is not. Disclosure rules still apply.
  • YouTube's paid promotion setting adds a disclosure label at the beginning of your video.

What the graph shows during a sponsor read

YouTube's help page on key moments for audience retention defines a dip as a part where viewers are "abandoning or skipping" and recommends reviewing dips to understand why interest dropped. Sponsor reads are one of the easiest dips to spot because you know exactly when they start and stop.

Two kinds of viewer behaviour show up:

  • Skipping: viewers drag the playhead past the read. The graph dips during the read, then partly recovers or flattens afterwards, because those viewers are still watching.
  • Leaving: viewers close the video. The graph drops and does not recover.

The YouTube Analytics API separates these with stoppedWatching (the last segment seen in a playback) and startedWatching (the first segment seen, including when someone resumes or jumps ahead). A spike in started-watching right after the read is a sign viewers are skipping rather than leaving.

Where to place a sponsor read

  • After the first payoff, not before it. Give viewers something the title promised (an answer, a result, a first step), then the read.
  • At a natural break. Between two sections, where a pause makes sense.
  • Short and relevant. A read that connects to the topic feels less like an interruption.
  • Not in the first 30 seconds if you can help it. YouTube's intro figure measures who is still watching at 30 seconds, and its Content tab tips list videos with at least 50% still watching at that point as above typical. A read here competes directly with your hook.

Your sponsor contract may fix the placement, so agree it before you record.

Stay compliant while you optimise

Moving a read to a better spot is fine. Making it hard to notice is not. The US Federal Trade Commission's Disclosures 101 for social media influencers says a disclosure "should be in the video and not just in the description", and that viewers "are more likely to notice disclosures made in both audio and video." In livestreams it should be repeated periodically.

YouTube's own help page on paid product placements, sponsorships and endorsements says you have to tell YouTube by selecting the paid promotion option in your video details, which "adds a disclosure label that appears at the beginning of your video", and that creators are responsible for complying with the law where they are. In the UK, the ASA and CAP set the rules for ads by influencers. This is general information, not legal advice.

Worked example

Example: a 12-minute productivity video opens with a 15-second hook, then goes straight into a 45-second sponsor read at 0:15. Retention falls from 81% at 0:15 to 58% at 1:00, a 23-point loss. In the creator's videos without an early read, the same stretch loses about 9 points. The read costs roughly 14 points, and every later minute of the video only reaches the viewers who stayed.

On the next video, the creator answers the title's main question first (0:00 to 1:40), then places a 35-second read at 1:40. Retention across the read falls from 63% to 58%, a 5-point loss against about 3 points normally. The sponsor is still clearly disclosed in audio and on screen. These figures are illustrative.

Quick checklist for each sponsored upload

  • Note the read's start and end timestamps when you edit.
  • Tick the paid promotion box in YouTube Studio.
  • Disclose in audio and on screen.
  • Two days after upload, record points lost across the read.
  • Compare with the same stretch in an unsponsored video of similar length.

FAQ

Is there a tool that flags the retention cost of a sponsor read on each upload?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor flags the seconds viewers left each upload, including sponsor dips, with a fix. YouTube Studio is the free, manual alternative.

Where is the best place for a sponsor read?

Usually after the first payoff and at a natural break, not in the first 30 seconds. Check your sponsor contract for required placement.

Do viewers skip or leave during sponsor reads?

Both happen. If the graph recovers after the read, many viewers skipped; if it keeps falling, they left.

Can I put the disclosure only in the description?

The FTC says a disclosure should be in the video, not just the description. YouTube also asks you to tick the paid promotion box, which adds a label.

How long should a sponsor read be?

There is no rule. Shorter, relevant reads usually cost fewer viewers. Measure the points lost on your own graph.
