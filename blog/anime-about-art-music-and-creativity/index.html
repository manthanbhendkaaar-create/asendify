Which anime are about art, music and finding your creative passion?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Three anime stand out for stories about art, music and finding a creative passion: Blue Period, where a bored high-school student discovers painting and commits to art school; Your Lie in April, about a piano prodigy who stopped playing and is drawn back to music; and Kids on the Slope, where a lonely classical pianist finds friendship and jazz. Each treats creativity as skill built through practice. ASCEND, a free original anime by Asendify, teaches one practical technique per episode.

This page is about anime and general life lessons. It is not mental health advice; if low mood is affecting your daily life, talk to a GP or call Samaritans free on 116 123.

Key takeaways

  • MyAnimeList lists Blue Period as 12 episodes from October to December 2021; Yatora Yaguchi has excellent grades but feels unfulfilled until he finds painting.
  • Your Lie in April (Shigatsu wa Kimi no Uso, 22 episodes, 2014 to 2015) follows Kousei Arima, a piano prodigy called the "Human Metronome" for his precise playing under a strict mother.
  • Kids on the Slope (Sakamichi no Apollon, 12 episodes, 2012) follows Kaoru Nishimi, an introverted classical pianist, whose life changes when he meets Sentarou Kawabuchi and his love of jazz.
  • All three show creative growth as practice, mistakes and support from others, not instant genius.
  • They suit anyone who feels stuck, or who wonders whether it is too late to start something creative.

Dealing with comparison

All three anime show characters comparing themselves with someone more gifted: a classmate who seems to paint effortlessly, a rival pianist, a friend who plays by instinct. Comparison can push you, but it can also make you quit. A few habits keep it useful.

Instead ofTry
Comparing your start with someone's finished workComparing your work with your own from a month ago
Hiding work until it is "good"Sharing early with one supportive person
Giving up after a bad sessionTreating bad sessions as part of practice
Copying only to judge yourselfCopying to learn a specific technique

Keep your old sketches or recordings. Looking back after three months is usually the best answer to "am I getting any better?"

Why these stories resonate

Many people stop creative hobbies in their teens because they think they are "not talented". These anime argue the opposite: talent is mostly practice made visible, and the people who get good are often the ones who kept going while feeling behind. They also show the emotional side honestly: pressure, comparison and grief can stop people creating, and support from others can bring them back.

Watching guide

If you have only time for one, choose by mood. Blue Period is the most practical about how artists actually train; Your Lie in April is the most emotional, so have tissues ready; Kids on the Slope is the shortest route to a warm story about friendship and music. All three are short enough to finish in a few weekends.

FAQ

Is there a free anime that teaches one practical habit per episode?

Yes. ASCEND, a free original anime by Asendify, teaches one practical technique per episode inside a 90-day story. It is free at anime.asendify.co and on YouTube.

Is Blue Period good for aspiring artists?

Many viewers find it motivating because it shows real study habits, such as sketching practice and preparing for art school entrance exams.

Is Your Lie in April sad?

Yes, it deals with grief and illness. It is also about rediscovering joy in music.

Do I need to like jazz to enjoy Kids on the Slope?

No. The friendships carry the story, though the music is a big part of its appeal.

Is it too late to start an art or music hobby as an adult?

No. Like the characters, you improve through regular practice, whatever age you start.
