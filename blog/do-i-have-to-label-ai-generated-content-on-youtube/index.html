Do I have to label AI-generated content on YouTube?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Yes, if it looks real. YouTube requires creators to disclose content that is "AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic", such as a real person appearing to say something they did not, altered footage of real places, or AI-generated music. You do not need to disclose clearly unrealistic animation or minor edits like colour correction, captions or beauty filters. You set it during upload in YouTube Studio.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help lists AI music, AI footage of a real place, a realistic AI match between real players and a realistic arrest of a real person as needing disclosure.
  • Unrealistic content, such as a unicorn in a fantasy world, and "primarily aesthetic" edits do not need a label.
  • You disclose by setting "AI use" to Yes under Attributes when uploading in YouTube Studio.
  • Disclosing does not limit your audience or monetisation, but repeatedly failing to disclose can lead to labels being added, removal or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program.
  • Since May 2026, YouTube can add a label automatically when its systems detect significant photorealistic AI use, and shows it more prominently, the YouTube Blog announced.

When the tool launched on 18 March 2024, YouTube's examples included replacing one person's face with another's and cloning a person's voice for narration. Its aim was to "strengthen transparency with viewers and build trust".

How to disclose in YouTube Studio

  1. Upload your video in YouTube Studio as usual.
  2. In the details step, find the Attributes section.
  3. Set "AI use" to Yes if the video contains realistic altered or synthetic content.
  4. Publish. YouTube adds the label in the appropriate place.

If you have already published, you can edit the video's details and change the setting.

Automatic labels and corrections

YouTube Help says it may label content automatically when it was made with YouTube's own generative AI tools, carries C2PA metadata, or is detected by its systems as AI-generated or altered. Creators are told in Studio and can usually correct a mistake by selecting No. Some labels cannot be changed: those for YouTube's own AI tools, C2PA content indicating full generative AI, and content that has been manually reviewed. YouTube's May 2026 post adds that "creators remain in control" of the status for most videos.

What happens if you do not disclose

  • Labels added for you. YouTube may apply the label itself.
  • Removal. Content may be taken down.
  • YPP suspension. Repeated failures can lead to suspension from the YouTube Partner Program.

Separately, any content that breaks the Community Guidelines, such as harmful deepfakes, can be removed whether it is labelled or not. A label is not a licence to mislead.

A quick test before you upload

Ask three questions about each AI element in the video:

  1. Would a reasonable viewer think it is real? If yes, it probably needs a label.
  2. Does it show a real person, place or event? Realistic depictions of real people, places and events are the core of the rule.
  3. Is it more than a cosmetic change? Colour, captions and filters are fine; changing what happened is not.

If you answer yes to the first two, set "AI use" to Yes. When in doubt, disclosing costs nothing, because YouTube says the label alone does not reduce reach or monetisation.

What editors and agencies should do

  • Ask about AI in every brief. Find out whether voice clones, face swaps or generated footage are in the edit.
  • Keep a production log. Note which shots or audio were generated and with which tool.
  • Agree who sets the attribute. If the editor uploads, they must know the rule; if the creator uploads, the editor should tell them.
  • Avoid real people without consent. Generating a real person's likeness raises legal and platform risks beyond labelling.
  • Check music rights too. AI music still needs a label, and other music still needs a licence.

Worked example

Example: Maya runs a history channel and works with a freelance editor. Her new video uses three AI elements.

Asendify is a YouTube growth agency built around retention editing, but it is currently full and invite-only. Freelance editors are the fair alternative for now.

Does labelling AI content reduce views or monetisation?

YouTube says disclosure alone does not limit audience or affect monetisation eligibility.

Do I need to label AI thumbnails or scripts?

YouTube's rule focuses on realistic altered or synthetic content in the video. Check YouTube Help for the latest wording if unsure.

What if YouTube labels my video wrongly?

You can usually correct it in Studio by selecting No, except for labels from YouTube's own AI tools, C2PA metadata or manual review.

Can I be removed from YPP for not disclosing?

Repeatedly failing to disclose can lead to suspension from the YouTube Partner Program, according to YouTube Help.
