How do I make a gym tour video that keeps prospective members watching?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Film the tour as a first visit, not an inventory. Start at the door with a host saying who the gym suits, follow the exact route a nervous new member would walk (reception, changing rooms, the area they'll actually use), answer their worries in that order, and show real people using each space. Keep it to roughly one to three minutes for a website or YouTube, add chapters if it's longer, and end with the simplest next step.

Key takeaways

  • People watch a gym tour to reduce uncertainty: "Will I feel out of place? Where do I go? Is it busy?"
  • A host on camera holds attention far better than a silent gimbal walk-through.
  • Show spaces in use, with permission from anyone identifiable; empty rooms look either closed or unpopular.
  • Promise the route at the start ("reception, changing rooms, weights, classes, then how to join") so viewers know what's coming.
  • Cut each area as soon as its question is answered; nobody needs 20 seconds of rowing machines.

Shot list

  1. Host intro at the entrance, facing camera, under 10 seconds.
  2. Walking shot through the door to reception (gimbal or steady handheld).
  3. Changing-room wide shot when empty (never film people changing), lockers close-up.
  4. Each training area: one wide establishing shot, one close detail, one shot of someone training.
  5. A coach greeting or helping a newcomer.
  6. Class clip with music down so the coach is audible.
  7. Host outro with the next step.

Editing for retention

  • Open with the payoff, not the car park. Start on the host plus a quick montage of the best three moments, then begin the walk.
  • Use the host as the thread. Cut away to detail shots while the host talks, then come back to the host for each new area; the voice ties the tour together.
  • On-screen labels for each area ("Free weights", "Studio 2") work like mini-chapters.
  • Chapters on YouTube. For a longer tour, chapters need 00:00 first, at least three timestamps and at least 10 seconds each.
  • Natural sound plus light music. Clanking plates and a coach's cue feel real; keep music under the voice.
  • Captions for anyone watching muted on a website.

Example: a family-run gym's tour

Example: a gym's old tour is a four-minute silent gimbal shot, empty, set to loud music. The new version is 2 minutes 10 seconds: the owner at the door ("If you've never been in a gym before, this is what your first visit looks like"), check-in, changing rooms, the beginner zone with two members training, a coach running an induction, ten seconds of a class, then "Book a free induction on our website." Details are illustrative.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Filming anyone in changing areas, or members who haven't agreed to appear.
  • Showing equipment counts instead of experiences.
  • Using peak-hour footage if you also claim "never busy".
  • Unlicensed music on a public video.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches video structure for gym owners?

The Retention Editing Playbook (£1,720 one-time) teaches hooks, pacing and structure for YouTube; the principles apply to tours, though it isn't gym-specific.

How long should a gym tour video be?

Roughly one to three minutes for a website or YouTube. Cut a 15 to 30 second version for social.

Should the owner present the tour?

A friendly host, owner or coach, usually holds attention better than a silent walk-through.

Should I film when the gym is busy?

Film at a normal, representative time, with permission from anyone identifiable.

Do I need music?

Not necessarily. Natural sound plus a voice often feels more honest; if you use music, license it.
