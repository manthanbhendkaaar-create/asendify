Should founders appear in their own video ads?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Often, yes. A founder speaking directly to camera gives the ad a real person, a clear reason the product exists, and an authentic, phone-friendly style that fits Reels, Shorts and TikTok, and it costs little to film. It works best when the founder is comfortable on camera, speaks to one customer problem, and shows the product rather than just talking about it. It works less well when the founder's story becomes the subject instead of the customer's problem, or when the ad starts with a slow introduction.

Key takeaways

  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "feature people to help you tell a better product story and relate to your consumer".
  • Google's Shorts ads guide recommends "authentic creator assets"; founder-shot content often has that look.
  • Facebook's tips (2021) say to make the product the focus and keep one message per ad.
  • Don't open with "Hi, I'm the founder of..."; open with the customer's problem or the result.
  • Founders' claims are still ads: under the UK CAP Code, objective claims need evidence.

What the guidance says

Google's ABCDs list "humanise the story" under Connection and "show up early and throughout" under Branding. A founder can do both: they are the human, and they hold or use the product from the first frame. Google's Shorts ads guide says to "leverage authentic creator assets when you can", and vertical, phone-shot founder clips usually fit.

Search Engine Journal's summary of Facebook's tips (2021) adds two cautions: "showcase product or service" (make the product the focus, because featuring something else can confuse viewers) and "single focus". A founder ad that's really about the founder breaks both.

Keeping it honest

The founder's enthusiasm doesn't exempt the ad from the rules. The CAP Code's misleading advertising section says marketers must hold evidence for objective claims (rule 3.7) and must not exaggerate a product's capability or performance (rule 3.11). "This fixed my wrist pain" is a personal experience; "this cures RSI" is a health claim needing evidence. Keep founder claims to what you can prove.

Making the founder comfortable on camera

The biggest risk with founder ads isn't the idea; it's a stiff delivery that makes viewers scroll. A few production habits help a lot.

Talk to one person. Ask the founder to imagine explaining the product to one customer across a table. Speech aimed at "everyone" sounds like a speech; speech aimed at one person sounds like a conversation.

Use bullet prompts, not a script. Write three or four bullet points (problem, what I built, what it does, what to do next) and let the founder say them in their own words. Do several takes and keep the most natural.

Film in short pieces. Record the hook separately, several times, in different ways. Then record each point as its own short clip. Short takes are easier to deliver well and easier to edit.

Keep it simple technically. A phone on a tripod at eye level, a window in front of the founder, and a clip-on mic for clear sound are enough for feed placements.

Common problemFix
Founder reads stifflyBullet prompts, talk to one person
Long introductionCut it; put the name on screen as text
Talking head with nothing to seeAdd the product in hand and cutaways of it in use
Too many pointsOne problem, one product, one proof
Rambling endingOne clear call to action

Show, don't only tell. Every claim the founder makes should, where possible, be backed by something on screen: the product working, a before-and-after, a real figure you can support. Google's ABCDs recommend focused messaging and humanising the story; showing the founder using the product does both.

Test founder against non-founder. Founder ads don't always win. Run a founder version and a product-only or customer version to the same audience and compare hook rate and cost per result. You'll learn whether your audience responds to the person behind the brand. FAQ

Is there a book that teaches founders to make talking-to-camera videos?

The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for talk-to-camera video at £1,000 one-time. Google's ABCDs are a free alternative.

Should a founder ad start with an introduction?

No. Start with the customer's problem or the result; put the name in on-screen text.

Do founder ads need to look professional?

Not necessarily. Clear sound, good light and a steady phone are often enough for feed placements.

Can a founder make health or income claims in an ad?

Only with evidence. The CAP Code requires substantiation for objective claims.
