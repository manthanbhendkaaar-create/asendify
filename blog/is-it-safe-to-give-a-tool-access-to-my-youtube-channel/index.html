Is it safe to give a third-party tool access to my YouTube channel?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

It can be safe if the tool asks only for read-only permissions and comes from a developer that follows YouTube's API rules. Check the Google permission screen before you approve: "View YouTube Analytics reports" and "View your YouTube account" let a tool read your numbers, while "Manage your YouTube account" lets it change things. You can remove access at any time from your Google Account.

Key takeaways

  • The permission screen tells you exactly what a tool can do. Read-only scopes cannot upload, edit or delete videos.
  • YouTube's developer policies require tools that use its API to have a privacy policy, link to Google's security settings page, and delete your data after you revoke access.
  • Stored data must be refreshed or deleted after 30 days under those policies.
  • People you add through YouTube Studio channel permissions are different from apps: they get a role, such as Viewer or Editor, not an API scope.
  • You can review and remove any connected app on the linked apps page of your Google Account (myaccount.google.com/linkedapps).

Two kinds of access: apps and people

Creators often mix up two different things.

  1. Apps (third-party tools). You click "Sign in with Google" and approve a list of permissions, called scopes. The app then reads or changes data through the YouTube APIs.
  2. People (editors, managers, agencies). You invite them by email in YouTube Studio under Settings > Permissions and give them a role.

YouTube's help page on channel permissions lists the roles. A Viewer can see all channel details and analytics but cannot edit anything; Viewer (limited) is the same without revenue data. An Editor can upload and publish but cannot delete published content or manage permissions. A Manager can do almost everything except delete the channel. The same page notes that invited users cannot use YouTube APIs on your behalf, and that an invite expires after 30 days if it is not accepted.

So if an editor needs to see retention graphs, give them Viewer (limited). If a tool needs to read your analytics, the question is which scopes it asks for.

What the permission screen means

Google publishes the scopes for its analytics APIs in the YouTube Analytics and Reporting API authorisation guide. These are the ones you will see most often:

Scope (what Google shows you)What the tool can doRisk level
View YouTube Analytics reports for your YouTube contentRead views, watch time, retention and traffic numbersLow: read only
View monetary and non-monetary YouTube Analytics reportsAs above, plus revenue figuresLow to medium: read only, but includes earnings
View your YouTube accountRead your channel and video listLow: read only
Manage your YouTube accountUpload, edit and delete videos, change channel settingsHigh: full control of content
View and manage your assets and associated content on YouTubeContent-owner level managementHigh: meant for networks and rights holders

A retention or analytics tool only needs the first and third rows. If an analytics tool asks for "Manage your YouTube account", ask why before you approve. Some tools have a genuine reason (for example, schedulers that upload for you), but a tool that only reports numbers does not.

What the rules say about your data

Every app that uses the YouTube APIs agrees to the YouTube API Services Developer Policies. Several of its rules protect you directly:

  • Privacy policy first. Each app must make users agree to a privacy policy that says it uses YouTube API Services, links the Google Privacy Policy, and explains that you can revoke access through Google's security settings page.
  • Delete after revocation. When you revoke access through Google's security settings page, the developer must delete all API data it holds about you within 30 calendar days. If you revoke inside the app itself, the deadline is 7 days.
  • No stale copies. After 30 calendar days, an app must delete or refresh stored data, and check every 30 days that it is still authorised.
  • Deletion on request. If you ask an app to delete your data, it must do so within 7 calendar days.
  • No reselling the API. Apps may not sell or redistribute YouTube API Services without YouTube's written approval.

These are rules, not guarantees. A careless developer can still break them, which is why the permission screen and the developer's reputation still matter.

How to check and remove access

Google's help page on managing connections between your Google Account and third parties explains the steps. Open your Google Account's linked apps page, select the app, and choose "See details" to see what it can reach. To cut it off, select "Remove access" and confirm. Google says that once you remove access, "the app can't access your Google Account."

Make this a habit: review connected apps every few months and remove anything you no longer use.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Scopes that do not match the job. A reporting tool asking to manage your account.
  • No privacy policy, or one that never mentions YouTube API Services. The developer policies require it.
  • Asking for your password. Legitimate tools use Google sign-in; they never need your password.
  • Pressure to approve quickly. Be wary of any unexpected email with a "connect your channel" link. Open tools from their own website instead.

FAQ

Is there a retention tool that only needs read-only access to my channel?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor uses read-only Google sign-in for one channel and shows where viewers left each video, with a fix. YouTube Studio's own retention report is the free alternative.

Can a read-only tool delete my videos?

No. Scopes such as "View YouTube Analytics reports" and "View your YouTube account" only allow reading. Deleting or editing needs "Manage your YouTube account".

Should I give my editor channel permissions or an app login?

Give people channel permissions in YouTube Studio. Viewer (limited) lets someone see analytics without revenue or editing rights.

How do I remove a tool's access to my YouTube channel?

Open the linked apps page in your Google Account, select the app, choose "Remove access" and confirm.

What happens to my data after I disconnect a tool?

Under YouTube's developer policies, the developer must delete your API data within 30 days of revocation through Google's security page, or within 7 days if you revoke inside the app.
