Do end screens hurt YouTube audience retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

End screens themselves rarely hurt audience retention much, because YouTube only shows them in the last 5 to 20 seconds of a video. What costs retention is the run-up: a long goodbye, a recap or a "that's it for today" that signals the video is over, so viewers leave before the end screen appears. Keep the ending short and place the end screen over content that is still worth watching.

Key takeaways

  • End screens appear in the last 5 to 20 seconds of a video and need a video of at least 25 seconds.
  • Up to four elements can appear on a standard 16:9 video: videos, playlists, subscribe, channel, links (Partner Program) and merchandise.
  • Retention almost always falls at the end; what matters is how early and how steeply it falls.
  • Verbal sign-offs ("thanks for watching") often trigger the drop before the end screen even appears.
  • Measure end screens by their click rate in the Engagement tab, not only by the retention curve.

What YouTube says about end screens

YouTube's help page on adding end screens sets the rules: end screens run in the last 5 to 20 seconds, the video must be at least 25 seconds long, and you can add up to four elements on a 16:9 video. Element types include a video or playlist, a subscribe button, a channel, an external link (Partner Program members only) and merchandise. They are not available on videos set as made for kids, and viewers can hide them. YouTube also warns that "your end screen may not always show, or may show differently than designed."

The same page lists the end screen reports in the Engagement tab: end screen element click rate for each video, plus top element types and top videos by end screen at channel level.

Why the end of the graph falls

Every retention curve ends lower than it starts. YouTube's key moments page calls a gradual decline normal across YouTube, and a dip a part viewers skipped or stopped watching. Near the end, viewers leave once they feel they have what they came for. The question is whether your ending makes them leave earlier than they need to.

The YouTube Analytics API has a metric that captures this directly: stoppedWatching, "the number of times a particular segment of the video was the last segment seen during a video playback." A spike in stopped-watching right after you say "that's all for today" is the clearest sign of a sign-off problem.

End screen vs outro: what actually costs viewers

Ending styleEffect on retention near the endEffect on end screen clicks
Long verbal goodbye, then end screenSteep drop before end screen appearsFewer viewers left to click
Recap of what was just saidGradual drop; viewers feel doneModerate
End screen over a final useful point or teaserSmaller drop until the last secondsMore viewers see it
Video ends abruptly with no end screenShort tail, no drop from goodbyeNo clicks possible
20-second black screen behind elementsViewers leave quicklyClick rate depends on elements

These are common patterns. Your own graph is the test.

How to check your own endings

  1. Open the retention graph two days after upload, when data has processed.
  2. Hover over the last minute. Note where the line starts to fall faster than the grey typical retention band.
  3. Play from 10 seconds before that point. Is it the moment you say goodbye, start a recap, or show the sponsor link again?
  4. Check the end screen click rate in the Engagement tab. YouTube's Content tab tips also suggest comparing only within the same format.
  5. Test one change. For example, drop the verbal sign-off and put the end screen over your final tip. Compare the last-minute curve and the click rate on the next two uploads.

Worked example

Example: a 9-minute cooking video. At 8:00, 41% of viewers are still watching. At 8:05 the creator says "That's it for today, thanks for watching, don't forget to subscribe", followed by 25 seconds of chat and then a 20-second end screen over a still image. By 8:40, when the end screen appears, only 22% remain. End screen click rate: 2.1%.

On the next video, the creator drops the sign-off and puts the end screen over the final plating shot with the line "if you want the sauce from this, it's in this video". At the same point, 35% are still there when the end screen appears, and the click rate rises to 3.4%. These figures are illustrative; your numbers will differ.

Practical rules

  • Don't announce the end. Phrases that signal "you're done" give viewers permission to leave.
  • Keep the outro under 20 seconds. That matches the end screen window.
  • Point to one next video. A specific, relevant recommendation gives viewers a reason to stay on your channel.
  • Leave space in the frame. YouTube advises leaving enough space and time at the end for elements.
  • Don't judge by average view duration alone. End screens that send viewers to your next video can be worth a small dip.

FAQ

Is there a tool that flags where viewers leave near the end of each video?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor shows the second viewers left each upload, with a fix, using read-only access. YouTube Studio's retention graph is the free alternative.

How long should a YouTube end screen be?

YouTube allows 5 to 20 seconds. Use enough time for viewers to see and click, but avoid long empty endings.

Do end screens work on Shorts?

YouTube's end screen help page lists where they are unavailable, such as made-for-kids videos and 360 videos, but does not cover Shorts. Check YouTube Studio for your video.

Should I say "thanks for watching"?

Many creators find a verbal sign-off causes an early drop. Test removing it and compare the last minute of the graph.

What is a good end screen click rate?

YouTube does not publish a benchmark. Compare your own videos and test one change at a time.
