How do I add re-hooks inside Meta video ads to stop people dropping off?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A re-hook is a short line or visual that renews the viewer's reason to keep watching, just as attention starts to fade. In a 15-60 second Meta ad, the usual places are right after the opening hook pays off (often around 3-8 seconds) and just before the offer or price, where many people leave. Find the exact spots in the ad's retention graph, then add a new question, a surprising proof point or a "here's the part most people miss" line there.

Key takeaways

  • Meta's video insights reference describes a retention graph that splits a video into "40 equal intervals" and shows plays at each point "as a percentage of all views". Use it to find where to re-hook.
  • Loewenstein's information-gap account says specific missing information creates "an unreasonably strong desire to fill these gaps" (Golman and Loewenstein). A re-hook opens a new, specific gap when the first one closes.
  • Richard Mayer's signalling principle (University of New Hampshire summary) says learning improves when "cues are added that highlight the organization of the essential material". "Step 2", "the catch" and "the result" are cues of that kind.
  • Google researchers found that ranking by clicks "often promotes deceptive videos that the user does not complete" (Covington, Adams and Sargin, 2016). Re-hooks must be honest, or they lose people at the payoff.
  • One or two re-hooks are enough in most ads. More can feel like stalling.

Hook vs re-hook vs pattern interrupt

TechniqueWhat it isWhenExample
HookThe reason to start watchingFirst 1-3 seconds"Your skincare is drying your skin out."
Re-hookA renewed reason to keep watchingWhen the first reason is used up"But the real problem is what you do after washing."
Pattern interruptA change in picture or sound that resets attentionAny time attention driftsCut to a close-up; sound drop
Open loopA question left open for laterEarly, paid off later"I'll show you the cheap fix at the end."

A re-hook is usually verbal or textual; a pattern interrupt is usually visual or audio. The strongest moments combine both.

These are starting points from editing practice, not Meta benchmarks. Place them where your own graph dips.

Five re-hook lines that work in ads

  1. The deeper problem: "But that's not why it keeps happening."
  2. The surprise proof: "This one cost less than a coffee." (only if true)
  3. The objection: "I know what you're thinking: does it work on curly hair?"
  4. The stakes: "Get this wrong and you'll be buying a new one in a year."
  5. The reveal promise: "Watch what happens when we turn it on." Then show it within seconds.

Example: adding two re-hooks to a 30-second ad

Example: an agency runs a 30-second ad for a client's standing desk. The retention graph shows a steady slide after the opening, then a sharp drop at 18 seconds when the price appears.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on re-hooks and open loops for Meta video ads?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops, re-hooks and pacing, with templates and a 30-day plan. Meta's own documentation explains the retention graph for free.

What is a re-hook in a video ad?

A short line or visual that gives viewers a fresh reason to keep watching once the opening hook has done its job.

How many re-hooks should a 30-second ad have?

Usually one or two. Place them where your retention graph dips, not on a fixed timer.

Do re-hooks work in Reels and Stories placements?

Yes, but keep them very short. Vertical placements move fast, so a re-hook must land in a second or two.

How do I know if a re-hook worked?

Compare the retention graph and ThruPlays of the version with the re-hook against the version without it, using the same audience and budget.
