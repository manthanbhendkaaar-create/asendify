What are Meta Reels trending ads, and how should creatives be edited for them?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 7 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Meta's Reels trending ads are a placement product that, in Meta's early description, delivers ads "immediately after the most popular, engaging creator-created Reels." Your ad appears right after a high-energy creator video, so it has to hold attention against that standard. Edit it like a Reel, not a TV spot: vertical, a hook in the first second, captions, a message that works with the sound on or off, and no slow brand intro. The product details may have changed since the reports cited here, so check Meta's current documentation before you plan a campaign.

Key takeaways

  • MediaPost reported that Meta's Reels Trending Ads were in testing and would "deliver ads immediately after the most popular, engaging creator-created Reels," with placement beside top Reels overall or topical lineups such as beauty or sports (MediaPost). That article gave no creative requirements.
  • Meta's developer guidance says "build for vertical: Reels is a full screen environment," "over 75% of Reels views on Instagram are sound on," and to put key messages "within the Reels safe zone" (Meta for Developers).
  • Because sound is on for most views, per Meta, audio matters, but captions still protect the message for the minority watching without it.
  • Your own result data is the test. Meta's reporting and your own conversion data tell you more than any guideline.

What trending ads mean for an editor

The placement changes the competition for attention. Your ad does not follow a random video; it follows one that is already holding viewers. That has three consequences for the edit:

  1. The first second matters more. A viewer who has just laughed or learned something will swipe at a flat opening.
  2. Native style fits better. A polished broadcast look can feel like a jolt after a creator video.
  3. The message has to land fast. There is no time for a slow logo reveal.

None of this is proven by Meta's published material; it is editing judgement based on how the placement is described. Treat it as a set of hypotheses to test.

Step by step: editing for a Reels trending placement

  1. Start with the creative brief. One audience, one problem, one promise. If you cannot say it in a sentence, the edit will wander.
  2. Write the hook as a first line. Show or say the most interesting thing in the first second: the problem, a result you can honestly show, or a question the viewer cares about.
  3. Shoot or crop vertical. Meta says "build for vertical." Do not letterbox a horizontal ad.
  4. Respect the safe zone. Keep text and the product away from the top and bottom areas covered by the interface. Meta's guidance tells advertisers to "put your key messages within the Reels safe zone."
  5. Add captions. Even if most views are with sound, captions help viewers who are in public or have sound off. Review auto-captions manually.
  6. Show the product early. Do not hold it for the end.
  7. Cut the dead frames. Remove the pause before speech, the slow zoom and the logo sting.
  8. Use sound with intent. Meta says most Reels views are sound on, so use a clear voice, music that supports the message and sound effects only where they help.
  9. End with one clear action. One call to action, not three.
  10. Export multiple versions. Different hooks, same body. Test which first second holds attention.

Compliance and honesty

Ads should follow Meta's advertising policies and your local advertising rules. Do not exaggerate, do not imply results you cannot show, and make sure testimonials are real and permitted. This page is editing guidance, not legal advice.

How to judge the edit

  1. Compare hook variants with the same body and the same targeting.
  2. Look at the metrics Meta reports for video views and engagement, and your own conversion data. Metric names change, so use the ones in your Ads Manager.
  3. Change one variable per test.
  4. Keep a log of what you changed and what happened.
  5. Do not assume a trend placement result will match a standard Reels placement; compare placements separately.

Common mistakes

  • Reusing a horizontal TV ad.
  • A logo sting before the hook.
  • Text hidden under the interface.
  • No captions.
  • Judging the placement on a single ad.

Building a testing plan for trending placements

Because trending placements are described as sitting after popular creator Reels, plan the test before you spend. Start with one product and one message. Make three hooks: a problem hook, a result hook and a demonstration hook. Keep the middle and end identical so the hook is the only difference. Run them with the same budget and audience, give each enough time to collect data, and compare the figures your Ads Manager reports for video views and engagement alongside your conversion data.

Review the creative weekly. If a hook wins, build the next round of variants on it: different voices, different first frames, different captions. If none wins, check the basics first: is the first second clear, is the text inside the safe zone, is the audio level right, and does the product appear early?

Working with creators and editors

Brief an editor with the hook, the one message and the call to action, and ask for two export versions. If you use a creator, ask for a few alternative opening lines recorded in the same take, so the editor can swap hooks without a reshoot. Keep a shared log of every version, the variable changed and the result, so a future editor can see what you learned.
