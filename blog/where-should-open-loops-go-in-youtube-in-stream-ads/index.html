Where should open loops go in a YouTube in-stream ad?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open the loop inside the first 5 seconds, before the skip button appears, and close it after the skip point but well before the ad ends. On skippable in-stream ads viewers can skip after 5 seconds, so the question has to be planted while everyone is still watching. Then pay it off with the product, so the answer and the brand arrive together.

Key takeaways

  • An open loop is a question, promise or unfinished moment the viewer wants resolved. In an ad, its job is to carry people past the skip button.
  • Plant it in seconds 0 to 5; Google says skippable ads can be skipped "after 5 seconds".
  • With CPV bidding you are charged at 30 seconds or the end of a shorter ad, so resolve the main loop by about 15 to 25 seconds and leave time for the call to action.
  • Never let the loop hide the brand. Google's ABCDs advise you to "show up early and throughout".
  • Close every loop you open. A teased answer that never arrives annoys people and can mislead.

What an open loop is in an ad

In a YouTube video, an open loop might be "I'll show you the mistake that cost me 40% of my views later on". In a 30-second ad there is no "later on" to spare. The loop has to be small, specific and answered quickly. Good ad loops are things like:

  • A visual question: a tap running into an empty bath with no plug, a receipt turning into numbers.
  • A number with no explanation yet: "This kitchen cost £0 in labour." (Only if it is true and you can prove it.)
  • A before without the after: the messy desk, the slow spreadsheet, the failed bake.
  • A direct question: "Why do most home gyms end up as clothes racks?"
  • A started action: the presenter begins a demo and the reveal comes after the skip point.

Where each loop goes in a 30-second ad

TimeJobLoop status
0 to 2 sShow the problem or striking image, brand visibleMain loop opened
2 to 5 sMake the question explicit, say who it's forMain loop sharpened, skip button appears
5 to 12 sStart the answer: product in useMain loop half-closed, small second loop opened ("and the bit nobody expects...")
12 to 20 sFull payoff and proofBoth loops closed
20 to 30 sOffer and one call to actionNo open loops left

The pattern is: one big loop to beat the skip button, one small loop to carry the middle, and a clean finish. More than two loops in 30 seconds tends to confuse.

For long skippable ads, check the "video played to" figures. Google defines them as the percentage of people who watched 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% of the video. A steep fall just after a loop closes tells you the next one is opened too late.

Mistakes that break ad loops

  1. The loop is only in the voiceover. Many people watch on small screens and some with sound low. Put the question on screen too.
  2. The brand appears only at the payoff. If someone skips at second 6, they should still have seen who you are.
  3. The tease is bigger than the answer. "You won't believe this" followed by an ordinary feature makes people distrust the next ad.
  4. The loop is unrelated to the product. A curiosity hook about something else wins attention but not sales.
  5. The claim can't be backed up. In the UK, objective claims in ads need evidence; a curiosity hook built on an unprovable number is a risk.

Example: a 30-second ad for a meal-prep service

Example: the first version opened on a chef in a kitchen saying "At FreshBox we believe in good food". The re-cut uses loops:

TimeShot and line
0 to 3 sA fridge with five identical lunch boxes, FreshBox logo on the lids. On screen: "Five lunches. 12 minutes."
3 to 5 sClose-up of a timer at 11:58. Line: "Here's how."
5 to 14 sFast real-time cooking from the kit, recipe card visible. Line: "And the part that saves the most time isn't the cooking..."
14 to 20 sShopping list app showing nothing to buy. Line: "It's never going to the shop."
20 to 30 sPrice per meal, offer, button: "Order this week's box"

The first loop (how five lunches in 12 minutes?) is planted before the skip button; the second (what saves the most time?) carries the middle; both close by 20 seconds. Details are illustrative; your own claims must be true and timed for real.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops for ads and videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook teaches open loops and re-hooks for £1,720 one-time; it is YouTube-first, so adapt the timings for ads.

How many open loops should a 30-second ad have?

One main loop, and at most one small second loop. More tends to confuse rather than hold attention.

Should the open loop come before the brand?

No. Show the brand inside the loop, on the product or screen, so viewers who skip still saw it.

When should the loop be closed?

Usually by 15 to 20 seconds in a 30-second ad, leaving time for the offer and call to action.

Do open loops work in 6-second bumper ads?

There is little room. A single quick reveal inside the 6 seconds works better than a loop that needs a payoff later.
