Do classic supercars over 40 years old need an MOT or road tax?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Usually not, once the car is old enough. In Great Britain, a vehicle built or first registered more than 40 years ago does not need an MOT, as long as it has not been substantially changed. Cars built before 1 January 1986 can stop paying vehicle tax from 1 April 2026 by moving into the historic tax class, though you still have to apply and keep it taxed (at £0) and insured. You must still keep the car roadworthy.

This page is general information and planning estimates, not legal or financial advice. Check GOV.UK for your car's exact position.

Key takeaways

  • MOT exemption: built or first registered more than 40 years ago, and no "substantial changes".
  • Tax exemption: the historic tax class moves forward every year; from 1 April 2026 it covers cars built before 1 January 1986.
  • You do not have to apply to stop getting an MOT, but you do have to apply for the tax exemption.
  • Using a car in a dangerous condition can mean a fine of up to £2,500 and 3 penalty points, MOT or not.
  • Insurance is still required unless the car is declared off the road (SORN).

The MOT rule

GOV.UK's guide to historic (classic) vehicles says you do not need an MOT if the vehicle was built or first registered more than 40 years ago and no substantial changes have been made, for example replacing the chassis, body, axles or engine in a way that changes how the vehicle works. If you are not sure, it suggests reading the full guidance on MOT exemptions or speaking to a historic vehicle expert.

The MOT exemption section adds that you do not have to apply to stop getting an MOT each year, but you must keep the vehicle roadworthy, and that you can be fined up to £2,500 and get 3 penalty points for using a vehicle in a dangerous condition.

The road tax rule

The same guide says that if your vehicle was built before 1 January 1986, you can stop paying vehicle tax from 1 April 2026. If you do not know when it was built but it was registered before 8 January 1986, you also qualify. Each April, the cut-off moves forward a year.

Why "substantial changes" matter for supercars

Older supercars are often restored, re-engined or modified. If the chassis, body, axles or engine have been changed in a way that alters how the car works, it may lose MOT exemption. A like-for-like rebuild of the original engine is different from fitting a modern engine with more power. Keep restoration records and photos.

Steps when your car reaches 40 years

  1. Check build and first registration dates on the V5C.
  2. Confirm no substantial changes have been made, or get expert advice if unsure.
  3. Apply to move into the historic tax class at a Post Office, or as GOV.UK describes, when the tax is due.
  4. Keep it insured, or declare it off the road if stored.
  5. Keep it roadworthy: tyres, brakes, lights, steering.
  6. Consider a voluntary inspection each year.

If you plan to store it over winter, see should you SORN a supercar over winter.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming tax stops automatically. You must apply for the historic tax class.
  • Skipping checks because no MOT is needed.
  • Forgetting London's ULEZ and other clean air zones. Rules for historic vehicles differ by scheme; see do supercars pay the London ULEZ charge.
  • Major modifications without advice. They can affect exemption and insurance.

Keeping records for an older car

Without an annual MOT certificate, your own records become more important. Keep invoices for servicing and repairs, notes of any inspections, photos of restoration work, and the V5C showing build and first registration dates. These help with insurance, with proving the car has not been substantially changed, and with selling it later.

Do cars over 40 years old need an MOT?

Not if they were built or first registered more than 40 years ago and have not been substantially changed.

Which cars are road tax exempt from April 2026?

Cars built before 1 January 1986, once moved into the historic tax class.

Do I need to apply for the MOT exemption?

No. But you must apply for the vehicle tax exemption, and keep the car roadworthy.

Do historic cars need insurance?

Yes, unless declared off the road with a SORN.

What counts as a substantial change?

GOV.UK gives examples such as replacing the chassis, body, axles or engine in a way that changes how the vehicle works.
