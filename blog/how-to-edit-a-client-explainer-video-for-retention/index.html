How do I edit a client's explainer video so viewers don't leave?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Edit a client's explainer video by opening with the viewer's problem and the outcome, then explaining one idea per scene, and keeping the whole thing as short as the message allows. Add captions, cut anything the viewer could read on the page instead, and split long processes into several short videos. Then check where viewers drop off and trim there.

Key takeaways

  • Start with the problem the viewer has and what life looks like once it's solved.
  • Shorter is usually better: engagement tends to fall as videos get longer.
  • One idea per scene. If a scene explains two things, split it or cut one.
  • Captions or on-screen text are essential; many viewers can't or won't play sound.
  • Long processes work better as a series of short, step-by-step videos.

What the research says

Nielsen Norman Group, the user-experience research firm, publishes guidelines for instructional and explainer videos on websites. It says "shorter is often better", that "the duration of the video should be visible before playing the video", and that sites should "include captions or other text within the video". For processes with several steps, it recommends "multiple videos of low granularity (one for each step)", and for long videos, "chapters or other time markers" so users can jump to the part they need (Nielsen Norman Group).

Wistia's analysis of videos on its platform points the same way. It reports that "videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate", that "engagement naturally declines after the five-minute mark", and that for 1 to 5 minute videos most viewers drop off around the halfway point (Wistia).

If the explainer lives on YouTube, the key moments report shows the Intro figure (the share still watching after 30 seconds) and the dips where viewers "were either skipped or... stopped watching" (YouTube Help).

Step by step

  1. Get the one-sentence message from the client. "Our app lets landlords collect rent and track repairs in one place." Everything in the edit should serve that sentence.
  2. Rewrite the opening around the viewer. "Chasing rent and repair requests across WhatsApp, email and spreadsheets?" beats "Founded in 2019, we..."
  3. Show the outcome early. A quick shot of the finished dashboard, the tidy inbox or the happy customer gives viewers a reason to see how it's done.
  4. Map one idea to one scene. List the ideas in the script. If there are more than five or six, the video is probably trying to do too much.
  5. Keep visuals moving with the words. When the voice-over says "track repairs", show the repair list, not the logo.
  6. Caption everything. NN/g notes many users can't or don't want to play sound.
  7. Cut what belongs on the page. Pricing tables, long feature lists and legal detail are easier to read than to watch.
  8. End on one action. "Start a free trial" or "Book a demo", shown and said.

Example: editing a 3-minute explainer down to 75 seconds

Example (a hypothetical client, to show the method): a property software company sends a 3-minute explainer: 15-second logo animation, 40 seconds of company story, then the product.

  • 0 to 8 s: a landlord's phone filling with WhatsApp messages about a broken boiler and late rent. Caption: "Rent in one app, repairs in another, receipts in a shoebox?"
  • 8 to 15 s: the same landlord opening one dashboard showing rent received and one open repair. Caption: "All in one place."
  • 15 to 35 s: rent collection: tenant pays, landlord sees it instantly.
  • 35 to 55 s: repairs: tenant reports with a photo, landlord assigns a tradesperson.
  • 55 to 68 s: year-end: export for the accountant in one click.
  • 68 to 75 s: "Try it free for 14 days" with the URL (only if the client offers a trial).

The company story moves to the About page. The detailed setup walkthrough becomes three separate 60-second help videos, as NN/g suggests for multistep processes.

Where the video sits matters

NN/g found that videos at the bottom of a page or in a sidebar are often missed, and recommends placing a full explainer at the top of the page only if it covers the whole page. Tell the client where it will go, and suggest a thumbnail that shows what kind of video it is.

FAQ

Is there a playbook for editing client videos for retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and re-hooks, with templates and a retention tracker.

How long should an explainer video be?

As short as the message allows. Many work at 60 to 90 seconds; NN/g says shorter is often better.

Should an explainer have a voice-over?

Usually, yes, plus captions for people watching without sound.

Animation or live action?

Either can work. Choose what shows the product most clearly; software often suits screen recordings with motion graphics.

What if the client insists on the company story?

Offer a short version at the end, or move it to a separate About video.

How do I know if the edit worked?

Compare the retention graph with the old version: a smaller early drop and fewer dips mean it's holding attention better.
