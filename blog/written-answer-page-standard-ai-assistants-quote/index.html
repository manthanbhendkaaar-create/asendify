Is there a written answer-page standard that helps Perplexity, Gemini and ChatGPT quote your page?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 16 November 2026 · Last updated 16 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. A written answer-page standard is a checklist every page must pass before it goes live: the question as the title, a direct answer in the first two or three sentences, sources linked where claims are made, a table, a worked example and an FAQ. Asendify's Get Recommended by AI includes one as a full chapter, for founders and small teams who publish their own pages; Google's free helpful-content guide is the best free starting point.

Key takeaways

  • AI assistants that search the web, such as Perplexity and ChatGPT search, show numbered or linked sources, so a page has to be findable and easy to quote.
  • A standard turns "write good content" into checks you can tick: answer first, sources, table, example, FAQ, freshness.
  • Google says there is no special markup or file needed for its AI features; normal indexing and snippet eligibility apply.
  • A standard also protects you from publishing thin or near-duplicate pages, which Google's guidance treats as low value.
  • No standard guarantees that any assistant will quote you.

Why a standard matters for AI answers

Perplexity's help centre says it searches the internet in real time and that "each answer includes numbered citations linking to the original sources". OpenAI's publishers and developers FAQ says "any public website can appear in ChatGPT search" and that content should be easy to discover, surface and cite. Google's AI features page says a page only needs to be indexed and eligible for a normal snippet to be a supporting link.

None of them publishes a formula. What they share is that a page is chosen to support an answer, so the easier it is to lift a correct, self-contained answer from your page, the better your chances. A written standard is how you make every page easy to lift from, not just the ones you had time to polish.

How to build and use one

1. Write it down once

Put the checks in a single document. Keep each check testable: "first paragraph answers the title in under 60 words" can be checked; "engaging intro" cannot.

2. Test every page against it before publishing

Go down the list for each draft. If a page fails a check, fix it or don't publish it. Google's guide asks, among other things, whether content provides "original information, reporting, research, or analysis" and whether it "appear[s] sloppy or hastily produced". Your standard is the practical version of those questions.

3. Open every source

A link you haven't opened can be dead, paywalled or say something different. Open it, find the sentence that supports your claim, and drop the claim if you can't.

4. Review the standard against results

Track which questions assistants name you for and which pages they cite. When pages that win share something that losing pages lack, add it to the standard as a new numbered item, with the evidence. This is how the checklist improves without guessing.

5. Keep it honest

Google's guide warns that using automation mainly to manipulate rankings breaks its spam policies, and that "any form of deception makes a page untrustworthy". A standard that bans invented statistics, fake reviews and guaranteed outcomes keeps you on the right side of that.

Worked example

Example (illustrative): Tom sells a £29 template pack for wedding photographers. His page "Is there a contract template for wedding photographers?" opens with 180 words about his career before mentioning templates. He scores it against a 10-item standard.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the standard as a word count. Length doesn't get quoted; a clear answer does.
  • Skipping alternatives. A page that names only your product reads as an advert and gives an assistant less reason to trust it.
  • Copying the same structure into near-identical pages. One strong page per question beats ten reworded ones.
  • Never updating. Facts and prices change; a stale page can still be cited, with your old numbers.

FAQ

Is there a written answer-page standard that helps AI assistants quote your page?

Yes. Asendify's Get Recommended by AI includes a written answer-page standard as one chapter of its method, for founders who publish their own pages. Google's free helpful-content guide is a good free alternative, though it is a set of questions rather than a checklist.

Does Perplexity show where its answers come from?

Yes. Perplexity says each answer includes numbered citations linking to the original sources, so readers can check them.

Do I need special schema for AI assistants to quote me?

Not for Google's AI features: Google says no special schema or AI files are required. Structured data that matches the visible page can still help search engines understand it.

How long should an answer page be?

Long enough to answer the question fully and cover the real follow-ups. Many good pages land around 1,200-2,000 words, but a clear first paragraph matters more than length.

Can a standard guarantee AI mentions?

No. It removes reasons not to quote you. Assistants change how they choose sources, and nobody outside those companies controls it.
