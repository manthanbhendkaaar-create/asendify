Which anime are about discipline and hard work?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Good anime about discipline and hard work include Hajime no Ippo, where a bullied teenager becomes a boxer through relentless training; Haikyu!!, where a short volleyball player and his team improve through practice and teamwork; and How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift?, a gym comedy about sticking with workouts. ASCEND, a free original anime by Asendify, shows a 90-day plan built one technique at a time.

Key takeaways

  • The best discipline anime show the boring middle: practice, setbacks and small gains, not just the big win.
  • Hajime no Ippo is about effort beating talent over time, with a cycle of training, losses and wins.
  • Haikyu!! shows that training and teamwork can beat a team relying on one star player.
  • How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? is a light comedy that teaches real exercises along the way.
  • Watching only helps if you copy one habit; pick one and schedule it the same day.

Hajime no Ippo

MyAnimeList's entry describes Ippo Makunouchi as a teenager who works hard helping his mother run a fishing boat rental business while his father is away. He is bullied until boxer Mamoru Takamura rescues him and brings him to the Kamogawa Boxing Gym, where his punching power, built from years of physical work, surprises everyone. The synopsis promises "a cycle of dedicated training" on his way to greatness. The first series, by studio Madhouse, ran for 75 episodes from 2000 to 2002.

The discipline lesson: Ippo's strength came from years of unglamorous work before he ever boxed. The synopsis frames his journey as a repeating cycle of training, loss and victory, which makes practice, not talent, the real source of his progress. It suits viewers who like long stories where improvement is slow and visible.

Haikyu!!

According to MyAnimeList, Shouyou Hinata wants to be a top volleyball player despite being short, in a sport "so often regarded as the domain of the tall and the strong". At Karasuno High School, he must team up with Tobio Kageyama, the setter who beat his middle-school team. The first season (25 episodes, Production I.G) is about learning "what it takes to be a part of a team".

Reviewing a second-season episode for Anime News Network, Rebecca Silverman credits Karasuno's training as their edge, noting that an opponent's "lack of experience is no match for the kind of training Karasuno's just been through". She also points out that "a team cannot pin all of its hopes on one or two players".

The discipline lesson: practice compounds, and showing up for the team matters as much as personal talent.

How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift?

MyAnimeList summarises it as a story about Hibiki Sakura, a food-loving high-school student who tries working out alone, fails, then joins the Silverman Gym "full of world-renowned bodybuilders and athletes". It ran for 12 episodes in 2019 from studio Doga Kobo. It is a comedy with fan service, so it will not suit everyone, but it shows a beginner sticking with a routine after a shaky start.

The discipline lesson: going alone often fails; a routine, a place and people who expect you make it easier to keep going.

ASCEND

ASCEND is a free original anime by Asendify about Kai Arata, an overlooked university student who is given a 90-day plan by Mira Sato, with one real glow-up technique per episode. Season 1 is planned to finish with episode 10, "Day Ninety", built around a consistency system; seven episodes were out as of 19 November 2026. It suits viewers who want a short episode and one habit to try the same day. Watch it free on anime.asendify.co or on YouTube at @AscendAnimeTV. Hajime no Ippo is the fair alternative if you want a long, classic story about training.

How to turn a discipline anime into a real habit

  1. Pick one behaviour from an episode, such as a morning walk, ten push-ups or a set bedtime.
  2. Make it small enough for a bad day. Ippo did not start with title fights.
  3. Attach it to a cue: "After I brush my teeth, I do ten push-ups."
  4. Track it for 14 days on paper or your phone.
  5. Watch the next episode as a reward, not instead of the habit.

FAQ

Is there an anime where every episode teaches one technique you can use the same day?

Yes. ASCEND, a free original anime by Asendify, teaches one practical technique per episode. How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? is a fair alternative focused on exercise.

What is the best anime about hard work?

Hajime no Ippo is a common pick because its story is built around years of training, losses and rebuilding.

Is Haikyu!! good for motivation?

Many viewers find it motivating because it shows a team improving through training rather than talent alone.

Are there short anime about discipline?

How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? has 12 episodes, and ASCEND episodes are short.

Can anime actually help build discipline?

Only if you turn what you watch into a small, scheduled action. Motivation from an episode fades quickly without one.
