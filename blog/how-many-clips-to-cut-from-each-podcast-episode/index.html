How many clips should I cut from each podcast episode?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

There is no fixed number of clips per podcast episode. Cut one clip for every moment that makes sense on its own and earns a stranger's attention in the first few seconds; for many one-hour interviews that is somewhere around three to six, not twenty. Fewer strong clips posted on a steady schedule usually do more than a pile of weak ones.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's own podcasting best practices guide tells shows to "Create Shorts from your full episodes" and give Shorts viewers "a one-click journey to your full episode". It does not set a number.
  • The same guide says clips should be kept separate from full shows: "don't place them in the same playlist."
  • Since 15 October 2024, square or vertical videos up to three minutes count as Shorts, so one clip can now hold a complete short story or argument.
  • A Short over one minute with an active Content ID claim is blocked worldwide, so clips that contain licensed music or other people's footage need checking before you post.
  • The real limit is quality: every clip needs a moment that works with no context and a reason to keep watching.

How to decide the number for your show

Episode typeTypical stand-alone moments per hourWhy
Interview with a well-known guest4-8Strong opinions, stories and named topics people search for
Two hosts chatting2-5More in-jokes and context; fewer moments stand alone
Solo expert episode3-6Clear tips and claims that can be lifted cleanly
Panel or roundtable2-4Cross-talk makes clean edits harder
Narrative or serial episode1-3The story depends on what came before

These ranges are a starting rule of thumb from how episodes are structured, not measured averages. Your own analytics should replace them after a month or two.

The three tests every clip must pass

Before a moment earns a clip, it should pass three quick tests.

  1. The stranger test. Would someone who has never heard of the show understand it from the first line? If the clip needs "as we said earlier", it fails or needs a short text line on screen.
  2. The first-seconds test. Does it open on a claim, a question, a surprise or a strong reaction? YouTube Studio reports the share of Shorts viewers who "stayed to watch past the initial seconds", which is the number this test tries to protect.
  3. The payoff test. Does the clip end on an answer, a punchline or a clear point? A clip that trails off teaches viewers to swipe next time.

If you find ten moments and only four pass all three, cut four.

Why more clips is not always better

Posting many clips can look like free reach, but it has costs. Each weak clip takes editing time, and it adds a low "stayed to watch" figure to your channel's history. Clips that all look the same can also tire the audience you already have.

There is also a practical limit on attention. Wistia's analysis of video engagement notes that most viewers drop off around the halfway point of longer videos. A clip is a way of showing the best part to people who would never sit through the whole episode, so it only works if it really is the best part.

Long clips and Shorts are different jobs

A one-hour episode can feed two kinds of clip:

Clip typeLengthBest useWhere it goes
Short (vertical)15 seconds to 3 minutesOne idea, one reaction, one punchlineShorts, Reels, TikTok
Horizontal highlightAbout 5-15 minutesA full story or argument from the episodeA clips playlist or a separate clips channel

Count them separately. A show might cut four Shorts and one longer highlight from the same episode, because they need different moments.

YouTube's guide stresses that "Consistency doesn't equal volume." A rhythm you can keep for months beats a burst of 15 clips one week and none the next.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on choosing podcast clips that keep viewers watching?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's free podcasting guide is a good companion.

Should every episode get the same number of clips?

No. Some episodes have many stand-alone moments and some have few. Let the three tests decide, not a quota.

How long should a podcast Short be?

As long as the moment needs, up to three minutes on YouTube. Many work best when they make one point and end.

Is it better to post clips daily or all at once?

Spread them out. A steady rhythm gives each clip room and keeps the channel active between episodes.

Should clips use music?

Only music you are licensed to use. On YouTube, a Short over one minute with a Content ID claim is blocked.
