Should I post podcast clips before or after the full episode goes live?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Post most podcast clips on the same day as the full episode or after it, so every clip can link straight to an episode people can watch. A clip posted days before the episode is live can't send viewers anywhere useful on YouTube. The one exception is a single teaser before launch, which works best for a big guest or a Premiere with a watch page to point at.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's related video feature lets a Short link to another of your videos, but "The video you select has to be public or unlisted". A Short posted before the episode is public can't link to it.
  • YouTube's podcasting best practices guide tells shows to "Create Shorts from your full episodes & provide Shorts audiences with a one-click journey to your full episode."
  • The same guide recommends a fixed weekly day and time for full episodes and notes that "Consistency doesn't equal volume."
  • YouTube told TechCrunch in 2023 that it "doesn't think about Shorts as cannibalizing long-form", so posting clips after an episode shouldn't be seen as stealing its views.
  • Premieres have their own pre-release watch page and URL to share, according to YouTube's Premiere tips. That gives a pre-launch teaser somewhere to send people.

Why "after" works for most shows

Every clip has somewhere to send people. A viewer who likes the clip can tap through to the full conversation at once. If the episode isn't out yet, that interest is lost or they have to remember to come back.

You know which moments work. After release, the full episode's retention graph shows where viewers rewound or stayed. YouTube's retention report shows spikes where moments "were rewatched or shared". Those are strong candidates for clips.

It spreads attention across the week. A full episode on Tuesday, then clips on Wednesday, Friday and Sunday, gives the channel steady activity without making new episodes.

When a pre-launch teaser makes sense

A single teaser before launch can help when there's something to anticipate: a well-known guest, a season premiere, a long-awaited topic. Make it work harder:

  1. Point it somewhere. Use a Premiere's watch page, a community post or a trailer, so viewers can set a reminder.
  2. Don't give away the best moment in full. Show the set-up, not the pay-off.
  3. Say when. "Full conversation Thursday at 6pm" in the clip and caption.
  4. Post close to launch. One or two days before, not two weeks.

For unlisted early access, the related-video rule allows linking to an unlisted video, but that's rarely useful for public promotion. Most viewers won't find or trust an unlisted link.

Using old episodes

Clips don't have to be new. Evergreen moments from older episodes can keep finding viewers, and each one can link to its own full episode. Many shows mix one fresh clip with one archive clip each week. Keep the archive clip honest about its age if it refers to dated events.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on making podcast clips that lead to the full episode?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and open loops with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's free podcasting guide is a good companion.

Can a Short link to a scheduled or private episode?

No. YouTube says the related video must be public or unlisted.

Will clips posted after the episode steal its views?

YouTube has said it doesn't see Shorts as cannibalising long-form. Clips can send new viewers to the episode.

How soon after the episode should the first clip go out?

The same day works well, once the episode is public and the link works.

Should clips go on TikTok and Reels at the same time?

You can, but those apps can't link to the YouTube episode the same way, so mention where to find it in the clip or caption.
