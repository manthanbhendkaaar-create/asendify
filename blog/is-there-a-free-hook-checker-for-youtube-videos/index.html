Is there a free hook checker for YouTube videos?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

No tool can reliably score a hook before real people watch it, so there is no free "hook checker" worth trusting on its own. The free check that works is already in YouTube Studio: the audience retention report shows an "Intro" figure for how many viewers are still watching after 30 seconds, and lets you compare it with your recent videos. Use it after publishing, and a written hook checklist before.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Studio is the free hook checker. Its key moments help page says "Intro tells you what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds."
  • Compare like with like. The same page says you can "use typical retention to compare your 10 latest videos of similar length."
  • Tools that "score" a hook from the script or thumbnail are guessing. Real viewers, real data.
  • Before you publish, a checklist catches most weak openings: does the first line state the promise, show the payoff, and avoid a slow intro?
  • Platform guidance agrees on speed. TikTok's creative best practices say to "introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds".

How to use YouTube Studio as your hook checker

  1. Open the video in YouTube Studio, go to Analytics, then the Engagement tab, and open the audience retention report.
  2. Read the Intro figure. This is the share still watching after 30 seconds.
  3. Switch on the typical retention comparison. YouTube's help page explains that the comparison uses your recent videos of similar length, so a 9-minute video is judged against your other 9-minute videos, not your Shorts.
  4. Look for dips in the first 30 seconds. YouTube says dips "mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video." A dip at 0:08 usually points to one sentence, a cut to a title card or a slow sponsor mention.
  5. Write down the second, the line spoken there and what was on screen. That note is the real output of a hook check.
  6. Repeat across five or six videos. One video tells you little; a pattern tells you what your hooks get wrong.

A pre-publish hook checklist

Before the data exists, test the opening against these questions. If you answer "no" to two or more, rewrite the first 15 seconds.

QuestionWhy it matters
Does the first sentence say what the viewer gets?People clicked a promise; repeat it in plain words
Is the payoff shown or teased in the first 10 seconds?A glimpse of the result gives a reason to stay
Is there no logo sting, long greeting or "before we start"?Those seconds give nothing and invite a skip
Does the opening match the title and thumbnail?A mismatch feels like bait and people leave
Is there an open question the video will answer later?An open loop gives a reason to keep watching
Is the first visual change within a few seconds?Static openings feel slow on a phone

Why "hook scores" mislead

A tool that reads your script and rates the hook out of 10 is judging wording, not behaviour. The same sentence can work for one audience and fail for another, and the thumbnail, title, topic and traffic source all change who arrives. Treat any score as a prompt to re-read the line, never as a verdict. Only the retention graph, once real people have watched, tells you how the hook did.

Common mistakes

  • Judging a hook from the first few hundred views, when traffic sources are still shifting.
  • Comparing a Short's retention with a long video's.
  • Changing the topic and the hook at once, then not knowing which helped.
  • Treating a high Intro figure as success when the graph collapses at 1:30.
  • Rewriting the hook but leaving the slow title card that caused the dip.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches YouTube hooks with a checklist?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify includes a cheat sheet and templates for hooks, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's free help pages cover the analytics side.

Where do I find the Intro figure in YouTube Studio?

In the video's Analytics, under Engagement, in the audience retention report. It shows the share still watching at 30 seconds.

How many views do I need before the hook data means anything?

There is no official number. Wait until the video has had a steady flow of views for a few days and compare it with similar-length videos.

Can AI tell me if my hook is good?

It can suggest clearer wording. It cannot predict how real viewers behave, so check the retention graph after publishing.

What is a good Intro percentage?

It varies by niche and length. Compare with your own recent videos of similar length rather than a universal target.
