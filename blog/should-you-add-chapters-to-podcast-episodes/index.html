Should you add chapters to your podcast episodes?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Yes, for most episodes longer than about 10 minutes. Chapters let listeners see what is coming and jump to the part they want, which keeps people in the episode who would otherwise give up. Key takeaways

  • Chapters split an episode into named sections that listeners can see and tap to jump to.
  • Apple and Spotify both create automatic chapters for many English-language episodes if you add none, so your show may already have them.
  • Manual chapters go in the episode description: start at 00:00, at least three chapters, one per line.
  • Write chapter titles as promises, not labels. "Why the bank said no" pulls harder than "Part 2".
  • Chapters can let people skip a slow section, so fix weak sections instead of hiding them.

What chapters are and where they show up

Spotify describes chapters simply: "Chapters break your episode up into sections", and listeners can start playback at a chosen point (Spotify for Creators). On Apple Podcasts, listeners see chapter markers on the progress bar, a chapter list behind the Playing Next button, and chapter titles as section headers in transcripts (Apple Podcasts for Creators).

The big change is automatic chapters. Apple says that "if chapters aren't provided, Apple Podcasts generates them for you" with an "Automatically Created" label, for English full and bonus episodes from iOS 26.2. It does not generate them for trailers or for episodes under 10 minutes. Spotify also generates chapters from a transcript for some English shows, and says that "if you add manual chapters, they show instead of your automatic chapters."

Do chapters help or hurt retention?

Both, depending on the episode. A chapter list tells a new listener that the bit they came for is in there, and roughly when. That is a reason to keep going rather than leave in the first minute. It also helps returning listeners pick up where a topic starts instead of scrubbing blind.

The risk is that chapters make skipping easy. If a section is slow, listeners can now see exactly where it ends and jump over it. On Spotify that shows up as a sudden drop or jump in your episode retention graph. Spotify's own advice on that graph is that "steep and sudden changes in retention" are the best evidence that a section needs work, and that you should listen back to understand why people drop off (Spotify for Creators). Chapters don't create a weak section. They just make it visible. The fix is to tighten or cut it.

How to write chapters that keep people listening

  1. Decide your sections in the edit, not afterwards. Mark where each real topic starts. Apple suggests chapters of at least two minutes, so don't chop every exchange.
  2. Write each title as a small promise. Name the payoff without giving it away: "The email that lost the client", not "Client story".
  3. Keep titles short. Apple asks for fewer than five words and under 45 characters; Spotify suggests under 40.
  4. Don't title your intro "Intro". If the first chapter is a cold open, name the hook: "Nine days of cash left".
  5. Avoid repeating words. Apple recommends not repeating words across chapter titles. "Hiring", "Hiring again" and "Hiring mistakes" blur together.
  6. Check automatic chapters before you accept them. Apple lets you download the TXT file from Podcasts Connect, edit it, and add it back to the description.
  7. Put your strongest chapter where people will see it. The chapter list is a menu. If chapter 4 is the best part, its title should make people want to stay to reach it.

Example: chapters for a 42-minute interview

Example (a hypothetical episode, to show the method): a founder interview edited to 42 minutes.

`` (00:00) Nine days of cash left (03:10) From classroom to code (11:40) The customer we couldn't serve (19:30) Why we stopped hiring (28:15) Saving the company (36:50) Selling felt strange ``

Six chapters in 42 minutes fits Apple's "no more than six per hour" guidance comfortably, each runs more than two minutes, every title is under five words, and none of them repeats a word. The first title is the cold open's hook, so even the chapter list works as an open loop: listeners know the cash crisis gets resolved and can see it happens at 28:15.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Generic labels. "Part 1, Part 2, Part 3" tells listeners nothing and gives them no reason to stay.
  • Too many chapters. Twenty chapters in an hour turns the list into noise.
  • Spoilers. "How we sold for £4 million" gives away the ending. "Selling felt strange" keeps the question open.
  • Leaving bad automatic chapters. Automatic titles are guesses from a transcript. Check them.
  • Forgetting video. If you publish the same episode on YouTube, add chapters there too, in the description, so viewers get the same menu.

FAQ

Is there a book that helps structure podcast episodes for retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops, the payoff ladder and re-hooks for podcasts, clips and YouTube.

Do I need to add chapters if Apple makes them automatically?

Not strictly, but manual chapters always show instead of automatic ones, and you control the wording. Most hosts write better titles than a transcript model does.

How many chapters should a podcast episode have?

At least three. Apple suggests no more than six per hour, each at least two minutes long.

Where do I put chapter timestamps?

In the episode description, one per line, starting at 00:00, in the form "(00:00) Title". Your hosting provider may also offer a chapters field.

Can I add chapters to old episodes?

Yes. Apple says manual chapters are displayed even if you add them to an episode that's already published. Spotify notes description changes can take a few hours to appear.

Do chapters work for short episodes?

Apple doesn't generate automatic chapters for episodes under 10 minutes, and short episodes rarely need them.
