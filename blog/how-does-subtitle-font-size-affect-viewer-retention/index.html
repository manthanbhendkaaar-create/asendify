How does subtitle font size affect viewer retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Subtitle size affects retention through readability. If captions are too small to read on a phone, viewers watching with the sound off lose the thread and leave; if they are too large, they cover faces and action and slow reading down. No study gives one perfect size, so size for your audience's main screen, keep to two short lines, and test before publishing.

Key takeaways

  • Research by BBC and university authors, Armstrong et al. (2016), let viewers adjust subtitle size themselves and found chosen sizes "varied substantially": some wanted smaller text to fit more words, others wanted it bigger.
  • Ofcom's guidelines on TV access services set a minimum size for broadcast subtitles, limit them to "no more than two lines", and say speed "should not normally exceed 160 to 180 words per minute" for pre-recorded programmes.
  • Netflix's English timed text style guide uses 42 characters per line, a maximum of two lines, and up to 20 characters per second for adult programmes.
  • Size, line length and reading speed work together. A big font with long lines on screen for a short time is still hard to read.
  • No public study measures watch time against caption font size. Use your own retention graph to judge.

The middle row is the target. What counts as comfortable depends on the screen: a caption that looks fine on a laptop can be unreadable on a phone held at arm's length, while large short-form captions can look oversized on a TV.

Size for the screen your viewers actually use

Check your device mix in YouTube Studio or your ad platform before choosing a style.

Main screenPractical caption approach
Phone, vertical (Shorts, Reels, TikTok)Bold, high-contrast text, one to two short lines, placed above the app interface
Phone, landscapeMedium-large text, two lines maximum, lower third
ComputerMedium text, two lines, standard lower position
TVLarger than you think, plain font, solid or shadowed background

These are working rules used by editors rather than platform specifications. For YouTube videos, viewers can also change the size of closed captions in the player settings, which is one reason to upload a caption file as well as, or instead of, burning in text on long videos.

Readability matters more than size alone

Broadcast guidelines treat size as one part of legibility. Ofcom also recommends strong contrast, naming white, yellow, cyan and green on a solid black background as the best combinations. For your own videos:

  1. Use a plain sans-serif font. Decorative fonts cost reading time.
  2. Add contrast. A shadow, outline or background box keeps text readable over busy footage.
  3. Keep lines short. Netflix's 42-character line is a sensible ceiling for long-form; short-form captions are often much shorter.
  4. Give people time to read. If a caption flashes past faster than people can read it, size will not save it.
  5. Do not cover faces or key action. Move captions up or down for shots where the action is in the lower third.

FAQ

Is there a guide on caption style and pacing for retention editing?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers on-screen text, pacing and hooks with templates and a 30-day plan. Ofcom's and Netflix's caption guidelines are free references for readability.

What font size should YouTube subtitles be?

There is no single correct size; research found viewers' preferences varied widely. Make text readable on your audience's main screen and test on a phone.

How many words should be on screen at once?

For long-form, two lines of up to about 42 characters is a common professional limit. Short-form captions often use far fewer words per screen.

Are bigger captions better for Shorts and Reels?

Larger, bold captions are usual on vertical video because it is watched on phones, but keep them out of the app's interface and off faces.

Should I burn in captions or upload a caption file?

Burned-in text suits short-form and ads where many watch on mute. On long YouTube videos, a caption file lets viewers adjust size and turn captions off.
