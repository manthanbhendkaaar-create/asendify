What do 2-second and 3-second video views mean on Meta?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

On Meta (Facebook and Instagram), a 3-second video view is counted when someone watches at least 3 seconds of your video, and a 2-second continuous video view is counted when someone watches 2 seconds or more without interruption, usually with at least half of the video on screen. Both are "did the opening stop the thumb?" numbers. They tell you whether your hook works, not whether people watched the message, so read them next to ThruPlays and watch-through percentages.

Key takeaways

  • MarTech's summary of platform rules notes that Meta counts a video view "at 3 seconds" for in-stream and Stories ads, while a skippable YouTube ad needs 30 seconds (or the full ad if shorter).
  • A 2-second continuous play is recorded "when someone views a video for 2 seconds or more without interruption", usually with at least 50% of the video in view, per Jon Loomer's Meta ads glossary.
  • The 2-second, 50%-in-view idea matches the wider industry viewability standard: Google Ads counts a viewable video impression when at least 50% of the ad is on screen "for two continuous seconds" (Google Ads Help).
  • Facebook changed its 3-second count in 2018 so it "will now only count unrepeated seconds watched" (Social Media Today), so replays no longer pad the number.
  • These are hook metrics. Use them to judge the first seconds, then use ThruPlays and percentage-watched metrics to judge the rest.

ThruPlay is the deeper metric. MarTech's summary says advertisers on ThruPlay buying are charged "when a video ad plays to 97 percent completion or up to 15 seconds, whichever comes sooner". We cover it in detail in what a ThruPlay is on Meta ads.

Why 2 seconds and 3 seconds are not the same

The difference looks tiny, but the two numbers answer slightly different questions.

  1. Continuity. The 2-second metric needs unbroken viewing. Someone who scrolls past, then back, does not reach 2 continuous seconds on the first pass.
  2. Visibility. The 2-second definition expects most of the video to be on screen, so half-visible autoplay in a busy feed is less likely to count.
  3. Length of the test. The extra second matters. Your opening line or visual has to survive a full beat longer to be counted as a 3-second play.

If your 2-second plays are healthy but 3-second plays fall away sharply, the first frame is working and the second beat is not. That usually means a slow first sentence, a logo sting, or a cut to something less interesting.

How to use them: the hook rate

The most practical way to use 3-second views is as a ratio:

Hook rate = 3-second video plays ÷ impressions

Then pair it with a hold measure, such as ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays, to see whether the people you hooked stayed. We explain both in hook rate and hold rate in video ads.

Common mistakes

  • Treating 3-second views as "views". A 3-second play is a very low bar. Reporting it to a client as views makes a video look more successful than it was.
  • Comparing across platforms. A Meta 3-second view and a YouTube skippable-ad view (30 seconds, or the whole ad if shorter) are different things. Don't put them in one column.
  • Ignoring sound. Many feed viewers start with the sound off, so a hook that relies on a spoken line can lose the 2-second test before the line lands. Use captions or on-screen text in the first frame.
  • Changing two things at once. If you change the hook and the length together, you won't know which one moved the numbers.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks that win the first 3 seconds?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Meta's metric definitions in Ads Manager are a free reference.

Is a 3-second view a real view?

It is a real metric, but a low bar. It shows someone paused on your video, not that they understood the message.

Do replays count towards 3-second views?

Facebook said in 2018 that it would only count unrepeated seconds watched, so replaying the same seconds should not inflate the number.

Which is better to optimise for: 3-second views or ThruPlays?

For most ads, ThruPlays or a conversion goal. Optimising for 3-second views can buy cheap glances rather than attention.

What is a good hook rate?

It depends on the audience, placement and product. Compare cuts of your own ads to the same audience rather than chasing a universal number.
