How do you see where listeners drop off in your podcast?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

You can see where listeners drop off using the retention graphs in Spotify for Creators and YouTube Studio, which show how many people are still listening or watching at each point of an episode, and Apple Podcasts Connect, which shows average consumption and engaged listeners. Look for steep, sudden drops at specific moments, then listen to what happens just before each one.

Key takeaways

  • Spotify says "episode drop-off data is the key to knowing which episodes and segments held your audience's attention".
  • YouTube Studio marks key moments: the intro (viewers left after 30 seconds), top moments, spikes and dips.
  • Apple Podcasts reports "average consumption" and "engaged listeners" (at least 20 minutes or 40% of an episode) rather than a full curve on every chart.
  • Apple only shows data once at least five unique listeners have played your show.
  • Dips at ad slots are common; Spotify notes the curve often "normalizes back" after the ad.

Spotify for Creators

Spotify's guide to understanding episode performance explains that "episode drop-off data is the key to knowing which episodes and segments held your audience's attention" and that "your retention percentage is proportional to the amount of people listening and watching" (Spotify for Creators). It also offers practical reading tips:

  • Dips "probably occur at the same time as your advertisements" and "don't necessarily mean that people left your podcast"; the curve often recovers afterwards.
  • "Steep and sudden changes in retention are the best evidence for deciding if your ad slot needs a new lead-in."
  • "Listeners have a tendency to drop off early", so the start matters most.
  • "As you gather more listeners, your curve will start to smooth out", so small shows should judge over several episodes.

YouTube Studio

For video podcasts, YouTube's help page on key moments explains four markers (YouTube Help):

  • Intro: "what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds."
  • Top moments: where "almost no one dropped off."
  • Spikes: moments that "were rewatched or shared."
  • Dips: where "viewers are abandoning or skipping."

You can compare an episode with "your 10 latest videos of similar length", the fairest benchmark for long shows.

Apple Podcasts Connect

Apple's analytics focus on episode-level summaries (Apple Podcasts for Creators):

  • Engaged listeners: "people who listened to or watched at least 20 minutes or 40% of an episode."
  • Average consumption: "a percent average indicating how much of the episode users played per device." Above 100% means someone replayed it.
  • Threshold: data appears only when "at least five unique listeners" have played your show.

Apple also notes that analytics rely on accurate episode durations, so check your feed's duration tags.

How to read a drop-off

  1. Find the steepest drops, not the gentle slope every long episode has.
  2. Note the timestamp and play 30 to 60 seconds before it.
  3. Name the cause. Common ones: a long welcome, an ad read with no lead-in, a tangent, a repeated story, audio problems, or the end of the topic people came for.
  4. Check other episodes. If drops happen at the same type of moment every time (the ad break, the recap), that's a format problem, not a one-off.
  5. Change one thing in the next episode and compare.

Example: one episode across three platforms

Example (a hypothetical 48-minute interview, to show how the data fits together):

  • YouTube: intro shows 58% still watching at 30 seconds, below the channel's recent long videos. A dip at 6:20 where the sponsor read starts.
  • Spotify: the curve falls sharply in the first two minutes (theme music and welcome), dips at 6:20, then recovers to its previous level.
  • Apple: average consumption 41%, lower than the show's last five episodes.

Diagnosis: the opening is too slow and the ad read lacks a lead-in. Next episode: a 30-second cold open and a line before the ad that teases what's coming after it. Compare all three numbers again.

Common mistakes reading podcast data

  • Treating downloads as listening. A download doesn't mean anyone played it.
  • Judging one small episode. Spotify notes curves smooth out with more listeners.
  • Panicking at ad dips that recover. A dip that bounces back means people skipped, not left.
  • Comparing different formats. Compare interviews with interviews, solo with solo.
  • Changing everything at once. You won't know what worked.

FAQ

Is there a book that explains podcast retention graphs?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches graph shapes, common leaks and fixes, and applies them to podcasts. Platform help pages are good free references.

Does Apple Podcasts show a retention graph?

Apple's listener analytics focus on listeners, engaged listeners, time listened and average consumption per episode.

What is a good average consumption?

Apple doesn't publish a benchmark. Compare episodes of the same format on your own show.

Why does my Spotify graph dip at the ads?

Listeners often skip ads. Spotify says the curve usually recovers; a steep drop that doesn't recover means the lead-in needs work.

Can I see drop-off for audio-only shows on YouTube?

Yes, if the episode is uploaded as a video, even with a static image. The retention graph works the same way.

How many listeners do I need for useful data?

Apple needs at least five unique listeners to show anything; curves become more reliable as audiences grow.
