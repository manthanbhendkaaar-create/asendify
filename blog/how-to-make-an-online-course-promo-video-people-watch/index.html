How long should an online course promo video be, and what should it show first?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

For most online courses, a promo or sales video of roughly one and a half to three minutes works: shorter for short courses, a little longer for big ones. Open with what students will be able to do by the end, shown with real results of the work (a finished design, a working app, a cooked dish), then say who it's for, show short clips of actual lessons, list what's included and end with one clear call to action. Your introduction can wait until the middle; people press play to learn about the course, not about you.

Key takeaways

  • In a Udemy community knowledge-base article, instructor Lindsay Marsh calls 2 minutes "a sweet spot", suggests about 3 minutes for courses over 5 hours and around a minute and a half or less for 2-hour courses. This is one instructor's experience, not a platform rule.
  • The same article says putting "your course topic and content first tends to grab people's attention", with your introduction "toward the middle/end".
  • Wistia's 2026 data says engagement "naturally declines after the five-minute mark" and that most viewers leave around the halfway point.
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "jump in" and "include a call to action".
  • Saying early who the course is not for keeps the right viewers and saves refunds.

Showing the finished result first opens a loop: viewers want to see how they would get there, which is exactly what the lesson clips answer.

Editing for retention

  1. Be visual. Show the work, the screen, the hands, the before-and-after of a student exercise, not only you talking.
  2. Keep each section short. About 15 seconds per section keeps a 2-minute promo moving, as the Udemy article suggests.
  3. Music under, not over. Keep it low while you speak.
  4. Caption everything. Many people preview courses on phones with sound off.
  5. Use real lesson footage. Viewers judge teaching style and audio quality from the promo.
  6. Spend real time on it. The Udemy article suggests far more editing time for the promo than for a typical lecture, because it's what most prospective students see first.

Stay honest

Promo videos are advertising. Don't promise income, jobs or results you can't support, don't show student work or testimonials without permission, and don't invent enrolment numbers or urgency. In the UK, "falsely stating that a product will only be available for a limited time" is a banned practice under the DMCC Act 2024. A real deadline, such as a cohort start date, is fine.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to open a course promo video?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCDs guidance is free.

How long should a course promo video be?

Roughly 1.5 to 3 minutes, depending on course size; about 2 minutes suits many courses.

Should I introduce myself first?

Usually not. Lead with the outcome; a short introduction can come in the middle or near the end.

Should the promo show real lessons?

Yes. Short clips of real lessons show teaching style and production quality.

Can I use student results in my promo?

Only genuine results shared with permission, without implying everyone will get them.
