Should you put your podcast on YouTube as a video podcast?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

For most shows, yes. YouTube is now where a large share of people consume podcasts: Edison Research found 33% of US weekly podcast listeners use YouTube most often. Putting your episodes there as video, with hosts on camera, gives you search, recommendations and clips. The catch is that video viewers can see when nothing is happening, so the edit has to give them a reason to keep watching.

The case for YouTube

The audience is there. Edison Research's Infinite Dial 2025 reported that 55% of Americans aged 12+ consume podcasts monthly, 51% have watched a video podcast, and, according to Edison Podcast Metrics, "33% of U.S. weekly podcast listeners use YouTube most often" to listen (Podnews press release). Edison's Megan Lazovick suggested it's "smart to think about it as consumption instead of just listening."

Discovery works differently. Podcast apps mostly serve people who already know your show. YouTube adds search, suggested videos and Shorts, so a single strong episode or clip can reach people who've never heard of you.

Clips have somewhere to live. YouTube's podcasting best-practices guide recommends treating clips like any other upload and giving Shorts viewers "a one-click journey to your full episode" (YouTube podcasting best practices, PDF).

YouTube's guide reports that, in its example, videos with "podcast" in the title, around 15 minutes long and featuring hosts on video, drew "2x" more views than those using "a static visualization". It also stresses that "editorial quality matters more than video quality", so a phone camera and good editing can beat a studio with a flat edit.

Who is actually watching?

Not everyone watches. Cumulus Media and Signal Hill Insights' spring 2025 Podcast Download found 58% of weekly podcast consumers still say audio is their primary mode, 28% listen with no video at all, 30% listen with the video "minimized or plays in the background", and 42% actively watch (Westwood One). The active-watching share rose from 30% in 2022.

So edit for two audiences at once: clean, self-explanatory audio for the background group, and visual change for the people watching.

What to change when you add video

  1. Start differently. On YouTube, the "intro" moment in Studio shows how many viewers are still there after 30 seconds. Open on a strong moment (a cold open), not a long welcome.
  2. Give the eye something. Switch between speakers, punch in on reactions, and add on-screen text or images when someone mentions a number, a product or a place.
  3. Use chapters. YouTube needs the first timestamp at 00:00, at least three timestamps and chapters of at least 10 seconds (YouTube Help).
  4. Package each episode. YouTube's guide advises accurate titles under about 70 characters with key words first, and thumbnails featuring the host's or guest's face.
  5. Separate clips from full episodes. Keep them in different playlists, as YouTube recommends.

Example: launching video on an audio show

Example (a hypothetical show, to show a sensible rollout): a weekly 50-minute business interview podcast, audio-only for two years.

  • Month 1: add one camera per person (phones on tripods), keep the audio set-up. Upload full episodes to YouTube with a 30-second cold open and chapters. Cut three vertical clips per episode.
  • Month 2: check the intro percentage and dips in YouTube Studio. Move the sponsor read from the start to a mid-episode break. Add punch-ins on the biggest reactions.
  • Month 3: keep what improved retention, drop what didn't, and decide whether better cameras are worth it. Often they aren't yet; editing changes move the numbers more.

When video isn't worth it (yet)

  • Your show is heavily produced narrative audio, where pictures add little.
  • Guests or hosts can't appear on camera for privacy reasons.
  • You can't sustain the extra editing time without missing episodes; a reliable audio schedule beats an erratic video one.

Even then, short clips with captions and simple visuals can bring people from YouTube to your audio feed.

FAQ

Is there a book on editing video podcasts so viewers stay?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook applies cold opens, open loops and pacing to podcasts and clips. YouTube's free podcasting guide is a good companion.

Do I need expensive cameras for a video podcast?

No. YouTube's guide says editorial quality matters more than video quality. Start simple and improve the edit first.

Should I upload full episodes or just clips?

Both, in separate playlists. Full episodes serve fans; clips bring in new viewers.

Will putting my podcast on YouTube hurt my Apple or Spotify numbers?

Some listeners may switch platforms, but YouTube also reaches people who'd never find you in podcast apps.

Is a static image with audio good enough?

It's a cheap test, but YouTube's own example found hosts on camera drew about twice the views of static visuals.

How long should a YouTube podcast episode be?

As long as it stays interesting. Use chapters and a strong opening rather than cutting to a fixed length.
