Can I offset share trading losses against gains for Capital Gains Tax in the UK?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. In the UK, losses on shares and other assets subject to Capital Gains Tax can be deducted from your gains in the same tax year, and any unused losses can be carried forward to reduce gains in future years. You must report the loss to HMRC to use it, and you have up to 4 years after the end of the tax year of the sale to claim. Losses inside an ISA, and usually spread betting losses, cannot be used this way.

This page is education only, not tax or financial advice. Ask HMRC or a qualified tax adviser about your own situation.

Key takeaways

  • Reported losses are "allowable losses": they come off gains in the same tax year first.
  • If gains are still above the tax-free allowance, earlier unused losses can then be used.
  • Carried-forward losses are only used to bring gains down to the allowance, so you do not waste the allowance.
  • The Capital Gains Tax allowance (Annual Exempt Amount) is £3,000.
  • Claim losses within 4 years of the end of the tax year you sold the asset.

How HMRC treats losses

GOV.UK's page on Capital Gains Tax losses explains the rules. You can report losses on a chargeable asset to HMRC to reduce your total taxable gains. When you report a loss, it is deducted from gains in the same tax year. If your total taxable gain is still above the tax-free allowance, you can deduct unused losses from previous years. If they bring your gain down to the allowance, you carry forward the rest.

You claim by including the loss on your Self Assessment return. If you have never made a gain and are not registered for Self Assessment, you can write to HMRC instead. You do not have to report losses straight away: you can claim up to 4 years after the end of the tax year you disposed of the asset.

GOV.UK's Capital Gains Tax allowances page gives the current allowance: £3,000, or £1,500 for trusts.

Order of use

StepWhat happens
1Add up all gains in the tax year
2Subtract all losses from the same tax year (in full, even below the allowance)
3If the net gain is above £3,000, subtract earlier carried-forward losses, only down to £3,000
4Pay tax on anything left above £3,000
5Carry forward any losses not used

The key difference: same-year losses must be used in full, while brought-forward losses are only used to the extent needed to bring gains down to the allowance.

Worked example: two tax years

Example (illustrative): Daniel trades shares in a general investment account (not an ISA).

Gains£4,000£9,000
Same-year losses£7,000£1,000
Net gain after same-year losses−£3,000 (a net loss)£8,000
Losses brought forward usedn/a£3,000 (all he has)

In Year 1, Daniel's same-year losses wipe out his gains and leave £3,000 of unused loss, but only if he reports it to HMRC within 4 years. In Year 2, he uses that £3,000 to bring £8,000 down to £5,000, then the allowance covers £3,000, leaving £2,000 taxable. Without reporting the Year 1 loss, he would pay tax on £5,000. Losses you usually cannot use

Account or productCan losses offset CGT gains?
Stocks and shares ISANo: ISA gains and losses are outside CGT. See trading shares tax-free in an ISA
Spread betting (for most individuals)Usually no: profits are generally not taxed, so losses generally cannot be used. See is spread betting tax-free in the UK
Shares in a general investment accountYes
CFDsGenerally treated within CGT for individuals; check with an adviser
Trading as a business (rare)Different rules: trading income, not CGT

If you buy back the same shares within 30 days of selling, special matching rules can change your loss; see the 30-day bed and breakfasting rule.

Records to keep

Keep contract notes, dates, quantities, prices and costs for every buy and sell, and a running list of losses claimed and carried forward. Our page on what trading records to keep for HMRC has a checklist.

Common mistakes

  • Not reporting losses because no tax is due. You lose the ability to carry them forward.
  • Missing the 4-year deadline.
  • Assuming ISA losses count.
  • Forgetting the 30-day rule when selling and rebuying the same share.

A year-end checklist for share traders

  1. Download your broker's annual statement and realised gains report.
  2. List every disposal with date, proceeds and costs.
  3. Note any shares bought back within 30 days of a sale.
  4. Total gains and losses for the tax year.
  5. Check whether losses from earlier years are available.
  6. Decide whether you need to report on Self Assessment, and diary the 4-year claim deadline for any losses.

Keeping this list each April makes the tax return much quicker and reduces the chance of missing a loss you could have used.

How long do I have to claim a capital loss?

Up to 4 years after the end of the tax year in which you sold the asset.

Can I carry forward share losses?

Yes, if you report them. Unused losses carry forward to future years.

Do I have to report losses if I have no gains?

Not immediately, but you must report them within 4 years to use them later.

Can losses reduce my income tax?

Not normally. Capital losses offset capital gains, not income such as salary.

What is the Capital Gains Tax allowance?

£3,000 for individuals, according to GOV.UK.
