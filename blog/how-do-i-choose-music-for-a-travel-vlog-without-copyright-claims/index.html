How do I choose music for a travel vlog without copyright claims?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use music that YouTube itself marks as safe, such as tracks from the YouTube Audio Library, or music you made or properly licensed, and keep proof of the licence. "Royalty-free" from other sites is not automatically claim-proof. Then choose tracks that suit each scene, so the music supports the travel story instead of fighting the ambient sound.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube says the Audio Library provides "royalty-free production music and sound effects", that its tracks "are copyright-safe", and that "Only music and sound effects from the Audio Library are known to YouTube to be copyright-safe" (YouTube Help).
  • Creative Commons tracks need credit: "you must credit the artist in your video's description" (same page).
  • YouTube's music tips say to "always check music licenses before you choose" and "For longer videos, stick to safe resources" (YouTube Help).
  • A Content ID claim can "Block", "Monetize" or "Track" a video, but claims "usually don't impact your channel or account" (YouTube Help).
  • YouTube "is not responsible for issues that arise from" royalty-free music from other libraries or channels (Audio Library page).

Choose music for the scene, not only for safety

Claim-free music is only the first test. Travel vlogs also need music that helps retention:

  1. Match energy to the footage. Fast cuts over a market need rhythm. A slow sunrise needs space.
  2. Let ambient sound speak. Street noise, waves and local voices make a place feel real. Duck the music under them.
  3. Use one main bed per section. Changing track every thirty seconds feels scattered.
  4. Mark transitions with music changes. A new place can get a new track or a lift in the same track.
  5. Keep speech clear. Keep the music low enough under speech that words are easy to follow.
  6. Avoid vocal tracks under talking. Lyrics compete with your voice.

Keep a music log

For each video, record: track name, artist, source, licence type, link, date downloaded and where in the video it plays. If a claim arrives, you can answer in minutes. If you ever move to a new editor, they can follow the same file.

If you get a claim anyway

A claim is not a strike. YouTube explains that, depending on the owner's settings, it can block the video, run ads on it or track viewing figures. Check the details in Studio. If the claim is right, remove or replace the track. If it is wrong, you can dispute it. For tricky cases, ask a lawyer, since YouTube cannot give legal advice.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on pacing travel vlogs?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and pattern interrupts with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's Audio Library is a free music source.

Is the YouTube Audio Library really safe?

YouTube states its tracks are copyright-safe and not claimed through Content ID. Creative Commons tracks still need credit in the description.

Is "royalty-free" music from other sites safe?

Not automatically. YouTube says only Audio Library tracks are known to it as copyright-safe, so check licences.

Will a claim hurt my channel?

YouTube says claims usually do not impact your channel or account, but they can block, monetise or track the video.

Can I record music playing around me?

It can still be matched. Keep it short and consider covering it with your own track.
