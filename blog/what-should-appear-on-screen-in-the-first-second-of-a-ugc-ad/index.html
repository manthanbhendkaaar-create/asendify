What should appear on screen in the first second of a UGC ad?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

In the first second of a UGC ad, show a real person or hands already in motion, the product or the problem in plain view, and at most a few large words of text, with the voice starting at once. Do not open on a logo, a fade from black, a title card or a pause. The viewer should be able to guess what the ad is about, and who it is for, before the second second arrives.

Key takeaways

  • TikTok's creative guidance says to "prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds", to "introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds", to use "sound/ music" and "vertically at 9:16", and to choose "a DIY or not overly polished style" (TikTok for Business).
  • It also advises "captions or text overlays to provide context" at "5-10 words per second" (TikTok for Business), so the first frame's text should be far below that ceiling.
  • YouTube's Shorts metric "How viewers engaged" is "the percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short" (YouTube Help), a direct view of how well the opening holds.
  • No source I opened measures what happens in exactly the first second. The checklist below is editing practice, to be tested on your own ads.

What the first second has to do

In a feed, a viewer sees your video in the middle of other content. The first second does not need to make the sale. It needs to stop the thumb long enough for the hook to land. That means answering three silent questions fast:

  1. Is this a person or an ad? A real face or hands, in a believable setting, signals UGC.
  2. What is it about? The product or the problem is visible.
  3. Is something happening? Movement, speech or a changing frame signals there is a reason to stay.

How to cut the first second

  1. Trim the dead start. Most raw UGC begins with the creator getting ready. Cut to the first moment of real action.
  2. Start mid-motion. Begin on the pour, the open, the reveal, not the setup.
  3. Lead with the line that names the problem or the result. "My white trainers were ruined, then this happened."
  4. Overlay a short caption in the first frame. A few words in large type that match the speech.
  5. Keep the face near the top-centre, product near the lower centre. Interface overlays cover the edges on many apps.
  6. Cut any intro animation, logo or brand slate. Put the brand in later, or in the end card.
  7. Check the frame as a still. If you paused on frame one, would a stranger understand something?

Matching the first second to the ad type

  • Problem-first ads. Show the problem on screen straight away: the cluttered desk, the stained shirt.
  • Result-first ads. Open on the after state, then rewind to the how.
  • Question hooks. Face to camera with the question as text.
  • Demo hooks. The product mid-use, with a short label.
  • Testimonial style. A person mid-sentence with their first words already spoken.

Why unpolished can work

TikTok's own guidance recommends a DIY or not overly polished style so it fits with user content. A polished brand intro in the first second signals "advert", and viewers who scroll quickly on social platforms tend to skip things that look like ads. That is an inference from the guidance, not a measured finding, so test a polished versus a native opening on your own audience.

Example: a reusable water bottle ad

Example: a brand opens its UGC ad with a one-second logo animation, then a creator saying "Hi everyone". In a second version, the first frame shows the creator tipping a leaking bottle over a bag, with the caption "Never again" and the line "My last bottle ruined my laptop bag" already starting. The logo moves to the end card. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result. The team runs both openings with the same audience and budget and compares view-through at three seconds and at completion on the ad platform's dashboard.

How to test it

Make two versions of the same ad that differ only in the first second. Run both to the same audience and compare the platform's early view metrics, not only purchases, since the first second affects early retention first. Watch for creative fatigue and wait for enough data to avoid reacting to noise. If you also run the ad on YouTube, use the retention graph for the first 30 seconds. Keep a log: version, first-second change, date, result.

Mistakes

  • A logo or brand slate first.
  • A fade from black.
  • Waiting for the creator to say hello.
  • Text that is too small or too long.
  • Product not visible for several seconds.
  • Horizontal footage in a vertical placement.
  • No audio, or music louder than speech.

Checklist

  • Person or hands in motion.
  • Product or problem visible.
  • A few large words at most.
  • Voice starts on frame one.
  • Vertical 9:16, safe margins.
  • No logo before the hook.

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. It does not guarantee ad results.

Should the first second include the product?

Usually yes, or the problem it solves.

Should I put a logo first?

Generally not. Put the brand later or in the end card.

How much text in the first frame?

A few large words. TikTok's guidance caps text at 5-10 words per second.

Should the voice start immediately?

Yes. Silence at the start loses viewers.
