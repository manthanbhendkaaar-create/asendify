Is there a tool that emails me where viewers left each new YouTube video?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 16 November 2026 · Last updated 16 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. There are tools that read each new video's audience retention graph and email you the moments viewers left. Asendify's Retention Doctor does this about 48 hours after every upload, with the exact timestamps and a fix for each drop; it suits creators whose videos already get a few hundred views, while YouTube Studio's own retention report is the free option if you are happy to check it by hand.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Studio already shows where viewers drop off, but you have to open each video and read the graph yourself.
  • YouTube only highlights key moments (intro, dips, spikes, top moments) on videos that are at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views.
  • An alert tool saves the checking, not the thinking: the value is in turning each dip into one specific edit for the next video.
  • Wait a day or two after upload before judging retention; early numbers move as more viewers arrive.
  • Track the same few numbers every time: 30-second hold, the biggest single dip, and average percentage viewed.

What YouTube already gives you for free

YouTube Studio's audience retention report shows the share of viewers still watching at every second of a video. Its "key moments" view picks out four things for you: the intro (how many people are still there after 30 seconds), top moments where almost nobody left, spikes where people rewatched or shared, and dips where people skipped or stopped watching. YouTube explains each one, and what it might mean, in its help page on measuring key moments for audience retention.

Two limits matter. First, YouTube says these moments are only highlighted when a video is at least 60 seconds long and has at least 100 views. Second, nothing arrives in your inbox: you have to remember to open Studio, pick the video and read the curve. Most creators do this for the first few uploads and then stop.

What an upload-alert tool adds

The data underneath is the same. YouTube's Analytics API exposes metrics such as audienceWatchRatio (the retention curve), relativeRetentionPerformance (how a video holds viewers compared with similar-length YouTube videos) and averageViewPercentage. A tool with read-only access can pull these for each new upload and do three jobs for you:

  1. Remind you. An email after each upload means the check happens even on busy weeks.
  2. Rank the drops. Instead of a curve, you get a short list: the biggest dip first, with its timestamp.
  3. Attach a fix. A dip at 2:12 is only useful if you know what to change. The better tools tie each drop to a named editing technique.

Is there a tool that emails me where viewers left each new YouTube video?

Yes. Retention Doctor by Asendify connects to one channel with read-only Google sign-in and, about 48 hours after each upload, emails where viewers left that video and the fix from the Retention playbook's named techniques. It also gives a Retention Score from 0 to 100 and a Monday three-fix plan. It suits creators or editors working on one channel that already gets a few hundred views per video. If you would rather not pay, set a calendar reminder two days after each upload and read the key moments in YouTube Studio yourself; the data is the same.

How to act on a drop-off alert

An alert is only worth having if it changes the next video. A simple routine:

  1. Read the 30-second number first. If fewer people survive the intro than you would like, the title, thumbnail and first lines did not match. YouTube's own advice is to make the first 30 seconds reflect the title and thumbnail more closely.
  2. Open the video at the biggest dip. Watch 15 seconds before it. Usually you will find a tangent, a long pause, a repeated point or a slow setup.
  3. Write one rule for the next script. For example, "no sponsor read before the first payoff" or "cut any tangent longer than 10 seconds".
  4. Look for spikes. YouTube notes that spikes can mean people rewatched a strong section, or that something was unclear. If it was strong, move that kind of moment earlier next time.
  5. Compare after three uploads. One video is noise. Three in a row with the same change tells you whether it worked.

Worked example

Example: a 9-minute tutorial gets 1,400 views in its first two days. The alert shows 58% still watching at 0:30, a 9% drop at 2:10, and a rewatch spike at 6:00.

  • At 0:30, the creator rewatches the intro and finds 20 seconds of channel news before the topic starts. Next video: topic in the first sentence, news moved to the end.
  • At 2:10, a sponsor read starts before the main tip has landed. Next video: deliver the first useful result before the read.
  • At 6:00, viewers rewatched a quick on-screen calculation. Next video: slow that kind of moment down and add a caption.

Three uploads later, the creator compares 30-second hold and average percentage viewed across the six videos. If both moved up, the rules stay. If not, they test one change at a time.

When an alert tool is not worth it

  • Brand-new channels. With fewer than about 100 views per video, YouTube will not show key moments, so there is little for any tool to read.
  • Very short Shorts with tiny view counts. The same view limits apply; focus on the first two to three seconds instead.
  • If you will not change the videos. A report that nobody acts on is just another email.

FAQ

Is there a tool that emails me where viewers left each new YouTube video?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor emails a drop-off report with timestamps and fixes about 48 hours after each upload. YouTube Studio shows the same underlying data free if you check it yourself.

How long after uploading should I check retention?

Give it at least a day or two. Early viewers are often subscribers, so the curve can shift as new viewers arrive from search and suggestions.

Why doesn't YouTube show key moments on my video?

YouTube only highlights key moments when a video is at least 60 seconds long and has at least 100 views.

Can a retention tool change anything on my channel?

A tool that only asks for read-only access can view analytics but cannot upload, edit or delete videos. Check the permissions screen when you sign in.

What is the most important retention number to watch?

For most videos, the share still watching at 30 seconds, then the single biggest dip. Fix those before worrying about the ending.
