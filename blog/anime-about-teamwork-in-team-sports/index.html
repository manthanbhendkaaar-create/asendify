Which anime are about teamwork in team sports?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The best-known anime about teamwork in team sports are Haikyu!! (volleyball), Kuroko's Basketball and Slam Dunk (both basketball). Each follows a talented but incomplete player who only gets good once he learns to play for, and with, his team. For a deliberate contrast, Blue Lock (football) asks the opposite question: what happens when a striker is told to put his own goals first?

Key takeaways

  • Haikyu!! (Production I.G, 2014, 25 episodes in season 1) follows short Shoyo Hinata, who must learn "what it means to be part of a team" alongside his rival-turned-teammate Tobio Kageyama.
  • Kuroko's Basketball (Production I.G, 2012, 25 episodes in season 1) pairs Tetsuya Kuroko, a player with almost no presence, as the "shadow" to Taiga Kagami's "light".
  • Slam Dunk (Toei Animation, 1993-1996, 101 episodes), from Takehiko Inoue's manga, follows hot-tempered Hanamichi Sakuragi, who joins the Shohoku High team for the wrong reasons and is drawn into the game.
  • Blue Lock (8bit, 2022-2023, 24 episodes) puts 300 under-18 strikers into a contest to produce one star striker for Japan.
  • If you want one show, start with Haikyu!!: it spends the most time on how a team actually learns to work together.

Haikyu!!: learning that you can't win alone

Hinata is small for volleyball and desperate to play. At Karasuno High, a once-strong team that has declined, he finds that Kageyama, the prodigy setter who beat his middle-school team, is now his teammate. Their early clashes are the show's engine. The teamwork theme is concrete: Hinata's speed is useless without Kageyama's sets, and Kageyama's skill is wasted if nobody trusts him. Supporting characters, from the libero to the bench players, get their own arcs, which shows that a team is more than its two stars.

Why it works for teamwork: matches are broken down point by point, so you see communication, rotation and trust in action.

Kuroko's Basketball: the value of the "shadow"

Kuroko was the "Phantom Sixth Man" of a famous middle-school team whose five prodigies, the "Generation of Miracles", drifted apart. At Seirin High he lacks presence and standout athletic talent, so he plays as the "shadow" to Kagami's "light". The show is more stylised than Haikyu!!, with near-superhuman abilities, but its central idea is about roles: the player who makes everyone else better is easy to overlook and hard to replace.

Why it works for teamwork: it argues that a collection of talented individuals who stop trusting each other can lose to a team that plays together.

Slam Dunk: the classic

Slam Dunk is older (1990s) and longer at 101 episodes, and it remains a reference point for sports anime. Sakuragi joins the basketball team mainly to impress a girl, knows almost nothing about the game, and has to learn basic skills like rebounding while his teammates carry the team. The comedy is broad, but the slow shift from showing off to playing a role is the payoff.

Why it works for teamwork: it shows the unglamorous side of being useful to a team, such as practising fundamentals and accepting a role.

Blue Lock: the counterpoint

Blue Lock opens with Yoichi Isagi missing a chance to reach nationals because he passed instead of shooting. He is then invited into a project that ranks 300 under-18 strikers, aiming to create Japan's star striker, and he finds himself competing "even at the expense of the other 299". It is useful precisely because it challenges the other three: watching it next to Haikyu!! makes you think about when individual ambition helps a team and when it hurts.

Is Haikyu!! good for people who don't like volleyball?

Many viewers come to it with no interest in volleyball; the character relationships carry it, and the rules are explained as you go.

Which is more realistic, Haikyu!! or Kuroko's Basketball?

Haikyu!! is generally more grounded; Kuroko's Basketball uses stylised, near-superhuman abilities.

Is Slam Dunk too old to enjoy now?

It is from the 1990s and looks it, but its humour and character growth still hold up for many viewers.

Is Blue Lock about teamwork?

Mostly the opposite: it focuses on individual ambition among strikers, which makes it a useful contrast.
