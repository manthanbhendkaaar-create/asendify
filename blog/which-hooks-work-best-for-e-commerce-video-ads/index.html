Which hooks work best for e-commerce video ads?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

The hooks that work best for e-commerce video ads show the product doing its job within the first one to three seconds: the product in use, the problem it solves, the result first, a common objection answered, or a surprising detail up close. No single hook wins for every product, so test three to five hooks on the same ad body and keep the one whose viewers watch furthest. Every hook must be true and supportable.

Key takeaways

  • Show the product early. Facebook's video ad tips, reported by Search Engine Journal, say the product "should be the main focus of the ad" and branding "should appear near the start".
  • TikTok advises introducing your proposition "in the first 3 seconds" and recommends 3 to 5 creatives per ad group.
  • Design for sound off: ads "should be able to be viewed, understood, and enjoyed without sound", according to the same Facebook tips.
  • Claims in hooks must be provable. The UK CAP Code says marketers "must hold documentary evidence to prove claims that consumers are likely to regard as objective".
  • Hook type matters less than fit: the right hook depends on whether the product's value is visual, practical or emotional.

How to choose the right hook for your product

Ask what makes someone buy it.

  • If the benefit is visible, lead with product in use or result first. Facebook's tips note that "movement or fast edits at the start of an ad can capture consumers' attention", and a product in motion does this naturally.
  • If the benefit solves a frustration, lead with the problem. Keep it short; one or two seconds of the frustration, then the fix.
  • If people hesitate because of price or doubt, lead with the objection and answer it straight away.
  • If the product is already known, for example in retargeting, an offer hook can work, provided it is accurate. The CAP Code bars calling something "free" if the buyer pays anything beyond unavoidable delivery or response costs.

After the hook: keeping them watching

The hook only buys a few seconds. To hold attention after it:

  1. Deliver the promise quickly. If the hook asks "does it fit?", show it fitting within the next few seconds.
  2. Add a second beat. A second benefit, a close-up of a detail, or a quick demonstration from another angle.
  3. Say less. Facebook's tips advise that ads "should be succinct in their messaging". One message per ad.
  4. Use text on screen. TikTok recommends "5-10 words per second" when using text. Keep each line short.
  5. End with a clear next step. "Shop now", the product name and the price if you include one.

Common mistakes

  • Opening on the logo or a slow lifestyle shot with no product.
  • Using the same hook for months until it stops working.
  • Copying a trend that has nothing to do with the product.
  • Making claims ("the best", "clinically proven") you can't support.
  • Results-first hooks that show untypical results.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks for short product videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. TikTok's and Google's free creative guides are alternatives.

How many hooks should I test?

TikTok recommends 3 to 5 creatives per ad group; testing 3 to 5 hooks on the same body is a practical start.

Should the product appear in the first second?

Usually within the first few seconds. Facebook's tips put the product at the centre and branding near the start.

Do UGC-style hooks work for e-commerce?

They can, if the creator is genuine and disclosed where required, and the claims are true.

How often should I change hooks?

When performance drops or frequency rises. Keep a pipeline of new hooks in the styles that have worked.
