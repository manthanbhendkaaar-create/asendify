What are the FCA's leverage limits for retail CFD traders in the UK?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The FCA caps leverage for retail CFD and spread betting customers at between 30:1 and 2:1, depending on the underlying asset: 30:1 for major currency pairs, 20:1 for major indices, minor currency pairs and gold, 10:1 for other commodities and minor indices, and 5:1 for individual shares. Firms must also close your positions when your funds fall to 50% of the required margin, and you cannot lose more than the money in your account. Crypto CFDs cannot be sold to retail customers in the UK at all.

This page is education only, not financial advice. CFDs and spread bets are leveraged; most retail accounts lose money.

Key takeaways

  • The rules came from ESMA's temporary 2018 measures and were made permanent by the FCA from 1 August 2019.
  • Leverage is set through minimum initial margin: 3.33%, 5%, 10% or 20% of the position, depending on the asset.
  • Margin close-out at 50% and negative balance protection apply to every retail account.
  • Firms must show the percentage of their retail accounts that lose money and cannot offer bonuses or other inducements.
  • Professional clients can get higher leverage but lose these protections.

The limits by asset

The FCA's policy statement PS19/18 set the rules out in its handbook (COBS 22.5). Firms must collect at least these initial margins from retail clients:

Underlying assetMinimum initial marginMaximum leverage
Major currency pairs; certain government bonds3.33%30:1
Major stock indices; minor currency pairs; gold5%20:1
Minor stock indices; commodities other than gold10%10:1
Individual shares and other assets20%5:1
Cryptocurrencies50% in PS19/182:1 (now banned for retail; see below)

PS19/18 also notes that the FCA set certain government bonds at 30:1, more generous than ESMA's 5:1.

The other protections

The FCA's press release confirming the permanent restrictions lists what firms must do for retail clients:

  • limit leverage to between 30:1 and 2:1
  • close out a customer's position when their funds fall to 50% of the margin needed to keep their positions open
  • guarantee a client cannot lose more than the total funds in their CFD account
  • stop offering monetary and non-monetary inducements to trade
  • show a standardised risk warning with the percentage of their retail accounts that make losses

The rules applied from 1 August 2019 for CFDs and 1 September 2019 for CFD-like options. The FCA's contract for differences page adds that the rules sit in COBS 22.5 of its handbook.

Crypto CFDs

Separately, the FCA banned the sale of crypto-derivatives to retail consumers from 6 January 2021, including CFDs on cryptoassets. So the 2:1 crypto limit in PS19/18 no longer matters for UK retail clients: those products cannot be sold to them. Our page on whether crypto trading is regulated in the UK covers the wider rules.

Worked example: what leverage means for your money

Example (illustrative): you have £2,000 in a CFD account.

TradeMargin rateMargin usedPosition sizeA 2% move against you costs
EUR/USD (major FX)3.33%£600£18,000£360
FTSE 100 (major index)5%£600£12,000£240
Oil (commodity)10%£600£6,000£120
A single share20%£600£3,000£60

The same £600 of margin controls very different exposures. On the currency trade, a 2% move costs 18% of the whole account. If losses push your equity below 50% of the margin needed for your open positions, the firm must start closing them. These are our sums to show how the rules work; they ignore spreads and financing costs.

This is why position size matters more than the leverage limit. A trader who risks 1% of the account per trade with a stop loss is using far less real leverage than the maximum. See what trading expectancy is and how to calculate it and why most CFD traders lose money.

Professional client status

Firms can treat you as a professional client if you meet tests on trading activity, portfolio size and experience. Professional clients can get higher leverage, but lose the retail protections above, including negative balance protection. Be cautious of firms that encourage you to opt up mainly to use more leverage.

Common mistakes

  • Treating maximum leverage as a target. It is a ceiling, not a recommendation.
  • Forgetting spreads and overnight financing. They add up on leveraged positions.
  • Assuming offshore brokers give the same protection. Firms not authorised by the FCA may not apply these rules.
  • Ignoring the loss percentage in the risk warning. It tells you how most customers fare with that firm.

Questions to ask a CFD provider before you open an account

  1. Is the firm on the FCA Firm Checker, and do the contact details match?
  2. What percentage of its retail accounts lose money, as shown in its risk warning?
  3. What margin does it require on the markets you want to trade?
  4. What are the spreads, commissions and overnight financing charges?
  5. How does it apply the 50% margin close-out?
  6. Will you be classed as a retail or professional client?

Written answers to these questions tell you more about your real costs and risks than the headline leverage figure.

What is the maximum leverage for forex in the UK?

30:1 on major currency pairs and 20:1 on minor pairs for retail clients.

What is the maximum leverage on shares CFDs?

5:1, through a 20% minimum initial margin.

Do the limits apply to spread betting?

Yes. The FCA's CFD rules cover spread bets and CFD-like options sold to retail clients.

Can I trade crypto CFDs in the UK?

Not as a retail client. The FCA banned crypto-derivatives for retail consumers from 6 January 2021.

What is the 50% margin close-out rule?

Firms must start closing your positions when your account funds fall to 50% of the margin needed to keep them open.
