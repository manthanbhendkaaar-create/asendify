How do I make a neighbourhood guide video for real estate that people watch?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open on the place locals would name first (the high street, the park, the station, the view) and say in one line why people move there. Then organise the video as a day in the area (morning commute, coffee, schools run, lunch, parks, evening) rather than a list of addresses, put practical facts on screen with their sources, and keep the main guide to roughly three to six minutes with chapters, plus short clips for social. Describe places and amenities, never the kind of people who live there.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia's 2026 data says engagement "naturally declines after the five-minute mark" and most viewers leave around the halfway point, so put the strongest content early and give the rest chapters.
  • Nielsen Norman Group's video guidelines recommend chapters or timestamps for long videos so people can jump to what they need.
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "get to the heart of the story faster" and "feature people to help you tell a better product story".
  • In the US, HUD says discriminatory housing advertisements are illegal under the Fair Housing Act, including ads that signal a preference; similar equality rules apply elsewhere.
  • Facts (commute times, school inspection reports, council tax bands) belong on screen with a source.

Morning0:20 to 1:15Station or main roads, commute time to the city with source
Daytime1:15 to 2:15Coffee, shops, gyms, GP and pharmacy

Outdoors3:00 to 3:45Parks, walks, river

Saying up front what's coming ("schools at 2:15, homes at the end") keeps people who want one section watching until it arrives.

Describe places, not people

Steer around phrases that describe the residents rather than the area: "perfect for young professionals", "family neighbourhood", "exclusive", "quiet, no students" or references to religion, ethnicity or nationality. HUD's examples of illegal preference statements include "no children" and "no wheelchairs". Describe what is there instead: "five primary schools within a mile", "step-free access at the station", "parks with playgrounds". Rules differ by country, so check what applies to you.

Get the facts right

FactWhere it should come from
Commute timesA journey planner, at a stated time of day
SchoolsOfficial inspection reports (in England, Ofsted) linked, not summarised as "the best"
CrimeOfficial police data, if you mention it at all, with context
Council tax or property taxesThe local authority
PricesOfficial house price data or your own sales, labelled

Filming tips

  1. Walk, don't drive, for the key places; a gimbal keeps it smooth.
  2. Film at the right time of day: the café busy in the morning, the restaurant lit at night.
  3. Ask permission before filming inside businesses, and avoid close-ups of members of the public.
  4. Natural sound of the market or park, with captions for anything you say.
  5. Cut short clips of each chapter for Reels and Shorts, each ending with a link to the full guide.

Worked example

Example: an agent makes a guide to a riverside suburb.

  • 0:00 Wide shot of the river at sunrise: "Why people leave the city for here, in five minutes."
  • 0:20 Station platform: "31 minutes to the centre at 8 am" (journey planner, labelled).
  • 1:15 Inside the independent bakery and the Saturday market.
  • 2:15 Outside two primary schools, with "Inspection reports: link below".
  • 3:00 Towpath walk and the park's new playground.
  • 3:45 High street at night.
  • 4:30 Typical Victorian terraces and new-build flats; "See what's for sale here" on screen.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to structure longer videos so people keep watching?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Nielsen Norman Group's video guidelines are free.

How long should a neighbourhood guide video be?

Around three to six minutes with chapters, plus short clips for social.

Can I say an area is good for families?

Describe amenities instead, such as schools and playgrounds. Statements about who should live somewhere can breach fair housing or equality rules.

Should I mention crime rates?

Only with official data and context, or leave them out and link to the official source.

What should a neighbourhood video end with?

A link to current listings in the area and one way to contact you.
