Is there a YouTube editing course for creators who edit their own videos?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 15 November 2026 · Last updated 15 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Accelerator is a self-paced course built for creators who edit their own videos (or work closely with an editor) and see viewers dropping off early. It teaches you to read your retention graph and re-edit your openings, structure and pacing, with an assignment on your own footage in every lesson. General software courses, YouTube's free help pages and free retention guides are fair alternatives, especially if your main gap is learning the editing software itself.

Key takeaways

  • Creators who edit their own videos usually need two different skills: using the software, and deciding what to cut and why.
  • Software courses teach the first. Retention-focused courses teach the second.
  • Your own YouTube retention graph is the best teacher: it shows exactly where your viewers leave.
  • Self-editing creators have an advantage: they can change the next video the same day they learn something.
  • No course can promise views or income; look for one that makes you practise on your own videos.

The self-editing creator's advantage

When someone else edits, every lesson has to be passed on as a brief. When you edit yourself, you can learn on Monday and apply it to Tuesday's upload. You also know your footage and your audience better than any outside editor.

The disadvantage is distance. After filming and editing for hours, it is hard to see your video as a stranger does. That is why data matters. YouTube's key moments for audience retention page explains how to read the graph: flat lines mean viewers watch a part start to finish, gradual declines mean fading interest, spikes mean viewers rewatching or sharing, and dips mean viewers leaving or skipping. The intro figure shows the share still watching at 30 seconds. Your graph is an outside view you can trust.

A self-editing workflow that uses data

  1. Before editing: open your last video's retention graph. Note the 30-second hold and the biggest dip.
  2. First pass: cut for story. Get to the promise of the title fast.
  3. Retention pass: look for anything like the moment that caused last time's dip (a recap, a slow set-up, a tangent) and cut or move it.
  4. Pacing pass: in long talking sections, add a visual change every so often (B-roll, text, a zoom or a new angle).
  5. Packaging check: make sure the first 30 seconds deliver what the thumbnail and title promise. YouTube's impressions and CTR FAQ warns that high click-through rate with low average view duration is a sign of clickbait.
  6. Two days after upload: check the new graph against the old one. YouTube says retention data takes 1 to 2 days to process.

Example: Tom makes 15-minute woodworking videos and edits them himself in DaVinci Resolve. He knows the software well. His 30-second hold is 52% and viewers drop steadily through long build sections.

How to choose a course if you edit your own videos

  • Check the audience. Does the course say it is for creators who edit, not only for freelance editors?
  • Check the exercises. Each lesson should ask you to change one of your own videos.
  • Check the scope. Hooks alone aren't enough; you need structure and pacing too.
  • Check access. Self-paced with instant access suits creators whose upload schedule changes week to week.
  • Check the claims. Avoid any course that promises views, subscribers or income.

Cancellation and digital access

Online courses with instant access are digital content. Which?'s guide to the Consumer Contracts Regulations explains that sellers must not supply digital content within the 14-day cancellation period unless you give express consent, and you must acknowledge that once the download starts you lose the right to cancel. Read a course's terms before buying.

FAQ

Is there a YouTube editing course for creators who edit their own videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Accelerator is built for creators who edit their own videos and lose viewers early, with an assignment on your own footage in every lesson. Software tutorials and free YouTube guides are alternatives.

Should I learn editing software or retention first?

Software first if you can't yet cut and export a video. Retention next, once you can.

Can I improve retention without a course?

Yes. Read your retention graph after every upload, find the biggest dip and fix its cause in the next edit.

How long until I see changes in my retention graph?

YouTube says retention data takes 1 to 2 days to process. Compare three or four new videos with your previous ones before judging.

Do I need expensive software?

No. Retention editing is about decisions, which work in any editor that can cut, add text and add B-roll.
