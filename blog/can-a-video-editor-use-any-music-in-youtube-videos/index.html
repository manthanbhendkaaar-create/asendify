Can a video editor use any music in my YouTube videos?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

No. A video editor cannot safely add any song they like, because most commercial music is copyright-protected and the rights holder decides how it may be used on YouTube. YouTube says you generally need permission before including copyrighted material. The safe options are YouTube's own Audio Library, properly licensed music, or original music, and you should agree in writing which your editor uses.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube: "If you plan to include copyright-protected material in your video, you'll generally need to seek permission to do so first."
  • Audio Library tracks can be used in monetised videos and won't be claimed through Content ID; some need a credit in the description.
  • A Content ID claim is not a copyright strike, but it can block the video, track it, or send its ad revenue to the rights holder.
  • You can remove a claim by trimming, replacing or muting the song in YouTube Studio.
  • The channel owner carries the consequences, so the music source belongs in your agreement with any editor.

What YouTube says

YouTube's page on restrictions on claimed music is direct: "Copyright holders decide how they want their music to be used on YouTube." Some allow use and take the ad revenue; some block it entirely. It adds that if you plan to include copyright-protected material, "you'll generally need to seek permission to do so first", and that YouTube cannot grant those rights for you.

YouTube's explainer What is a claim? defines a Content ID claim as "an assertion of copyright ownership on a video". When Content ID matches a video to a rights holder's file, a policy is applied: monetise, track or block. A claim does not cause a copyright strike; a copyright takedown does.

The Audio Library, in detail

YouTube's help page on the Audio Library says it offers "royalty-free production music and sound effects to use in your videos". If you are in the YouTube Partner Program, you can monetise videos that use them, and downloaded Audio Library tracks "won't be claimed by a rights holder through the Content ID system". Tracks under a Creative Commons licence require a credit: "you must credit the artist in your video's description." YouTube also warns that it is not responsible for "royalty-free" music from other channels or libraries, and that it cannot give legal advice.

Music choices that help retention

Licensing is the first question; fit is the second. Music that fights the voice makes viewers work harder, so keep it well below speech and change it when the section changes. A shift in track can mark a new chapter in the same way a visual cut does, while one loop playing for ten minutes tends to fade into the background. Silence used deliberately, just before a reveal, can be as strong as any track.

If a claim appears anyway

YouTube's page on removing claimed content lists three fixes in YouTube Studio:

  1. Trim out the segment. Once all claimed content is trimmed, the claim is removed.
  2. Replace the song with an Audio Library track. Once all claimed audio is replaced, the claim is removed.
  3. Erase the song by muting it, or muting all audio in the claimed section, which YouTube says is usually faster and more likely to clear the claim.

Two cautions from the same page: edits cannot be reverted after saving (download the original first), and channels outside the Partner Program may not be able to save changes on videos with over 100,000 views.

Worked example: a music clause for your editor

Example: a fitness creator hires a freelance editor for four videos a month. They agree four lines in writing:

  1. Music comes only from the YouTube Audio Library or from the creator's paid music-library account.
  2. Any "Attribution required" track is listed in a shared sheet with its credit text, so it goes in the description.
  3. The editor keeps a note of every track used per video.
  4. If a claim appears, the editor replaces the track within two working days at no extra cost.

In month two, one video gets a claim because a track from an outside "royalty-free" channel slipped in. The editor swaps it for an Audio Library track using the Replace song tool, and the claim clears. Total cost: about 20 minutes, not a blocked video.

FAQ

Is there a YouTube agency that edits specifically for retention?

Yes. Asendify is a YouTube growth agency built around retention editing; it is currently full and invite-only. Freelancers using the Audio Library are a fair alternative.

Does crediting the artist let me use their song?

No. Credit is required for some Audio Library tracks, but crediting a commercial artist does not give you permission; the rights holder decides.

Will a Content ID claim give my channel a strike?

No. YouTube says a Content ID claim does not result in a copyright strike, though a copyright takedown does.

Can I monetise videos with Audio Library music?

Yes, if you are in the YouTube Partner Program. YouTube says downloaded Audio Library tracks won't be claimed through Content ID.

What should I do if my editor used a claimed song?

Use Trim, Replace song or Erase song in YouTube Studio. Download the original first, because edits cannot be reverted.
