Is there an AI that compares my best and worst YouTube videos using my own analytics?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Asendify AI connects to your YouTube Analytics with read-only access and can compare your best and worst videos side by side, pointing to differences in click-through rate, the first 30 seconds and where viewers leave. It suits creators who want answers from their own data; YouTube's free Ask Studio, or ChatGPT with an exported spreadsheet, are fair alternatives.

Key takeaways

  • A best-versus-worst comparison is one of the fastest ways to learn what your audience wants, because it uses your own viewers as the test.
  • Compare videos of similar length and format. A Short and a 20-minute video are not a fair pair.
  • Look at three layers in order: packaging (click-through rate), opening (30-second hold), and body (where the curve drops).
  • An AI can do the sorting and spotting, but you still rewatch the moments it flags.
  • You can do the same comparison by hand in YouTube Studio for free.

Why compare best and worst videos?

Every channel has winners and losers. The gap between them holds the clearest lessons, because the creator, the audience and the style are the same. Only the video changed.

Generic advice is written for an average channel that does not exist. Your own best and worst videos show what your particular viewers reward and what they skip. That is why this comparison often teaches more than any list of tips.

YouTube's guide to how videos are discovered says its systems use average view duration and average percentage viewed as ranking signals, along with whether viewers pick or skip a recommended video. So a comparison should cover both whether people clicked and how long they stayed.

How to run the comparison yourself

  1. Pick fair pairs. Choose your three best and three worst long videos from the last six months, of roughly similar length. Use views per impression or average percentage viewed, not raw views, so older videos do not win just for being older.
  2. Line up packaging. Write down each video's impressions click-through rate. YouTube's impressions help page notes that half of channels and videos sit between 2% and 10%, and that videos shown a lot on the homepage tend to have lower rates. Check traffic sources before blaming the thumbnail.
  3. Line up openings. Note the Intro figure for each. If the best videos hold 60% and the worst hold 40%, the openings are where to look.
  4. Overlay the curves. Note where each curve drops sharply. Then rewatch 10 seconds before each drop.
  5. Write the rule. Turn the difference into one sentence you can apply next time, such as "Show the result in the first five seconds."

What AI adds, and what it does not

AI saves the slow part: pulling numbers for six videos, lining them up and spotting where curves split. It can also suggest likely reasons. What it cannot do is watch your video the way a viewer does. Treat its findings as a shortlist of timestamps to rewatch.

Watch out for false patterns. With only six videos, one odd result can look like a rule. Test the rule on your next two or three uploads before treating it as proven.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing across formats. A tutorial and a vlog are watched for different reasons. Keep pairs within one format.
  • Ignoring age. A video from three years ago grew in a different YouTube. Stick to recent uploads where you can.
  • Blaming the thumbnail first. If a video got most of its impressions from the homepage, a lower click-through rate is normal. Check the source mix before redesigning.
  • Changing everything after one comparison. Pick the single clearest difference and test it.

FAQ

How many videos should I compare?

Three best and three worst is enough to start. Make sure they are similar in length and format, and come from the same recent period.

Should I compare by views?

Not on their own. Older videos and videos that got lucky with one source can win on views. Use average percentage viewed or click-through rate alongside views.

Can I compare Shorts with long videos?

No. Shorts are watched and looped very differently. Compare Shorts with Shorts and long videos with long videos.

Is the comparison free in YouTube Studio?

Yes. All the metrics in this guide are in YouTube Studio at no cost. Tools simply save time lining them up.

What if my best and worst videos look the same?

Look at traffic sources. Sometimes the videos are similar and one simply reached a different audience, for example through Browse or Suggested rather than Search.
