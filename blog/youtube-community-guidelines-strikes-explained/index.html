How do YouTube Community Guidelines strikes work, and how long do they last?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

YouTube usually gives a warning for a first Community Guidelines violation. After that, each violation brings a strike: the first stops you uploading for 1 week, a second within 90 days stops you posting for 2 weeks, and three strikes in the same 90-day period may get the channel permanently removed. Each strike expires 90 days after it was issued, and you can appeal each one once.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help says the first violation is typically only a warning, which expires after 90 days if you complete an optional policy training.
  • A first strike blocks uploads, live streams, scheduling, Premieres, custom thumbnails and some playlist actions for 1 week; the strike stays on the channel for 90 days.
  • A second strike within the same 90 days blocks posting for 2 weeks; three strikes in 90 days may lead to permanent removal. Deleting the video does not remove a strike.
  • You may appeal each strike only once (YouTube Help).
  • To join or stay eligible for the YouTube Partner Program, you need no active Community Guidelines strikes; with strikes, you can apply after they expire (YouTube Help).

Community Guidelines strike vs copyright strike

They are different systems. Community Guidelines strikes are about content rules, such as harassment, violence or spam, as set out in YouTube's Community Guidelines. Copyright strikes come from rights holders' takedown requests. Both can lead to termination at three strikes, but they are counted separately and have different routes for resolving them.

What to do if you get a strike

  1. Read the notice carefully. YouTube tells you what was removed, which policy it broke and how it affects your channel.
  2. Decide whether it was a mistake. If you believe the decision was wrong, appeal. You only get one appeal per strike, so explain the context clearly.
  3. Do not re-upload the same content. That can lead to another strike.
  4. Pause risky uploads for 90 days while the strike is active; a second strike doubles the freeze.
  5. Check monetisation. If you are in the Partner Program, read any messages about eligibility.
  6. Brief your team. Editors, thumbnail designers and anyone scheduling uploads need to know the rules that were broken.

Working with editors and agencies

Strikes often come from small things a creator did not notice: a thumbnail that crosses a line, a title that reads as misleading, or a clip that breaks a policy. If you work with editors or an agency, agree in writing who checks content against YouTube's policies before upload, and give them channel access through permissions rather than your password so you can see who did what.

Why strikes matter for growth

A strike freezes uploads at the worst possible time for a channel's momentum: a missed upload week breaks audience habits, and scheduled videos are set to private. Repeated strikes also block Partner Program eligibility. For a growing channel, staying clean is as important as any editing trick.
