How often should a personal finance YouTube video re-hook the viewer?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Re-hook whenever the topic or step changes, which in most personal finance videos means roughly every one to two minutes, and tighten the gaps where your own retention graph dips. No research gives one magic interval, so treat the number as a starting point. A re-hook is a new reason to keep watching, such as a question, a number or a promise of the next step. This is education, not financial advice.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube says dips on the retention graph "mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video", so dips mark where a re-hook is missing (YouTube Help).
  • Spikes "appear when more viewers are watching, rewatching, or sharing those parts", so copy what sits just before and inside your spikes.
  • Mayer's segmenting principle says "people learn better when the information is presented in user-paced segments, rather than in one continuous unit" (Dartmouth), which is the reason to break money topics into steps.
  • Wistia reports that engagement "naturally declines after the five-minute mark" (Wistia), so longer finance videos need more deliberate re-hooks later on.
  • Any promised figure or result must be accurate; the FCA expects promotions to be "fair, clear and not misleading" (FCA FG24/1).

What counts as a re-hook

A re-hook is not a shout of "stay till the end". It is a small, honest reason to stay for the next segment.

Re-hook typeExample line in a finance videoWhen to use
Open question"But which of these costs you more over ten years?"Before a comparison
Next-step promise"Step three is where most people lose money to fees"Between steps in a how-to
Number tease"The gap between these two is one figure; I'll show it in a minute"Before a reveal
Visual changeSwitch from talking head to the spreadsheetWhen the topic shifts
Stakes reminder"This matters because it changes your monthly payment"After a dense definition

How to set your own interval

  1. Mark every topic change in the script. Each change is a re-hook slot.
  2. Count the minutes between slots. If any gap is over about two minutes, add a small hook or a visual change.
  3. Publish, then read the graph. Find the first sharp dip after the intro and check what was said just before it.
  4. Fix the dip, not the average. Move the interesting part earlier or cut the setup.
  5. Compare across your last few videos of similar length. YouTube's report lets you compare with videos of similar length, so use that rather than guessing.

Personal finance specifics

Money videos have a built-in problem: viewers want the answer, but the answer needs context, such as what an ISA or a pension actually is. Give the headline early, then re-hook into the "why" and the "how". Avoid hooks that hint at secret tricks or guaranteed results. They invite trust problems and, if you promote products, regulatory ones.

Keep re-hooks truthful. "Most people never check this fee" is a claim you should be able to support, so rephrase as "this fee is easy to miss" if you cannot.

Example: a 12-minute video on starting an ISA

Example: a creator plans "How do I start a Stocks and Shares ISA?" at 12 minutes. The script has five topic changes: what an ISA is, the yearly allowance, choosing a platform, choosing funds, and fees. The first draft has gaps of three to four minutes between visual or verbal resets. The creator adds a one-line re-hook before each section, switches to a screen recording for platform choice, and puts a comparison table on screen for fees. After upload, the retention graph shows a dip at the start of the fees section, so a later version moves the fee example earlier. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Re-hooking with the same line every time. "But wait" repeated becomes noise.
  • Teasing a payoff you do not deliver. Viewers leave and trust drops.
  • Ignoring the graph. The interval that works for your audience is in your own data.
  • Making money claims in hooks. Keep them factual and dated.

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's free retention report is the companion for reading results.

Is there a proven number of seconds between re-hooks?

No. Use topic changes as the guide and let your retention graph tell you where viewers leave.

Should every re-hook be a cliffhanger?

No. A visual change or a clear next step is often enough, and over-teasing can feel manipulative.

Do shorter finance videos need re-hooks?

Fewer, but yes. Even a 60-second video needs a reason to keep watching at the halfway point.

Can a re-hook promise a financial result?

Avoid it. Keep hooks factual and dated, and take advice if your video may count as a financial promotion.
