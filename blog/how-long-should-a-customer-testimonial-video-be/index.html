How long should a customer testimonial video be to keep viewers watching?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

For most uses, a customer testimonial video should be as short as the story allows: roughly 15 to 60 seconds for ads and social feeds, one to three minutes for a sales page or YouTube, and longer only for a full case study people choose to watch. No study sets an ideal length. What holds viewers is a clear before, a specific change and a believable after.

Key takeaways

  • Facebook's video research, summarised by Social Media Today, found "People overwhelmingly prefer shorter ads to longer ones", and advised not saving the punchline for the end on mobile.
  • Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014), studying 6.9 million video sessions, found "Shorter videos are much more engaging". Their setting was education, but the pattern is widely seen.
  • Google's ABCDs recommend featuring people and keeping the message simple to build connection, both of which testimonials do naturally.
  • The UK ASA's guidance on testimonials says testimonials must be genuine, used with permission, and must not be edited in misleading ways. Shortening is fine; changing the meaning is not.
  • Check where viewers leave with YouTube's key moments report or your ad platform's video play percentages.

Length by placement

PlacementTypical lengthWhy
Paid social feed ad (Meta, TikTok, Shorts ads)15-30 sCompetes with scrolling; one result, one line of proof
Organic social (Reels, LinkedIn)30-60 sRoom for a short before-and-after story

Full case study3-6 minFor buyers doing research; chapters help
Compilation reel (several customers)60-90 sShort clips, one point per person

These are working ranges from marketing practice, not research-backed limits. Test against your own data.

What actually decides length

Length should follow how much true, specific story the customer has. A strong testimonial has three parts:

  1. Before: the real problem, in the customer's words.
  2. Turning point: what they did or used.
  3. After: a specific, believable result.

If each part takes ten seconds, you have a 30-second testimonial. If the customer gives rich detail about a complex change, a two-minute version can hold viewers well. What loses people is padding: long introductions ("Hi, I'm... and I've been running my business for twelve years..."), repeated praise ("they were great, really great to work with"), and generic statements without specifics.

Where viewers usually leave a testimonial

MomentWhy viewers leaveFix
First 3 secondsLogo, name card or "Hi, I'm..." with no reason to stayOpen on the problem or the result line
After the result is statedThe payoff came, and nothing new followsEnd soon after, or add a second specific detail
Long background sectionCompany history no viewer asked forCut to one sentence
Repeated praiseViewers have heard the pointKeep the most specific version only
Slow endingThank-yous and a long logo stingEnd on the strongest line and a short call to action

In ads, the first seconds matter most, because Facebook's research found much of a mobile video's value is delivered early. On a sales page, the middle matters more, because viewers who press play are already curious and leave when the detail turns vague.

How to cut a long interview into several lengths

  1. Transcribe the interview and highlight every specific line: numbers, names of problems, concrete outcomes.
  2. Build the long version first (1-3 minutes) in before, turning point, after order.
  3. Cut a 30-60 second version using only the strongest line from each part.
  4. Cut a 15-second version for ads: the result line first, then one line of context, then the product.
  5. Check each version against the original to make sure no cut changes what the customer meant.
  6. Keep records of consent and the original footage, as the ASA expects.

Example: one interview, three lengths

Example: a bookkeeping firm films a 25-minute interview with a café owner who moved from spreadsheets to their service.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on editing testimonial and customer story videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. The ASA's free guidance covers the rules.

Can I shorten what a customer said?

Yes, as long as the edit doesn't change the meaning. The ASA says testimonials must not be edited in misleading ways.

Should a testimonial start with the customer's name?

Usually not. Start with the problem or result and show the name on screen.

Do I need written permission?

The ASA says testimonials shouldn't be used without consent, and you should keep evidence that they're genuine.

Is a compilation better than one story?

A compilation shows breadth; a single story builds more depth. Many brands use both.
