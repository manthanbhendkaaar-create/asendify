How do I make Pinterest video ads that people stop and watch?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open with thumb-stopping visuals that show your product and logo in the first one to two seconds, keep the ad to about 6 to 15 seconds, and tell the story in pictures and text because Pinterest video ads autoplay silently in the feed. Pinterest's specs allow 4 seconds to 15 minutes, but its own recommended length is 6 to 15 seconds.

Key takeaways

  • Pinterest video ads start playing as they come into view and play in the feed without audio.
  • Length: 4 seconds to 15 minutes allowed; 6 to 15 seconds recommended.
  • Square (1:1) or vertical (2:3, 4:5, 9:16) is recommended for standard video ads.
  • Pinterest: "Place your logo and ideally your product front and center in the first 1-2 seconds."
  • Plan the story to work with sound on or off.

How Pinterest plays video ads

Pinterest's help page on promoted video autoplay says videos start playing "when they come into view in a person's home feed", and standard video "plays in feed without audio". A tap opens a larger player with sound.

People on Pinterest are usually planning something: a room, a recipe, an outfit, a trip. They're searching and saving ideas. A video ad that looks like a useful idea fits that mindset; one that looks like a TV spot gets scrolled past.

An edit plan for a 12-second Pinterest video ad

SecondsShowText
0 to 2The finished result (the room, the dish, the outfit) with product and logo visibleThe idea in 3 to 5 words
2 to 7How it's done, in two or three quick stepsOne word or short line per step
7 to 10Close-up of the productProduct name
10 to 12Finished result againWhat to do next

Editing habits:

  1. Start on the result. Pinners are drawn to the outcome; show it first, then how.
  2. Make each step visual. Hands doing the task, product clearly in shot.
  3. Use text for the "how". Short overlays that make sense muted.
  4. Loop-friendly endings. Ending on the same result you opened with makes a replay feel natural.
  5. Pick vertical or square. Pinterest recommends these for standard video; they take more of the feed than 16:9.

Testing your Pinterest video ads

Make two or three versions that change only the first two seconds: one opening on the finished result, one on the product close-up, one on a person using it. Keep everything after the opening the same so you know what caused any difference. Run them in the same ad group with the same audience and budget, give them enough time and spend to compare fairly, then keep the strongest opening and test a new one against it. Because Pinterest is used for planning, also compare saves and clicks, not just views; a video people save may pay off later when they come back to buy.
