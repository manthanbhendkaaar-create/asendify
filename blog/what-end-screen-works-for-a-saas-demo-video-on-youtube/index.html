What end screen works for a SaaS demo video on YouTube?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use one clear next step. For a SaaS demo, that is usually a related demo or playlist element plus a spoken line that says what to do ("see how it connects to your CRM next"), with a link to your site only if your channel can use external-link elements. YouTube allows end screens in the last 5 to 20 seconds, so keep the close short and specific.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube says "End screens can be added to the last 5-20 seconds of a video" and allows "up to four elements" on standard 16:9 video (YouTube Help).
  • Element types are video or playlist, subscribe, channel and external website; the website option needs YouTube Partner Programme membership (YouTube Help). A SaaS channel outside the programme cannot rely on a website element, so the description link and the spoken line matter more.
  • Viewers "have the ability to hide end screens" (YouTube Help); say the next step aloud as well.
  • Wistia reports videos "under a minute average a 52% engagement rate" and that "engagement naturally declines after the five-minute mark" (Wistia). Most demos are longer than a minute, so the viewers who reach your end screen are your most engaged.

What a demo end screen is for

By the end of a product demo, a viewer is either interested or not. Those who reached the end have watched the whole thing, which makes them the best audience for a next step. The end screen should answer "what do I do now?" in one move, not offer a menu.

For SaaS, the possible next steps are: watch a deeper demo of one feature, see a use case close to theirs, start a trial or book a call on your site, or subscribe for updates. Pick one as the main step.

Step-by-step: build the end screen

  1. Decide the one action. Write it in a sentence: "After this demo, the viewer should watch the reporting demo."
  2. Choose the next video by user problem. A viewer who just saw setup likely wants integrations or reporting next.
  3. Plan 10 to 15 seconds in the last 5 to 20. Leave the screen static or slowly moving so elements are readable.
  4. Say it aloud. "To see how this reports to your team, watch the next demo." Specific beats "thanks for watching".
  5. Add the link in the description's first line. Put the trial or booking link there, with the spoken instruction "link below".
  6. Use at most two elements. One main video or playlist, one subscribe or channel element.
  7. Keep the product on screen. A calm last frame of the dashboard works better than a black card.
  8. Do not stack offers. A trial and a call and a newsletter dilute each other.
  9. Check eligibility. If you are outside the Partner Programme, do not plan around a website element.
  10. Test on a laptop and a phone. Both are common for business viewers.
  11. Review clicks and retention in the last 20 seconds and compare with other demos of similar length.

Example: a 6-minute invoicing demo

Example: a small software company posts a 6-minute demo of its invoicing app. The old end card says "Thanks for watching, like, subscribe" over a black frame with four elements. The new ending holds the final dashboard frame for 12 seconds while the presenter says "Next, see how invoices sync to accounting." One video element for the accounting demo sits in the main space; a subscribe element sits below. The first line of the description gives the trial link, and the voice line says "trial link is below". They compare the last 20 seconds of retention and clicks to two earlier demos. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result.

What to avoid

  • A menu of choices. Fewer choices make the next step easier to act on.
  • Promising the next video does not deliver. Match the line to the content.
  • Hiding the interface. Covering the product with elements wastes the last frame.
  • Old links. Check the trial link and pricing page are current.
  • Overclaiming. Do not promise results in the spoken line.

Fit with retention

Retention in a demo is about the viewer seeing their own problem solved. If the graph falls before the end screen, the issue is in the demo, not the close. YouTube's report separates dips (viewers abandoning or skipping at that part) from gradual declines (losing interest over time), which helps you tell a bad section from a slow demo.

Can I link to my site from the end screen?

An external website element needs YouTube Partner Programme membership; otherwise use the description and a spoken line.

How long should the end screen be?

Within the 5 to 20 second window; 10 to 15 seconds is usually enough.

Should I show a trial offer?

Yes if that is your one action, stated in the voice line and the description.

Which video should I link?

The demo that answers the most likely next question, not simply the newest.
