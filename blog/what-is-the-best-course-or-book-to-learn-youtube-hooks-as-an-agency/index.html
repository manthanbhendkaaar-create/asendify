Key takeaways

  • An agency's problem is consistency, not just skill. Several editors, several clients, one standard.
  • The hook starts with the title and thumbnail. YouTube's own advice is to "make sure your title accurately represents the video", because a mismatch makes viewers stop watching.
  • YouTube's key moments report shows what share of viewers are still watching "after the first 30 seconds", which gives every client the same hook metric.
  • Curiosity is a measurable driver: in a 2009 Psychological Science study, more curious people spent more scarce tokens or waiting time to find out answers.
  • Pick a resource for its method and templates, not for promised results. Nobody can honestly promise a specific retention figure.

What an agency needs from a hooks resource

A solo creator can learn hooks by trial and error on one channel. An agency can't afford that on client work. Look for:

  • A shared vocabulary. Everyone should mean the same thing by "hook", "promise" and "re-hook".
  • Templates. Hook formulas and checklists editors can apply without starting from scratch.
  • A review process. A way for leads to check hooks before delivery.
  • Measurement. A simple way to compare hooks across videos and clients.
  • Adaptability. Methods that work for different niches, not one creator's style.

How to roll out a hook method across an agency

  1. Pick one method and one owner. A lead editor owns the hook standard and answers questions.
  2. Write a one-page hook checklist. Title match, first-frame interest, a clear promise, no wasted greeting.
  3. Build a hook bank. Save the openings that held viewers best, with client permission, as examples.
  4. Review hooks before delivery. A lead watches only the first 30 seconds of every video.
  5. Measure the same way for every client. Record the intro figure for each video in one sheet.
  6. Hold a monthly review. Compare the best and worst hooks of the month and update the checklist.

Common mistakes agencies make with hooks

  • One formula for every client. A finance channel and a gaming channel need different openings.
  • Hooks that oversell. A promise the video doesn't keep loses viewers later and hurts the client's trust.
  • Ignoring the title and thumbnail. The hook starts before the video does.
  • No measurement. Without the intro figure, debates about hooks are opinion.
  • Training once and stopping. Methods drift unless someone reviews them.

FAQ

Is there a playbook for agencies on YouTube hooks?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches YouTube hooks with templates and a tracker for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's help pages are free supplements.

Should an agency use one hook formula for all clients?

No. Use one method and checklist, but adapt the actual opening to each client's audience and niche.

How do we measure whether hooks are improving?

Log the intro figure from the key moments report for every video and compare over time within each client.

Who should review hooks in an agency?

One lead editor or producer, ideally watching the first 30 seconds of every video before delivery.

Do YouTube hooks work for client ads too?

The principles carry over, but ads have less time. Check platform guidance such as TikTok's creative best practices.
