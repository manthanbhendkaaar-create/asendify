Why does my video ad get views but no clicks?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A video ad that gets views but no clicks is usually holding attention without giving a reason to act. The common causes are: the ad never states a clear offer, the call to action is missing or vague, the hook attracts curious people who aren't buyers, the ad is optimised for views rather than clicks, or the landing page doesn't match the ad. Check where viewers drop off, whether the offer appears before most of them leave, and what the campaign is optimising for.

Key takeaways

  • Google's ABCDs end with Direction: "Say what you'd like viewers to do," and reinforce the on-screen CTA with voiceover.
  • Campaigns optimised for views or ThruPlays find watchers, not necessarily clickers.
  • If the offer comes after most viewers leave, few ever see it. Check "played to" or plays-at-percentage metrics.
  • A hook that's interesting but unrelated to the product brings the wrong viewers.
  • If clicks happen but nothing sells, the problem is the landing page, not the ad.

1. Optimisation

If you optimise for ThruPlay on Meta, MarTech's report on ThruPlay explains Meta delivers to people likely to watch the whole video or at least 15 seconds. On YouTube, CPV bidding pays for views. Both work as intended when watching is the goal, but they don't hunt for clickers. If you want traffic or sales, choose that objective.

2. Timing of the offer

Use the drop-off data. Google's view metrics guide shows the share of viewers who reach 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of your ad; TikTok's video play metrics report 2-second and 6-second views and plays at each quarter. If only a small share reach 75% and the offer appears at 80%, most viewers never see it. Show the offer, or at least the product and benefit, early. How to make the first 3 seconds of a video ad stop people scrolling covers putting the value up front.

3. The call to action

Google's ABCDs are blunt: "Be intentional and add a CTA to drive a specific objective. Say what you'd like viewers to do," and "Reinforce your onscreen CTA with voice-over to ensure that the next step is clear." "Learn more" is weaker than a concrete step: "Get your free quote in 2 minutes", "See this week's menu". Note that Google's Shorts ads guide says the CTA button appears 3 seconds into Performance Max ads and 10 seconds into video view and Video Reach campaigns, so don't rely on the button alone.

4. Hook-product mismatch

A surprising stunt that has nothing to do with your product can win views from people who'll never buy. Make the hook about the buyer's problem or the result they want. You may get fewer views and more clicks, which is the trade you want.

5. Landing page mismatch

If people click and then leave, look at the page. It should repeat the ad's promise in its headline, show the same product and make the offer easy to take.

Worked example

Illustrative figures for a 30-second skincare ad optimised for ThruPlay:

Changes: switch the objective to traffic, move "20% off your first order" (a real offer) to second 6 as on-screen text, end with "Tap Shop Now to see your routine" spoken and written, and match the landing page headline to the ad. The next step is to measure clicks per impression again, not ThruPlays.

To see where people leave, work out your hook rate and hold rate.

A quick audit you can do today

If an ad is getting views but no clicks, work through this list in order. It takes about 15 minutes per ad.

CheckHowPass if...
ObjectiveOpen campaign settingsIt matches what you want (traffic, leads or sales), not views

Call to actionWatch the last 5 seconds mutedA specific action is written on screen
Spoken call to actionWatch with soundThe action is also said aloud

Mobile experienceOpen the page on a phoneIt loads fast and the next step is obvious

Look at who's watching. Check the age, location and placement breakdowns. Views from an audience or placement that rarely buys can inflate view counts without adding clicks. Narrow to the segments that do click.

Separate curiosity from intent. Hooks built on pure curiosity ("You won't believe what happened") attract people who want the payoff, not the product. Hooks built on the buyer's problem ("Still paying for software you don't use?") attract fewer, better viewers.

Make the next step small. "Buy now" is a big step for someone who has known your brand for 20 seconds. A smaller step, such as "see the menu", "get a free quote" or "take the 2-minute quiz", often gets more clicks, and the landing page does the rest.

Test one change at a time. Change the objective, or the offer timing, or the call to action, not all three at once, so you know what made the difference. See how to A/B test video ad hooks.

Use watchers anyway. Even if a views campaign didn't produce clicks, the people who watched most of the ad have shown interest. Retarget them with a conversion-focused ad; see what a ThruPlay is on Meta ads.
