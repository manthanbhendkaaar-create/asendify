How many words should a YouTube script be per minute of video?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Most YouTube scripts work out at roughly 130-170 spoken words per minute, so a 10-minute talking-head video usually needs about 1,300-1,700 words. The right figure depends on your natural pace, how many demos or visuals carry the video without speech, and how much you tighten in the edit. Script length matters less for retention than whether every line moves the viewer forward.

Key takeaways

  • In a study of 6.9 million edX video sessions, Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014) measured speaking rates from 48 to 254 words per minute, with a mean of 156. Viewers "generally engaged more with videos where instructors spoke faster", but the authors said speed mostly reflected enthusiasm, not a target to force.
  • VirtualSpeech, citing the National Center for Voice and Speech, puts the average US English conversation rate at about 150 wpm.
  • Wistia's 2025 analysis found many viewers drop off around the halfway point of a video, so a longer script needs more reasons to keep watching, not just more words.
  • If you put the script on screen as text, TikTok's guidance of 5-10 words per second is an upper limit for reading speed.
  • YouTube's audience retention report shows where viewers leave, which is the real test of whether a script is too long.

These are arithmetic estimates for continuous speech. Videos with demonstrations, B-roll without voice-over or long screen recordings need fewer words for the same runtime.

How to find your own words per minute

  1. Pick 300 words of a past script that you have already recorded.
  2. Time that section in the finished video, after editing.
  3. Divide words by minutes. 300 words in 2 minutes 6 seconds is about 143 wpm.
  4. Use that figure for planning, not a generic number. Your edited pace already includes the pauses you cut.

Writing tips that help retention more than word count

  • Read it aloud before filming. Lines that trip you up will trip viewers up too.
  • Mark every point that answers the title. If a paragraph does not, cut or move it.
  • Use short sentences. They are easier to say naturally and easier to follow.
  • Leave space for visuals. Note where a shot, chart or demo will say it better than words.
  • Script the first 30 seconds word for word, even if you improvise the rest. YouTube's intro figure shows the share of viewers still watching after 30 seconds.

Script length for ads, Shorts and podcasts

The same arithmetic works for other formats, but each has its own constraint:

FormatMain constraintPractical script length
15-second adSkippable ads can be skipped after five seconds30-40 spoken words, with the key message in the first sentence
30-second adBrand and offer need to land early60-80 words
60-second ShortViewers decide in the first seconds120-160 words, often fewer with visuals
Podcast clip (2 min)Taken from unscripted speechCut to the strongest 250-300 words
20-minute explainerMid-video drop-off2,600-3,400 words, split into clear sections

For ads, Google's video ad formats page confirms that skippable in-stream ads can be skipped after five seconds, so the first sentence carries most of the weight. For long videos, check whether your words-per-minute figure changes between sections: many creators speak faster in familiar parts and slower in technical ones, and the edit evens this out.

A final check before filming is to read the whole script aloud with a timer. If it runs well over your target, cut whole points rather than speaking faster; viewers notice rushed delivery more than a slightly shorter video.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on scripting YouTube videos for retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and open loops with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's analytics help pages are a free companion.

How many words is a 10-minute YouTube video?

Usually about 1,300-1,700 spoken words, depending on your pace and how much of the video runs without voice-over.

Is speaking faster better for retention?

Not by itself. The edX study linked faster speech with engagement but put it down mainly to enthusiasm. Speak at a natural, energetic pace and cut dead air.

How long should a Short script be?

Around 130-170 words for a full 60 seconds of speech, and fewer if visuals carry part of it.

Should I script every word?

Script the opening word for word. For the rest, a tight outline works for many creators, as long as each point is planned.
