How do I work out a supercar's real cost per mile?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Add up everything the car costs you in a year, split into fixed costs (depreciation, insurance, tax, servicing, storage) and per-mile costs (fuel and tyres), then divide by the miles you drive. Because a supercar's fixed costs are high, low mileage makes each mile very expensive: the same car can cost several pounds a mile at 2,000 miles a year and far less per mile at 8,000. For comparison, HMRC's approved rate for business mileage in an ordinary car is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 miles in 2026 to 2027.

This page gives planning estimates, not financial advice. Use your own quotes.

Key takeaways

  • Cost per mile = (fixed yearly costs + per-mile costs × miles) ÷ miles.
  • Depreciation and insurance usually dominate; fuel is often a small share at low mileage.
  • Check your real mileage from your current car's MOT history rather than guessing.
  • HMRC's approved mileage rate rose to 55p for the first 10,000 business miles in 2026 to 2027 (it was 45p from 2011 to 2026).
  • People in England travel a few thousand miles a year by car on average, so many owners overestimate how far they will drive a weekend car.

The formula

Fixed yearlyDepreciation, insurance, vehicle tax, servicing, storage, breakdown cover£ per year
Per mileFuel, tyres, mileage-related wear£ per mile
Cost per mile(Fixed + per mile × miles) ÷ miles

To estimate fuel per mile: fuel price per litre × 4.546 (litres per gallon) ÷ miles per gallon. At £1.50 a litre and 20 mpg, that is about 34p a mile.

Finding your real mileage

GOV.UK's MOT history service shows the mileage recorded at each MOT test for any vehicle with an MOT history. Subtract one year's reading from the next to see how far you really drive.

For context, the Department for Transport's National Travel Survey 2025 reports that, in England, males travelled about 4,640 miles and females about 3,885 miles a year by car (as driver or passenger) on average. A second car used mainly at weekends will often do far fewer miles than a daily car.

HMRC's rate as a benchmark

HMRC's travel mileage and fuel rates set the approved mileage allowance for business journeys in your own car or van: 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles in 2026 to 2027 and 25p after that. From 2011 to 2026 it was 45p. It is designed to cover the running costs of a typical car, not a supercar, so it is a useful reminder of how much more a supercar costs per mile. It is not a measure of what your car costs.

Comparing two cars properly

  1. Use the same mileage for both.
  2. Get real insurance quotes for each car and your address.
  3. Estimate depreciation from the price of the same model one and three years older.
  4. Use real fuel economy, not the brochure figure.
  5. Add tyres: performance tyres can be expensive and wear quickly.
  6. Include storage if you need a garage.

A cheaper-to-buy car can cost more per year if it depreciates faster or costs more to insure. Our page on the cheapest supercar to buy and run shows this effect.

Common mistakes

  • Counting fuel only. At low mileage, fuel is a small part of the cost.
  • Using the brochure mpg.
  • Assuming you will drive more than you do. Check your MOT history.
  • Leaving out depreciation because it is not a bill. It is the biggest cost for most cars.

Why mileage is the lever most buyers forget

Most buyers compare purchase prices and maybe insurance quotes. Very few check how far they will really drive. Because fixed costs are spread over fewer miles, an owner doing 2,000 miles a year pays far more per mile than one doing 8,000, even in the same car. Checking last year's MOT readings takes two minutes and makes every other comparison more accurate.

FAQ

Is there a planner that ranks supercars by yearly cost of ownership at your own mileage?

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner ranks cars by yearly cost at the mileage you enter. A spreadsheet is the free alternative.

How do I calculate cost per mile?

Add yearly fixed costs to per-mile costs times miles, then divide by miles.

What is HMRC's mileage rate for 2026 to 2027?

55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p after that, for cars and vans.

How many miles does the average person drive?

DfT figures for England in 2025 show about 4,640 miles for males and 3,885 for females by car, as driver or passenger.

Is fuel the biggest cost of a supercar?

Usually not. Depreciation and insurance are typically larger, especially at low mileage.

How can I find out how far I really drive?

Check the mileage readings on your current car's MOT history on GOV.UK.
