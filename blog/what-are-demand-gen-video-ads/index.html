What are Google Demand Gen video ads, and how should I edit for them?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Demand Gen is a Google Ads campaign type that runs your video and image ads across YouTube (including Shorts), Discover, Gmail and the Google Display Network, aimed at people who aren't searching for you yet. Because one campaign can show your video in a Shorts feed, a YouTube in-stream slot or a feed card, the best approach is to supply several cuts (vertical, square and landscape) of one strong idea, each with its own fast opening and clear call to action.

Key takeaways

  • Google says Demand Gen "integrates video and image assets into a single campaign" across YouTube, Discover, Gmail and the Display Network.
  • Supported video orientations include landscape, portrait, square and vertical, and Google says more assets improve your chances of good performance.
  • In Shorts placements, the call-to-action button appears 3 seconds into Demand Gen ads.
  • Google reports that using sound (music, voiceover or both) in Shorts ads has been shown to increase conversions by over 20%.
  • You can limit delivery to specific YouTube placements, such as Shorts only, if your creative is built for vertical.

What Demand Gen is

Google's Demand Gen help page says these campaigns "capture engagement and action across YouTube (including Shorts), Discover, Gmail, Maps, and the Google Display Network", can reach people who aren't actively searching for your brand, and support video in landscape, portrait, square and vertical orientations. It advises using your highest-quality assets, repurposing top-performing assets from email or social, and notes that "You'll increase your chances of achieving optimal performance by including more assets."

Editing plan: one idea, three cuts

  1. Write one promise the ad will prove, such as "fix a squeaky door in 30 seconds".
  2. Shoot for the tallest frame. Keep the subject in the centre so you can crop to 1:1 and 16:9 later.
  3. Cut a vertical version first (15 to 30 seconds): product or result in the first second, sound on, the CTA hinted early because the button appears at 3 seconds.
  4. Cut a square version for feeds: the first frame must work as a still, because many people scroll past without sound.
  5. Cut a landscape version for in-stream: deliver the promise within five seconds, before viewers can skip.
  6. Write headlines to the specs. Google's video ad specs list headline and description lengths for each format; front-load the benefit.

How to judge a Demand Gen campaign

Look at results per asset and per placement, not just the campaign total. A vertical cut might win in Shorts while the landscape version does better in in-stream slots. For video assets, compare view rates and how long people watch alongside clicks and conversions: a version that gets clicks but loses viewers in the first seconds is usually over-promising in its opening. Change one thing at a time, such as the first two seconds, so you can see what caused any difference.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches retention editing for vertical ads?

The Retention Editing Playbook teaches hooks and pacing for YouTube and Shorts at £1,000 one-time. Google's ABCDs and Shorts guides are free.

Where do Demand Gen ads show?

On YouTube (including Shorts), Discover, Gmail and the Google Display Network, with Google video partners as an option.

What video sizes should I use for Demand Gen?

Supply vertical, square and landscape versions so each placement gets a fitting cut.

Can I run Demand Gen on Shorts only?

Google says you can limit delivery to specific YouTube placements, such as Shorts only.

How long should a Demand Gen video be?

For Shorts placements, Google recommends under 60 seconds, and 10 to 30 seconds for action-focused ads.
