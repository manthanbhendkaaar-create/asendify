How do I get more out of one video shoot for ads and social?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Plan the edits before you film. Write a shot list that covers several hooks (not just one), the product in use from close and wide, short testimonials or talking points, and plenty of B-roll, and frame everything so it works vertically and horizontally. Record clean audio and leave room for text. One well-planned shoot day can then supply a month of ads, Reels, Shorts and posts, plus hook variants for testing.

Key takeaways

  • The biggest gains come from filming many openings. Hooks are what you'll test and refresh most, so capture eight to ten of them on the day.
  • Shoot for vertical. Meta's Reels guidance says vertical content (9:16) helps ensure your reels are immersive, and it warns against reposting videos with other apps' watermarks, so export native versions for each platform.
  • Google's ABCDs recommend tight framing for Shorts and introducing the product early. Plan close-ups.
  • Plan music rights before you post: on TikTok, businesses are told to use the Commercial Music Library for organic posts, ads and branded content.
  • Get written consent from anyone who appears, covering ads as well as organic posts.

Framing for every placement

  1. Film 4K horizontal with the subject centred, so you can crop to 9:16 and 1:1 without losing heads or products, or
  2. Film a second camera vertically for the key talking-head shots.
  3. Leave headroom and space for captions and on-screen text inside safe areas.
  4. Record clean audio with a lav or shotgun mic; you'll reuse lines across many edits.
  5. Slate each hook ("Hook 3, take 2") so editors can find them quickly.

Example: a local gym's shoot day

Example: a gym books one day with a two-person crew. The night before, the marketer writes 10 hooks ("You don't need an hour a day", "Hate the gym? Same.", "What 3 months of 2 sessions a week looks like") and a shot list. On the day they film each hook with two trainers, 15 close-ups of equipment and hands, a member's 30-second story (with a signed consent form covering ads), and 25 B-roll clips. The editor builds four ad concepts with three hooks each for testing, 12 Reels from the talking points, and a 2-minute website video. When the winning ad starts to tire six weeks later, the unused hooks provide the refresh without another shoot. Details are illustrative.

Mistakes that waste a shoot

  • Filming one perfect script and no alternatives.
  • Only wide shots, so nothing works on a phone.
  • No consent forms, so testimonials can't be used in ads.
  • Popular music played on set, which ruins audio you might reuse.
  • No naming or slating, so the editor wastes hours searching.

FAQ

Is there a set of hook templates for planning a video shoot?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook includes hook templates and a 30-day plan. Writing hooks from your best past ads is the free alternative.

Should I film vertical or horizontal?

Both if you can: 4K horizontal with room to crop, plus a vertical camera for key shots.

How many hooks should I film?

Eight to ten gives enough for testing and later refreshes.

Do I need consent from people in the video?

Yes, ideally in writing, and covering paid ads as well as organic posts.

Can I use trending music in the ads I cut?

Not unless it's licensed for commercial use. On TikTok, businesses should use the Commercial Music Library.
