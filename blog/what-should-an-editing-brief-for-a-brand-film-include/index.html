What should an editing brief for a brand film include to get better retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A brand film brief that leads to better retention tells the editor six things: the one feeling or idea viewers should leave with, who they are, where the film will run (and so its length and format), which moment should open it, what the payoff is, and how the brand should appear without stalling the story. Add must-keep shots, legal or approval constraints, and the metric you'll judge it on, such as the share still watching at 30 seconds.

Key takeaways

  • One message per film. Facebook's video ad guidance, as reported by Search Engine Journal, says each video "should communicate a clear and single message".
  • Brand presence matters, but it doesn't have to be a logo card. Google's ABCDs say "introduce your brand or product from the start and maintain that presence", and to "humanise the story".
  • Length should follow placement. Wistia reports videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate and an 11% drop in engagement past 30 minutes, so a brief should name every cut-down needed.
  • Tell the editor how success will be measured, using the platform's own figures such as YouTube's audience retention intro figure.
  • A brief is not a script. Leave room for the editor to find the best opening in the footage.

Writing the opening and payoff lines

The two most valuable lines in a brand film brief are the opening and the payoff. Without them, editors often default to a slow build: logo, drone shot, music swell. With them, the editor can open on a human moment that raises a question and close on the answer.

Good examples:

  • Opening: "Start on Maya's hands shaking as she opens the first box from the factory."
  • Payoff: "End on the same hands, steady, packing order number 10,000."

If you don't know which shot should open, ask the editor to propose two options from the footage. Editors often find a stronger first moment than the script imagined.

Brand presence without losing viewers

Brand films sit between story and advertising. Google's ABCDs recommend showing the brand early and throughout, and "audio brand mentions" to reinforce visuals. That doesn't mean a five-second logo sting. In the brief, specify brand presence that lives inside the story: the product in use in the first scene, branded clothing or locations, the founder or a customer saying the name naturally, and a short end card. For whether to open with a title sequence at all, see should a brand film have an intro or title sequence.

Common brief mistakes

  • Five messages in one film.
  • "Make it cinematic" with no purpose or audience.
  • No cut-down plan, so the 15-second version is just the first 15 seconds of the long one.
  • Several stakeholders giving conflicting notes.
  • Approving claims late, forcing re-edits.

FAQ

Is there a playbook that teaches hooks and payoffs for brand films?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks, open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCDs are a free alternative.

How long should a brand film brief be?

One or two pages. Enough to set purpose, audience, opening, payoff and constraints.

Should the brief include a script?

Only if the film is scripted. For documentary-style films, give purpose and moments and let the editor shape the story.

Should the logo appear at the start?

Brand presence early helps, but it can be in the scene rather than a logo card that stalls the opening.

How many cut-downs should I ask for?

One per main placement, each with its own opening, rather than trimming the long version.
