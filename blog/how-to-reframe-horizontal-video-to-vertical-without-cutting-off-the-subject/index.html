How do I reframe a horizontal 16:9 video to vertical 9:16 without cutting off the subject?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

To turn a horizontal 16:9 video into a vertical 9:16 one without losing the subject, duplicate your edit into a vertical timeline, let an auto-reframe tool (Premiere Pro's Auto Reframe, DaVinci Resolve's Smart Reframe or Final Cut Pro's Smart Conform) track the main subject, then go shot by shot and fix what it gets wrong. A 9:16 crop of a 16:9 frame keeps only about a third of the width, so wide shots, two-person shots and on-screen text are where it breaks. Rebuild titles and captions for the new frame, keep everything important inside the platform's safe area, and re-cut the opening: a vertical feed needs a tighter, faster first second than the horizontal original.

Key takeaways

  • A full-height 9:16 crop from a 16:9 frame keeps roughly 32% of the width (9/16 x 9/16), so most of the picture goes.
  • Auto-reframe tools track faces and points of interest, but you still need to check every shot.
  • Work from the edit's timeline of individual clips, not an exported file, so the tool can reframe each shot separately.
  • Titles, lower thirds and captions must be redesigned; they don't survive a crop.
  • If you know a vertical version is needed, shoot with space around the subject or shoot vertical as well.

The figures are simple geometry. A 4K source helps, because you can crop in and still have enough pixels for a 1080 x 1920 export.

Larry Jordan notes that Resolve's Smart Reframe works on a timeline of individual clips, and that titles and graphics have to be redesigned afterwards.

Step by step

  1. Duplicate the finished timeline. Change only the duplicate's resolution to vertical (for example 1080 x 1920), so the horizontal master stays untouched.
  2. Choose "scale to fill" (crop) for mismatched clips, not "fit", or you'll get black bars.
  3. Run the auto-reframe tool on all clips.
  4. Review every shot at full speed. Look for: the subject drifting out of frame, the camera "chasing" movement, the wrong person picked in a two-shot, and hands or products cut off.
  5. Fix by hand. Reposition, add a keyframe or two, or zoom slightly. For a two-person conversation, cut between close-ups instead of trying to fit both.
  6. Replace shots that can't work. A wide landscape or a whiteboard won't survive. Swap in a closer shot or a graphic made for vertical.
  7. Rebuild text. Titles, lower thirds and captions in a vertical layout, larger and centred, inside the safe area.
  8. Re-cut the first 3 seconds. In a vertical feed, the first frame has to work as a phone-screen image. Start on the subject's face or the product, close up.
  9. Check on a phone with the platform's interface on top, before you export.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to re-cut openings for Shorts?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Editing software help guides are free alternatives.

Can I reframe without losing quality?

Cropping a 1080p frame to 9:16 leaves a narrow strip that gets enlarged. A 4K source keeps the vertical export sharper.

Does auto reframe work on two people talking?

Often badly. Cut between single close-ups instead.

Should I add blurred bars instead of cropping?

It keeps the whole frame, but the subject ends up small on a phone. Cropping and re-cutting usually looks more native.

Should I shoot vertical instead?

If the vertical version matters, yes, or at least frame with room around the subject so a crop works.
