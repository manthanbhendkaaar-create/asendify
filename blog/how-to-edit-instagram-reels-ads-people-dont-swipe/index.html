How do I edit Instagram and Facebook Reels ads so people don't swipe away?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Edit Reels ads as full-screen 9:16 videos that hook in the first moments, with key text inside the safe zone and the bottom 35% of the frame kept clear of text and logos. Unlike most feeds, Reels is a sound-on place: Meta says "over 75% of Reels views on Instagram are sound on," so a voice or sound hook earns its keep here, with captions as backup.

Key takeaways

  • Meta recommends "vertical video (9:16) with audio, in the safe zone" for Reels ads.
  • Meta's developer docs: "Keep the bottom 35% of your 9:16 creative free of text, logos, and other key elements."
  • Over 75% of Instagram Reels views are sound on, according to Meta.
  • Organic reels promoted as ads must be 9:16 and under 90 seconds, and free of third-party copyrighted music.
  • The swipe decision happens fast, so the opening must say what's in it for the viewer.

How a Reels ad is watched

A Reels ad sits between organic reels in a full-screen, vertical feed on Instagram and Facebook. One swipe moves the viewer on. There's no skip button and no countdown; the swipe is always available. That makes the first seconds the whole battle.

Meta's developer blog post on Reels ads frames it simply: "Reels is a full screen environment - take advantage of this expansive surface to tell your story." It also says to put key messages within the safe zone, which is "where you can invite your audience in with a 'hook' or land your CTA at the end of your ad."

The specs that change the edit

ElementMeta guidanceEditing move
Aspect ratioFull-screen 9:16Shoot vertical; don't letterbox a 16:9 ad
Bottom of frameKeep bottom 35% free of text, logos, key elementsCaptions and product sit above it
SoundOver 75% of Instagram Reels views are sound onUse voice or sound in the hook; caption too
Length (promoted organic reels)Under 90 secondsMost ads should be far shorter
Music in promoted reelsNo third-party copyrighted musicUse licensed or original audio

The 35% rule and the 90-second limit come from Meta's Reels ads developer documentation. The bottom of the screen is covered by the caption, account name and call-to-action button, so anything placed there may be hidden. The top and sides also carry interface icons, so keep the middle of the frame for faces, products and text.

An edit that holds past the swipe

SecondsWhat happensWhy
0 to 2A face or the product, mid-action, with a spoken lineNative-looking, sound-on hook
2 to 6The problem or the promise in plain wordsGives a reason to stay
6 to 15Proof: demo, before/after, a real customer momentEarns trust
15 to 20Simple next step, said and shownInside the safe zone

Habits that help:

  1. Look like a reel, not a TV ad. Native vertical footage, natural light and a person talking to camera fit the feed.
  2. Hook with sound and picture together. Because most people have sound on, a spoken line can open the ad, but the picture must still make sense muted.
  3. Caption every spoken line. Some viewers are muted, and the W3C notes captions serve people who are Deaf or hard of hearing and others who can't hear the audio.
  4. Cut every pause. Jump cuts on breaths keep the pace of the feed.
  5. Change something every few seconds. A new angle, a close-up or a text beat resets attention.
  6. Use licensed audio. Promoted reels can't contain third-party copyrighted music.

How to know it's working

Watch how many people get past the opening and how many reach the end. Meta's ThruPlay metric counts plays to completion or at least 15 seconds. Test two or three hooks on the same body, keep the winner and test again.
