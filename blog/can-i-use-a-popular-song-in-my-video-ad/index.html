Can I use a popular song in my own video ad?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Usually not. A trending song you can add to a personal post is not licensed for a paid ad or a business account, and using it can get the ad muted, rejected or taken down, or bring a copyright claim. For your own ads, use the platform's commercial library (TikTok's Commercial Music Library, for example), a royalty-free subscription, or a licence from the rights holder, and pick a track that helps the first seconds hold attention.

Key takeaways

  • Adobe's guidance: the same song "can be licensed for use on your personal video feed, but not licensed for use in a commercial project".
  • TikTok's general library, "including anything trending or viral, isn't cleared for commercial or promotional use", according to Adobe's summary.
  • TikTok's Commercial Music Library is a pre-cleared library of about 1 million tracks for business content, video ads and branded content on TikTok.
  • Meta offers free Meta Sound Collection tracks in some ad formats, but they don't cover every format.
  • Whether your use is commercial depends on who is using the music and why, not which account posted it.

Why trending sounds don't carry over to ads

Adobe's help page on personal versus commercial music use explains the split clearly. Platforms license music for people posting personal videos. A business promoting a product is a different use, and commercial use "requires music from a separate, usually more limited, licensed library". It adds that switching to a Business or Creator account can restrict which library you can use, even before you spend anything on ads.

What each platform offers

TikTok's help page About the Commercial Music Library describes a "pre-cleared global music library of 1 million songs" from independent artists and music houses, free for businesses, for organic business content, video ads and branded content. It says businesses shouldn't use the general music library for commercial activity, and that using other users' original sounds needs a proper licence check.

Meta announced in 2022 that it was adding free Meta Sound Collection songs to Reels carousel ads, with restrictions on which formats they apply to. Check Ads Manager for what's available in your format today.

Picking music that helps retention

Licensing is the first filter. The second is whether the track helps people keep watching:

ChoiceEffect on the first secondsUse when
Track starts on a beat or hookEnergy from frame oneProduct demos, fast cuts
Slow build introOften wasted secondsRarely in short ads; trim to the drop
Music under a voiceoverVoice carries the messageExplaining a benefit
No music, natural soundFeels native and personalFounder-to-camera, UGC style
Sound effect on the payoffMarks the key momentBefore/after reveals

Practical edits:

  1. Start the track at its strongest point. Cut the intro so the beat lands on your first frame.
  2. Cut on the beat. Shot changes timed to the rhythm feel faster without being rushed.
  3. Duck the music under speech. Voice should be clear on a phone speaker.
  4. Plan for sound off. Captions and on-screen text should still tell the story.
  5. Export a clean version. Keep stems separate so you can swap music if a platform rejects it.

Worked example

Example: a small coffee-equipment brand cuts a 20-second ad showing its grinder. The founder wants a trending pop song from TikTok.

OptionLicensingResult
Trending song from the general libraryNot cleared for adsRisk of muting or rejection; can't reuse on Meta
Similar-tempo track from TikTok's Commercial Music LibraryCleared for TikTok adsFine on TikTok only
Track from a royalty-free subscriptionLicensed for ads across platforms (check terms)One master edit for TikTok, Reels and YouTube

They choose the subscription track, start it on the beat drop at 0:00 with the grinder already running, duck it under a two-line voiceover, and cut each shot on the beat. The same edit runs on all three platforms. The scenario is illustrative.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming "it's on TikTok, so I can use it". Personal and commercial libraries are different.
  • Using a platform library track elsewhere. TikTok's commercial library is for TikTok.
  • Letting a slow intro eat the first three seconds. Trim to the hook.
  • Music drowning the voice. Test on a phone speaker.
  • No licence record. Save licence certificates with the project files.

Rules and libraries change and vary by country, so check the current terms before a campaign. This page is general information, not legal advice.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches pacing so viewers keep watching?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Platform creative guides are free alternatives.

Can I use TikTok trending sounds in ads?

Not from the general library. Use TikTok's Commercial Music Library for TikTok ads.

Can I use Commercial Music Library tracks on Instagram or YouTube?

TikTok describes it for TikTok business content and ads. For other platforms, license the music separately.

What happens if I use unlicensed music in an ad?

The ad can be muted, rejected or removed, and the rights holder can make a claim.

Is royalty-free music free?

Not always. Royalty-free usually means you pay once or subscribe, then don't pay per use. Check the licence covers paid ads.
