How do you post event videos on LinkedIn so people actually watch them?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Upload the video directly to LinkedIn rather than sharing a YouTube link, open on your strongest moment because LinkedIn video autoplays in the feed, and keep most event clips to roughly 30-90 seconds. Add captions, lead with a useful insight rather than "what a great event", and post several short cuts over a week instead of one long recap.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia's LinkedIn video guide says "native video uploads are the preferred route" and that "the first few seconds are crucial."
  • Feed videos can run from 3 seconds to 10 minutes, but about 30-90 seconds works best for most brands, according to the same guide.
  • LinkedIn supports 16:9, 1:1 and 9:16 feed video; square or vertical takes more screen space on mobile.
  • Captions are recommended for all formats; many people scroll with sound off at work.
  • LinkedIn audiences respond to professional value: an insight from a talk beats a montage of drinks and lanyards.

Upload natively and win the first seconds

Wistia's guide to LinkedIn video says native uploads are preferred and typically reach more people than shared links. Native videos autoplay in the feed, so "the first few seconds are crucial for capturing attention as people scroll." That rules out logo intros. Start on the speaker mid-sentence with the most useful or surprising line, and put that line on screen as text too.

Specs and length

The same guide lists feed video at 3 seconds to 10 minutes, MP4, in 16:9, 1:1 or 9:16, from 75 KB to 200 MB, and video ads at 3 seconds to 30 minutes. It suggests 30-90 seconds for most brand videos while noting longer formats such as testimonials can justify more time. For event content, that means cutting several short pieces rather than uploading the whole session.

Cvent's event video guide makes the same point for all channels: cut square or vertical versions for social and landscape for websites.

Captions and on-screen text

Wistia recommends captions for every LinkedIn format, supplied as an SRT file for feed video. The W3C's captions guidance explains captions provide the speech and non-speech audio needed to understand the content, for people who are Deaf or hard of hearing and for those who process text better than audio. Add a short headline at the top of the frame too, so the point lands even if someone watches only three seconds.

Write the post, not just the video

The text above the video decides whether people tap to watch with sound. Lead with the insight ("The CFO panel agreed on one thing: budgets for 2027 are being set in Q4, not Q1"), then say who said it, then tag the speaker and the event. Avoid opening with "What an amazing two days!", which tells the reader nothing.

Example: a B2B SaaS conference on LinkedIn

Example: a software company hosts a 400-person customer conference.

  • Day 1 evening: a 50-second square clip of the CEO's keynote opening on the line "We're removing three features next year, and here's why." Captions burnt in, headline at the top.
  • Day 2: a 40-second clip of a customer explaining how they cut onboarding time, with the customer's permission, tagged.
  • Day 4: a 70-second panel exchange where two speakers disagree about AI pricing.
  • Day 6: a 75-second highlights recap with a link to session recordings in the first comment.
  • Each post opens with the insight in text, not with thanks.

Four short, specific posts give the event a week of presence instead of one recap that gets a day.

How to tell what worked

LinkedIn shows views and engagement for each post on a company page. Compare clips against each other rather than against other brands: which opening lines held people, which topics got comments from the buyers you want, which speakers' clips travelled furthest when they reshared them. Cvent's guide suggests tracking completion, clicks, shares and conversions for every event video. After two or three events you will know which kind of clip deserves the most editing time, and which can be dropped. Use that to brief the crew for the next event: if practical tips outperform highlight montages, film more short interviews with speakers and less B-roll of the venue.

Common mistakes

  • Sharing a YouTube link instead of uploading.
  • Opening with a logo or a slow pan of the venue.
  • No captions.
  • Posting the full session as one video.
  • Writing "great event" posts with no insight.
  • Using attendee footage without permission.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches cold opens and pacing for short clips like these?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches cold opens and pacing for YouTube, which carries over to LinkedIn clips. Wistia's free LinkedIn video guide is a platform-specific alternative.

How long can a LinkedIn video be?

Feed videos can run from 3 seconds to 10 minutes, and video ads up to 30 minutes, per Wistia's guide. Most brand clips work best at about 30-90 seconds.

Should I upload to LinkedIn or share a YouTube link?

Upload natively. Native videos autoplay in the feed and typically reach more people than links.

Square or vertical for LinkedIn event clips?

Both are supported. Square is a safe choice for desktop and mobile; vertical fills more of a phone screen.

Do LinkedIn videos need captions?

Yes. Captions are recommended for every format, and many people watch at work with sound off.

Should I post event clips from a company page or a personal profile?

Both can work. Clips posted by speakers and staff from their own profiles often feel more personal; supply them with ready-made cuts.
