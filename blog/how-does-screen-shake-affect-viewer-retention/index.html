How does screen shake affect viewer retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A short, well-placed screen shake can act as a pattern interrupt and mark an important moment, which can help hold attention. Used constantly, it tires viewers, makes text hard to read and can trigger dizziness or nausea in people sensitive to motion, which pushes them to leave. Use it rarely, keep it brief and never combine it with rapid flashing.

Key takeaways

  • The W3C's accessibility guidance on motion notes that "some users experience distraction or nausea from animated content" and that vestibular reactions "include dizziness, nausea and headaches".
  • In a randomised trial with 50 participants, Litleskare and Calogiuri (2019) found cyber sickness symptoms were less severe with stabilised 360° footage than with footage that kept small camera oscillations, and three viewers of the unstable version stopped early.
  • Ofcom's guidance on flashing images says rapidly changing sequences are "provocative if they result in areas of the screen that flash", and limits flashes to no more than three a second over large areas.
  • No published study measures YouTube or ad retention against screen-shake use. Judge it with your own retention data.
  • A shake works best as a rare accent tied to something that happens: an impact, a reveal, a punchline.

Why constant motion pushes viewers away

The 360° study above was watched in a headset, which is far more immersive than a phone or laptop, so its numbers do not transfer directly to normal video. But the mechanism is the same one the W3C describes: motion that the body does not feel can cause discomfort for some people. On a small screen that is milder, yet it still adds effort. Viewers who are mildly uncomfortable or straining to read shaking text have an easy fix: they leave.

Shake also stops being a signal when it is everywhere. A pattern interrupt works because it breaks the pattern. If every cut shakes, nothing stands out.

How to use screen shake well

  1. Tie it to an event. An impact sound, a surprising number, a punchline or a hard cut to a new section.
  2. Keep it short. A fraction of a second is usually enough to register.
  3. Keep it small. A few pixels of movement reads as energy; large swings read as chaos.
  4. Ease out. Let the motion settle smoothly rather than stopping dead.
  5. Protect text. Do not shake frames where viewers need to read captions, numbers or code.
  6. Avoid flashes. If you combine shake with white flashes or strobing, check it against flashing-image guidance, and avoid more than three flashes a second.
  7. Watch the graph. Compare dips in YouTube's key moments report with the moments where you used effects.

Better alternatives for energy

If a section feels flat, these usually work with less risk: a tighter cut, a punch-in zoom, a change of shot, a sound effect, a graphic that answers a question, or removing the slow part entirely. Shake is a seasoning, not a structure.

FAQ

Is there a guide on using pattern interrupts and effects for retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers pattern interrupts, pacing and hooks with templates and a 30-day plan. Free editing tutorials cover the mechanics of effects.

Does screen shake increase watch time?

There is no published data showing it does. Used sparingly it can mark moments; used constantly it is more likely to tire viewers.

Can screen shake make viewers feel sick?

It can for people sensitive to motion. Accessibility guidance links motion to dizziness, nausea and headaches, so keep it brief and small.

Is screen shake allowed in ads?

Shake itself is generally fine, but flashing sequences face rules. In the UK, Ofcom's guidance for TV broadcasts, including TV ads, limits rapid flashing over large areas of the screen, and it is a sensible standard online too.

How long should a screen shake last?

Many editors use a fraction of a second. Long enough to notice, short enough not to distract.
