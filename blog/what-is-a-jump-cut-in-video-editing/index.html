What is a jump cut, and how do YouTubers use them?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A jump cut is an edit that removes part of a single continuous shot, so the person or action appears to jump forward in time while the camera angle stays roughly the same. In films it's often used to unsettle or speed things up; on YouTube it's the standard way to cut pauses, mistakes and filler out of talking-head videos. Used with purpose and varied with B-roll or zooms, jump cuts keep a video tight without feeling choppy.

Key takeaways

  • Columbia University's film glossary: "A jump cut is an editing technique in which some frames are taken out of a sequence."
  • Jean-Luc Godard's Breathless (1960) made the modern jump cut famous, breaking the "invisible" editing style of the time.
  • Adobe notes it's "common for makers of talking-head videos to use jump cuts to skip awkward pauses or mistakes."
  • Adobe's advice: "Every jump cut should have a clear narrative or emotional reason behind it."
  • Too many identical jump cuts can make a video feel choppy; break them up with B-roll, cutaways or framing changes.

Where the jump cut comes from

Most film editing tries to be invisible, so viewers don't notice the cuts. The jump cut does the opposite. Columbia University's Film Language Glossary says it's "typically used to draw the audience's attention to the conventions of filmmaking", using a scene from Godard's Breathless (1960) as its example.

StudioBinder's guide to jump cuts describes how, in an early car scene in Breathless, the camera stays on Jean Seberg's character while the edits skip unpredictably forward, deliberately disrupting the smooth editing style of mainstream cinema.

Why YouTubers use them

Adobe's guide to jump cuts explains the YouTube logic directly: talking-head creators use them to skip awkward pauses or mistakes. Videographer Nick Cann, quoted by Adobe, says "no one wants to watch that, so you can cut those parts out and speed up the video."

How to make a jump cut in Premiere Pro

Adobe gives a simple method:

  1. Put your clip on the timeline and turn on Snap. Lock audio if you need it to stay synced.
  2. Press C for the Razor tool and cut where the unwanted section starts, then where it ends.
  3. Select the gap and use Alt/Option + Delete to ripple delete it.
  4. Zoom in on the timeline to refine cuts.
  5. Watch it back to check the rhythm.

Ctrl/Cmd + K splits the clip at the playhead, which many editors find faster than the Razor.

How to stop jump cuts feeling choppy

TechniqueHow it helps
Vary the framingAlternate between a normal and a slightly zoomed-in frame, so cuts feel intentional
Cover cuts with B-rollShow what you're talking about while the cut happens underneath
Use sound to bridgeAdobe suggests a sound bridge or audio cue to make cuts less jarring
Cut on sentencesCut between thoughts, not mid-word
Don't overdo itAdobe warns choppy footage becomes hard to watch

StudioBinder also describes the axial cut, changing the framing from the same camera position without skipping time, which is related to the punch-in many YouTubers use between jump cuts.

Planning for jump cuts while filming

Jump cuts are easier to hide when you plan for them on the day. Pause for a beat before and after each sentence so there is a clean place to cut. If you fluff a line, stop, take a breath and start the whole sentence again rather than patching mid-phrase. Film a few seconds of B-roll for each main point so you have something to lay over the cuts later. These small habits turn a messy raw take into an edit that flows.

Why are they called jump cuts?

Because the subject seems to jump from one position or moment to the next within the same shot.

Are jump cuts bad?

Not if they have a reason. Adobe says every jump cut should have a narrative or emotional purpose; overuse makes videos choppy.

What's the difference between a jump cut and a punch-in?

A jump cut skips time; a punch-in changes the framing. YouTubers often combine them so cuts feel intentional.

What film made jump cuts famous?

Jean-Luc Godard's Breathless (1960).

How do I hide a jump cut?

Cover it with B-roll, change the framing or use a sound bridge.
