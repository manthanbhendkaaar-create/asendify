How should you end a podcast episode?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

End a podcast episode on its strongest payoff, then close quickly: one line that sums up the point, one clear call to action, and a short teaser for the next episode. Avoid long goodbyes, full recaps and a list of five things to do. Listeners who reach the end are your most engaged audience, so the ending is your best chance to get them to the next episode.

Key takeaways

  • Put the best payoff near the end, not in the first ten minutes followed by a slow fade.
  • Keep the outro short: a one-line takeaway, one call to action, a teaser.
  • Apple describes a "teaser trailer" as "usually played at the end of your show to promote the next episode."
  • Spotify suggests trailers include a call to action such as following the show; the same single ask works at the end of an episode.
  • On YouTube, end screens can cover the last 5 to 20 seconds of videos at least 25 seconds long.

Why endings matter

Most listeners never reach the end, which makes the ones who do especially valuable. They liked the episode enough to finish it, so they're the most likely to follow, listen to another episode or share. A slow, rambling ending wastes that moment. A sharp ending turns it into the next listen.

What a good ending includes

The teaser: an open loop into the next episode

Apple's guide to podcast trailers describes the teaser trailer as one "usually played at the end of your show to promote the next episode", previewing "a few captivating moments" (Apple Podcasts for Creators). It works best when it stops before the answer. Carnegie Mellon's George Loewenstein argued that curiosity arises "when attention becomes focused on a gap in one's knowledge" (quoted in PBS NOVA); a teaser that ends on a question gives the listener exactly that gap.

One call to action, not five

Spotify's trailer tips suggest including a call to action, such as asking listeners to follow, subscribe or leave a review (Spotify for Creators). At the end of an episode, choose one. "Follow, rate, review, share, join the newsletter and check the website" asks for six things and gets none. Rotate the ask across episodes if you need several.

Video podcasts: use the end screen

On YouTube, end screens "can be added to the last 5–20 seconds of a video", the video must be "at least 25 seconds long", and you can add up to four elements on a standard 16:9 video (YouTube Help). Point the end screen at the next episode or a playlist, and leave the frame clean (a simple background or the hosts) for those seconds. Say out loud which video you're pointing to, so the end screen and your words match.

What to cut from your ending

  • Long goodbyes and thank-yous that run for a minute or more.
  • Full recaps of what listeners just heard.
  • Housekeeping that could go in show notes.
  • The post-interview chat ("that was great, shall we stop?") left in by accident.
  • A new topic started at the end with no payoff.

Example: rewriting a three-minute outro

Example (a hypothetical episode, to show the method). Original ending, after the guest's last answer:

  • 40 seconds of thank-yous and small talk.
  • A 60-second recap of the episode.
  • Five asks: follow, rate, review, newsletter, merch.
  • 30 seconds of music.

Rewritten ending:

  • The guest's last answer, which pays off the cold open (kept in full).
  • Host: "Never let one client be more than a third of your income. That's the one to remember." (8 s)
  • "Follow the show so next Tuesday's episode is waiting for you." (6 s)
  • Teaser: next guest saying "I turned down £2 million, and I'd do it again" (12 s), cut before the reason.
  • Sign-off sting. (3 s)

The outro drops from about three minutes to under 30 seconds, and it now gives listeners a reason to come back.

How to check if your ending works

  • Spotify and YouTube curves: look at the last few minutes. A sharp drop as soon as the goodbyes start means the outro is too long.
  • Next-episode listens: compare how many people play the following episode after you add a teaser.
  • End screen clicks on YouTube: Studio reports how often viewers click end screen elements.
  • Follows: watch for changes in follows after you cut the call to action down to one ask.

Should you end with a cliffhanger?

Occasionally, and only if the next episode pays it off. A cliffhanger in every episode of an interview show feels manipulative; in a narrative series it's often the format. Whatever you tease, deliver it clearly next time, or listeners stop trusting your teasers.

FAQ

Is there a book on ending podcast episodes well?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers endings, open loops and the payoff ladder for podcasts, clips and YouTube.

How long should a podcast outro be?

Usually under 30 to 60 seconds: a takeaway, one call to action and a teaser.

Should I recap the episode at the end?

Rarely. A one-line takeaway works better than a full recap.

What should my podcast call to action be?

One clear ask, usually to follow the show. Rotate other asks across episodes.

Should I tease the next episode?

Yes, if you know what it is. A short clip that ends on a question works best.

How do end screens work on YouTube?

They appear in the last 5 to 20 seconds of videos at least 25 seconds long, with up to four elements on 16:9 videos.
