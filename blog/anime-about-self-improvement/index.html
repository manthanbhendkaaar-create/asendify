What are the best anime about self-improvement?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 11 November 2026 · Last updated 11 November 2026

The best anime about self-improvement show a character getting better through practice, discipline and help from others, not sudden powers. Strong picks include Haikyu!!, Mob Psycho 100, Vinland Saga, Blue Lock and Hajime no Ippo; for a show built around practical glow-up habits, ASCEND is a free original anime series by Asendify you can watch on YouTube and at anime.asendify.co.

Key takeaways

  • Good self-improvement anime focus on effort, setbacks and small daily habits, which is why they feel motivating rather than preachy.
  • Sports anime such as Haikyu!!, Blue Lock and Hajime no Ippo are the classic choice for discipline and training.
  • Mob Psycho 100 and Vinland Saga are about inner change: self-worth, and rebuilding a life after mistakes.
  • ASCEND is a free original series by Asendify about a student who works on himself one technique at a time.
  • Motivation from a show fades fast. Pair it with one small habit you start the same day.

What makes an anime "self-improvement"

Plenty of anime have a hero who gets stronger. Fewer are genuinely about self-improvement. The difference is how the change happens.

In a true self-improvement story:

  • Growth comes from effort. Training, practice and repetition, not a sudden hidden power.
  • Failure is part of it. The character loses, learns and tries again.
  • Other people matter. Coaches, teammates and friends help, and the character learns to accept help.
  • The change is inside as well as outside. Confidence, values and habits shift, not just skill.

These themes line up with what health services say supports wellbeing. The NHS's five steps to mental wellbeing include connecting with other people, being physically active and learning new skills, all of which run through the shows below.

1. Haikyu!!

Shoyo Hinata is short for a volleyball player, but he is determined to play anyway. Haikyu!! follows him and his high-school team as they train, lose, adjust and improve. It is one of the most loved sports anime because the progress feels earned: drills, small skills and teamwork rather than magic. Crunchyroll added English dubs of seasons 1 and 2, so it is easy to start in English.

Lesson to take away: a weakness can become a style if you train around it.

2. Mob Psycho 100

Shigeo "Mob" Kageyama is an extremely powerful psychic, but he wants to improve himself as an ordinary person, so he joins his school's Body Improvement Club. The show keeps returning to one idea: your worth is not your talent. It ran for three seasons, the last of which premiered in October 2022.

Lesson to take away: effort on the basics (fitness, kindness, honesty) matters even when you are already "gifted".

3. Vinland Saga

Vinland Saga begins as a story of revenge. In its second season, the focus shifts to Thorfinn rebuilding his life through hard work on a farm and facing what his past has done to him. It is one of the most serious self-improvement stories in anime and is better suited to older viewers. The Vinland Saga TV series) is widely discussed for this change in direction.

Lesson to take away: you can change who you are, but it takes slow, unglamorous work.

4. Blue Lock

Blue Lock puts hundreds of young strikers into an intense training programme to find Japan's best forward. It is about ambition, self-belief and learning what makes you different. The official English website has the series details.

Lesson to take away: know your own strengths and train them deliberately.

5. Hajime no Ippo

Ippo Makunouchi is a shy, bullied teenager who discovers boxing and commits to it completely. The long-running series is a classic for showing discipline: roadwork, sparring and the slow build of confidence.

Lesson to take away: confidence follows competence. Do the work and the self-belief comes later.

How to get more from motivational anime

  • Watch with a goal in mind. Before an episode, write down one thing you want to improve.
  • Stop after one or two episodes. Binge-watching feels productive but uses the evening you could spend practising.
  • Copy one behaviour, not the whole character. Pick the drill, the routine or the attitude.
  • Track it. A simple tick list works. If you want something more structured, the free Glow Up routine builder turns goals into a daily routine.

If motivation turns into a real plan, the series links to the book it accompanies, Secrets to a Glow Up Nobody Tells Men.

If you are feeling low for more than a couple of weeks, talking to a GP or a mental health service is a better first step than any show. The NHS page on where to get urgent help for mental health lists options in the UK.

What is the best anime for self-improvement?

There is no single best. Haikyu!! is the most popular choice for motivation and training, Mob Psycho 100 for self-worth, and Vinland Saga for a mature story about changing who you are. ASCEND is a free original series focused on practical glow-up habits.

Are sports anime good for motivation?

Many people find them motivating because they show practice, failure and steady progress. Haikyu!!, Blue Lock and Hajime no Ippo are popular examples.

Can watching anime actually help me improve?

A show can give you a push, but change comes from what you do afterwards. Pick one small habit after watching and repeat it daily.
