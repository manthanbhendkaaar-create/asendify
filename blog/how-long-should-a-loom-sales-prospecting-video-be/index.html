How long should a Loom-style sales prospecting video be, and what should the first 10 seconds say?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Keep a cold prospecting video to roughly 30 to 60 seconds, and use the first 10 seconds to prove it was made for this person: say their name, show something of theirs on screen (their website, their ad, their product page) and name one specific thing you noticed. Then give one idea, and end with one small ask. Mailchimp's guide puts the ideal at "between 30 and 60 seconds", with product demos as the main exception, and Loom's own advice is to keep it "about one thing and not too long".

Key takeaways

  • Aim for 30 to 60 seconds for a first-touch video; save longer walkthroughs for people who have replied.
  • The first 10 seconds must show it is personal: their name, their screen, one specific observation.
  • One video, one idea, one ask. A reply or a 15-minute call is a better ask than "let me know your thoughts".
  • The thumbnail or GIF preview does the job of a hook before they press play.
  • Never fake personalisation (a name pasted into a generic script is obvious within seconds).
  • Short is a courtesy: you're asking a stranger for attention they didn't plan to give.

Notice the last line: even very short videos are, on average, watched only about halfway. Whatever matters most has to come first.

The first 10 seconds

The prospect's real question when they press play is "is this a mass email with a face on it?" Answer it immediately:

  1. Their name, spoken. "Hi Jordan."
  2. Their thing, on screen. Start the recording already on their homepage, their latest ad, their pricing page or their LinkedIn post, not on your slide.
  3. One specific observation. "Your new pricing page has three plans, but the demo button only appears on the top one."

That's it. No introduction of yourself beyond a first name, no company history, no "I hope this finds you well". Who you are can come in a sentence later, once they're curious.

A 45-second structure

SecondsWhat to doExample line
0 to 10Name, their screen, one observation"Hi Jordan, I'm on your pricing page and noticed..."
10 to 25Why it matters to them, in their terms"Visitors on the two cheaper plans can't book a demo from here."
25 to 38One idea, and a hint of how you help"A small change here is something we set up for SaaS teams; here's what it might look like."
38 to 45One small ask"If it's useful, reply 'yes' and I'll send the two-minute version."

The example is illustrative. Don't promise outcomes ("this will double your demos") you can't back up.

Editing and recording tips

  • Record in one take, but trim. Cut the first second of you clicking "record" and the last seconds of you reaching for the stop button.
  • Look at the camera for the opening line, then at the screen. The face matters most at the start.
  • Keep the face bubble small but visible. People respond to a person; they also need to read the screen.
  • Speak at normal speed. The edX study by Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014) found engagement rose with speaking rate within a length range, which the authors put down mainly to enthusiasm. Don't slow down to sound careful.
  • Turn on captions if your tool supports them; many people watch at work with the sound off.
  • Name the video well. "Jordan: an idea for your pricing page (45s)" tells them what and how long.

The email around the video

The video lives inside an email or message, and that is what they see first.

ElementWhat works
Subject lineSpecific to them, not to you
First lineOne sentence on why you recorded this
ThumbnailGIF or still that shows their site or name; Mailchimp notes a thumbnail with the prospect's name or logo can spark curiosity
Length shownSay "45 seconds" so it feels cheap to watch
Text summaryTwo lines summarising the point, for people who won't press play
AskThe same single ask as the video

Follow the usual rules for cold outreach in your country (for UK B2B email, identify yourself and give an easy way to opt out).

Example: re-cutting a 3-minute prospecting video

Example: a freelance editor records a 3-minute video for a coaching brand. It opens with 40 seconds about the editor's background and ends with a portfolio tour.

The re-cut is 50 seconds: it opens on the coach's latest YouTube video at the moment viewers likely leave (a long logo intro), suggests opening on the strongest line instead, shows a rough five-second alternative cut, and asks whether they'd like the edited version. The portfolio link goes in the email text. Brand, video and timings are illustrative.

When a longer video is fine

  • They replied and asked for a walkthrough.
  • It's a demo they requested (Mailchimp allows up to about six minutes for demos and tutorials).
  • It's a proposal walkthrough for a deal already in progress.

Even then, add a one-sentence summary at the top and chapters or timestamps in the message.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to hook viewers in the first seconds?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Loom's and Mailchimp's free guides cover prospecting basics.

Should I script a prospecting video?

Use three bullet points rather than a script. Loom advises "you don't need a video script", and reading sounds generic.

Should I show my face or just the screen?

Both, with your face small in a corner. The face builds trust at the start; the screen proves the video is about them.

Can I reuse one video for many prospects?

You can reuse a structure, but a video that clearly isn't about them loses the point of video. Record the first 10 seconds fresh each time if nothing else.

What's a good ask at the end?

Something small and specific: reply "yes", or a short call at a named time.
