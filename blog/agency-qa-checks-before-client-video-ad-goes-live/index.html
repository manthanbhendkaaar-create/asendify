What should agency QA check before a client video ad goes live?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Before a client video ad goes live, agency QA should check five areas: the creative still does its job (hook, single promise, call to action), the ad works muted with accurate captions, every version meets the platform's specs and safe zones, the legal side is clean (claims, testimonials, ad labels, music and footage licences), and the ad matches the landing page and tracking. QA should be done by someone who didn't edit the ad, on a phone, in a mock-up of the feed.

Key takeaways

  • QA is the last gate, separate from the editor's own checklist on the rough cut.
  • Check on a phone, muted, inside the platform's interface, not on an editing monitor.
  • Auto-captions are not finished captions; read every line.
  • Testimonials need evidence and permission; paid creator content needs an "ad" label upfront.
  • Confirm the offer in the ad matches the landing page word for word.

Why a separate QA pass matters

An editing checklist (run on the rough cut) catches retention problems while they are cheap to fix. QA happens at the end, after colour, graphics, client changes and exports, when new faults creep in: a price changed in one version but not another, captions drifted out of sync, a logo landed under the "Follow" button, a music track was swapped for one with no ad licence. These faults are small, but a single one can get an ad rejected, pulled or complained about.

Creative

Even at QA, re-check the basics. TikTok's creative best practices recommend introducing the proposition in the first 3 seconds and ending with a clear call to action. If late client changes pushed the offer back to second 8, flag it.

Sound off and captions

Watch every version muted. Then read the captions line by line against the audio. The W3C's guidance on captions warns that automatically generated captions don't meet user needs or accessibility requirements unless they are fully accurate. Brand names, prices and product names are where auto-captions most often go wrong.

Specs and safe zones

Check each export against the placement. TikTok's in-feed ad specifications, for example, recommend vertical 9:16 at 540 x 960 pixels or more, cap files at 500 MB, and tell you to keep content inside the UI safe zone, which differs by format and caption length. Overlay the platform's safe-zone template on each version before signing off.

Claims, testimonials and labels

  • Claims: every factual claim (price, result, comparison) needs evidence the client can produce.
  • Testimonials: the ASA's testimonials and endorsements guidance says advertisers must hold documentary evidence that a testimonial is genuine, along with contact details, and must not use one without permission.
  • Ad labels: if a creator was paid or given an incentive, the ASA expects a prominent "ad" label upfront, and both brand and creator are responsible.
  • Reviews: fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews became a banned practice under UK consumer law in April 2025, according to the government's online reviews and endorsements guidance.

Licences

Check that the music, stock clips and fonts are licensed for paid advertising, on every platform and in every country the ad will run. A track licensed for organic social posts may not cover paid ads.

Landing page and tracking

Click the link in each version. Confirm the price, offer wording and imagery match the landing page, the page loads on mobile, and the tracking parameters are right. A mismatch between ad and page loses people who were persuaded by the ad.

Who should do QA

Someone who didn't cut the ad: a producer, an account manager or another editor. Editors stop seeing their own mistakes after the tenth watch. Give the QA person the brief, the approved script and the client's must-haves so they check against the agreed version, not their own taste.

Worked example

Example: an agency is about to launch six versions of a client's 20-second ad for a home insurance quote tool (two lengths, three ratios). The editor has signed off. QA, done by a producer on a phone, finds:

  1. The 9:16 version's end card text sits behind TikTok's caption area. Fix: move it up.
  2. Auto-captions spell the brand name wrongly in two versions. Fix: correct the caption file.
  3. The 4:5 version still shows "from £9 a month" after the client changed it to "from £10" in the others. Fix: update and re-export.
  4. One customer quote has no signed consent on file. Fix: hold that version until the client supplies it.

Four faults, none of them about creativity, all of them avoidable. Without QA, at least one version would have gone live with a wrong price.

FAQ

Is there a pre-launch checklist for client video ads?

Yes: the nine-area table above. For the editing stage before it, a system such as Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook supplies a pre-export checklist.

How is QA different from the editor's checklist?

The editor's checklist improves the cut while it is rough. QA checks the final exports for errors, specs, legal issues and links.

How long does QA take per ad?

For a short ad, usually a few minutes per version: a muted watch, a sound-on watch, a caption read and a link check.

Should QA check the targeting too?

That is normally the media buyer's job, but QA should confirm that each version is going to the placement it was built for.

What if QA finds a problem on launch day?

Hold the affected version only. Launch the others and fix the faulty one, rather than delaying the whole campaign.
