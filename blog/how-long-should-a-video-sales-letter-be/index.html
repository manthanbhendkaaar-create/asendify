How long should a video sales letter be to keep the most viewers watching?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A video sales letter (VSL) should be as short as the buying decision allows: often 2 to 5 minutes for a simple, low-cost offer and 10 to 20 minutes for an expensive or considered one. There is no magic length. Wistia's data shows engagement falls after the five-minute mark and drops again past 30 minutes, so every extra minute has to answer a real buyer question. Show what you're selling early, and put the call to action where people still are.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia's 2026 analysis of over 13 million videos says engagement "naturally declines after the five-minute mark" and reports "an 11% drop in engagement once videos cross" 30 minutes.
  • For longer "convert or nurture" videos, Wistia notes that "the 1st, 2nd, and 4th quarters are prime spots for a lead generation form."
  • A study of 6.9 million video sessions on edX found median engagement time was at most about 6 minutes whatever the video length (Guo, Kim and Rubin, 2014).
  • Google's ABCDs for video ads advise you to "Get to the heart of the story faster".
  • If your VSL makes income or results claims, you need evidence. The US FTC has proposed extending its ban on deceptive earnings claims to business coaching (FTC, January 2025).

Length by offer type

OfferTypical VSL lengthWhat the time is for
Low-cost product or app1 to 3 minProblem, demo, offer
Course or digital product3 to 10 minWho it's for, what's inside, proof, price
Coaching or service8 to 20 minMethod, fit, objections, next step
High-ticket B2B10 to 20 min, or split into chaptersDetailed proof and answers to common objections

These are starting points. The right length is the one that answers the questions a buyer has at that price, and no more.

Why long VSLs lose people

Long VSLs rarely lose viewers because they are long. They lose them because:

  1. The opening is a wind-up. Two minutes of backstory before the viewer knows what's on offer.
  2. The price is hidden. People who want to know the cost leave to find it.
  3. Repetition. The same promise said five ways.
  4. No proof, or vague proof. "Life-changing results" with nothing specific.
  5. One long scene. A single talking head with no change of picture for minutes.

The edX study above also found that shorter videos were much more engaging, and its authors advise splitting material into short chunks. For a long VSL, that suggests clear chapters on the page.

A structure that holds attention

SectionTime in a 6-minute VSLJob
Hook0:00 to 0:20Name the viewer's problem and the outcome
What this is0:20 to 0:50Say plainly what you're selling and who it's for

Put a button or link under the video from the start, so ready buyers don't have to sit through the rest.

How to test your VSL length

  1. Check where people leave. Most video hosts show a drop-off graph. Look for the steepest fall.
  2. Fix that moment first. If 40% leave at the same 15 seconds, rewrite or cut those 15 seconds.
  3. Make a short cut. Keep the hook, the offer and the best proof. Drop the rest.
  4. Compare conversion, not watch time. A shorter VSL may be watched less in total but sell more.
  5. Watch the call-to-action timing. If most buyers click before the end, show the offer earlier.

Quick checklist before you publish a VSL

  • The first sentence names the viewer's problem or the result they want.
  • Within the first minute, the viewer knows what you sell and who it's for.
  • The price appears once the offer is clear, and also on the page under the video.
  • Every proof point is real, permitted and specific, with evidence kept on file.
  • No section repeats a point already made; if it does, cut the weaker version.
  • The picture changes at least every minute or so: screen share, example, chart or new angle.
  • Captions are on for sound-off viewers.
  • A button or link sits under the video from the start, so ready buyers can act early.
  • You have checked the drop-off graph after the first week and fixed the steepest fall before changing anything else.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops and pacing for sales videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Wistia's free research is a useful alternative.

Do long VSLs still work?

They can, for expensive or complex offers, if every section answers a real buyer question. Padding loses viewers at any length.

Should I hide the price until the end?

It's safer to show it once the viewer understands the offer. People who want the price will leave to find it otherwise.

Should I disable the progress bar on my VSL?

Hiding controls frustrates viewers and doesn't fix a slow video. Shorten the video instead.

Can I use income claims in a coaching VSL?

Only if they're true, typical and backed by evidence. In the UK, the CAP Code says ads must not mislead.
