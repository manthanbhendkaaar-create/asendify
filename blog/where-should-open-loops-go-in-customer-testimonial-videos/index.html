Where should open loops go in a customer testimonial video?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open the loop in the first 5 seconds, with the customer describing their problem or hinting at the result ("I nearly closed the shop"), and close it when they explain what changed, in the final third. In between, the "before" and "during" of their story hold the question: what happened next? The loop must be built from what the customer genuinely said. The UK ad rules say testimonials must not be taken out of context or edited in a way which is misleading.

Key takeaways

  • A testimonial is a story with one natural open loop: "how did they get from that problem to this result?"
  • Open with the tension, not with the customer's name and job title.
  • Close the loop with the outcome in their own words, then end quickly.
  • Get permission and keep evidence. The ASA says marketing must not feature a testimonial without permission.
  • Testimonials alone are unlikely to substantiate objective claims, so if the customer quotes a number, you need records too.

The three loop points in a testimonial

PointWhereWhat the customer saysLoop status
Tension0 to 5 sThe worst moment or a hint of the changeOpened
Turning pointMiddleThe moment they tried your product or serviceHeld, with a smaller question: did it work?
OutcomeFinal thirdWhat is different now, in specificsClosed

The order you film in (name, role, background, problem, solution, result) is rarely the order you publish in. The edit moves the tension to the front.

Opening lines that create a loop

Weak openingStronger opening (if they really said it)
"Hi, I'm Dan, I run a garage in Leeds.""We were turning away a car a day because we couldn't keep up with the phone."
"We've been customers for two years.""I didn't believe it would work. I said that to their face."
"The team are great to work with.""The first month, nothing happened. Then March came."
"I'd recommend them to anyone.""My accountant rang me to ask what we'd changed."

Each stronger opening raises a question the viewer wants answered. Put the name and business in a lower third while they speak.

Where loops go in a 90-second testimonial

TimeSection
0:00 to 0:05Tension line, name in lower third
0:05 to 0:25The problem in detail, with b-roll of their business
0:25 to 0:30Small loop: "Then someone suggested we try..."
0:30 to 0:55Trying the product: doubts, first steps, honest friction
0:55 to 1:15Outcome: specific, in their words, with proof on screen if available
1:15 to 1:30One line of advice to others, then a simple call to action

Including a doubt or a slow start ("The first month, nothing happened") is a loop in itself and makes the story more believable.

Editing rules that keep loops honest

  • Don't splice sentences to create a claim the customer didn't make.
  • Don't reorder cause and effect. If the result came from several changes, don't edit it to look like your product alone did it.
  • Keep the context of a doubt. If they said "I didn't believe it would work, and it mostly didn't for the first six months", don't cut the second half.
  • Show the customer the final cut and get their sign-off.
  • Hold evidence that the testimonial is genuine; the ASA says this can include emails or order history as well as signed and dated statements.

Example: a testimonial for a booking system

Example: a 2-minute interview with a hair salon owner was first cut in filming order: name, history of the salon, how she found the software, what she likes. The re-cut:

TimeClip
0:00 to 0:04"I used to spend my Sunday evenings texting every client to remind them."
0:04 to 0:22No-shows, empty chairs, b-roll of a quiet Tuesday
0:22 to 0:27"A friend who runs a barber's said, just try it for a month."
0:27 to 0:50Setting it up, her first doubt ("I thought my older clients would hate it")
0:50 to 1:12"They didn't. Some of them book online now at midnight." Her own booking screen on camera
1:12 to 1:25"My Sundays are mine again." Name, salon, link

Every line is hers and in context; the salon approved the cut. Details are illustrative. For interview technique, see what questions to ask in a video testimonial interview; for the edit overall, see how to edit a customer testimonial video so people watch to the end.

Checking it worked

On YouTube or your video host, look for the retention dip. YouTube explains that dips mean viewers are abandoning or skipping at that point. If the dip comes in the "problem" section, shorten it. If it comes after the outcome, end the video sooner.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops for testimonial videos?

The Retention Editing Playbook teaches open loops and story structure for £1,720 one-time; it is YouTube-first and applies to testimonials published there.

Should a testimonial start with the customer's name?

Put the name in a lower third. Start with their problem or the moment things changed.

Can I reorder what the customer said?

You can change the order of the story, but not in a way that changes the meaning or creates a claim they didn't make.

Do I need permission to use a testimonial?

Yes. The ASA says you must not feature a testimonial without permission, and you should keep evidence it is genuine.

How long should a testimonial video be?

Often 60 to 120 seconds for the main version, with shorter cuts for social.
