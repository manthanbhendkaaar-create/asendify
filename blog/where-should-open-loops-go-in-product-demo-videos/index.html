Where should open loops go in a product demo video?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

At the very start, by showing the finished result before showing how you got there, and then before each new feature. In a demo, the open loop is "how did it do that?" Show the outcome in the first few seconds, close the loop by walking through the steps, and open a small new one ("the part that saves the most time is next") before each section. That keeps viewers past the middle, where Wistia says the most important information should already have appeared.

Key takeaways

  • Result first, process second. The finished dashboard, the clean report or the shipped order is the hook.
  • Google's ABCDs say to "get to the heart of the story faster"; in a demo, the heart is the outcome.
  • Wistia reports viewers typically watch over 50% of educational and tutorial videos of 1 to 5 minutes, so the strongest features belong in the first half.
  • Open a small loop before each feature and close it within 30 to 60 seconds.
  • Demos make factual claims. In the UK, marketers must hold documentary evidence for objective claims, so the result you show must be real and typical.

Why demos lose viewers

Most demo videos follow the product's menu: log in, here's the dashboard, here are the settings, here's feature one. That order makes sense to the team that built it, but the viewer has no reason to care about step one until they've seen what step five produces. Common leak points:

  • A login screen or onboarding in the first 20 seconds.
  • A slow tour of the navigation.
  • Every feature given equal time.
  • The best feature saved for the end.

Loop lines that work in demos

PurposeExample line
Main loop at the start"This took 40 seconds. Here's every click."
Bridge to next feature"That's the setup. The bit that saves Friday afternoons is next."
Objection loop"You might be thinking: what if the data's wrong? I'll show you in a minute."
Before/after"This is the spreadsheet we started with. By the end of this video it'll be a dashboard."
Callback"Remember the 40 seconds? This is where they went."

The objection loop is especially useful: it names the doubt the viewer already has, promises an answer and keeps them watching until it comes.

Where demo videos live, and how loops change

PlacementTypical lengthLoops
Homepage hero30 to 90 sOne loop, result first, closed by 70% of the runtime
Product page1 to 3 minMain loop plus one per feature
YouTube tutorial or full walkthrough5 to 15 minMain loop, plus a loop before each chapter; add chapters
Sales follow-up2 to 5 minMain loop built on the prospect's own problem

On YouTube, chapters let viewers jump to the feature they care about. The first timestamp must be 00:00, with at least three timestamps and chapters of at least 10 seconds. Write each chapter title as the outcome, not the menu name.

Common mistakes

  • Opening loops you never close. Every "I'll show you in a minute" must be shown.
  • Stacking too many loops. Two at a time is the most viewers can hold.
  • Using fake data that looks too perfect. Viewers notice; realistic sample data builds more trust.
  • Hiding the price question. If price is the obvious doubt, say where to find it rather than teasing it.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops for product demos?

The Retention Editing Playbook teaches open loops and re-hooks for £1,720 one-time; it is YouTube-first and applies to demos and tutorials published there.

Should a demo start with the login screen?

No. Start with the finished result and show setup only when it is needed.

How many open loops should a demo have?

One main loop for the whole video, plus a small loop before each feature, with no more than two open at once.

Where should the best feature go?

In the first half. Many viewers won't reach the end of a demo.

Can I use sample data in a demo?

Yes, if it is realistic and the results you show are ones customers can actually get.
