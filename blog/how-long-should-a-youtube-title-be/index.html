How long should a YouTube title be?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

YouTube allows up to 100 characters in a video title, but there is no official ideal length. A practical target is a title short enough to read at a glance, with the words that matter most in the first few, because long titles can be cut off in some layouts. Write the clearest promise first, then trim anything that does not help a viewer decide to click.

Key takeaways

  • The hard limit is 100 characters; YouTube's API documentation states it, and titles cannot contain the characters < and >.
  • YouTube search matches the title, tags, description and the video itself against the query, so the title is one relevance signal among several.
  • Length matters less than clarity: a short title with a vague promise loses to a slightly longer specific one.
  • YouTube's title and thumbnail test picks winners by watch time, so you can test a short and a long version on your own audience.
  • Misleading titles that do not deliver what was promised break YouTube's spam policy, whatever their length.

The rules YouTube actually sets

The YouTube Data API videos reference defines a title as having "a maximum length of 100 characters" and allows "all valid UTF-8 characters except < and >". That is the only hard rule. YouTube does not publish a recommended length.

On search, YouTube's help page How YouTube search works says relevance is estimated from "how well the title, tags, description, and video content match your search query", alongside engagement signals such as watch time for that query and quality signals about expertise and trust. So the words in a title matter for search, but stuffing more words in does not automatically help.

YouTube's spam, deceptive practices and scams policies set the honesty limit: no "maliciously misleading titles, thumbnails, descriptions or imagery" that trick people into clicking a video that does not deliver what was promised.

How to write the right length

  1. Write the promise in one plain sentence. "What happened when I quit sugar for 30 days."
  2. Move the key words to the front. "Quit sugar for 30 days" should appear early, so it shows even if the end is cut.
  3. Cut filler. Words like "very", "really", "ultimate" and "in this video" rarely help.
  4. Check the thumbnail. If the thumbnail already says "30 DAYS", the title can carry a different detail instead of repeating it.
  5. Read it on a phone screen. Most titles are seen small; if it takes effort to read, shorten it.

Worked example: trimming a title

Example: a gardening channel drafts this title (95 characters):

"In This Video I Will Show You the Easiest Way to Grow Tomatoes on a Small Balcony for Beginners"

  • Remove "In This Video I Will Show You the" (33 characters of filler).
  • Keep the search phrase "grow tomatoes on a small balcony".
  • Add the concrete benefit.

Result (52 characters): "Grow Tomatoes on a Small Balcony: The Easiest Method"

The new title keeps every useful search word, leads with them, and is roughly half the length. The channel then runs YouTube's title and thumbnail test with the long and short versions; YouTube says tests optimise for overall watch time rather than click-through rate, can compare up to three options and usually finish within two weeks.

Title length for Shorts and for search-led videos

The 100-character limit applies to every upload, but the job differs by format:

  • Shorts: viewers in the Shorts feed often decide from the video itself, so a short, plain title that names the topic is usually enough.
  • Search-led tutorials: people type a problem, so include the words they would type ("how to fix a leaking tap") early in the title.
  • Story and entertainment videos: curiosity and emotion carry the click, so a shorter title paired with a strong thumbnail often reads better.
  • Series: keep a consistent pattern ("Week 12: ...") so returning viewers recognise the next episode at a glance.

Common title-length mistakes

  • Using all 100 characters because you can. Extra words dilute the promise.
  • Hiding the topic at the end. If the subject only appears after 60 characters, many viewers never see it.
  • Repeating the thumbnail text word for word. Use the title and thumbnail as a team, each adding something.
  • Keyword lists. "Tomatoes | Balcony | Gardening | Beginners" reads like spam and gives no reason to click.
  • Clickbait that the video does not pay off. It can raise clicks and lower watch time, which is what YouTube's tests reward.

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast reviews your latest titles and thumbnails, scores the channel out of 100 and gives three fixes, with no sign-up. YouTube's Test & compare is the built-in alternative.

What is the maximum length of a YouTube title?

100 characters. YouTube's API documentation also says titles cannot include the characters < and >.

Do longer titles rank better in YouTube search?

Not by themselves. YouTube says relevance comes from how well the title, tags, description and video match the query, plus engagement and quality signals.

Should I put keywords at the start of my title?

Yes, where it reads naturally. Leading with the topic means it stays visible even if the end of the title is cut off.

Can I test two title lengths?

Yes. YouTube's Test & compare lets you test up to three titles and thumbnails, and it picks winners by watch time.
