How do I use speed ramps in long YouTube videos without losing viewers?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use speed ramps sparingly, to mark a moment that matters: speeding through a boring stretch (travel, setup, a long process), slowing down for a reveal or payoff, or moving between sections. Keep each ramp short, ease into and out of the speed change so it feels smooth, and avoid ramping while someone is speaking. In a long video, a handful of well-placed ramps adds energy; a ramp on every shot quickly looks like a template and stops meaning anything.

Key takeaways

  • Adobe defines speed ramping as "when you gradually change the speed of a video clip" and says ramps "are a great way to emphasize pivotal moments" (Adobe).
  • Adobe's guide warns that unless you drag the keyframe handles apart, "the transition will be instantaneous". Smooth ramps need eased keyframes.
  • YouTube says "Spikes appear when more viewers are watching, rewatching, or sharing" and dips show where viewers abandon or skip (YouTube Help). Check whether your ramps line up with either.
  • Cornell research on 150 films found shot transitions, not dialogue, are what prompt viewers to pay attention (Cornell Chronicle). A ramp is a kind of transition: it works when it signals change.
  • Google's ABCDs recommend "engaging pacing". A ramp is one pacing tool among many, not a fix for slow content.

How to make a smooth ramp

  1. Shoot for it. Slow motion needs extra frames, so film the moments you might slow down at a higher frame rate than your timeline.
  2. Mark the moment. Decide exactly where the speed should change: a footstep, a jump, an object landing.
  3. Add keyframes. In Premiere Pro, Adobe's guide uses Show Clip Keyframes > Time Remapping > Speed, then adds keyframes with the Pen tool.
  4. Change the speed between keyframes by dragging the line up (faster) or down (slower).
  5. Ease it. Drag the keyframe handles apart so the change is gradual.
  6. Cut on the beat. If there's music, time the change to a beat or a sound effect.
  7. Mute or replace the audio during the ramp. Sped-up speech sounds odd; a whoosh or music bridge usually works better.

How many ramps in a long video?

There's no rule, but a useful test: every ramp should have a reason you could say out loud ("this shows time passing", "this is the reveal"). If you can't name the reason, remove it.

Checking the effect on retention

  1. Publish, then wait for retention data to settle.
  2. Find each ramp on the retention graph. Spikes suggest people rewatch it; dips suggest it's confusing or annoying.
  3. Compare with similar videos without ramps in the same spot.
  4. Keep what helps, remove what doesn't, and note it for your style guide.

Quick checklist for speed ramps

  • Each ramp has a reason you could say out loud.
  • Slow-motion moments were filmed at a higher frame rate.
  • Keyframes are eased so changes are smooth, not sudden.
  • No ramps sit under someone speaking.
  • Ramp audio is replaced with music, a whoosh or natural sound.
  • Speed changes land on a beat or a clear action.
  • The video doesn't rely on ramps to make slow content feel fast; slow sections are cut first.
  • You've checked the retention graph at each ramp after publishing.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches pacing and pattern interrupts for long videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook by Asendify covers pacing and pattern interrupts for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's retention help pages are free.

What frame rate do I need for slow motion?

Higher than your timeline. Shooting at double your project frame rate or more gives smooth slow motion.

Do speed ramps improve retention?

They can, when they cut waiting or emphasise a payoff. Used everywhere, they become noise.

Can I speed ramp in free editing software?

Many free and phone editors offer speed curves or speed ramps. The principle is the same.

Should I ramp in the hook?

Only if the ramp shows the payoff quickly. Don't let an effect delay the point.
