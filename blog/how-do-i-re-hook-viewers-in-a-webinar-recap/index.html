How do I re-hook viewers in a webinar recap?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 25 November 2026 · Last updated 25 November 2026

Do not replay the webinar in order. Open on the single most useful moment, then re-hook at each section change with a question or a promise of what comes next, and trim everything that does not earn its place. A recap is a highlight reel, not a shorter recording.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia defines a highlight reel as "an edited compilation of the most compelling and impactful moments of a longer video," like "a teaser trailer," and says "most highlight reels range from one to three minutes" (Wistia).
  • Wistia warns that "a compilation of random clips from a long video doesn't make a good highlight reel" and that you need to be "strategic in choosing the right moments" (Wistia).
  • TechSmith says "Trimming is the most impactful edit you can make," because it "tightens pacing and cuts run time without removing any real content," including long pauses between speakers (TechSmith).
  • YouTube says "dips mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video" (YouTube Help); in a recap, each dip is a place to add a re-hook.

What a re-hook is in a recap

A re-hook is a short moment that gives a viewer a fresh reason to keep going: a question the next section answers, a number about to be explained, a promise of the next point. A webinar recording rarely has them, because the live audience was committed by the registration and the schedule. A recap viewer is different. They did not attend or half remember it, they chose to click and they can leave at any time.

So the recap needs its own structure. Open on the payoff, then bring a new reason to stay at every change of topic.

Step-by-step: edit a webinar recap

  1. Decide who the recap is for. Someone who missed it, someone who attended or a prospect. Each needs a different cut.
  2. Find the best moments in the transcript. Wistia suggests reading a time-coded transcript or searching it for keywords.
  3. Pick three to five moments that stand alone. Each should make sense with no context beyond a title.
  4. Open on the strongest one. The payoff comes first, then you say what the recap covers.
  5. Trim hard. Cut pauses, chat reading, technical setup and repeated points.
  6. Add a re-hook at each section change. Use the moves in the table, with a spoken line or text.
  7. Title each section on screen. A short label tells viewers where they are and lets them skip to what they want.
  8. Keep it to one to three minutes. That is Wistia's general range for highlight reels; use longer only if your audience asks for it.
  9. Add captions. TechSmith notes captions make webinars accessible to more people than you might expect.
  10. End with one next step. The full recording, the slides or the next event, not three.

Example: a 50-minute marketing webinar

Example: a team has a 50-minute webinar with three speakers. The first recap plays the opening, the first speaker's first five minutes, then a few slides, and runs seven minutes. The second recap is two minutes. It opens on a speaker saying the one result that surprised the room, shows a title, "Three tactics that worked," and then moves through three moments of 25 to 30 seconds each. Before each, a line says what comes next ("The second one cost almost nothing"). It ends on the link to the full recording. This scenario uses invented details and is not a measured result.

When to keep it longer

If the audience wants the detail, such as a training recap for people who will use it, a longer cut with chapters is better than forcing it into two minutes. The same rules apply: open on value, trim, label sections and re-hook at the changes.

Common mistakes

  • Playing the webinar in order and calling it a recap.
  • Opening with the welcome and housekeeping.
  • Picking moments that need the rest of the webinar to make sense.
  • Adding too many next steps at the end.
