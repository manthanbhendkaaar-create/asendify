What music bed works under a cooking channel voiceover without drowning it?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 3 min read · Published 26 November 2026 · Last updated 26 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use an instrumental track with a steady, light rhythm and no vocals, mix it clearly below the voice, and lower it further on lines with quantities, times or temperatures. If a viewer cannot catch "180 degrees for 25 minutes", the music is too loud.

Key takeaways

  • There is no single best track. The best bed is one the viewer does not notice while the voice stays clear.
  • Google's ABCDs guidance says to support visuals with audio and text and to avoid competing elements; vocals under a voiceover compete.
  • TikTok's creative help recommends using sound or music in ads, which includes choosing it with care.
  • YouTube Help says dips show where viewers leave; compare sections where the music changes.
  • Use music you have the licence to use on the platform.

What a good bed does

It sets mood and tempo, fills silence between lines and gives edits a rhythm to land on. It should not carry a melody that competes with speech, and it should not change dramatically under a line the cook needs to hear.

Choosing and mixing

ChoiceGood optionAvoid
VocalsNoneLyrics under speech
RhythmSteady, lightBig drops and builds
InstrumentsAcoustic, soft keys, light percussionBusy lead melodies
Level under voiceClearly lower than the voiceSame level as voice
Key linesDuck the music furtherLeave it flat
LicenceLicensed or platform-clearedUnlicensed pop

Steps

  1. Edit the voiceover first, with breaths and pauses tidied.
  2. Pick a track that matches the pace of the cooking: slower for baking, brisker for a quick weeknight dish.
  3. Place it under the voice and set the level so speech is clear on phone speakers.
  4. Duck on key lines such as quantities and times.
  5. Raise the music slightly in wordless stretches like plating.
  6. Fade at the end and avoid cutting abruptly.
  7. Listen on a phone and on headphones.
  8. Check the licence for the platform you publish on.

Example: a 9-minute one-pan recipe

Example: the editor chooses a soft instrumental guitar track. The voice sits clearly above it. On "two tablespoons of oil, medium heat" the music drops a little more for three seconds. During the simmer, with no speech, the music rises slightly. A friend listens on a phone speaker and can follow every quantity. This scenario is illustrative and not a measured result.

Checks

Test on a small speaker. If you need to turn the volume up to hear quantities, lower the music. If a track repeats a noticeable phrase every few seconds, choose another.

How loud should the music be?

Clearly below the voice. Test on a phone speaker.

Should I use music with lyrics?

Not under speech; lyrics compete with the words.

Should I use the same track every video?

A consistent style helps recognition, but vary the track to avoid fatigue.

What about silence?

Short stretches of silence or kitchen sound can work well for sizzling or plating.

Is any music safe to use?

No. Use licensed or platform-cleared music only.
