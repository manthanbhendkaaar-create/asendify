How long should a personal finance YouTube video be for good retention?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 3 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

As long as the topic needs and no longer. There is no proven best length for personal finance videos, so use your own retention graph and your own topic. Plan extra re-hooks past the five-minute mark, where one large data set shows engagement declining, and remember that YouTube only offers mid-roll ads on monetised videos of 8 minutes or more. Key takeaways

  • Wistia's analysis says engagement "naturally declines after the five-minute mark" and that 1 to 5 minutes is the "sweet spot" for explaining a concept (Wistia). That is marketing video data, not finance-specific.
  • YouTube says "on monetized videos that are 8 minutes or longer, you can turn on ads during the middle of the video" (YouTube Help). Length affects ad options.
  • A flat retention line means "viewers are watching that part of your video from start to finish" and dips mean "abandoning or skipping" (YouTube Help); that is the real test of length.
  • Longer is not better by default. Mayer's segmenting principle says learning is better "in user-paced segments, rather than in one continuous unit" (Dartmouth); chapters help.

Length by question type

These are planning ranges from editing practice, not research findings. Test them on your channel.

How to decide

  1. List the questions the title promises to answer. Each one needs minutes.
  2. Write the script and time it. Read it aloud at your normal pace.
  3. Cut anything that does not help the title question. Tangents are where dips start.
  4. Add chapters if past about 8 minutes. They help viewers find the part they need.
  5. Publish and read the graph. Look at where average viewers leave.
  6. Compare with your recent videos of similar length. YouTube lets you compare typical retention for similar-length videos.
  7. Adjust next time. If viewers leave at minute 4 on every 12-minute video, test a tighter edit.

The 8-minute question

Because mid-roll ads need 8 minutes or more, some creators stretch videos. Padding hurts the video and the audience's trust. If a topic naturally takes 9 minutes, fine. If it takes 5, publish 5 minutes, which gives viewers more of what they wanted.

Example: ISA video, two versions

Example: a creator publishes "How do I start a Stocks and Shares ISA?" as a 14-minute video with a long personal story at the start. The retention graph drops steeply in the first two minutes. A later 9-minute version opens on the answer, moves the story to a short aside and adds seven chapters. The creator compares the graph for both and checks watch time, not just percentage. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result.

Is there a playbook on finance video length and structure?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and pattern interrupts with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. It does not set a fixed length.

Is 8 minutes the best length?

No. It is the minimum for mid-roll ads on monetised videos, not a quality target.

Do shorter videos get better retention?

Often the percentage is higher, but watch time and usefulness matter too.

Should I cut a long finance video into parts?

If the topics are separate, yes. Otherwise use chapters.

How do I check if my length is right?

Read the retention graph and compare with similar-length videos.
