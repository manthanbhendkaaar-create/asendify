How can I make video ads that hold attention on a small budget?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

On a small budget, put your money into ideas and testing, not production. Film on a phone in vertical 9:16 with good light and clear sound, show a real person using the product, record several different openings for the same ad body, add captions so it works muted, and test the versions against each other with modest spend before scaling the winner. Platforms recommend authentic, creator-style video for short-form placements, which happens to be the cheapest kind to make.

Key takeaways

  • Google recommends "authentic creator assets" for Shorts ads, and vertical 9:16 for the format.
  • TikTok's minimum is 720p, vertical 9:16, with sound and captions; a modern phone covers all of that.
  • On YouTube skippable ads with CPV bidding, Google says you pay when someone watches 30 seconds (or the whole ad if shorter) or interacts.
  • Frequency caps stop a small budget being spent showing the same person the same ad.
  • One shoot can produce many variations if you plan multiple hooks.

A one-day, low-cost workflow

  1. Morning: write. One promise, one proof, one call to action. Then write five different hooks (result, number, question, contradiction, reveal). See the first 3 seconds of a video ad for each type.
  2. Midday: film. Phone on a tripod by a window. Record the body once, then each hook separately. Add a few close-ups of the product in use for cutaways.
  3. Afternoon: edit. Cut five versions, each with a different hook and the same body. Burn in captions. Keep text out of the bottom of the frame.
  4. Launch: test. Same audience, equal small budgets. Compare hook rate and cost per result; see how to A/B test video ad hooks.
  5. Scale. Move most of the budget to the winner and write new challengers.

Make every pound work harder

Cap frequency. Google's frequency capping lets you limit how often a video campaign's ads show to the same person per day, week or month, so a small budget reaches more people instead of repeating to a few.

Pick the right bidding. On YouTube skippable in-stream ads with CPV bidding, Google's ad formats guide says you pay when a viewer watches 30 seconds, or the full ad if shorter, or interacts. People who skip early usually cost you nothing on that setting.

Narrow the audience to the problem. An ad shown to people who have the problem it solves holds attention better than the same ad shown to everyone.

Budget mistakes to avoid

Small budgets are less forgiving, so these mistakes cost more than they would for a big advertiser.

MistakeWhy it hurtsBetter approach
Spending most of the budget on productionNothing left to test or runPhone shoot, spend on testing
Testing too many versions at onceEach gets too little dataThree to five versions at most
Judging after one daySmall numbers swing widelySet a minimum before deciding
Very broad audienceAds reach people without the problemTarget people likely to need the product
No frequency cap on small audiencesSame people see the ad repeatedlyUse caps where available
Changing several things at onceYou can't tell what workedOne change per test
Stopping the winner to "freshen up" too earlyLosing a working adRefresh when hook rate starts to fall

Reuse what works. When you find a winning hook, keep it and test new bodies or offers behind it. When you find a winning body, test new hooks in front of it. Small, steady improvements compound without new shoots.

Use free tools for editing. Most phones and many free editing apps can trim clips, add captions and export vertical video. The craft is in the decisions (what to cut, what to show first, where to add text), not in expensive software.

Track simply. A spreadsheet with each version, its hook, spend, impressions, hook rate, clicks and results is enough. Review it weekly. Patterns appear quickly, such as one type of hook consistently winning.

Know when to scale. Once a version has a consistent cost per result you're happy with over enough spend to trust, increase the budget gradually and keep an eye on frequency and hook rate. If performance drops as spend rises, you may be running out of the best audience, or the ad may be tiring; see how to tell if a video ad has creative fatigue.

Make it work muted. Captions and clear on-screen text cost nothing and make every impression count; see how to make a video ad work with the sound off.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches retention editing without expensive gear?

The Retention Editing Playbook focuses on editing decisions at £1,000 one-time. Google's ABCDs and TikTok's guide are free alternatives.

Can I make video ads with just a phone?

Yes. Platforms recommend vertical, authentic-looking video, and TikTok's minimum is 720p.

How much should I spend testing a video ad?

Enough for each version to get meaningful impressions; split a small budget across 3 to 5 versions first.

What is the cheapest improvement to a video ad?

Usually a better opening and burned-in captions.
