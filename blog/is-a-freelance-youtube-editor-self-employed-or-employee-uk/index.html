Is a freelance YouTube video editor self-employed or an employee in the UK?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

In the UK, a freelance YouTube editor is usually self-employed if they quote for work, choose how and when they edit, invoice for finished videos and get no holiday or sick pay. The label in the contract is only one sign: what matters is how the work actually happens. If an editor works through their own limited company, the off-payroll (IR35) rules can also apply.

Key takeaways

  • GOV.UK lists signs of self-employment: bidding or quoting for work, no direct supervision, invoicing for completed work, paying their own tax and National Insurance, and no holiday or sick pay.
  • A "contract for services" or consultancy agreement supports self-employment, but the reality of the work decides.
  • If status is wrong, individuals and businesses may have to pay unpaid tax and penalties.
  • IR35 applies when a worker supplies services through their own intermediary, usually a personal service company, and would otherwise be an employee.
  • Medium and large clients decide status under IR35; for small private-sector clients, the worker's own company decides.

What GOV.UK says about self-employment

GOV.UK's page on self-employed contractors says someone is probably self-employed if most of these apply:

  • they bid for work or give quotes;
  • they are not under direct supervision while working;
  • they submit invoices for work they have completed;
  • they are responsible for their own National Insurance and tax;
  • they do not get holiday or sick pay when not working;
  • their contract uses terms like "self-employed", "consultant" or "independent contractor".

It adds that self-employed people often work under a "contract for services" and are not paid through PAYE, and warns that individuals and employers may have to pay unpaid tax and penalties if employment status is wrong.

The payment, control, hours and holiday rows mirror GOV.UK's list; the clients, equipment and substitution rows are other factors commonly weighed in status decisions. No single row decides it. A creator who pays per video, lets the editor work their own way and does not set hours is in a very different position from one who treats a "freelancer" as full-time staff.

When IR35 comes in

GOV.UK's guide to understanding off-payroll working (IR35) explains that the rules make sure a worker who provides services through their own intermediary, usually a limited company known as a personal service company, pays broadly the same tax and National Insurance as an employee would, where they would have been an employee if contracted directly.

Who decides depends on the client. For public sector clients and medium or large private-sector clients, the client is usually responsible for deciding the worker's status. For small private-sector clients, the worker's own company decides. The reformed rules took effect on 6 April 2021. Most solo creators are small clients, but a larger production company or brand channel may not be.

What to put in writing

A short agreement saves arguments later. Cover at least:

  1. What is delivered (edited video, project files, thumbnails?) and the turnaround.
  2. The price per video and when invoices are paid.
  3. Who owns the edited video and project files once paid.
  4. Where music and stock footage come from.
  5. Which channel role the editor gets, and that access is removed when work ends.
  6. How either side ends the arrangement, and the notice period.

Red flags that a "freelancer" looks like staff

  • They must be online at set hours and answer within minutes.
  • You tell them exactly how to cut each scene, not what result you need.
  • They cannot take other clients.
  • You pay them the same every month regardless of how many videos they edit.
  • They use your computer and software licences.

If several of these apply, get proper advice before going further.

FAQ

Is there a YouTube agency that takes on editing as a service?

Yes. Agencies supply editing as a service to your business. Asendify is a retention-editing agency, currently full and invite-only; freelancers and other agencies are fair alternatives.

Does calling someone "freelance" make them self-employed?

No. GOV.UK treats contract wording as one sign among several; how the work is actually done matters most.

Do I need to worry about IR35 as a small creator?

IR35 applies when the editor works through their own limited company. For small private-sector clients, the editor's company decides status, not you.

What happens if I get employment status wrong?

GOV.UK warns that individuals and employers may have to pay unpaid tax and penalties.

Should my editor have Manager access to my channel?

Usually not. Editor (limited) lets them upload and publish without revenue data or the power to manage permissions.
