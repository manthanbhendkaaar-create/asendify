Should I send my audio-only podcast to YouTube by RSS, or make a real video version?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

If you have no video, sending your audio podcast to YouTube by RSS is a low-effort way to be listed there: YouTube turns each episode into a static-image video using your show art, and it's available on YouTube and YouTube Music. But a static image gives people on YouTube nothing to watch, so if you want viewers (and clips, Shorts and recommendations that feed off watch time), a real video edit, even a simple one, is the better long-term choice. Many shows do both: RSS for the back catalogue, video for new episodes.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's RSS delivery is for "audio-first podcast creators"; it "will use your podcast's show art to create a static-image video" for each episode.
  • It's limited to select countries and regions, and ownership is checked through the email on your feed.
  • YouTube says podcast content uploaded this way "cannot contain advertisements", and branded content such as host-read promotions must be declared.
  • An RSS video's audio "cannot be updated once published"; replacing an episode creates a new video.
  • A static image is easy to listen to in the background but gives viewers no visual reason to stay.

All RSS facts below come from YouTube's help page Deliver podcasts using an RSS feed unless another source is linked.

How RSS delivery works

  1. You submit your feed in YouTube Studio and verify ownership via the feed's email.
  2. You choose which back-catalogue episodes to upload. To avoid duplicates, YouTube recommends only uploading episodes newer than the latest one already on your channel.
  3. Episodes uploaded during setup start as private; once the podcast is published, new episodes are public by default (you can change the default to private or unlisted).
  4. New episodes in your feed are uploaded automatically. YouTube won't notify subscribers about back-catalogue additions.

Things to know:

  • Show detail changes in your feed don't update automatically.
  • Deleting the video on YouTube doesn't remove the episode from your feed, and it will be re-uploaded unless you remove it there too.
  • Titles and descriptions can't contain HTML or characters like "<" and ">".

When RSS is the right choice

  • You have a big back catalogue of audio episodes and no footage.
  • Your audience mostly listens (commuters, walkers), and you want to be findable in YouTube Music.
  • You're testing whether YouTube brings any listeners before investing in cameras.
  • Your format is audio-native (fiction, soundscapes, narrated stories) where video would add little.

When to make a real video version

  • You want people to watch on YouTube's main app, where viewers can see when nothing changes on screen.
  • You want Shorts and clips as discovery.
  • Your hosts and guests are personable on camera.

A simple step up from a static image: two cameras (or a remote recording tool), cutting to whoever is speaking, with captions on key lines and on-screen text for names and figures.

Worked example

Example: a history podcast has 180 audio episodes. The host sends the RSS feed to YouTube for the back catalogue so the whole archive is listed in YouTube Music. From episode 181, the show records video and uploads it directly, with a cold open, chapters and two Shorts per episode. The RSS cutoff date is set so the new video episodes aren't duplicated as static-image versions. Details are illustrative.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches video podcast editing for retention?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers cold opens and pacing for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's RSS help page is free.

What does an RSS podcast look like on YouTube?

YouTube creates a static-image video for each episode using your show art.

Can my RSS podcast episodes include ads on YouTube?

YouTube says podcast content uploaded this way cannot contain advertisements; host-read promotions and sponsorships must be declared.

Can I fix the audio of an RSS episode on YouTube?

No. The audio can't be updated once published; re-uploading creates a new video and makes the old one private.

Will RSS put my podcast on Spotify too?

No. YouTube says it's only available on YouTube and YouTube Music.

Is RSS delivery available everywhere?

No. YouTube limits it to select countries and regions.
