What is TikTok's Commercial Music Library, and can brands use it in video ads?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

TikTok's Commercial Music Library (CML) is a collection of songs and sound effects that TikTok has pre-cleared for businesses to use in ads and branded content on TikTok. Yes, brands can use it in TikTok video ads without buying a separate licence. What brands cannot do is use trending sounds from TikTok's general library, which are for personal use. Using a CML track outside TikTok needs its own licence check.

Key takeaways

  • TikTok's help centre calls the CML "a pre-cleared global music library of 1 million songs" and says "Businesses should instead use the Commercial Music Library for all commercial TikTok activities" (TikTok Ads Help).
  • An earlier TikTok business blog post described "more than 600,000 tracks pre-cleared for royalty-free use in branded content and ads", so the library has grown over time (TikTok for Business).
  • TikTok is clear that "Businesses cannot use the general music library for commercial usage."
  • Soundstripe's licensing guide says "Trending sounds in TikTok's general library are licensed for personal use only" and that mainstream chart hits are "largely absent" from the CML (Soundstripe, updated November 2026).
  • The TikTok pages above describe the CML for TikTok activity. They do not say it covers Meta, YouTube or TV, so do not assume it does.

Why music matters for ad retention on TikTok

TikTok is a sound-on platform. TikTok's business blog cites a Kantar study in which TikTok was the only platform where ads with audio produced "significant lifts in both purchase intent and brand favorability." That is TikTok's own cited research, so treat it as a platform claim, but it matches what editors see: on TikTok, music sets pace, signals mood in the first second and helps cuts land.

A good CML track for a retention edit has:

  • A clear start. No long fade-in. The energy should be there at frame one.
  • A beat you can cut to. Cuts on the beat feel faster without adding more of them.
  • Space for voice. If the ad has a spoken hook, choose a track without busy vocals in the first 5 seconds.
  • A natural end or loop point. So a 15- or 21-second cut does not end mid-phrase.

How editors find tracks in the CML

  1. Search inside TikTok Ads Manager or Creative Center with filters such as genre, mood, theme and length. TikTok's blog notes "only 10,000 songs can be displayed at a time" in Creative Center, so use filters and search rather than scrolling.
  2. Shortlist 3-5 tracks that match the ad's pace.
  3. Lay the hook on each track and watch the first 3 seconds with sound. Pick the one where the hook lands hardest.
  4. Record the track title and source in the project notes, in case the client asks later.

Example: replacing a trending sound on a client's ad

Example: a coffee brand's in-house team posts a TikTok ad using a trending song from the general library. Their agency is asked to put it into paid rotation.

StepWhat the agency does
1Flags that the trending sound is for personal use, not commercial use
2Searches the CML for an upbeat track of a similar tempo with no lyrics in the first 4 seconds
3Re-times the cuts so the first three land on the new beat
4Keeps the voice-over hook louder than the music in the first 3 seconds
5For the Meta and YouTube versions, licenses a track from a stock music subscription that covers those platforms

The client ends up with one concept, two music beds: one for TikTok from the CML and one for other platforms. This is an illustrative scenario.

What about creators making ads for brands?

TikTok's help page lists branded content among commercial activities, so creator content made for a brand should also use commercially cleared music. If a brand plans to run a creator's post as a paid ad, check the music in the original post before boosting it. A post with a general-library sound is a problem once it becomes an ad.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on pacing video ads to music?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and pattern interrupts with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. TikTok's Creative Center is a free place to find cleared tracks.

Is the Commercial Music Library free?

Yes. TikTok describes the songs as free for businesses to use on TikTok.

Can I use CML music in Instagram Reels or YouTube ads?

TikTok's help and blog pages on the CML do not say so. Read the CML terms or license a separate track for other platforms.

Are popular chart songs in the CML?

Mostly not. Licensing guides note mainstream chart hits are largely absent.

Do I need a Business Account to use the CML?

TikTok's blog says the library is reached in-app through a Business Account, and it is also available in Ads Manager and Creative Center.

This is general information, not legal advice. For high-spend campaigns, check music rights with a lawyer.
