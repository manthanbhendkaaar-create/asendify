Why do viewers leave video sales letters right after the hook, and how do I fix it?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Viewers usually leave a video sales letter (VSL) straight after the hook because the next 20 to 60 seconds break its promise: the hook raises a sharp question, then the video switches to a long personal backstory, a list of credentials, or vague claims before giving anything useful. The fix is to pay off part of the hook quickly with something concrete, open the next question before the first is fully answered, and keep every claim specific and provable.

Key takeaways

  • The drop right after the hook is a broken-promise problem, not a hook problem. YouTube's retention report calls these moments "dips", where "viewers are abandoning or skipping".
  • Get to the point. Facebook's video guidance, as reported by Search Engine Journal, says "having the ad's message up front makes it easier for consumers to get the key details", and to keep interest with "more peaks in the story via twists and surprises".
  • Vague or inflated claims drive sceptical viewers away and can break the rules. The UK CAP Code says marketers "must hold documentary evidence to prove claims that consumers are likely to regard as objective".
  • Fake urgency is a trust killer. The CMA warns that countdown timers are "likely to be misleading" where there is "in fact no limit to the time".
  • Move the backstory later and shorter; lead with a demonstration or a specific insight.

How to rebuild the first 90 seconds

  1. Hook (0 to 10 s): state the problem or surprising idea clearly.
  2. Partial payoff (10 to 30 s): give one concrete, useful piece: a quick demonstration, a specific insight, a before-and-after. This proves the video is worth watching.
  3. New open question (30 to 45 s): introduce what's still unanswered: "That fixes the first part. The bigger problem is..."
  4. Brief credibility (45 to 60 s): one relevant reason to trust you, tied to the point, not a list.
  5. Main body: continue alternating answer and new question. Keep the offer for after the value has been shown.

For where those questions should sit, see where open loops go in a video sales letter.

Finding the exact point people leave

  • On YouTube, open the retention graph and find the steepest drop after the first 10 seconds. Watch what happens on screen at that timestamp.
  • On landing pages, most video hosts show a drop-off curve; look for the same pattern.
  • In ads, compare "video played to 25%" with 3-second views to see how many stay past the hook.
  • Change one thing at a time: move the backstory, shorten the credentials, or add the partial payoff, then compare.

Claims and urgency: keep them honest

VSLs often lean on income claims, testimonials and countdowns. Each can cost trust and can breach rules:

  • Results claims need evidence; avoid implying untypical results are normal.
  • Testimonials must be genuine and used with permission; the ASA requires documentary evidence.
  • Deadlines and scarcity must be real; timed offers should end when stated.

For overall length decisions, see how long a video sales letter should be, and on openings, should a VSL have an intro at all.

A quick script check for the first minute

Read the first minute of your VSL script aloud and mark each sentence with one of three labels: "gives something" (a fact, step, example or demonstration), "asks something" (a question the viewer now wants answered) or "neither". Any run of three "neither" sentences in a row is a likely drop-off point. Rewrite or cut them before you film. Then check that the hook's exact promise is visibly addressed before the first minute ends. This takes ten minutes and prevents most of the drops that otherwise appear in the retention curve after launch.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops after the hook?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks and open loops for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's help pages are a free alternative.

Should a VSL start with my story?

Usually not. Give a useful insight first; a short story works better once viewers care.

How fast should the hook be paid off?

Give a partial, concrete payoff within roughly the first 30 seconds, then open the next question.

Do countdown timers help VSLs?

Only real deadlines are acceptable. The CMA says fake timers are likely to be misleading.

How do I know the drop is after the hook and not during it?

Check the curve: a drop in the first few seconds is a hook problem; a steep drop at 15 to 60 seconds is a body problem.
