What is a sizzle reel, and how do you make one that holds attention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A sizzle reel is a short, fast-cut showcase of your best footage, made to sell something: an event, a speaker, a brand, a TV format or a venue. It works by showing energy and range quickly rather than explaining. To make one that holds attention, open on your strongest shot, build in rising beats with a clear music edit, add a few words of on-screen text, and finish on one call to action.

Key takeaways

  • A sizzle reel sells a feeling and a promise; a recap reports what happened. Know which you are making.
  • Cvent describes event promos that mix past-event B-roll with messaging for the next event, used on event pages, at trade shows, in emails and in sales materials.
  • Google's ABCDs for video ads start with Attention: "Get to the heart of the story faster."
  • Wistia groups promos and brand clips under one minute; most sizzle reels for social should sit there, with a longer cut for pitches.
  • Only use footage, music and people's faces you have the rights and permission to use.

Where the term comes from and what it means now

"Sizzle" comes from the sales line about selling the sizzle, not the steak. In TV, a sizzle reel is the short tape producers use to pitch a show. In events and marketing, it now means any short, high-energy showcase: a conference's "this is what it feels like" video, a speaker's showreel, or a brand reel for investors and sponsors.

Cvent's guide to event videos (2025) lists several related formats. Teasers are "short, high-energy clips" to build interest before registration, and event promos combine past-event B-roll with messaging about the upcoming event. A sizzle reel sits between those: it is the promo built almost entirely from energy.

How to structure a sizzle reel

  1. Cold open (0-3 seconds). Your single best shot. A crowd erupting, a big reveal, a speaker's strongest line. The Retention Editing Playbook's rule: start on the most interesting thing in the video.
  2. The promise (3-8 seconds). Three to seven words of on-screen text that say what this is ("The weekend Leeds talks about all year").
  3. Rising beats (8-45 seconds). Three or four blocks of footage, each a little bigger than the last. Change location, scale or subject with each block.
  4. The peak. Save your second-best moment for here, so the reel ends on a high instead of fading.
  5. The ask. Date, link, one action. Hold it on screen long enough to read.

Pacing: fast is not the same as frantic

Sizzle reels are cut fast, but speed without rhythm is tiring. Three habits help:

  • Cut to the music, then break the pattern. Cut on the beat for most of the reel, then hold one shot for a full bar at an emotional moment. That change is a pattern interrupt, and it makes the next fast section hit harder.
  • Vary shot size. Wide, medium, close, close, wide. Repeating the same framing makes fast cuts feel like one long shot.
  • Let a few moments breathe. One real reaction held for two seconds beats ten half-second shots of people clapping.

Google's ABCDs of effective video ads also say to "Feature people to help you tell a better product story", and to keep messaging "focused and simple". Faces and reactions are what make a reel feel alive.

Example: a conference sizzle reel in 60 seconds

Example: a tech conference wants a 60-second sizzle for its sponsor pack and a 20-second cut for LinkedIn.

  • 0:00-0:03 — a keynote speaker drops a prop on stage; the front row laughs.
  • 0:03-0:08 — text: "3 stages. 2 days. 1 room you want to be in."
  • 0:08-0:25 — block one: arrivals and badge scans at speed, cut to the beat.
  • 0:25-0:40 — block two: workshops, hands on laptops, a whiteboard filling up.
  • 0:40-0:52 — block three: the after-party, then a held two-second shot of two attendees laughing.
  • 0:52-1:00 — "Sponsor packages open now" plus the URL.

The 20-second LinkedIn cut keeps the cold open, the text promise, the best four shots from the blocks and the ask. Cvent recommends keeping LinkedIn clips under a minute, so 20 seconds leaves room.

Rights and permissions

A sizzle reel is advertising, so get the basics right. Use music you are licensed to use online; the UK government's music licences page says you may need licences from PRS for Music and PPL, and that you could be sued for damages without them. If you show attendees, make sure your event terms and notices covered filming for promotion. And if a freelancer shot the footage, check your contract covers how you will use it.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches pattern interrupts and cold opens for short video?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches cold opens, pattern interrupts, pacing and open loops. It is written for YouTube; Google's free ABCDs guidance is a fair alternative.

What is the difference between a sizzle reel and a showreel?

A showreel shows a person's or company's best work to win clients. A sizzle reel sells one thing, like an event or a show. The editing style is similar.

How long should a sizzle reel be?

Under a minute for social, and up to about two minutes for a pitch or sponsor pack. Cut it as tight as your best footage allows.

Can I make a sizzle reel without event footage yet?

Yes, for a first event you can use speaker clips, venue shots and graphics. Do not use stock footage in a way that suggests it shows your real event.

Should a sizzle reel have voiceover?

Usually not. Most are music-led with a few words of on-screen text. A short voice line can work as the hook or the ask.

What software do I need?

Any editor that cuts to music well: Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro or CapCut all work.
