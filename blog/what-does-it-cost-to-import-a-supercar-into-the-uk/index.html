What does it cost to import a supercar into the UK?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Importing a supercar into Great Britain means paying VAT on the full cost of the car, including delivery and any customs duty, plus customs duty itself if it comes from outside the UK (the rate depends on the vehicle and where it was made). You must tell HMRC within 14 days of arrival, get the car approved for UK roads, and register it with the DVLA for a £55 fee before driving it.

This page is general information, not financial or legal advice. Confirm rates with HMRC, a customs agent or your shipping company.

Key takeaways

  • Notify HMRC within 14 days of the car arriving; self-importers using the Channel Tunnel or ferry use the NOVA service.
  • VAT is charged on the car's total cost plus accessories, delivery and other charges, plus customs duty; it must be paid before registration.
  • Customs duty applies to cars brought into Great Britain from outside the UK; rates depend on vehicle type and origin.
  • Approval: EU-registered cars need a Certificate of Conformity, non-EU cars need Individual Vehicle Approval (IVA), and left-hand-drive EU cars a £100 GB conversion IVA; some cars over 10 years old are exempt.
  • DVLA registration costs £55 and the V5C can take up to 6 weeks; you can be prosecuted for driving before completing the steps, except to a pre-booked test.

The steps

GOV.UK's guide to importing vehicles into the UK sets out what to do when bringing a vehicle permanently into Great Britain:

StepWhat happensWho usually does it
1. Import declarationDeclares the car to customsShipping company or customs agent
2. Tell HMRCWithin 14 days of arrival (NOVA if you drive it in yourself)You or your agent
3. Pay VAT and dutyUsually at the borderShipping company or agent
4. Vehicle approvalShows the car meets UK safety and environmental standardsYou, via the manufacturer or DVSA test
5. Register and taxDVLA issues a registration numberYou
6. InsureBefore driving on UK roadsYou

You must not use the car on a public road until these steps are done, except to drive to a pre-booked MOT or approval test. Damaged or salvaged cars may not be registrable at all, so check before buying one abroad.

VAT and customs duty

GOV.UK's page on paying VAT and duty on imported vehicles explains that VAT is charged on the total cost of the vehicle plus any accessories, delivery and extra charges, and customs duty. Customs duty applies to vehicles imported into England, Wales and Scotland from outside the UK. Rates depend on the type of vehicle and its country of origin, so ask your shipping company, a customs agent or HMRC for the figure for your car. VAT-registered businesses can reclaim the VAT through their VAT return, and people moving to the UK with their own car, or bringing back a car they exported, may qualify for relief from VAT and duty.

VAT and duty must be paid before you can register the car.

Approval and registration

GOV.UK's page on getting vehicle approval lists the routes:

Car's originApproval routeCost noted by GOV.UK
EU-registeredEuropean Certificate of Conformity from the manufacturerManufacturer's charge
EU-registered, left-hand driveGB conversion IVA£100
Not registered in the EU (for example Japan, US, UAE)Individual Vehicle Approval (IVA)Test fee
First registered or made more than 10 years agoMay be exempt (check the list)n/a

Then register with the DVLA. GOV.UK's page on registering an imported vehicle says you send original documents, including proof of approval, evidence of the collection date such as the supplier's invoice and the original foreign registration certificate (which you will not get back), with proof of VAT and duty paid. The fee is £55, the V5C can take up to 6 weeks, and the DVLA may inspect the car.

He also has to live with a left-hand-drive car, which can affect resale value and insurance; our page on what it costs to own a supercar covers running costs, and how much road tax is on a supercar covers VED.

When importing can make sense

  • The model was never sold in the UK or is far cheaper abroad after all costs.
  • You are moving to the UK with your own car and qualify for relief.
  • It is an older classic that may be exempt from approval; check the rules first.

For most mainstream supercars, a UK-supplied right-hand-drive car avoids approval work and is easier to sell later. If you do import, get your agent's full quote, including duty, before you pay for the car.

How long do I have to tell HMRC about an imported car?

14 days from when it arrives in the UK.

Do I pay VAT on an imported supercar?

Yes, on the car's total cost including delivery and any customs duty, before you can register it.

How much is customs duty on a car?

It depends on the vehicle type and country of origin. Ask your shipping company, a customs agent or HMRC.

Does an imported car need an IVA test?

Cars not registered in the EU usually need Individual Vehicle Approval. EU cars need a Certificate of Conformity, and left-hand-drive EU cars a £100 GB conversion IVA. Some cars over 10 years old are exempt.

How much does it cost to register an imported car with the DVLA?

£55, and the V5C can take up to 6 weeks to arrive.
