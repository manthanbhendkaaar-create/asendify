How do I make a webinar recording into an evergreen automated webinar people watch?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 26 November 2026 · Last updated 26 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

To turn a live webinar into an evergreen one, cut the dead time, rebuild the opening so it works without a live host, remove anything time-bound, and split the rest into clear sections. Be open that it is a recording. Then watch where viewers leave and edit again.

Key takeaways

  • ON24's 2026 benchmarks report says 43% of attendees watched on-demand webinars (67% joined live, per the report's body), and that average engagement duration held at 49 minutes. The source's overview and body differ on some figures, so treat them as approximate.
  • Wistia's State of Video analysis says engagement declines after the 5-minute mark and drops 11% once videos pass 30 minutes, and that viewers who stay are more likely to act.
  • Mayer's segmenting principle (median effect size 0.67 in 7 of 7 tests) favours learner-paced chunks over one continuous unit.
  • Do not present a recording as live. Say it is a replay.

Why a raw recording is not an evergreen webinar

A live webinar depends on timing: a countdown, "welcome to those still joining", a poll, a live question. In a recording those moments are dead air or confusing. An evergreen version has to make sense on any day, for someone who clicks play alone.

ON24's data suggests on-demand viewing is common, though live attendance is still larger. Wistia's data suggests engagement falls with length, so the talk itself should be tightened. The goal is not to fake a live event; it is to give viewers a good replay.

Steps to make it

  1. Watch the whole recording and write down timestamps for dead time, strong moments and time-bound claims.
  2. Write a new opening. Say who it is for and what they will get. Use the strongest 10 to 20 seconds of the talk as a preview.
  3. Cut the waiting, welcome and housekeeping.
  4. Remove or reword anything dated. "Today", "this quarter", "this week".
  5. Chapter the content. Give each section a plain title.
  6. Rebuild slides that are hard to read or add text overlays for key figures.
  7. Handle Q&A. Keep three or four of the best exchanges, with the question shown on screen.
  8. Add a clear ending. One action and one link.
  9. Say it is a recording. A line such as "This is a replay of a session we ran" avoids misleading viewers.
  10. Publish, then check where viewers leave in the host's analytics and re-edit that stretch.

Example: a 62-minute product webinar

Example: a software firm has a recording of 62 minutes. Seven minutes are waiting and welcome, six are a live poll, and the rest are three teaching sections plus 14 minutes of questions. The editor makes a 12-second opening from the best demo moment, cuts the first 13 minutes, removes the poll, keeps four questions and ends on a single sign-up link. The final video is 41 minutes with four chapters. The offer slide with an end date is replaced by plain terms. This scenario uses invented numbers and is not a measured result.

Think about length

Wistia says engagement declines after 5 minutes and drops after 30, but viewers who stay are more likely to act. A 41-minute replay can be fine if the audience is warm and the sections are clear. If viewers leave early, make shorter clips for the best parts and link to the full replay.

Be honest about the format

Some evergreen systems pretend the event is live. Do not do that. It can mislead viewers and can create legal and platform risks. Check the consumer-protection rules in your market and the terms of your webinar tool.

Should I pretend an evergreen webinar is live?

No. Say it is a replay. Pretending can mislead viewers.

How long should an evergreen webinar be?

There is no single number. Wistia says engagement declines after 5 minutes and drops after 30, so cut dead time and use chapters.

What do I do with the live Q&A?

Keep a few strong answers with the question on screen, and cut the rest.

Should I keep the offer?

Yes if it is true and stays valid. Remove fake deadlines.
