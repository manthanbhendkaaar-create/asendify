Where should open loops go in Facebook and Instagram video ads?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

In the very first frame, written on screen, and closed by about 10 to 15 seconds. On Facebook and Instagram nobody has to wait for a skip button; they simply scroll past, often with the sound off. So the question has to be visible before anyone hears a word, and the payoff should land around the point where Meta counts a ThruPlay: 15 seconds, or the whole video if shorter.

Key takeaways

  • Feed and Reels ads have no grace period. The loop must be open in frame one.
  • Write the loop on screen. Facebook found captions raised average view duration by 12%, as reported by Social Media Today.
  • The same report cites a Facebook-commissioned Nielsen study finding nearly half of a video ad's value comes in the first 3 seconds, so the brand belongs inside the loop, not after it.
  • ThruPlay billing counts 97% completion or up to 15 seconds, whichever is sooner. Close your main loop before then.
  • Meta's own creator guidance says popular Reels often have "a strong hook or payoff", such as a reveal or plot twist. An open loop is how you connect the two.

Why Meta ads need a different loop from YouTube ads

On a YouTube in-stream ad, everyone sees the first 5 seconds before they can skip. On Meta, the viewer is in control from the first frame: thumb on the screen, autoplay often muted, your ad between a friend's holiday photo and a news post. That changes three things:

  1. Timing. There is no guaranteed audience for second 4. The loop must open in second 0.
  2. Channel. Many people see your first seconds without sound. A loop that lives only in the voiceover doesn't exist for them.
  3. Format. Reels and Stories are vertical and full-screen; feed is 4:5 or square. Text needs to sit in the safe area of each.

Where each loop goes in a 15-second Reels or feed ad

TimeWhat's on screenLoop status
Frame 1 (0 to 1 s)Striking image plus text question ("Why does this jar float?")Main loop opened
1 to 3 sProduct and brand visible, context in textLoop sharpened

For a 30 to 60 second ad, add a small second loop at about 10 to 12 seconds ("and the second reason is the one most people miss") and close it by 25 seconds.

Placement mistakes to avoid

  • Opening on a logo slate. Brand presence is good; a static logo with no question is a scroll signal.
  • Burying the loop under platform buttons. On Reels, captions and buttons cover the bottom and right edge. Keep text in the centre.
  • A loop that resolves at 25 seconds in a 15-second-optimised campaign. Most people never reach the answer.
  • Clickbait loops. A tease the product can't deliver gets negative feedback, wastes spend and may break advertising rules if the claim isn't true.
  • Unclosed loops. End every ad with the question answered. Viewers should leave satisfied, not tricked.

How to check if the loop is working

In Ads Manager, compare 3-second views, ThruPlays and the video retention curve across versions. If many people stop at the same second, the loop either opened too late or closed too early. FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops for Reels and short ads?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook teaches open loops and hooks for £1,720 one-time; it is YouTube-first but applies to Reels.

Should the open loop be in text or voiceover?

Both, but text first. Many viewers see the opening without sound, so a voice-only loop is lost on them.

When should I close the loop in a Meta ad?

Usually by 10 to 15 seconds, before the ThruPlay point, so most viewers who stay get the payoff.

Can I use more than one open loop?

In ads under 20 seconds, one is enough. Longer ads can add a small second loop around 10 to 12 seconds.

Does the brand go before or after the loop?

Inside it. Show the product and brand in the opening seconds so even people who scroll away saw it.
