How do I keep people watching my Snapchat Spotlight videos?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Post original vertical videos with sound, open on the most interesting moment rather than a greeting, add captions so the Snap works on mute, tag a #topic, and keep the edit moving. Just as important, avoid what Snap will not recommend at all: blurry or sideways footage, videos that are missing audio, and reposts of other people's content you have not transformed.

Key takeaways

  • Snap's creator site says Spotlight Snaps "should be vertical videos with sound"; photos, horizontal, blurry and text-only Snaps will not appear.
  • To be recommended beyond your friends and subscribers, a Snap must meet Snap's Content Guidelines for Recommendation Eligibility.
  • Those guidelines treat blurry footage, wrong orientation, missing audio, unoriginal reposts and repeated near-identical posts as reasons not to recommend.
  • Snap's creator advice is to "start strong" with a compelling hook and use captions to reach sound-off viewers.
  • Adding a #topic helps people find Snaps like yours.

What Snap says Spotlight needs

Snap's creator hub is direct about the basics: Spotlight Snaps "should be vertical videos with sound", and still photos, horizontal Snaps, blurry Snaps and text-only Snaps will not appear in Spotlight. It suggests creative tools such as captions, Sounds, Lenses and GIFs, and adding a #topic on the Send To page. Its Stories advice applies just as well to Spotlight openings: "Open your story every day with a strong and compelling hook", and use captions "to appeal to a sound-off viewer".

Snap's Spotlight Guidelines say content must meet its recommendation standards to be shown beyond your friends or subscribers. The quality section of those standards lists what holds a Snap back.

Snap's quality ruleExampleWhat to do instead
Substandard video qualityBlurry, low-resolution or pixelatedShoot in good light, export at full quality

Why people tap past

Spotlight is a full-screen feed, so leaving costs one swipe. People go when the first second shows nothing, when they cannot tell what the Snap is about, when the sound starts late, or when the middle drags. A Snap that is filmed sideways or arrives silent may never be recommended in the first place.

An edit that keeps people watching

  1. First frame: the action. The trick mid-air, the dish being cut, the dog already sliding across the floor.
  2. First second: the promise. One caption line: "Wait for the landing" or "Cheapest lunch in London?"
  3. Sound from frame one. Music or voice should start with the picture.
  4. Cut every pause. Trim the walk-up, the set-up chat and the moment after the payoff.
  5. Keep a reason to stay. Count down ("3 places, the last is best") or hold the reveal for the end.
  6. Finish on the payoff. End on the reaction, not on you reaching for the phone.
  7. Tag a #topic. It helps Snapchatters exploring that topic find you.

How to check what worked

Snap does not publish a ranking formula for Spotlight, so judge your own Snaps against each other. Compare views and how long people watched for Snaps on similar topics, change one thing at a time (the opening shot, the caption line or the sound), and give each Snap a few days before deciding. If a Snap gets almost no views beyond friends, check it against the quality rules above first: orientation, audio and originality problems stop a Snap before the edit is ever tested.

Can I post horizontal videos to Spotlight?

Snap says horizontal Snaps will not appear in Spotlight. Film vertical.

Does a Spotlight Snap need sound?

Yes. Snap says Spotlight Snaps should be vertical videos with sound, and lists missing audio as a quality problem.

Can I repost my TikToks on Spotlight?

Posting your own work is allowed, but Snap does not recommend content you did not create or transform, or the same content posted repeatedly with minimal changes.
