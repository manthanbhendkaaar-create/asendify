Key takeaways

  • Training works best when it's tied to client work, not separate from it.
  • Free platform guides set the baseline: Google's ABCDs ("Hook and sustain attention") and TikTok's creative tips (hook "in the first 6 seconds").
  • Teach editors to read retention data. YouTube explains that "dips mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping".
  • A shared method reduces revision rounds because editors and account managers use the same words.

What agency editors need to learn

Most agency editors are already good at software. Retention training covers decisions, not tools:

  1. Finding the hook in the footage. Choosing the first frame and first line.
  2. Structuring a short ad or a long video. Hook, body, payoff, call to action.
  3. Cutting for attention. Removing repetition, tightening pauses, adding visual change.
  4. Open loops and re-hooks. Giving viewers a reason to keep watching.
  5. Platform differences. Sound-off feeds, safe zones, aspect ratios.
  6. Reading data. Early-view rates on ads, retention graphs on YouTube.

A 4-week training plan

WeekFocusExercise
1Hooks and first framesEach editor recuts the opening of three past client videos; team picks the strongest
2Structure and pacingRough-cut a new project to a fixed structure; peer review on a phone
3Open loops and re-hooksAdd one loop and one re-hook to a long video; mark them on the timeline
4Data reviewEach editor presents one video's retention numbers and what they'd change

Keep sessions short, around 45 minutes, and use real client footage where the contract allows.

Mistakes in editor training

  • Teaching tools instead of decisions. Editors already know the software.
  • No real data. Theory without numbers doesn't change habits.
  • One-off workshops. Retention habits form over weeks of practice.
  • Different rules from different seniors. Write the rules down once.

Measuring whether training worked

Training is only worth the time if it changes the work. Pick a small set of measures before you start, and record a baseline from the month before training.

MeasureHow to collectWhat good looks like
Revision rounds per videoProject tool or trackerFewer rounds over time
Early-view rate on adsPlatform reportsMoving up against the editor's own past work
Intro retention on YouTubeYouTube Studio, vs typical retentionMoving up on comparable videos
QA checklist failuresQA logFewer repeat failures per editor
Time to first approved cutProject toolShorter for new editors

Look at trends over two or three months, not week to week. Client mix changes, seasons change and some briefs are harder than others.

Share results with the team in the monthly review. Editors respond well to seeing their own numbers improve, and the review is also where you learn which rules don't fit certain clients. A rule that works for e-commerce ads might not suit a law firm's brand films; record those exceptions in the method rather than letting each editor improvise. Over time, the training material becomes a record of what your agency has learned, which is far more valuable than any single course.
