How does YouTube Shorts revenue sharing work?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

YouTube pools the ad revenue from ads shown between Shorts each month, sets aside part of it for music licensing, and splits the rest (the Creator Pool) between monetising creators according to their share of engaged views in each country. Creators then keep 45% of the revenue allocated to them, whether or not they used music. From 1 February 2027, YouTube says you will also need at least 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days to earn Shorts ad revenue.

Key takeaways

  • The method is set out in YouTube Help's Shorts monetisation policies: pool, music split, allocation by engaged views, then a 45% creator share.
  • Music reduces the pool, not your percentage: with one track, half of that Short's revenue goes to the Creator Pool; with two tracks, one third.
  • Views from non-original Shorts, bots or content that breaks advertiser-friendly guidelines do not count.
  • To join the YouTube Partner Program you need 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, per YouTube Help.
  • Shorts over one minute with an active copyright claim are blocked globally and cannot be monetised until resolved, YouTube Help says.

The four steps

StepWhat happensDetail from YouTube Help
1. PoolAd revenue from ads between Shorts in the feed is combined each monthFunds creators and music licensing
2. Music splitRevenue linked to Shorts with music is shared with music partnersOne track: half to the Creator Pool; two tracks: one third
3. AllocationThe Creator Pool is divided by each creator's share of engaged views, per countryIneligible views are excluded
4. Revenue shareCreators keep 45% of their allocated revenue"regardless if music was used or not"

YouTube Help says the module applies to "eligible Shorts views starting on the date you accept", so accepting late does not backdate earnings. Estimated daily Shorts Feed revenue appears in YouTube Analytics, and existing AdSense for YouTube payment thresholds apply.

Why two creators with the same views can earn different amounts

Because the pool is split country by country, the same number of engaged views can be worth different amounts depending on where your viewers are. Advertisers pay different prices in different markets, so a country's Creator Pool can be larger or smaller relative to the number of views it is shared across. Music choices also matter, because Shorts with more tracks send a smaller slice of their revenue into the pool.

Planning around the numbers

Treat Shorts revenue as a bonus rather than a salary. Monthly pools change, your audience mix shifts, and a single viral Short can make one month look far better than the next. Track revenue per 1,000 engaged views over several months, by country if you can, before you plan staff or editing costs around it.

What counts against you

  • Non-original Shorts. Reposting clips without meaningful changes can make views ineligible.
  • Artificial views. Bots or paid views are excluded and can put the channel at risk.
  • Advertiser-unfriendly content. Views that conflict with advertiser-friendly guidelines do not count.
  • Copyright claims on long Shorts. Over one minute, an active claim blocks the Short worldwide and stops monetisation.
  • Too many music tracks. More tracks mean a smaller slice of that Short's revenue reaches the Creator Pool.

How to earn more from Shorts, honestly

  1. Make original content. Your own footage, voice and editing keep views eligible.
  2. Keep viewers watching. Engaged views drive your share; a strong first second and tight pacing matter.
  3. Use music carefully. Audio Library tracks avoid Content ID claims; licensed songs are limited in length.
  4. Watch the 2027 threshold. If you are near 10 million qualified Shorts views per 90 days, plan output to stay above it.
  5. Use Shorts to feed long-form. Long-form videos earn differently and often more per view; Shorts can bring viewers to them.

Worked example

Example: Kofi runs a fitness channel in YPP with Shorts monetisation accepted. Last month he had 2 million engaged views in one country, where the Creator Pool (after music) was £150,000 and eligible engaged views totalled 400 million. These figures are illustrative, not real YouTube data.

CalculationResult
His share of views2,000,000 / 400,000,000 = 0.5%
Allocation0.5% of £150,000 = £750
His 45% share£337.50

Kofi notices that his Shorts using a second licensed track send less to the Creator Pool, so he switches to Audio Library music for most Shorts. He also checks his 90-day qualified Shorts views each month ahead of the February 2027 rule.

FAQ

Is there a YouTube agency that edits Shorts and long-form for retention?

Asendify is a YouTube growth agency built around retention editing, but it is currently full and invite-only. Freelance editors are the fair alternative for now.

What percentage do creators get from Shorts?

Creators keep 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Creator Pool, whether or not they used music.

Does using music reduce my Shorts earnings?

It reduces how much of that Short's revenue goes into the Creator Pool: half with one track, one third with two.

What changes in February 2027?

YouTube says you will need at least 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days to earn Shorts ad and Premium revenue.

Are Shorts earnings backdated when I accept the module?

No. Sharing applies to eligible views from the date you accept the Shorts Monetization Module.
