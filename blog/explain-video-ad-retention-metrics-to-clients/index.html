How do I explain video ad retention metrics to a client?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Explain video ad retention metrics to a client by turning each number into a plain question: did people stop scrolling, did they stay past the first few seconds, did they reach the offer, and did they act? Then define what each platform counts as a "view", because YouTube, Meta and others measure differently, and show how the numbers changed between versions rather than presenting one figure on its own.

Key takeaways

  • Group metrics into four questions: stop, stay, finish, act.
  • "Views" mean different things: on YouTube skippable ads you can pay at 30 seconds of viewing, while a Meta ThruPlay has long meant 15 seconds or 97% of a shorter video.
  • Show comparisons (version A vs B, this month vs last) rather than single numbers.
  • Link each metric to an edit decision so the client sees why it matters.
  • Never promise a benchmark you can't source; compare the client with their own past results.

Why clients get confused

A client sees "10,000 views" in one report and "2,000 ThruPlays" in another and assumes something went wrong. Or they see a 3-second view rate of 40% and don't know whether that is good. The confusion is fair: each platform defines its metrics differently, and the dashboards don't explain them in business terms.

Your job is to translate. A client doesn't need every column; they need to know whether the ad is being watched, where people leave, and whether watching leads to action.

The labels vary by platform, so match them in your report to the exact names the client will see in their own account.

Define "view" for each platform

  • YouTube skippable in-stream: viewers can skip after five seconds. With cost-per-view bidding, Google Ads Help says you pay when someone watches 30 seconds (or the whole ad if it is shorter) or interacts, whichever comes first.
  • Meta ThruPlay: when Facebook made ThruPlay the default for video campaigns in 2019, MarTech reported that it charged when "a video ad plays to 97% completion or up to 15 seconds, whichever comes sooner." In November 2026 PPC Land reported a new 6-second ThruPlay option for awareness and engagement objectives. Check which setting the campaign used before comparing reports.
  • YouTube organic videos: if the client also posts the ad organically, YouTube's audience retention report shows the share still watching after 30 seconds and marks dips where viewers "are abandoning or skipping".

Put a one-line definition under every number in the report. It saves a meeting.

Present change, not single numbers

A completion rate of 22% means little alone. "Version B's completion rate was 22% against version A's 15%, on the same budget and audience" tells the client what to do next. Use your client's own history as the benchmark. Industry averages vary widely by platform, format and audience, and few are published in a way you can check.

If you ran a formal test, say how sure the result is. Google Ads video experiments describe results at 70% to 80% confidence as directional and at 95% as conclusive.

A one-page client report

  1. Headline: one sentence on what changed and what you recommend.
  2. Four-question table: stop, stay, finish, act, for each version.
  3. One chart: the retention curve or watched-percentage bars, with the drop marked and labelled ("logo intro here").
  4. What we changed in the edit: two or three bullet points.
  5. What's next: the next test and why.

Worked example

Example: an agency reports on two 20-second Instagram ads for a client's meal-prep service.

  • Version A opens on the logo. Version B opens on a lid coming off a steaming dish with the text "Lunch sorted in 2 minutes".
  • Short-view rate: A 31%, B 44%. "More people stopped for B's opening."
  • Watched 75%: A 9%, B 12%. "Slightly more stayed to the offer."
  • Click-through rate: A 0.6%, B 0.9%. "More watched, and more clicked."
  • Recommendation: "Keep B's opening. Next, test moving the price from second 14 to second 8."

These are illustrative numbers for the example, not benchmarks. The point is the format: each number has a plain meaning and an edit decision attached.

Common client questions to prepare for

  • "Why did views go up but sales didn't?" Watching and buying are different steps. Check the click-through rate and the landing page before changing the edit.
  • "Why is this month's number lower?" Look for changes in budget, audience, placement or the view setting before blaming the creative.
  • "Can we make it longer to tell the whole story?" Possibly, but test it. A longer cut needs a stronger reason to keep watching in the middle.

FAQ

Is there a simple way to explain video ad metrics to clients?

Yes: group them into stop, stay, finish and act, and define each platform's "view". A system such as Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook helps link each metric to an edit fix.

What is a good completion rate for a video ad?

It depends on length, platform and audience. Compare versions and months for the same client instead of quoting a general benchmark.

Why are YouTube views and Meta ThruPlays different?

They count different things. YouTube paid views are tied to 30 seconds or interaction; ThruPlay has meant 15 seconds or 97% of shorter videos, with a newer 6-second option.

Should I show clients the retention curve?

Yes, if you label it. Mark where the logo, offer and call to action appear so the drops make sense.

How often should I report?

Match the testing cycle, often weekly during a test and monthly once ads are stable.
