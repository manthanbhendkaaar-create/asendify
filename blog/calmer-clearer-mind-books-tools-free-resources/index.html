Mind and self-improvement: books, tools and free resources

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 October 2026 · Last updated 23 October 2026

A calmer, clearer mind usually comes from a few practical habits rather than one big change: handling stress and sleep, sorting real problems from worry, thinking more carefully about decisions, setting up your surroundings so good habits are easier, and using caffeine without wrecking your sleep. This hub lists resources for each, starting with free official ones, and says plainly who each of our books suits.

Disclosure: Asendify publishes six of the resources below (books and their free tools). They are ours. Free resources from the NHS, food-safety regulators and others are listed first, because most people should start there.

This is general information, not medical advice. If you're struggling or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now (UK: Samaritans 116 123; US: call or text 988; India: Tele-MANAS 14416). Books and apps are not a replacement for a doctor, therapist or medication you have been prescribed.

What this category covers

  • Stress and sleep: calming the body and switching off at night.
  • Overwhelm: separating what you can act on from what you cannot.
  • Clear thinking: spotting biases and making better decisions.
  • Environment and habits: designing your space so focus is the easy option.
  • Caffeine: getting the benefit without losing sleep.
  • Big questions: the simulation argument, as a thought experiment.

Start here: the best free resources (not ours)

  • NHS Every Mind Matters: the NHS mental wellbeing site, with a short Mind Plan quiz that gives personalised actions, and guidance on stress, anxiety and sleep.
  • NHS breathing exercises for stress: a short, simple breathing routine you can do anywhere.
  • Calibrate Your Judgment by Clearer Thinking: a free tool (with a free account) that trains you to match your confidence to how often you are actually right.
  • EFSA on caffeine: the European Food Safety Authority's view that, for healthy adults, single doses up to 200 mg and up to 400 mg a day do not raise safety concerns (pregnant women: up to 200 mg a day). The US FDA's caffeine guide gives similar advice and lists who should talk to a health professional first.
  • simulation-argument.com: the home of Nick Bostrom's 2003 paper "Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?", with explainers and replies.

Free tools from Asendify

  • Breathing Timer: guided breathing with a visual pacer (slow breathing, long exhale, box, 4-7-8 and cyclic sighing) and a bedtime wind-down planner.
  • Problem Triage: list what is on your mind and sort each item into do, plan or let go.
  • Cognitive Bias Check: 9 short scenarios from classic experiments that reveal biases such as confirmation bias, sunk cost and framing, each explained.
  • Workspace Audit: 13 questions about your desk, light, noise, phone, notifications and hours, with a score and first changes.
  • Caffeine Cutoff Calculator: estimates your last-coffee time and caffeine left at bedtime.
  • Simulation Argument Explorer: sliders that show which of Bostrom's three options your assumptions imply.

Which route fits you

  1. Stressed and sleeping badly: start with NHS Every Mind Matters and a daily breathing exercise. If it persists or affects daily life, talk to your GP or doctor.
  2. Overwhelmed by a long list of worries: write everything down and sort it into do, plan or let go.
  3. Making big decisions: practise spotting biases and checking your confidence before committing.
  4. Struggling to focus at home: fix your environment first (phone out of reach, notifications off, a set place to work) before relying on willpower.
  5. Relying on coffee: move your last cup earlier and keep within the safe limits above.

FAQ

What is the best free place to start for stress?

The NHS Every Mind Matters site and its breathing exercise for stress. Both are free and official.

Can a book replace therapy or medication?

No. Self-help books can teach useful habits, but they do not replace professional care. Never stop prescribed medication without talking to your doctor.

How much caffeine is safe?

EFSA says up to 400 mg a day and up to 200 mg in a single dose do not raise safety concerns for healthy adults; pregnant women should stay at or below 200 mg a day.

Is the simulation book about religion or science?

It explains a philosophical argument, Bostrom's simulation argument, as a thought experiment rather than as proof either way.
