What is a good average view duration for a video podcast episode on YouTube?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

There is no official benchmark for a "good" average view duration on a video podcast, and YouTube doesn't publish one. A good figure is one that beats your own recent episodes of similar length, which YouTube lets you compare directly. Because episodes are long, the average will be a small share of the runtime; what matters is whether it is rising and where viewers leave.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's engagement help defines average view duration as "Average minutes watched among those who stayed to watch".
  • YouTube's key moments report lets you "compare your 10 latest videos of similar length". That is the fairest benchmark for a podcast.
  • Podnews notes that "YouTube measures views, not downloads", so YouTube figures aren't comparable with your audio host's download numbers.
  • Edison Research data reported by SSRS found "most new consumers and established podcast fans are actively watching while listening to video podcasts". Visual editing matters.
  • Any "good AVD" number you see online for podcasts is an estimate, not a YouTube standard. Treat it with care.

Why there's no single "good" number

Average view duration depends on episode length, topic, audience, traffic source and whether people watch on a TV, phone or in the background. A 20-minute solo episode and a 2-hour interview can't share a target. Even two interviews of the same length can differ widely depending on the guest.

So instead of chasing an outside number, use three comparisons:

Why average view duration matters for a podcast's growth

YouTube's impressions and CTR FAQ explains that YouTube recommends a video when its average view duration indicates that viewers find it interesting. For a podcast, that means watch time affects whether episodes are shown to new people on the home page and in suggested videos, not just how your current audience feels. A rising AVD on similar-length episodes is a sign your editing is helping discovery. A falling one, while views rise, can simply mean the episode is reaching colder viewers, so read the two together before changing your format.

How to read your podcast's AVD

  1. Note the runtime. A 25-minute AVD on a 90-minute episode is very different from 25 minutes on a 30-minute episode.
  2. Look at the first minutes. A steep early fall usually means the opening (greetings, sponsor reads, housekeeping) is losing people.
  3. Find dips. Long answers, tangents and ad reads are common culprits.
  4. Check chapters and skips. Viewers often jump to a topic. Many skips forward suggest the opening isn't the episode's strongest part.
  5. Compare traffic sources. Viewers from search or suggested videos may watch differently from subscribers.

Example: setting a fair target

Example: a marketing podcast publishes 60-minute interviews on YouTube. Its last 10 episodes of similar length average around 12 minutes of view duration, and the retention graph falls steeply in the first three minutes, where the host reads two sponsor messages and introduces the guest at length.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on increasing watch time for video podcasts?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops, pacing and pattern interrupts with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's podcast creator pages are a free alternative.

Does YouTube publish podcast AVD benchmarks?

No. YouTube lets you compare a video with your own recent videos of similar length instead.

Should long episodes be split up?

Not necessarily. Many viewers watch long episodes. Tighten weak sections and post clips separately.

Do sponsor reads hurt AVD?

They often cause dips, especially at the start. Placing them after a strong moment usually helps.

Do background listeners count?

Views and watch time count when the video plays, but YouTube's podcast stats aren't comparable with audio downloads.
