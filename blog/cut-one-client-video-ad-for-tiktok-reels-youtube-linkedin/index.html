How do I cut one client ad into versions for TikTok, Reels, YouTube and LinkedIn?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

To cut one client ad into versions for TikTok, Reels, YouTube and LinkedIn, plan for vertical from the shoot, build the edit in swappable blocks (hook, problem, product, proof, call to action), then re-cut each version's opening, text and length for how that platform is watched. Simply reframing a 16:9 master rarely works: text ends up under buttons, the hook arrives too late and the pace suits nobody.

Key takeaways

  • Frame the shoot so the subject works in 9:16, 4:5 and 16:9 at once.
  • Build the edit as blocks so each version can drop or reorder sections.
  • Re-cut the first seconds per platform; a YouTube viewer can skip at 5 seconds, a TikTok viewer can swipe at once.
  • Keep text and logos inside each platform's safe zone.
  • Burn in captions for feeds that autoplay muted, such as LinkedIn and Meta.

Why one master edit doesn't fit every feed

Each platform shows video differently. On TikTok and Reels the ad fills a vertical screen among creator videos. On YouTube, a skippable in-stream ad plays before or during a video the viewer chose, and Google Ads Help says viewers can skip it after five seconds. On LinkedIn, the ad sits in a professional feed where, LinkedIn says, a large portion of members watch with the sound off.

The same 30 seconds can't be ideal in all of these. What can be shared is the footage, the message and most of the structure.

Step 1: shoot for every frame

Ask the crew to frame wide enough that the subject can be cropped to 9:16 without losing heads or products, and to keep key action near the centre. Film a few extra seconds of each product shot so the editor has room to re-time openings. If on-screen talent will speak to camera, capture a short version of the key line as well as the full one.

Step 2: build a modular edit

Cut the master as five labelled blocks:

  1. Hook (2-3 alternatives)
  2. Problem
  3. Product in use
  4. Proof
  5. Call to action (with an end card per platform)

Lock audio per block so sections can be dropped or reordered without breaking the music or voice-over. This is what makes a 6-second, 15-second and 30-second version possible from the same project.

Step 3: re-cut each version

  • TikTok and Reels: open on movement or a face, with a short text line in the first second. Keep text within the safe zone; TikTok publishes safe-zone files for each format. Remove anything that looks like a TV advert opening.
  • Meta feed (4:5): captions burned in, product visible by about the second beat, brand mark inside the frame from the start.
  • YouTube in-stream (16:9): earn the first 5 seconds before the skip button appears. Google's ABCDs recommend bringing the brand in from the start and reinforcing the call to action with voice-over.
  • YouTube bumper: Google describes bumper ads as non-skippable and 5 to 6 seconds long, so cut one idea: the hook plus the brand.
  • LinkedIn: a shorter cut that states the business benefit in the first few seconds, with subtitles burned in.

Step 4: check each export

Watch every version once on a phone, muted, inside a mock-up of the platform's interface. Check that no text sits under the like, share or caption areas, that the call to action is readable, and that file names say platform, ratio and length (for example "client_offer_tiktok_9x16_15s_v2.mp4").

Worked example

Example: an agency films one shoot for a client's new running shoe and needs four deliverables.

  • Master blocks: hook options (a runner splashing through a puddle; a close-up of the sole flexing; a runner saying "My knees stopped complaining"), problem, product, proof (a timed 5k with a watch on screen), call to action.
  • TikTok, 9:16, 15s: the runner's line as the hook, with the text "Week 3 in these", then product and proof. End card inside the safe zone.
  • Instagram feed, 4:5, 15s: the sole close-up as the hook, captions burned in, logo on the shoe visible from frame one.
  • YouTube in-stream, 16:9, 30s: the puddle shot with the brand clearly on the shoe in the first 2 seconds, then the full story, call to action said and shown.
  • YouTube bumper, 16:9, 6s: puddle shot, product name, done.

One shoot, one project, four ads, each opening the way its platform is watched.

FAQ

Is there a way to make one ad work on every platform?

Share the footage and structure, but re-cut the opening, text and length for each. A shared system such as Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook helps editors rebuild openings the same way each time.

Can I just crop a 16:9 ad to 9:16?

You can, but check every shot. Cropping often cuts off faces, products and text, and the pace of a horizontal ad usually feels slow in a vertical feed.

Which version should I edit first?

Usually the vertical one, as it is the most demanding. A good vertical cut adapts to 4:5 and 16:9 more easily than the reverse.

Do I need different music for each platform?

Not always, but check the licence covers every platform and paid use. If in doubt, use a track licensed for advertising.

Should each version have a different call to action?

The action can stay the same; the wording and end card may change to suit the platform's button and audience.
