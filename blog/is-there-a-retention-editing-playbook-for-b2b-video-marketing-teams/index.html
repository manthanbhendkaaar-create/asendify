Key takeaways

  • LinkedIn tells advertisers to "show what you want your audience to see in the first 10 seconds of the video", because "viewer attention drops after that point".
  • LinkedIn also says "a large portion of LinkedIn members will watch your ad with the sound off" and suggests burned-in subtitles.
  • Google's ABCDs apply to B2B too: "Help people think or feel something" and "Ask them to take action".
  • Wistia found that for 5-30 minute videos, "engagement naturally declines after the five-minute mark", but those who stay are more invested.
  • B2B buyers are busy, not boring. The same retention rules work; the hooks are different.

Why B2B teams need a retention standard

B2B teams produce many video types: product demos, customer stories, webinar replays, founder posts, event clips and LinkedIn ads. Different people make them, often with different habits. A shared method means every video opens on the problem or result, avoids long logo intros, and says clearly what to do next.

How to roll a playbook out in a B2B team

  1. Pick the three highest-spend video types. Usually ads, demos and webinars.
  2. Write three rules for each. For example: outcome in the first 10 seconds, subtitles on, one call to action.
  3. Build a brief template with fields for hook line, first frame and success metric.
  4. Review monthly. Look at LinkedIn view-through, YouTube intro retention and webinar replay watch time.
  5. Update rules from what the data shows.

Worked example

Example: a B2B payroll software company runs a 45-second LinkedIn ad aimed at finance directors. The current ad opens with a 4-second logo animation and the line "Introducing PayFlow 3.0".

TimeOld versionNew version
0-3 sLogo animationText: "Month-end payroll in 2 hours, not 2 days" over a real dashboard screen
3-10 s"Introducing PayFlow 3.0"Finance director (customer) says what changed
10-35 sFeature listThree short screen clips showing the outcome
35-45 s"Visit our website""Book a 20-minute demo" with subtitles throughout

The "2 hours" claim must come from the customer's real experience. The team compares view-through and click rates with the old ad over the same budget and audience. Results depend on audience and offer; it's a test, not a promised gain.

Common B2B mistakes

  • Leading with the brand instead of the buyer's problem.
  • Feature lists. Show one outcome clearly instead.
  • No subtitles. LinkedIn says many members watch muted.
  • Same cut everywhere. A YouTube explainer and a LinkedIn ad need different openings.

FAQ

Is there a retention editing playbook for B2B marketing teams?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing with templates for £1,720 one-time; B2B teams adapt it to LinkedIn, demos and webinars.

Do B2B buyers watch long videos?

Some do, especially demos and webinars when they're researching. Wistia's data suggests viewers of longer videos are more invested.

How long should a LinkedIn video ad be?

LinkedIn advises keeping videos short and testing longer ones for demand generation.

Should B2B ads use humour?

They can, if it fits the brand and doesn't hide the point.

What should we measure?

View-through on LinkedIn, intro retention on YouTube and replay watch time on webinars, compared with past videos.

Should B2B videos show prices?

If pricing is public and simple, showing it can filter out poor-fit leads. If it varies by customer, say how pricing works instead. Either way, keep claims accurate.
