Why does my YouTube audience retention go above 100%?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Your retention graph goes above 100% when viewers rewind and watch the same part again. YouTube counts how many times each moment was watched compared with the number of views, so repeated viewing pushes that point past 100%. YouTube calls these rises spikes: they can mean a strong moment people replay or share, or a confusing one they had to watch twice.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's Analytics API says its audience watch ratio "could be greater than 1" if users "rewind and watch the same portion of a video multiple times".
  • In YouTube Studio, rises in the curve are labelled spikes: "moments in your video that were rewatched or shared".
  • Above-100% points are most common at the very start (people restarting) and at key moments (replays).
  • A spike is not automatically good. Check whether it is a highlight or a confusing passage.
  • Retention data takes one to two days to process, so read spikes after that.

What the numbers actually measure

The YouTube Analytics API metrics reference defines audienceWatchRatio as "the absolute ratio of viewers watching the video at the given point in the video", calculated by comparing how many times a portion was watched with the total views. It adds that if users "rewind and watch the same portion of a video multiple times", the ratio "could be greater than 1". The same reference describes relativeRetentionPerformance, a separate 0 to 1 score comparing your retention at each point with "all YouTube videos" of similar length, where 0.5 is the median.

YouTube Studio's key moments for audience retention guide uses plainer labels: flat sections mean viewers watched through, gradual declines mean waning interest, dips mean people skipped or left, and spikes are "moments in your video that were rewatched or shared". It says spikes can indicate strong content, or content viewers had to replay because it was unclear.

How to investigate a spike in three steps

  1. Open the video's retention graph in YouTube Studio about two days after upload, once data has processed.
  2. Watch the 15 seconds before and during the spike. Ask: is this a highlight, or did I go too fast?
  3. Check for a dip before it. A dip followed by a spike usually means people skipped ahead to this point. YouTube's guide notes that dips can be viewers skipping, not only leaving. If the video has chapters (YouTube requires at least three timestamps, starting at 00:00, each chapter at least 10 seconds long), viewers can jump straight to a section, which tends to produce exactly this dip-then-spike shape at chapter starts.

Why spikes matter less on small videos

On a video with a few hundred views, a handful of people rewatching one section can create a visible spike. YouTube's guide says key moments only appear on videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views, and even above that threshold small samples are noisy. Before changing your editing because of one spike, check whether the same pattern appears on two or three similar videos. A spike that repeats at the same kind of moment, such as every on-screen formula or every price, is a pattern worth acting on; a one-off is often just a few curious viewers.

Spikes vs dips: which to fix first

Dips cost you viewers; spikes usually do not. So work in this order:

  1. The biggest dip in the first minute.
  2. Any dip deeper than the rest of the curve later on.
  3. Spikes caused by confusion (fast numbers, small text).
  4. Spikes caused by highlights: study and repeat, don't fix.

FAQ

Is there a tool that reads my retention graph and flags each drop-off with a fix?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor reads one channel with read-only access and shows drop-off points with a fix for each. YouTube Studio's retention report is the free alternative.

Is retention above 100% good?

Not always. It means a moment was watched more than once. That can be a highlight, or a part viewers had to replay because it was unclear.

Why does my graph start above 100%?

Viewers often restart a video or replay the opening seconds, which pushes the first points past 100%.

What is the difference between a spike and a dip?

YouTube says spikes are moments rewatched or shared, and dips are where viewers skipped ahead or stopped watching.

Does relative retention go above 1?

No. YouTube's relativeRetentionPerformance is always between 0 and 1. Only the absolute watch ratio can exceed 1.
