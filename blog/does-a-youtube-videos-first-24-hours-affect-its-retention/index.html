Does a YouTube video's first 24 hours decide its retention and reach?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • YouTube added a "First 24 hours" option to video analytics in January 2021, showing views, watch time, subscribers and revenue, as reported by Search Engine Journal.
  • YouTube's growth lead Todd Beaupré said, in a 2024 interview summarised by Search Engine Journal, "the algorithm for Discovery is focused more on individual videos", and YouTube aims "to not overemphasize historical data".
  • In a 2025 interview, Beaupré described how a video can become "relevant again" later, sometimes for a different audience.
  • YouTube's content tab tips compare each video's retention with "the last 10 of your videos of a similar length".
  • The fixable part is the opening: YouTube's tips say to "edit the first 30 seconds of your video and experiment with different styles".

Why the first day can mislead

Early viewers are often different. In the first hours, many views can come from subscribers and people who get notifications. They already know you, so they may watch longer, or skip intros they've seen before. Later, viewers from suggested videos or search arrive cold and may behave differently. This is a common pattern creators report; check your own traffic sources rather than assuming.

YouTube looks at videos individually. Beaupré's comments suggest one slow start doesn't lock a video, or a channel, into a fixed outcome. YouTube aims not to overemphasise historical data that doesn't predict future performance.

Some videos are slow burners. Evergreen tutorials, explainers and search-led topics can keep finding viewers long after the first week. Beaupré described videos becoming relevant again for new audiences.

What to do in the first 24 hours

  1. Open the video's analytics with "First 24 hours" selected. Compare views with your recent uploads.
  2. Check retention against typical. YouTube compares a video's retention with your last 10 videos of similar length. Note whether it is above or below.
  3. Look at the 30-second intro figure. A low number points to a weak opening or a mismatch with the title and thumbnail.
  4. Find the first big dip. Note the timestamp and what happens on screen there.
  5. Decide what you can change now. You can change the title or thumbnail, or test them. Big edits to the video itself are limited, so save editing lessons for the next upload.
  6. Re-check at 7 and 28 days. Retention and traffic sources often settle as more viewers arrive.

FAQ

Is there a playbook that teaches how to read retention and improve the next video?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and open loops, with a tracker, spreadsheet and a 30-day plan. YouTube's analytics help is a free starting point.

Should I delete a video that does badly in the first 24 hours?

Usually not. YouTube says it looks at videos individually, and videos can pick up later. Deleting also loses the data you can learn from.

Why is retention higher on day one than later?

Early viewers are often subscribers who already like your videos. Later viewers from browse or search may be less familiar with you.

Can I change the thumbnail after upload?

Yes. You can change it or test several with YouTube's A/B tool, which picks the winner by watch time.

When should I judge a video's performance?

Look at day one for quick lessons, then re-check at 7 and 28 days for a fairer picture.
