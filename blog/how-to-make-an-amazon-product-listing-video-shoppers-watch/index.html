How do I make an Amazon product listing video that shoppers actually watch?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Show the product being used in the first two or three seconds, then answer, in order, the questions shoppers ask in your reviews and Q&A: what it does, how big it is, how it works, what's in the box. Make it work with the sound off (captions and on-screen text), keep it short (often 30 to 90 seconds for a main product video), and avoid anything you can't back up. A listing video is watched by someone already on your page and close to buying, so it should remove doubts, not sell a dream.

Key takeaways

  • Amazon's guide to product page videos advises sellers to "keep your video short and concise" and to "showcase your product in action or demonstrate how it is used".
  • Amazon reports that detail pages with uploaded videos "saw an average 21.8% increase in sales" compared with those without; its footnote says this covered January to June 2024 in 17 non-US marketplaces, for listings that previously had no video.
  • Wistia's 2026 data puts the average engagement rate of product videos under a minute at 50%.
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "jump in" and introduce the product "from the start".
  • Videos are uploaded in Seller Central under Catalog, Upload and Manage Videos, with a title, the ASINs and a video type.

Find the script in your reviews

Your reviews and questions (and your competitors') are the best brief you'll get:

  1. List the five most common questions and complaints.
  2. Turn each into a shot that answers it visually.
  3. Drop anything nobody asks about.
  4. Put the most-asked question first after the opening shot.

If people often ask "does it fit a standard cup holder?", a two-second shot of it in a cup holder will hold more attention than a minute of lifestyle footage.

Editing for shoppers

  • Sound off by default. Many shoppers browse muted. Put the key point of each scene on screen in a few words.
  • Change shot every few seconds while staying on the product. Close-ups, hands, in-use, packaging.
  • Real use, real scale. No misleading angles that make it look bigger, and no effects the product doesn't have.
  • No external links or contact details, and no prices or time-limited offers that will go out of date.
  • One video per job. A main overview, then separate short videos for setup, comparison or care if needed.

Keep it accurate

Every claim in the video is an advertising claim. Don't show features a variant lacks, don't use results you can't substantiate, and use only footage and music you have rights to. In the UK, the ASA enforces the CAP Code, which "must be followed by all advertisers, agencies and media"; check its scope section for how it applies to your listings.

Worked example

Example: a seller of a collapsible silicone water bottle sees three recurring review themes: "does it leak?", "is it hard to clean?" and "how small does it fold?".

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to open a product video?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Amazon's free seller guide covers product video basics.

How long should an Amazon product video be?

Short enough that every scene answers a shopper's question; 30 to 90 seconds suits most main product videos.

Do Amazon product videos need sound?

They should work without it. Use captions or on-screen text for every key point.

Can I use the same video as my ad?

You can, but an ad needs a faster hook. Cut a separate short version for ads.

Where do I upload a product video?

In Seller Central under Catalog, then Upload and Manage Videos.
