How do I run a test edit when hiring a short-form video editor?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Shortlist two to four editors from their portfolios, then give each the same raw footage, the same written brief and the same deadline, and pay them for it. Score the results against a short checklist (hook, pacing, captions, sound, finish) and how well they followed the brief. A paid, time-limited test with footage you'll actually use tells you more than any portfolio, and treats editors fairly.

Key takeaways

  • Pay for test edits. For people who may become workers, UK guidance says an unpaid "trial", "test" or "recruitment exercise" can still create a right to the minimum wage, depending on the facts. Paying also gets you better applicants.
  • If you use the test edit, agree rights in writing. UK guidance says a freelancer on a contract for services will usually retain copyright in any works he produces unless agreed otherwise.
  • Use a fixed price for the test. Upwork's own guidance says fixed-price contracts work best when the project is clearly defined from the start, which a test brief is.
  • Same footage, same brief, same deadline. Otherwise you are comparing footage, not editors.
  • Judge the hook first. In short-form the first seconds decide whether anyone sees the rest.

How to set up the test

  1. Pick real footage. One 3 to 10 minute raw clip or long video, the kind you'll actually send. Include a slow start so you see who finds the hook.
  2. Write a one-page brief. Platform and length (for example a 30 to 45 second vertical Short), audience, goal, brand fonts or caption style, what to avoid.
  3. Set a fixed fee and a deadline. Agree a fixed fee for a single deliverable and a 48 to 72 hour turnaround. The fee depends on your market; ask what each editor charges for a similar Short.
  4. Ask for a short note. "Why did you open where you did?" Two or three sentences show how they think.
  5. Agree the rights. If you might post it, the agreement should say so. If not, say you won't use it.
  6. Score blind if you can. Strip names from the files, then score.

Example: a fitness coach hiring a Reels editor

Example: a fitness coach shortlists three editors from 40 applicants. Each gets the same 8-minute raw clip of a gym session, a one-page brief (35-second vertical Reel, beginners, captions in white with a black outline, no trending sounds) and a fixed fee for the test, agreed in writing with a line saying the coach may post the chosen edit. Editor A opens with the coach saying "Most beginners do this wrong" over a close-up of the mistake. Editor B opens with a 4-second wide shot of the gym. Editor C follows the brief well but uses a trending sound. A scores highest on hook and pacing, and asked a smart question about whether to show the coach's face first. A gets a paid trial month at four Reels a week. Details are illustrative.

Red flags in a test

  • Ignores the brief on length or platform.
  • Opens on the first frame of the footage every time.
  • Heavy effects that hide weak structure.
  • No questions, then late delivery.
  • Watermarks or stock music with no licence.

FAQ

Is there a retention guide I can give an editor before a test?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook has a cheat sheet and templates for hooks and pacing. A one-page brief based on your best Shorts is the free alternative.

Should I pay for a test edit?

Yes. It's fairer, attracts experienced editors and, in the UK, unpaid trials can raise minimum wage issues for workers.

Who owns a test edit?

Usually the freelancer who made it, unless you agree otherwise in writing. Put the rights in the test agreement.

How long should the test be?

One Short, 30 to 60 seconds, with a 48 to 72 hour deadline is enough.

How many editors should I test?

Two to four from your shortlist. More than that takes too long to review well.
