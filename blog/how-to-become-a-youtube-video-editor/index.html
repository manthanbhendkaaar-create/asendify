How do I become a YouTube video editor?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 14 November 2026 · Last updated 14 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

You become a YouTube video editor by learning one editing program well, building a small portfolio of YouTube-style edits that show you can keep viewers watching, and then pitching creators directly or through freelance platforms. You do not need a degree: Prospects says one is not required to be a video editor in the UK, and the National Careers Service lists routes through college courses, apprenticeships, starting as a runner or assistant, and volunteering.

Key takeaways

  • No degree required; a portfolio and reliable delivery matter more.
  • The UK National Careers Service puts video editor pay at about £24,000 (starter) to £48,000 (experienced) a year.
  • YouTube editing is mostly freelance: you are paid per video or on a monthly retainer.
  • What creators pay for is retention: pacing, hooks, cutting dead air and keeping the story moving.
  • Start with one niche (gaming, education, podcasts, finance) so your portfolio speaks to the creators you pitch.

What the job involves

The National Careers Service video editor profile describes the core tasks: agreeing the visual style with the team, making rough cuts, arranging footage into a storyline, improving picture quality, supporting sound and effects work, and producing a final cut for approval. For YouTube, add:

Traditional editing taskYouTube version
Arranging footage into a storyBuilding a strong hook in the first 30 seconds and a clear payoff
Rough cut, then final cutRough cut, creator notes, final cut, often within days
Picture and sound qualityClean audio, captions, zooms and B-roll to hold attention
Working with a teamOften working directly with one creator, remotely
Final approvalPlus thumbnails, Shorts cut-downs and sometimes upload prep

These figures cover film and TV as well as online video. YouTube freelancers set their own rates per video or per month, so earnings vary widely with niche, speed and the size of the creators you work for.

Prospects stresses an "up-to-date and well-prepared" showreel, and TargetJobs suggests practising in your spare time, sharing your work online and networking through LinkedIn and creative communities.

Skills creators look for

The National Careers Service lists attention to detail, communication, handling criticism and pressure, time management and confident use of editing software. YouTube adds a specific one: understanding the audience retention graph. A creator does not care how many effects you know; they care whether viewers stay. Learn to read retention in YouTube Studio and explain how your edit fixes a drop.

FAQ

Do I need a degree to be a video editor?

No. Prospects says a degree is not required in the UK. Media or film courses can help with skills and contacts, but portfolios and experience matter more.

How much do YouTube video editors make?

It varies widely. UK video editor salaries run from about £24,000 to £48,000 according to the National Careers Service, while freelance YouTube editors are usually paid per video or monthly.

What software do YouTube editors use?

Common choices include Adobe Premiere, Final Cut Pro and DaVinci Resolve, which has a free version. Learn one well before trying others.

How do I get my first YouTube editing client?

Pick a niche, build a few samples in its style, and pitch small and mid-size creators with a short re-edit of the opening of one of their videos.

Can I learn video editing in a month?

You can learn the basics of one editor in a few weeks. Editing well enough to keep viewers watching takes months of practice and feedback.
