How loud should YouTube audio be (LUFS)?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

There's no official loudness target in YouTube's help pages, but streaming services, YouTube among them, turn loud tracks down to a reference level that audio engineers commonly report as around -14 LUFS. For most YouTube videos, a sensible aim is an integrated loudness between about -16 and -14 LUFS, with peaks kept a little below 0 dB and dialogue clear and consistent. Mixing much louder doesn't make you louder on YouTube; it just costs you dynamics.

Key takeaways

  • LUFS (loudness units relative to full scale) measures how loud audio sounds over time, not just its peaks.
  • The broadcast standard EBU R 128 normalises programmes to -23 LUFS.
  • The EBU's streaming supplement allows an interim distribution level "in the range of -20.0 to -16.0 LUFS" for streamed broadcast content.
  • The AES streaming recommendation TD1008 suggests -18 LUFS for speech and about -16 LUFS overall for music, according to Production Advice.
  • Production Advice reported that most streaming services still used -14 LUFS as of 2021, and that YouTube moved its reference to -14 LUFS.

What LUFS means

Peak meters show the loudest instant in your audio. Loudness meters estimate how loud it sounds to a listener over time. The EBU's loudness page explains that R 128 "tells how broadcasters can measure and normalise audio using Loudness meters instead of Peak Meters (PPMs) only." Its EBU Mode meter gives three readings:

ReadingTime windowUse
Momentary400 msMoment-to-moment loudness
Short-term3 secondsHow a section sounds
IntegratedWhole programmeThe single number you compare with a target

Most editing apps now include a loudness meter showing integrated LUFS.

The EBU R 128 s2 supplement (November 2023) notes that streaming platforms increasingly use loudness metadata to normalise playback, and that competing streaming services typically use higher target levels than broadcast standards. Production Advice's summary of AES TD1008, by mastering engineer Ian Shepherd, gives the AES figures and explains that "Distribution Loudness is simply the overall loudness value of the stream that your device plays."

What normalisation means for your mix

When a platform normalises, it turns quieter-than-target audio up less (or not at all) and louder-than-target audio down. So squashing a mix to be very loud gains you nothing in volume: it gets turned down, and it sounds flatter than a mix with natural dynamics. What matters more for viewers is that speech is clear and consistent from start to finish, so nobody has to reach for the volume button.

A practical target for YouTube

ElementPractical aim
Integrated loudness of the whole videoAbout -16 to -14 LUFS
PeaksKept below 0 dBFS, with a limiter on the master as a safety net
DialogueClear and consistent; music and effects sit underneath
Music bedsLower than the voice, often several dB under
Sudden effectsLoud enough to land, not loud enough to make people jump

These are working targets, not YouTube rules. If your channel is mostly speech, being a little quieter (around -16 LUFS) is fine. Clean recordings help most; see how to remove background noise from YouTube audio. Music choice matters too; see where to get free music for YouTube videos.

Why audio matters for retention

Voices that are too quiet, music that drowns speech or a sudden loud effect all give viewers a reason to stop watching, or at least to fiddle with the volume instead of following the video. Consistent loudness is part of the same job as tight pacing: removing anything that gives the viewer a reason to leave. It sits alongside your export settings as a final check before upload.

FAQ

Is there a self-paced course that teaches retention editing to creators who edit their own videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Accelerator is a self-paced course with 6 modules and 24 lessons for creators who edit their own videos. The EBU's and AES's free loudness documents are fair alternatives for audio.

Does YouTube publish an official LUFS target?

Not in its help pages. Engineers commonly report YouTube normalising to around -14 LUFS.

Will a louder mix sound louder on YouTube?

Usually not. Loud mixes are turned down by normalisation, and they lose dynamics.

What is -23 LUFS?

The EBU R 128 broadcast loudness target used by European TV and radio.

Should speech-only videos be quieter?

They can be. The AES recommendation for speech streaming is -18 LUFS; around -16 LUFS works well for many talking-head videos.

How do I measure LUFS?

Use the loudness meter in your editing software or a plug-in, and read the integrated value for the whole video.
