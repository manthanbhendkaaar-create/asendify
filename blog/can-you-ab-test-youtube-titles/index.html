Can you A/B test YouTube titles?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. YouTube Studio's Test & compare feature lets you A/B test up to three titles on a long-form video, on their own or paired with thumbnails, and YouTube keeps the version that earns the most watch time. It is free on a computer, but it does not work on Shorts, and you should not edit the title while the test runs.

If you want to try something on the Asendify AI site before paying for anything, there is also a free test.

Key takeaways

  • Title testing is built into YouTube Studio as part of Test & compare.
  • You can test titles only, thumbnails only, or title and thumbnail combinations, up to three of each.
  • YouTube picks the winner on watch time, so honest titles that keep viewers tend to win.
  • Test meaningfully different angles, not one-word swaps.
  • If you change the title during a test, the test stops.

How title testing works on YouTube

YouTube's help page on A/B testing titles and thumbnails says creators "can test and compare up to 3 different titles and thumbnails". You choose between three modes:

ModeWhat viewers seeBest for
Title onlyDifferent titles, same thumbnailTesting the angle or wording
Thumbnail onlySame title, different thumbnailsTesting the image
Title and thumbnailMatched pairsTesting a whole packaging idea

YouTube shows the versions to different viewers. In its words, "the title or combination of title and thumbnail with the highest watch time will be shown to all viewers." Results are labelled Winner, Performed same, or Inconclusive. If there is no clear winner, the first option you entered stays.

Who can test titles

According to YouTube's help page:

  • The feature is only on computers, through YouTube Studio.
  • You need advanced features enabled.
  • Shorts, scheduled lives and premieres cannot be tested. Premieres become eligible after they convert to long-form, and live archives are eligible.
  • Videos made for kids, marked for mature audiences, or set to private are not eligible.
  • A test should finish within two weeks, sometimes in a few days.

Why titles are worth testing

The title is often the main thing a viewer reads before deciding. YouTube's discovery guide says its system pays attention to whether viewers choose to watch a recommended video, skip it or say they are not interested. A clearer title can change that choice.

But winning the click is only half of it. YouTube explains in a post on its recommendation system that it started using watch time partly because clicks alone did not show whether a viewer was satisfied. That is why Test & compare judges titles on watch time. A title that over-promises may pull clicks and then lose viewers.

What to test in a title

Test different angles, not tiny edits. Each version should promise the same video in a different way.

AngleExample for a budgeting video
Result"How I saved £5,000 in a year on an average salary"
Mistake"The budgeting mistake that kept me broke for years"
Method"The simple 3-account budget I use every month"
Question"Why does your money vanish before payday?"
Specific audience"A budget plan for people who hate budgets"

Every version must be true to the video. If the video does not show a £5,000 saving, that title should not be in the test.

Step by step: running a title test

  1. Open YouTube Studio on a computer and go to Content.
  2. Open the video's details and choose to test and compare titles.
  3. Enter two or three titles. Put the one you would use without a test first, since it stays if the result is inconclusive.
  4. Save, then leave the video alone until the test ends.
  5. Check the result. If one title wins, YouTube applies it to all viewers.
  6. Write down the lesson, for example: "Method titles beat result titles for this topic."

How to judge results beyond the verdict

YouTube's verdict is based on watch time. You can add context from the video's reach report. YouTube's page on impressions and click-through rate notes that half of channels and videos sit between 2% and 10% CTR, and that videos with a lot of homepage impressions tend to have lower rates. So do not panic if all versions show modest click-through rates; the comparison between them is what counts.

If a test is inconclusive, it usually means the titles were too alike or the video did not get enough impressions. Try bolder differences on your next upload.

Example: testing three angles

Example: Amara posts a 14-minute video on learning guitar chords. She tests three titles:

VersionTitleWatch-time shareVerdict
A"Guitar chords for beginners"27%Not chosen
B"The 4 chords that play hundreds of songs"44%Winner
C"I learned guitar in 30 days (here's how)"29%Not chosen

Version C got plenty of clicks, but viewers who expected her personal 30-day story left once the lesson started. Version B promised exactly what the video delivered. Her takeaway: specific, useful promises work better for her audience than personal-journey titles. These figures are illustrative; the method is what matters.

When a title test is not worth running

Testing takes time and impressions, so it is not always the best move.

  • Very small channels. If a video gets only a few dozen impressions a day, a test may never reach a result. Spend the effort on the opening instead.
  • Time-sensitive videos. News or event videos earn most of their views in the first days, which may be too short for a clear verdict.
  • When the problem is retention. If people click but leave in the first 30 seconds, a new title will not fix the opening.
  • When you have no real alternative. If you cannot write a second title you genuinely like, work on the idea first.

Title testing mistakes to avoid

  • Swapping one word. "Easy" versus "simple" rarely gives a clear result.
  • Testing a dishonest title. It can lose on watch time and annoy viewers.
  • Editing during the test. YouTube stops the test if you change the title or thumbnail.
  • Testing on a quiet video. Low impressions often mean an inconclusive result.
  • Forgetting to record what won. The lesson is worth more than one video's result.

Writing better titles before you test

  • Put the most important words first.
  • Make the promise specific: numbers, results or a clear problem.
  • Match the title to the first 30 seconds of the video.
  • Avoid stacking clickbait words; they tend to set expectations the video cannot meet.

FAQ

Can you change a YouTube title after uploading?

Yes, you can edit a title at any time in YouTube Studio. If a title test is running, though, editing the title stops the test.

How many titles can I test at once on YouTube?

Up to three titles per test, either on their own or paired with thumbnails.

Can I A/B test titles on YouTube Shorts?

No. YouTube's help page says Shorts cannot be tested with Test & compare.

Does YouTube choose the title with the most clicks?

No. It chooses the title or combination with the highest watch time, which rewards titles that bring in viewers who keep watching.

How long should a title test run?

Let it run until YouTube shows a result. YouTube says tests should be completed within two weeks.

Should I test titles on old videos?

You can, if the video still gets steady impressions. Older videos with little traffic are less likely to give a clear result.
