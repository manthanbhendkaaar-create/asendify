What is a good 30-second retention rate on YouTube?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

YouTube does not publish an official "good" 30-second retention rate. The useful test is your own: compare a video's Intro figure, the share of viewers still watching at 30 seconds, with your recent videos of similar length. If it is clearly higher than your usual, your opening worked; if it is lower, the opening is the first thing to fix.

You can work out your 30-second hold, and spot the drops around it, with the free YouTube Retention Curve Checker.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Studio reports 30-second retention as the "Intro" figure on each video's retention report.
  • There is no universal benchmark. Your own recent videos of similar length are the fairest comparison.
  • A low Intro figure usually means the opening did not confirm what the title and thumbnail promised.
  • Raise it by showing the payoff early, cutting greetings and matching the first line to the title.
  • Track the figure over several uploads, not one.

Where to find your 30-second retention

YouTube's guide to key moments for audience retention defines the metric directly: "Intro tells you what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds."

To see it:

  1. Open YouTube Studio and go to Content.
  2. Select a video and open its Insights (formerly Analytics).
  3. Choose the Overview or Engagement tab and find the audience retention report.
  4. Look for the Intro key moment.

The report is only available for individual videos and can take one to two days to process.

Why there is no single "good" number

Search online and you will find confident claims such as "aim for 70%". YouTube does not back any such figure. Several things change what is normal for a video:

FactorEffect on 30-second retention
Video lengthShort videos and long videos attract different viewing intent
Traffic sourceViewers from Search often know what they want; Browse viewers may be sampling
TopicTutorials, entertainment and commentary are watched differently
AudienceReturning subscribers often stay longer than first-time viewers
FormatShorts loop and are swiped, so their early curve is not comparable with long videos

That is why YouTube built a comparison into the report. Its guide explains that you can use typical retention to compare your 10 latest videos of similar length, and that you can also compare against all YouTube videos of similar length.

How to build your own baseline

A baseline takes ten minutes and saves a lot of guessing.

  1. List your last eight to ten long videos of roughly similar length.
  2. Write down each one's Intro figure.
  3. Sort the list and take the middle value. That median is your baseline. A median is better than an average here, because one unusual video will not drag it up or down.
  4. Note the highest and lowest figures too. They show your normal range.
  5. Update the baseline every month or so as your channel changes.

From then on, every new upload has a clear question: is the Intro figure above, inside or below your normal range?

What a strong or weak Intro figure tells you

YouTube says a high Intro percentage suggests that the opening matched the thumbnail and title and kept viewers interested. If the percentage is low, it suggests revisiting your thumbnail and title or testing different opening styles.

In practice:

  • Higher than your usual: the first 30 seconds delivered on the click. Note what you did and repeat it.
  • About your usual: nothing urgent. Look further into the curve for mid-video drops.
  • Lower than your usual: rewatch the first 30 seconds with fresh eyes. Ask whether a stranger who just read the title would feel they were in the right place.

Why the first 30 seconds matter for discovery

YouTube's discovery guide says its systems use both average view duration and average percentage viewed as signals to inform ranking. Viewers who leave in the first 30 seconds drag both down. Since most videos lose the largest share of viewers early, the opening is usually where small improvements add up to the most watch time.

YouTube's page on understanding content performance defines average view duration as the average minutes watched among those who stayed to watch, so early exits shape that number too.

How to raise your 30-second retention

  1. Open with the payoff. Show the result, the finished thing or the answer in the first five seconds.
  2. Echo the title in your first sentence. If the title says "3 mistakes", the first line should mention the three mistakes.
  3. Cut the welcome. "Hey guys, welcome back" adds nothing a new viewer needs.
  4. Move logos and intros later, or drop them. A 10-second animated intro is 10 seconds of nothing for the viewer.
  5. Get to the first useful point fast. Give one quick win before any background.
  6. Make sure the packaging is honest. An exaggerated title brings people who leave. YouTube's Test & compare tool lets you test titles and thumbnails, with the winner chosen on watch time.

Example: tracking the Intro figure over five uploads

Example: Hana runs a language-learning channel. Her last five videos held a median of 56% at 30 seconds. She changes one thing per upload and records the result.

UploadChangeHeld at 30sCompared with 56% median
1Baseline: welcome, then topic55%About the same
2Cut the welcome61%+5 points
3Cut welcome and opened with the sentence viewers would learn67%+11 points
4Same as 3, but title over-promised ("fluent in a week")52%−4 points
5Back to an honest title, opening as in 366%+10 points

The lesson: a quick, title-matching opening helped, and an over-promising title undid it. These figures are illustrative; the method is what matters.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Chasing a number from the internet. Use your own baseline.
  • Judging on day one. Wait for the data to process.
  • Comparing a Short with a long video. Keep formats separate.
  • Changing title, opening and topic at once. You will not know what worked.
  • Hooking with a false promise. It may raise clicks but tends to cut watch time.

FAQ

What does "Intro" mean in YouTube Studio?

It is the percentage of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds of a video. YouTube shows it as a key moment on the video's audience retention report.

Is 50% retention at 30 seconds good?

It depends on your channel, topic and video length. Compare it with your recent videos of similar length. If it is above your usual, the opening did its job.

Does 30-second retention apply to Shorts?

Shorts are often under a minute and loop, so their early curve behaves differently. Compare Shorts only with Shorts and use the Shorts reports in Studio.

How quickly can I improve my Intro figure?

Often on the next upload, because the opening is fully in your control. Track it across several videos to be sure the change is real.

Can a good thumbnail lower my 30-second retention?

Yes, if it promises something the video does not deliver early. Viewers who feel misled tend to leave quickly.

Should I worry if one video's Intro figure is low?

Not on its own. Check whether that video got unusual traffic, such as a lot of Browse viewers, and look at the next two uploads before changing your approach.
