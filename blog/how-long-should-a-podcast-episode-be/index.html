How long should a podcast episode be?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A podcast episode should be as long as it can stay interesting, and no longer. There is no ideal length that suits every show: a tight 20-minute news briefing and a 2-hour interview can both hold their audience. Find your length by cutting anything that doesn't earn its place, then checking where listeners actually stop in Spotify, Apple and YouTube analytics.

Key takeaways

  • Spotify tells creators "longer shows don't necessarily entail lower retention". Length is not the problem; empty minutes are.
  • Apple counts an "engaged listener" as someone who listens to at least 20 minutes or 40% of an episode, which is a useful yardstick.
  • Decide length by format and by your own retention data, not by a rule from another show.
  • A consistent length helps listeners fit you into a routine (a commute, a workout).
  • Cut tangents and repeats first; the right length usually appears on its own.

What the platforms say

None of the big platforms sets a recommended episode length, and you should be wary of anyone quoting one magic number. What they do say is useful:

  • Spotify: its guide to episode performance says "longer shows don't necessarily entail lower retention", and that listeners "have a tendency to drop off early", so the start matters more than the total (Spotify for Creators).
  • Apple Podcasts: its analytics define "engaged listeners" as people "who listened to or watched at least 20 minutes or 40% of an episode", and report "average consumption", the share of an episode played per device (Apple Podcasts for Creators).
  • YouTube: its podcasting best-practices guide gives no general length rule; it notes that tips "are not a guarantee for success" and that "editorial quality matters more than video quality" (YouTube podcasting best practices, PDF).

Length by format

FormatCommon rangeWhat sets the length
Daily news or briefing5 to 20 minutesFits a routine; listeners want it done
Solo expert tips10 to 30 minutesOne idea, explained well
Co-hosted chat30 to 70 minutesChemistry; easy to drift
Long-form interview45 to 120+ minutesGuest quality and story depth
Narrative or documentary25 to 50 minutes per partStory beats; often in series
Video podcast clipsUnder 1 to 10 minutesOne moment, one point

These ranges describe what shows commonly do, not what you must do. A narrative show at 45 minutes with no wasted scene will hold people better than a 25-minute chat full of filler.

A simple test: does each section earn its place?

Before you settle on a length, go through the edit and ask of each section: "If I cut this, would a listener miss it?"

  • Cut: repeated points, long greetings, inside jokes with no payoff, tech problems, "let's get into it" said three times.
  • Tighten: long setups. Keep the line that sets up the story and cut the five that circle it.
  • Keep: stories with a turn, strong opinions, specific examples, honest disagreement.

What remains is your episode's natural length. If it's 38 minutes, publish 38 minutes.

How to find your length from data

  1. Pull your last 10 episodes. Note each one's length, Apple average consumption and the Spotify or YouTube retention curve.
  2. Look for the knee. Where does the curve fall faster? If it's always around minute 35, that's a hint, but check what happens there (often an ad break or the end of the main topic).
  3. Look for sharp dips, not slopes. Spotify says "steep and sudden changes" are the evidence to act on; the gentle decline every long episode has is normal.
  4. Compare like with like. Interviews against interviews, solo against solo.
  5. Test one change. Cut 10 minutes of tangents from the next three episodes and compare consumption and curves.

Example: a 90-minute interview that should be 60

Example (a hypothetical show, to show the method): a business interview podcast publishes 90-minute episodes. The YouTube curve falls steadily, with sharp dips at the 4-minute mark (end of a long welcome and sponsor read), around minute 50 (a long tangent about the guest's favourite tools) and again at minute 70 (the host recaps the whole episode). Apple average consumption sits around 35%.

The fix is not "make it 30 minutes". It is:

  • Start on a cold open; move the sponsor read to a natural break.
  • Cut the tools tangent (or turn it into a separate clip).
  • Drop the recap.

The next episodes come in at about 60 minutes with the same guests and the same best stories. Judge the change by comparing average consumption and the curve shape with the previous 90-minute episodes.

Does length matter differently on video?

On YouTube, long episodes compete with the whole platform, and viewers can see a static frame. A long episode can hold well if the edit keeps giving the eye something new (angle changes, punch-ins, on-screen text) and chapters let people jump. Cumulus Media and Signal Hill Insights found in spring 2025 that 30% of weekly podcast consumers listen with the video minimised or in the background, and 42% actively watch (Westwood One). Edit for both: clean audio for the background group, visual change for the watchers.

Should every episode be the same length?

Roughly, yes. Regular listeners build your show into a routine, and a 25-minute show that suddenly runs 2 hours can lose them. Let length vary within a band (say 40 to 60 minutes) rather than swinging wildly, and tell listeners when an episode is a special long one.

FAQ

Is there a book on pacing long podcast episodes?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches pacing, cutting and re-hooks and applies them to podcasts. Spotify's and Apple's creator guides are useful free references.

Is a 2-hour podcast too long?

Not if it stays interesting. Long interviews can hold big audiences; the risk is filler, not minutes.

What is a good average consumption on Apple Podcasts?

Apple does not publish a target. Compare each episode with your own recent ones of the same format.

Are shorter podcasts better for new shows?

Often easier, because there's less room for filler while you learn. But cut to quality, not to a number.

Should I split a long episode into two parts?

If there's a natural break and each part has its own question and payoff, yes. Don't split mid-story without a strong cliffhanger.

Does episode length affect Spotify or YouTube recommendations?

Neither publishes a length rule for podcasts. Focus on holding listeners for whatever length you choose.
