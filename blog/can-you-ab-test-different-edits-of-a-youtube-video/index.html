Can you A/B test different edits of a long-form YouTube video to improve retention?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Not directly. YouTube's built-in A/B tool tests up to three titles and thumbnails and picks the one with the most watch time, but it can't serve two different edits of the same video. To test edits, creators use fair workarounds: compare matched videos with one editing change, trim the published video and compare before and after, or test the edit as a paid ad where platforms do split traffic.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's A/B testing help says you can compare up to three titles and/or thumbnails, and the winner is "the title or combination of title and thumbnail with the highest watch time". Video content itself isn't tested.
  • YouTube's trim help lets you cut the start, end or middle sections of a published video, and "The video's URL, view count and comments will stay the same." That makes before-and-after tests possible.
  • The trim tool has limits: unedited videos with more than 100,000 views may not be editable unless the channel is in the YouTube Partner Programme, and saved changes can't be reverted.
  • YouTube's key moments report and its comparison with your 10 latest videos of similar length give you the measures to judge a change.
  • Change one thing at a time. If you change the opening, pacing and thumbnail together, you won't know what worked.

How to run a fair before-and-after trim test

  1. Pick a video with steady views, ideally an older evergreen video, so traffic isn't changing fast.
  2. Record the baseline: share still watching at 30 seconds, average view duration and where the main dips are, over a fixed period (for example 28 days).
  3. Make one change with the trim tool, such as removing a 40-second intro or a slow section.
  4. Wait the same period and record the same numbers.
  5. Compare with care. If traffic sources shifted (for example, a spike from an external link), the comparison may not be fair.
  6. Keep a log of every change and result so patterns build up over time.

Remember that trims can't be undone after saving, so keep your original project file.

How to test editing techniques across videos

Some changes can't be made with a trim, such as adding B-roll, re-hooks or a new caption style. For those, use matched pairs. Alternate the technique across similar videos (same series, similar length and topic) and compare against YouTube's typical retention for videos of similar length. After several pairs, a consistent difference is more convincing than one result.

For brands and agencies with ad budgets, paid split tests can help. Running two edits of the same video as ads with the same targeting gives a cleaner comparison of the opening seconds, which is where most edits differ. Just remember that people who see an ad behave differently from people who chose to click your video.

FAQ

Is there a playbook with a testing tracker for retention edits?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook includes a tracker, spreadsheet, templates and a 30-day plan covering hooks, open loops and pacing, for £1,720 one-time. A simple spreadsheet is a free alternative.

Can YouTube split test two versions of my video?

No. Its built-in tool tests titles and thumbnails only, judged on watch time.

Does trimming a video reset its views?

No. YouTube says the URL, view count and comments stay the same.

How long should a before-and-after test run?

Use equal periods long enough to smooth out daily swings, such as several weeks each.

Can I test Shorts edits?

YouTube's A/B tool doesn't support Shorts. Compare similar Shorts with one change instead.
