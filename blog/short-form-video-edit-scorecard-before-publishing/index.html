Is there a scorecard to check a short-form video edit before it goes live?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. A simple editor scorecard rates each Short on seven things before it is posted: hook, clarity of the idea, pacing, on-screen text and captions, sound, payoff and technical finish, each scored 0 to 2. Use it when more than one person edits, when you're training an editor, or when results are inconsistent. Then check whether high scores match a high "stayed to watch" figure, and adjust the card.

Key takeaways

  • A scorecard turns "I don't like it" into feedback an editor can act on, and makes quality the same across editors.
  • Base the items on published platform advice. Meta's Reels guidance says strong Reels have a strong hook or payoff, like a big reveal, punchline or plot twist, and warns against blurry, low-resolution video and visible watermarks from other apps.
  • Google's ABCDs for YouTube ads ask editors to "hook and sustain attention with an immersive story" and recommend tight framing for Shorts.
  • Test the card against results. YouTube's "stayed to watch" figure for Shorts is the clearest check on whether hook scores mean anything.
  • Keep it short. Seven items scored in two minutes gets used; a 30-item checklist doesn't.

The seven-point scorecard

Item012
Hook (first 1-2 s)Logo, greeting or slow buildClear but genericThe most interesting image or line, instantly
ClarityViewer can't say what it's aboutPoint arrives lateOne idea, clear by second 3
PacingDead air, repeated pointsMostly tightEvery shot moves it on; visual change every few seconds
Text and captionsNone, or unreadablePresent but cluttered or in unsafe areasReadable, inside safe zones, matches speech
SoundUneven levels, music over voiceAcceptableClean voice, music under it, no clipping
PayoffEnds abruptly or nothing deliveredDelivered but flatLands the promise of the hook; loop or clear next step
Technical finishBlurry, wrong ratio, watermarkMinor issuesSharp, 9:16, no third-party watermark

A total of 11 to 14 is ready to post. 8 to 10 needs one more pass. Below 8, go back to the hook and structure first. These cut-offs are a starting point, not a published standard; set your own once you have results.

How to use it in a team

  1. Score blind first. The editor scores their own cut, then the reviewer scores it without seeing the editor's numbers. Differences start the useful conversation.
  2. Comment on the lowest item only. One clear fix per round is faster than ten notes.
  3. Log scores in a spreadsheet with the video link and, a week later, its stayed-to-watch figure and average view duration.
  4. Review monthly. If hook scores don't predict stayed to watch, your definition of a 2 is off. Rewrite it with examples from your best Shorts.
  5. Keep examples. For each item, save one clip that scored 0 and one that scored 2. New editors learn faster from clips than descriptions.

Example: an agency's Reels for a dental clinic

Example: an agency edits 12 Reels a month for a dental clinic using two editors. Before the scorecard, the client sent long notes on every cut. The agency introduces the seven-point card. Editor A's first cut of "3 foods that stain your teeth" scores 9: the hook is the dentist saying "Hi, I'm Dr Patel" (0), but everything else is solid. The fix: open on the coffee cup and wine glass with the text "Your teeth hate these 3 things", greeting removed. Rescored at 12, it's sent to the client with the card attached. After a month, the agency checks the log: the five Reels with a hook score of 2 had higher stayed-to-watch than the seven scoring 1, so they keep the hook definitions as they are. FAQ

Is there a ready-made retention checklist and tracker for video editors?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook includes a cheat sheet, tracker and spreadsheet. A card built from Meta's and Google's free guidance is the alternative.

Should the editor score their own work?

Yes, then compare with the reviewer's score. The gaps show where your standards differ.

How do I know the scorecard works?

Log scores next to stayed to watch and average view duration. If scores don't track results, rewrite the item definitions.

Can I use the same card for ads and organic Shorts?

Mostly. For ads, add branding and a clear call to action, as Google's ABCDs suggest.

How long should scoring take?

About two minutes per Short. If it takes longer, the card has too many items.
