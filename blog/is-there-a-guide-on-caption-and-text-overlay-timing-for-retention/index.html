Is there a guide on caption and text overlay timing for retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. The core rules for caption and text timing are: put the hook text on screen in the first frame, keep reading speed comfortable (TikTok suggests no more than 5-10 words per second), sync captions to speech, hold key text long enough to read twice, and keep everything inside platform safe zones. Key takeaways

  • TikTok recommends "displaying 5-10 words per second when using text" and keeping content inside the UI safe zone.
  • LinkedIn notes "a large portion of LinkedIn members will watch your ad with the sound off" and suggests burned-in subtitles.
  • Google's ABCDs say to "reinforce your message with audio and text" and to "avoid competing elements".
  • W3C defines captions as "a text version of the speech and non-speech audio information needed to understand the content". Overlays are extra text; captions transcribe.

Captions vs text overlays

CaptionsText overlays
WhatTranscription of speech and key soundsHeadlines, labels, key points

PurposeAccess and sound-off viewingHook, emphasis, structure
RiskToo fast, out of syncToo much text, competing with captions

Timing rules that protect retention

  1. Frame 1 text. The hook line on screen before the first word is spoken.
  2. Reading speed. Stay below TikTok's 5-10 words per second ceiling; slower for complex words.
  3. Hold time. Key overlays should stay long enough to read twice. A useful rough check: if you can't read it aloud comfortably before it disappears, it's too short.
  4. One message at a time. Don't stack a headline overlay on top of fast captions.
  5. Sync. Captions should appear as words are spoken, not a beat late.
  6. Position. Inside safe zones; away from platform buttons and caption areas.
  7. Contrast. Readable on bright and dark footage, with an outline or background box.

Common mistakes

  • Captions and overlays fighting for the same space.
  • Whole sentences flashed for half a second.
  • Text in the bottom fifth of a vertical video, where platform captions sit.
  • Auto-captions left uncorrected. Errors make viewers stop trusting the video.
  • Overlays that repeat captions word for word. Use overlays for emphasis, not duplication.

Workflow: adding captions and overlays without slowing the edit

Text timing goes wrong most often when it's added in a rush at the end. A simple order of work avoids that.

StageTaskTip
ScriptWrite the hook overlay and key-point overlaysKeep each to a few words
Rough cutPlace hook overlay on frame 1Check it reads before the first spoken word
Fine cutGenerate captions from the final audioGenerate after picture lock to avoid re-timing
Caption passCorrect errors, split long linesBreak lines at natural pauses
Overlay passTime overlays to the editAvoid overlapping with captions
QAWatch muted on a phoneEvery line should be readable in time

Auto-generated captions save time but need a human check, especially for names, numbers and brand terms. A wrong number in a caption can undo the credibility of the whole video.

Style matters for readability as much as timing. Use one font family, a consistent size and a high-contrast outline or background box. Highlighting one keyword per caption line in a different colour is popular in short-form; used sparingly, it can guide the eye, but colouring every other word makes captions harder to read. Finally, check the platform preview. TikTok, Reels and Shorts each place their own captions and buttons in slightly different spots, so a layout that is safe on one may be partly covered on another.

FAQ

Is there a guide on caption and text timing for retention?

Yes. TikTok's free guidance gives a 5-10 words per second ceiling; Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers text as part of hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time.

Should captions be burned in or uploaded?

Burned in for short-form and social feeds where viewers scroll muted; uploaded for long YouTube videos so viewers can turn them off.

How many words should a hook overlay have?

Usually 3-7, short enough to read in a second or two.

Do captions help retention?

They help viewers who watch muted or prefer reading follow along, which can keep them watching. Test on your own content.

Where should text go on vertical video?

Upper or middle third, inside the platform's safe zone.
