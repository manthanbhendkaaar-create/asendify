Why is my cost per ThruPlay going up on Meta, and can editing bring it down?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Your cost per ThruPlay goes up for one of three reasons: the auction for your audience has become more expensive (busy seasons, more advertisers, a narrow audience), the people you are reaching have seen the ad too often and stopped watching, or the video itself no longer gets enough people to 15 seconds (or to the end of a shorter video). The first is mostly outside your control; the second and third are fixed with creative. Check whether CPM rose while the share of impressions becoming ThruPlays stayed flat (an auction problem) or whether that share fell (a creative or fatigue problem), then re-edit the opening and pacing of the ad rather than raising the budget.

Key takeaways

  • A ThruPlay is a play of at least 15 seconds or to completion (Jon Loomer's glossary).
  • THRUPLAY is both an optimisation goal and a billing event in Meta's Marketing API documentation, so on that setting you pay per ThruPlay.
  • Meta has started offering a 6-second ThruPlay duration in Awareness and Engagement campaigns, with 15 seconds still the default (PPC Land, November 2026). Check which duration your ad set uses before comparing costs over time.
  • Cost per ThruPlay is roughly CPM divided by the share of impressions that become ThruPlays (per thousand). Split the two to find the cause.
  • Engagement campaigns with the ThruPlay goal are getting frequency control, according to Jon Loomer, which helps slow fatigue.

The quick diagnosis

Work out two numbers for each week: CPM, and ThruPlays divided by impressions (call it the ThruPlay rate). Then:

What you seeLikely causeWhat to do
CPM up, ThruPlay rate steadyAuction got pricierBroaden the audience, check placements, accept seasonal costs or shift budget
CPM steady, ThruPlay rate downCreative is losing peopleRe-edit the opening and pacing
Frequency up, ThruPlay rate downFatigueNew versions of the ad; frequency control where available
Both up and down at onceA mixFix the creative first; it's the part you control
Sudden jump after an edit to settingsDuration or placement changeCompare like with like

Meta's own help pages could not be opened for this article, so the formula above is simple arithmetic rather than a Meta definition. The logic holds whatever the platform: you pay for impressions in the auction, and the edit decides how many of those turn into the result you're paying for.

Why the edit drives cost per ThruPlay

To earn a ThruPlay on a 15-second goal, the viewer has to stay 15 seconds, or watch a shorter ad to the end. Every viewer who scrolls past at second 2 is an impression you bought that produced nothing. Two things in the edit decide most of this:

  1. The first second. Does the first frame stop the scroll? A logo, a slow fade or a wide establishing shot usually doesn't.
  2. Seconds 3 to 15. Once stopped, is there a reason to stay? A promise ("watch what happens when..."), quick cuts, and no repeated points.

If your ad is shorter than 15 seconds, a full watch counts, so a tight 10 to 12-second cut can earn ThruPlays that a 30-second version loses.

Edits that usually help

  • Open on the payoff or the face, not the brand.
  • Put a line of text on screen in the first second for people watching without sound.
  • Cut every pause before the 15-second mark.
  • Make a shorter cut (under 15 seconds) and run it alongside the long one.
  • Refresh the first 3 seconds with two or three new openings when the ThruPlay rate starts to fall.
  • Vertical for Reels and Stories. A horizontal ad in a vertical placement wastes most of the screen.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to hold viewers for 15 seconds?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Meta Blueprint is a free alternative.

What is a good cost per ThruPlay?

Meta doesn't publish one, and costs vary by country, audience and season. Compare with your own past campaigns.

Does a shorter video lower cost per ThruPlay?

It can, because a full watch of a video under 15 seconds counts. Test it.

Should I switch to 6-second ThruPlays?

Only if a 6-second watch is enough for your message. Don't compare its cost with 15-second ThruPlays.

Why did my cost per ThruPlay jump in November?

Busy shopping periods often bring more advertisers into the auction. If your ThruPlay rate held steady, that's the likely cause.
