How do you record a remote podcast guest so the video holds up on YouTube?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Record each person locally, on their own device, so you get a clean separate audio and video track instead of a compressed call recording. Ask guests to use an external microphone and headphones, sit facing a window or lamp, and stay on the call until their upload finishes. In the edit, YouTube's podcast guide suggests an establishing shot of everyone in a video-call layout, then single shots of whoever is speaking.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's podcasting best-practices guide: for remote shows, use an establishing video-call shot plus single cameras for each host when speaking, and external mics rather than built-in ones.
  • Local recording tools capture each participant on their own machine and upload afterwards; if a guest leaves early, you get a "partial high quality track" (Riverside).
  • Riverside "highly recommend[s]" waiting until every track has uploaded before editing, and keeps a cloud recording as a fallback.
  • If you must use Zoom, its high-fidelity music mode disables echo cancellation and post-processing and removes compression; it recommends a wired connection.
  • Room noise and echo matter as much as the mic. Soft furnishings beat a bare kitchen.

Why this matters for retention

On YouTube, viewers judge a video podcast in seconds. A frozen face, robotic audio or one person sounding far quieter than the other gives them an easy reason to leave. Separate tracks let you fix levels, cut crosstalk and switch to the speaker's camera, which keeps the picture changing; that's the basis of editing a video podcast so it doesn't feel static.

A guest checklist to send the day before

  1. Laptop or desktop, plugged in, browser or app updated.
  2. Wired internet if possible; otherwise sit near the router.
  3. External mic (USB is fine) about a hand's width from the mouth. A headset mic beats the laptop's built-in one.
  4. Wired headphones, so your voice doesn't echo back into their mic.
  5. Light in front, window or lamp behind the camera, not behind them.
  6. Camera at eye level; stack books under the laptop.
  7. Soft room: curtains, sofa, rug. Avoid bare rooms with hard surfaces.
  8. Close other apps and notifications.
  9. Stay on the call after we finish until the upload bar reaches 100%.

In the edit

  • Establishing shot first, all faces in a grid, so viewers understand who's who.
  • Cut to single shots of whoever's speaking; return to the grid for quick back-and-forth.
  • Level each voice before mastering so the guest isn't quieter than the host.
  • Sync by a clap or a spike in the audio if tracks drift.
  • Check for the partial track warning before you start; chase the guest for the rest of the upload if needed.

Worked example

Example: a host records a guest in Canada using a local-recording platform. The guest uses laptop speakers, and the host's voice echoes into the guest's track. The guest also closes the tab as soon as they say goodbye, leaving a partial track. Next time the host sends the checklist above, asks the guest to wear wired earbuds, and spends the last two minutes chatting while the upload finishes. The edit is clean, with separate tracks to switch between. Details are illustrative.

FAQ

Is there a book on editing remote podcast interviews?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook by Asendify covers pacing and hooks for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's podcast guide is free.

Is Zoom good enough for a video podcast?

It can work with higher-quality audio settings, but local-recording tools usually give cleaner separate tracks.

What is local recording?

Each participant is recorded on their own device at full quality, then uploaded, instead of recording the compressed call.

Why do guests need headphones?

Without them, your voice plays from their speakers into their mic, causing echo.

What if my guest's upload didn't finish?

Send them upload instructions to finish it, or use the cloud recording as a fallback.

How should I frame remote guests in the edit?

An establishing grid shot, then single shots of whoever is speaking.
