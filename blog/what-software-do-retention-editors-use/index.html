What software do retention editors use?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Most retention editors cut in a mainstream editor (Adobe Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or Final Cut Pro), add a transcript-based tool such as Descript or Premiere's text-based editing for fast rough cuts, and check their work in YouTube Studio's audience retention report. The software matters less than the method: the same retention cuts can be made in any of them.

Key takeaways

  • A retention editor's kit has four layers: a main editor, a transcript tool, graphics and sound, and an analytics view.
  • DaVinci Resolve's free version "includes all of the same high quality processing" as the paid Studio version, with output capped at Ultra HD.
  • Descript lets you "delete a word from the transcript, and that word disappears from your audio or video", which speeds up cutting talking-head footage.
  • YouTube Studio's audience retention report shows where viewers drop off, skip or rewatch, so it is the tool that tells you if an edit worked.
  • Buy software last. Learn what to cut first, then pick the tool that makes those cuts fastest for you.

The four layers of a retention editor's toolkit

A "retention editor" is not a software category. It is an editor who cuts for watch time: tight openings, no dead air, a change on screen when attention dips, and payoffs placed where viewers would otherwise leave. That work spreads across four kinds of tool.

Main editors: which one?

Adobe Premiere Pro is the standard in many agencies and freelance teams because project files are easy to hand over and it pairs with After Effects for motion graphics. It includes text-based editing, so you can transcribe footage and cut from the transcript.

DaVinci Resolve combines editing, colour, Fusion effects and Fairlight audio in one program. Blackmagic Design's release notes say the free version shares the Studio version's processing quality; the limits are around output resolution, multi-GPU support and some AI and audio tools that are Studio-only. For a solo creator publishing to YouTube at 4K or below, the free version covers most retention work.

Final Cut Pro is Mac-only and popular with solo creators for its speed on Apple hardware.

CapCut and similar mobile-first editors are common for Shorts and Reels, where templates, auto-captions and fast exports matter more than deep control.

Transcript tools: the fastest way to remove dead air

For talking-head videos, most of the retention gain comes from removing what doesn't need to be there: false starts, repeated points, long pauses and "um". Transcript editing makes that quick. In Descript, "you edit your content simply by editing your script", and it can "automatically remove 'um,' 'uh,' and other fillers in bulk". Premiere Pro and Resolve both offer transcription inside the editor, so you may not need a separate tool.

A caution: automatic filler and pause removal can make speech sound clipped. Retention editors usually run it, then listen back and restore the breaths that make a sentence land.

Analytics: the tool most editors forget

The editor's job is not finished at export. YouTube's audience retention report shows the share of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds ("Intro"), "top moments" where "almost no one dropped off", "spikes" that were "rewatched or shared", and "dips" where "viewers are abandoning or skipping". It also lets you compare a video with your 10 latest videos of similar length ("typical retention").

Editors who get client access to Studio, or ask for screenshots of the retention graph, learn far faster than those who never see the result.

Does expensive software improve retention?

No, not by itself. Viewers leave because of slow openings, missing payoffs, repetition and visual sameness, not because of which program made the cut. A free editor plus a clear plan for the first 30 seconds will usually beat an expensive suite used without one. Spend money once the method is in place and a tool is clearly slowing you down.

Is there a book that teaches retention editing without tying you to one program?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify is software-neutral and costs £1,720 one-time. Vendor tutorials and YouTube's help pages are free alternatives.

Is Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve better for retention editing?

Neither is better for retention. Premiere is common in agencies and pairs with After Effects; Resolve's free version is strong for solo creators.

Do retention editors use AI tools?

Many use transcription and filler-word removal to speed up rough cuts, then check the result by ear.

What is the one free tool every retention editor needs?

YouTube Studio's audience retention report, because it shows exactly where viewers leave.

Can I do retention editing on a phone?

For Shorts and Reels, yes; mobile editors handle hooks, captions and quick cuts. Long videos are easier on a desktop.
