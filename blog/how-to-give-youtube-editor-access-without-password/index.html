How do I give a YouTube editor or agency access to my channel without sharing my password?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use YouTube's channel permissions instead of sharing your password. In YouTube Studio, go to Settings, then Permissions, invite the person by email and pick a role such as Editor or Manager; they sign in with their own Google account, and you can remove them at any time. YouTube's own advice is never to share your password, including with your team.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help lists seven roles: Owner, Manager, Editor, Editor (Limited), Subtitle Editor, Viewer and Viewer (Limited).
  • Invitations are sent from Settings > Permissions in YouTube Studio and expire after 30 days if not accepted.
  • If your channel uses a Brand Account, you must first move to channel permissions before managing access this way.
  • YouTube's 2020 security advice says to create a strong password and "never share it", and that if you work with a team, "do not share your password with them, either".
  • YouTube's channel security tips say to remove access for people who leave your team and to set up two-step verification.

Why not just share the password?

Sharing your Google password hands over far more than your channel: email, Drive, payments and every other Google service on that account. You cannot tell who did what, you cannot take access back without changing the password for everyone, and a phished freelancer becomes a phished channel. Channel permissions solve all three problems. Each person signs in as themselves, with only the access their role allows.

Editors can view channel data and edit nearly everything, but cannot delete published content, manage permissions, enter contracts, or delete or reset stream keys. Managers can do almost everything except delete the channel. Owners cannot transfer ownership to another user through permissions.

Start with the least access that lets the person do the job, then raise it if needed. Moving someone up later is easy; undoing damage is not.

How to invite someone

YouTube Help describes the steps in the YouTube Studio app; the desktop Studio uses the same Settings and Permissions area.

  1. Open YouTube Studio and go to Settings, then Permissions.
  2. Choose Invite, enter the person's email address and pick a role.
  3. Send the invitation. The person must accept it within 30 days.
  4. To remove someone, return to Permissions, select them and choose Remove.

If your channel is on a Brand Account, YouTube says you need to migrate to channel permissions first. Only managers and the owner can see the names and email addresses of everyone with access.

Limits to know about

  • Not everything carries over. YouTube Help says invited users cannot access YouTube Music, the YouTube Kids app or YouTube APIs through your channel, even though the owner can. Tools that connect through the API usually need the owner to authorise them.
  • Teen access is visible to guardians. If you give access to someone aged 13 to 17 (or the local equivalent), their supervising guardian gets a dashboard with public information about your channel.
  • Invitations expire after 30 days.

What to agree before you give access

Permissions control what someone can do in YouTube Studio, not what they should do. Before you send an invitation, agree a few things in writing, even in a short email.

  • Scope: which videos they may upload, edit or publish, and whether anything goes live without your approval.
  • Titles, thumbnails and descriptions: who has the final say.
  • Comments and community posts: whether they reply on your behalf.
  • Analytics: what data they may see and whether they can share screenshots.
  • Ending the work: that you will remove access on the last day, and that they will hand back any project files.

This keeps expectations clear on both sides and makes removing access a routine step rather than an awkward conversation.

Keep the channel secure

YouTube's security pages give a short checklist that applies to everyone with access, not only the owner.

  1. Turn on two-step verification for your Google account. YouTube recommends it, with a passkey, "for the strongest protection against threats like phishing". Its 2020 advice says everyone listed in channel permissions should have it on too.
  2. Review who has access regularly and remove anyone who has left or whom you do not recognise.
  3. Check connected apps in your Google account and remove ones you no longer use.
  4. Be wary of fake sponsors. YouTube suggests verifying brand deal emails by calling the company on a publicly listed number. YouTube will never ask for your password in an email, message or call.
  5. Ask everyone with access to read the safety tips, as YouTube recommends.

FAQ

Is there a YouTube agency that edits Shorts and long-form for retention?

Asendify is a YouTube growth agency built around retention editing, but it is currently full and invite-only. Freelance editors are the fair alternative for now.

Can an editor delete my videos?

Not with the Editor role. YouTube Help says Editors cannot delete published content or the channel. Managers can delete content but not the channel.

Can I hide my earnings from my editor?

Yes. Choose Editor (Limited), which has Editor permissions without access to revenue data.

How long does a channel invitation last?

It expires after 30 days if the person does not accept it.

Do I still need a Brand Account to add people?

No. YouTube says Brand Account channels should migrate to channel permissions to manage access this way.
