How fast should the cuts be in a TikTok ad?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Fast at the start, then paced by what's being said. Most TikTok ads work best when pauses and breaths are jump-cut out, the picture changes every 1 to 3 seconds in the opening, and the product appears within 3 seconds, as TikTok advises to introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds. The limit is reading speed: TikTok suggests 5 to 10 words per second for on-screen text, so never cut faster than viewers can read.

Key takeaways

  • TikTok's guidance: "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds" and put the proposition in the first 3.
  • A 6-second watch counts as a focused view, so the pace of the first 6 seconds matters most.
  • On TikTok, "fast" usually means tight speech (no pauses) more than many separate shots. Creator-style jump cuts are native to the platform.
  • Text sets the speed limit: TikTok recommends 5 to 10 words per second when you use text overlays.
  • Cuts cost attention. Film researchers note that cuts "trigger eye movements"; cutting without new information adds effort, not interest.

Two kinds of fast on TikTok

StyleWhat's cutTypical shot lengthFeels like
Talking-head jump cutsPauses, breaths, "um"s, repeated words1 to 4 s between cuts, same framing or small punch-insA creator talking straight to you
Visual montageDifferent shots, angles, locations0.5 to 2 sA trailer or product reel

Most performing ads mix the two: a talking head that keeps the thread, with quick inserts of the product or result.

A pacing plan for a 20-second TikTok ad

TimePaceContent

3 to 6 s2 cutsThe problem made concrete; still on the product

18 to 20 sHeldCall to action and text

When to slow down

Fast isn't always better. Slow down when:

  • Text is on screen. At 5 to 10 words per second, an 8-word caption needs at least a second, more if it is the key message.
  • The result needs proof. A before-and-after flashed for 0.4 seconds looks like a trick. Hold it.
  • The product detail matters. Texture, size, a screen interface: give it time.
  • Emotion is the point. A reaction lands better when the face is held for a beat.
  • The call to action appears. Hold the last shot so viewers know what to do.

Example: re-cutting a skincare ad

Example: the first version was a 25-second polished brand video with 8 slow shots and music. The re-cut, using phone footage from a creator:

TimeCutsContent
0 to 3 s3"I've had dry patches on my cheeks all winter." Close-up of dry skin, then the tube in hand
3 to 6 s2Pauses removed: "Dermatologist told me to look for one ingredient." Tube label close-up
6 to 13 s4Applying it morning and night, jump cuts between days
13 to 18 s2Day 14 close-up, held in the same light as day 1
18 to 22 s1Creator holding the tube: "Link's below."

Twelve cuts in 22 seconds, but the result shot is held. Skincare claims need evidence, the creator's experience must be genuine and the ad must be labelled; health details here are general information, not medical advice. Details are illustrative.

How to test pace

TikTok suggests running 3 to 5 different creatives per ad group. Use that to test pace on purpose: the same script cut tight, medium and with more held shots. Keep everything else the same so the comparison is fair. For more on hooks for TikTok-style ads, see where open loops should go in a TikTok ad.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches pacing for TikTok-style videos?

The Retention Editing Playbook teaches pacing and jump cuts for £1,720 one-time; it is YouTube and Shorts first and applies to TikTok.

How many cuts should a 15-second TikTok ad have?

There is no set number. Many have 6 to 12, but cut when a shot has done its job, not on a timer.

Should I jump-cut out every pause?

Most pauses, yes. Keep a beat after a key line or a reaction so it lands.

How fast can on-screen text go?

TikTok recommends 5 to 10 words per second. Key messages should stay up longer.

Do polished, slow ads work on TikTok?

They can, but they usually need a strong opening shot and the product shown early.
