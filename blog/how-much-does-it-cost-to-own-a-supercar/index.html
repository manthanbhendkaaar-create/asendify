How much does it cost to own a supercar?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The yearly cost of owning a supercar is the purchase price plus depreciation, insurance, servicing, tyres, fuel and tax, and for most cars depreciation and insurance are the biggest items. In the UK, a petrol car with a list price over £40,000 pays £640 a year in vehicle tax for five years from its second tax payment. The rest varies so much by car, age and driver that you need real quotes before you buy.

This page is for education only, not financial advice.

Key takeaways

  • Budget for running costs as well as the price. They can add up to a large share of the car's value every year.
  • Depreciation is usually the biggest hidden cost. MoneyHelper notes a typical new car could be worth half its original value after three years.
  • UK vehicle tax includes an expensive car supplement on cars with a list price over £40,000 (over £50,000 for some electric cars).
  • Insurance depends heavily on your age, location, driving record and the car. Get quotes before committing.
  • Keep a separate fund for repairs. Specialist parts and labour can be costly, especially out of warranty.

Depreciation: the cost you do not see each month

Depreciation is the money the car loses in value while you own it. MoneyHelper's guide to the cost of running a car says "New cars depreciate very quickly" and that "after the first three years it could be worth half its original value". Its example is a £30,000 car worth £15,000 after three years, which means you have "effectively lost £15,000". It also says buying used is usually better value, because used cars lose value more slowly.

Some supercars lose value faster than average, and a few rare models can hold their value well. There is no rule that covers every car. The honest way to estimate depreciation is to look up current listings for the same model at different ages and mileages.

Vehicle tax on expensive cars

The GOV.UK vehicle tax rate tables set out what you pay for cars registered on or after 1 April 2017:

The first-year rate is usually included in the price of a new car, but it is still part of the cost. Check the GOV.UK tables for the current figures before you buy, as rates change.

Insurance, servicing and tyres

MoneyHelper says insurance can be one of the biggest running costs, especially for younger drivers, people who have recently had an accident, high-mileage drivers, and owners of expensive or powerful cars. It does not give a typical figure, because quotes vary so widely.

Servicing is not a legal requirement, but MoneyHelper notes it helps keep the car running well, supports resale value and can keep warranties valid. For supercars, servicing at a franchised dealer is often expected by buyers when you come to sell, so a full service history matters.

Tyres on high-performance cars are wide, soft and can wear quickly if driven hard. Price a set for the exact car and assume you may replace them more often than on an ordinary car.

The MOT

GOV.UK's MOT fees page says the maximum fee for a car is £54.85. The fee is small; the repairs needed to pass can be expensive.

How to work out your own number

  1. Pick the exact car, including year, mileage and spec.
  2. Get three insurance quotes on that car with your real details.
  3. Look up the tax on GOV.UK using the registration date and list price.
  4. Ask for service prices from a dealer and an independent specialist.
  5. Estimate depreciation from current listings for older examples of the same model.
  6. Add fuel and tyres based on how far you will drive.
  7. Add a repair fund, especially for cars out of warranty.
  8. Divide by 12 to see the monthly cost, and compare it with what you can save each month.

FAQ

How much does a supercar cost per year to run?

It depends on the car, its age and you. Add depreciation, insurance, tax, servicing, tyres, fuel and a repair fund, using real quotes. For many cars, depreciation and insurance are the biggest items.

Do supercars pay more road tax in the UK?

Yes, if the list price is over £40,000 (or over £50,000 for some electric cars). They pay an extra £440 a year on top of the standard rate for five years from the second time the car is taxed.

Is it cheaper to buy a used supercar?

Usually, because the first owner absorbs the steepest depreciation. MoneyHelper notes used cars lose value more slowly. Running costs such as servicing and repairs can be higher on older cars.

Should I include depreciation in my budget?

Yes. It is a real cost, even if you do not pay it monthly. You see it when you sell or part-exchange the car.
