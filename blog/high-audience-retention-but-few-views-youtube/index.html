Why does my YouTube video have good retention but few views?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Good retention with few views usually means the problem is before the click, not after it. Retention measures how well you keep the people who start watching; views depend on how many people YouTube shows your video to (impressions) and how many of them choose to click. If impressions are low, or your title and thumbnail do not earn clicks, strong retention has little to work with. YouTube also weighs satisfaction, not just watch time, when deciding what to recommend.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help defines impressions as how many times your thumbnails were shown, and impressions click-through rate as "how often viewers watched a video after seeing a thumbnail."
  • YouTube's VP of Engineering wrote that clicks, watch time, survey responses, shares, likes and dislikes all feed recommendations, and that only videos rated four or five stars count as "valued watchtime" (YouTube blog, 2021).
  • YouTube's recommendations help page says surveys help the system understand "satisfaction, not just watch time."
  • So views need three things: enough impressions, a click, and a satisfied viewer. Retention covers only part of the last one.
  • Diagnose with the Reach tab before changing your editing.

The three stages a view goes through

StageMetric in YouTube StudioIf this is weak
1. YouTube shows your thumbnailImpressionsFew people ever see the video
2. A viewer clicksImpressions click-through rateThe packaging does not earn the click
3. The viewer watches and is satisfiedAverage view duration, retention, likes, survey responsesViewers leave or are not satisfied

Good retention means stage 3 is working for the people who arrive. If stages 1 or 2 are weak, there are not many people at stage 3.

Common causes, and how to tell which is yours

Low impressions

If YouTube has shown the video only a few hundred times, it has not been tested widely yet. This is common for new channels, very niche topics and videos that do not fit what your current viewers watch.

Check: Reach tab, impressions over the first 7 and 28 days compared with your other videos.

Low click-through rate

If impressions are healthy but few people click, the title and thumbnail are not doing their job, even if the video is good. Our guides to writing good YouTube titles and impressions but no views go deeper.

Check: impressions CTR compared with your channel's usual range.

A topic with little demand

A brilliantly edited video on something few people search for or browse will stay small. Retention cannot create demand.

Check: traffic sources. If search is your main source and search terms show low volume, the topic may be the limit.

High retention from a tiny audience

A video watched by 80 loyal subscribers may show excellent retention because those viewers already like you. That does not mean new viewers will behave the same.

Check: new vs returning viewers, and retention for each if available.

Short video, high percentage

A 2-minute video can show 70% retention but only 84 seconds of average watch time. Percentage alone can flatter short videos.

Check: average view duration in minutes, not just percentage.

Example

A small channel posts a 9-minute video on fixing a rare camera fault. YouTube Studio after 28 days shows:

MetricThis videoChannel typical
Impressions3,20018,000
Impressions CTR6.1%5.5%
Average view duration5:403:50
Views2101,100

Click-through and watch time are both above the channel's normal, so packaging and editing are fine. Impressions are the gap: the topic is narrow. The fix is not more editing. Options include a broader title that still matches the content ("Camera won't focus? Try this first"), a companion Short pointing to it, or accepting it as a small, useful search video that will collect views slowly.

Retention or reach: which to work on first

What you seeWork on first
Low impressions, normal CTR, good retentionTopic and angle; Shorts and playlists that lead to the video
Normal impressions, low CTR, good retentionTitle and thumbnail
Normal impressions, normal CTR, weak retentionEditing: openings, pacing and re-hooks
Everything lowTopic choice first, then packaging

This order matters because each fix only helps the stage it targets. Better editing cannot raise impressions on its own, and a better thumbnail cannot keep viewers who leave at 20 seconds. Work on the weakest stage, change one thing at a time, and compare against your own channel's typical numbers over the same number of days.

What to do next

  1. Check Reach first. Impressions and CTR tell you whether the issue is before the click.
  2. Compare with your own typical. Judge against your channel, not other creators.
  3. Fix packaging before editing. Test a new thumbnail or title if CTR is low.
  4. Think about the topic. Is there an angle with more demand that still uses your strength?
  5. Keep the editing. Strong retention is an asset; when impressions come, it helps the video keep going.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches named retention editing techniques, with a 30-day plan?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops, re-hooks and pattern interrupts, with a Bonus Pack including a 30-day plan. YouTube Studio's Reach tab is the free place to diagnose low views.

Does YouTube only care about watch time?

No. YouTube says it also uses clicks, survey responses, shares, likes and dislikes, and weighs satisfaction.

Will a video with good retention eventually get more views?

Sometimes, especially search-driven videos. But if impressions stay low, retention alone will not change that.

What is a good click-through rate?

It varies by channel and traffic source. Compare against your own channel's typical range.

Should I re-upload a video with good retention and few views?

Usually no. Try a new title or thumbnail first.

Can low views be caused by my channel's other videos?

Your audience's habits matter. A video unlike your usual content may get fewer impressions from your existing viewers.
