How do I label UGC creator ads as ads in the UK?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Label a UGC creator ad with a clear, upfront "Ad" (or "Advert") label that viewers see straight away, in the video itself. UK guidance says a brand tag, a discount code or a mention in the bio is not enough on its own, and the label should not be buried in hashtags or sit behind "See more". If a brand pays, gifts or controls the content, assume it is an ad.

Key takeaways

  • The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) guidance says "It must be immediately clear that your content is an ad", and it "should not be hidden or buried in many other hashtags" (GOV.UK).
  • For video, the same guidance says disclosure must be given "at the beginning of the ad" and be clear "from the face of the content", not from a bio or an earlier post.
  • The ASA expects "a prominent 'ad' label upfront" and says it is "not necessary to use a '#'" (ASA).
  • Labels such as "Sponsored", "aff", "spon", "Gifted" or "In association with" are discouraged or likely to be insufficient, according to the ASA.
  • Editors can build the label into the edit, so it survives reposts, whitelisting and re-cuts.

Why this is an editing job, not only a legal one

A UGC ad is usually re-cut. The brand's agency takes the creator's raw clips, cuts a new hook, adds captions and music, and runs it as a paid ad, sometimes from the creator's own handle. Each of those steps can strip a label. If the label lived only in the creator's original caption, the new version may carry none.

So treat the label as an editing element, like a caption style:

  1. Put "Ad" on screen at the start. Use a plain, readable font in a corner or lower third that is clear of platform interface buttons. Keep it up for the opening seconds.
  2. Keep it consistent across variants. If you cut five hook versions, every one carries the same label.
  3. Do not rely on the paid-partnership tool alone. Use it, and add the visible label.
  4. Write the caption's first words as "Ad". Many platforms truncate captions, so a label at the end may never be seen.
  5. Record it in the brief. Add a line to the creator brief and the edit checklist: "Label: Ad, on screen from 0:00 and in caption line one."

What the guidance says about incentives

The CMA guidance says any form of incentive or reward counts, "including money, commissions, discounts", and that this includes posts "about your own business". It also warns that "It is not enough just to tag a brand in your post, use discount codes or affiliate links." If a post has more than one item, such as a carousel, the guidance says each item containing promotional messaging must be labelled.

That matters for UGC ad agencies because a creator may be paid in product only. A free product with an expectation of a post is still an incentive for these purposes. If you are unsure, label it.

Common mistakes editors make

  • Cutting the label out with the intro. If the label sits in the first two seconds and you trim the first two seconds for a faster hook, it goes too. Re-add it to the new opening.
  • Placing it where platform buttons cover it. Check it on a phone preview with the interface visible.
  • Using a tiny or low-contrast label. It should be readable at a glance, including with the sound off.
  • Labelling only the main version. Cut-downs, Shorts and Reels versions need it too.
  • Assuming a disclaimer in the end card is enough. For video, the guidance points to the beginning.

What this page does not cover

This page covers labelling, not contracts, usage rights or health and finance claims in ads. Those have their own rules. For high-spend campaigns, ask a lawyer or your agency's compliance lead. The CMA and ASA pages above are free and are the sources to check for current wording.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on editing UGC ads for retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and pattern interrupts with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. For labelling rules, use the free CMA and ASA guidance.

Is "#ad" enough for a UGC video ad?

Only if it is clear and upfront. The ASA says a "#" is not required, but burying the label in hashtags at the end is not acceptable. For video, put the label on screen at the start as well.

Does the platform's paid-partnership label count?

It can help, but the ASA says it "may not be enough on its own". Add a visible "Ad" label.

Do I need a label if the creator only got free product?

Possibly yes. The CMA guidance treats gifts and other incentives as payment, so label it if the post is part of an agreement.

Who is responsible for the label?

Both the brand and the creator can be. Put the label requirement in the brief and check it in every edited version.
