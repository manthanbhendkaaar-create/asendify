Should you buy a Cat S or Cat N supercar?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Only if the discount is big enough to cover what you lose, and you can verify the repair. What Car? says Cat N cars are sometimes 20-40% cheaper and Cat S up to about 50% cheaper, but insurance usually costs more, a Cat S car's manufacturer warranty is likely to be invalid, and the write-off record stays with the car for good, cutting what you get when you sell. For a supercar, an expert inspection matters more than the headline price.

This page is education only, not financial advice. Figures in the example are planning assumptions, not quotes.

Key takeaways

  • Cat S means structural damage that has been repaired; Cat N means non-structural damage such as cosmetic, mechanical or electrical faults.
  • The current categories date from October 2017, when Cat S and Cat N replaced Cat C and Cat D.
  • Insurers write cars off when repairs cost more than the car is worth, and their estimates assume main-dealer labour and new parts, so a write-off is not always badly damaged.
  • Discounts are real, but so are the costs: higher insurance, harder resale, a likely void warranty on Cat S, and the need for specialist checks.
  • Compare the yearly cost of ownership of the Cat car and a clean one, not just the purchase price.

evo's guide to write-off categories explains that the categories were refreshed in late October 2017 so that they judge the car's condition rather than the cost of repair. It also points out why prestige cars end up written off: insurers estimate repairs using official workshops and new manufacturer parts, which inflates the bill, while an independent repairer may use used parts and cheaper labour.

For supercars, that cuts both ways. A modest-looking accident can write off a car with carbon panels, expensive lights and sensors, which is why some Cat N supercars have relatively light damage. But the same expensive parts are where a cheap repair can cut corners.

The paperwork

What Car?'s guide to write-offs (updated November 2025) sets out what happens to the logbook:

  • Cat S: the insurer destroys the original logbook. After repair and an MOT, the owner tells the DVLA the car is going back on the road and applies for a new registration document on form V62, which states "Cat S".
  • Cat N: the existing V5C can be kept, and the logbook does not show the write-off, so you need a history check to find it.

The owner whose car is written off must tell the DVLA. GOV.UK says you can be fined £1,000 if you do not.

What you give up for the discount

  • Insurance. What Car? says insurers typically charge more, noticeably more for Cat S. You must declare the write-off or a future claim may be refused.
  • Warranty. What Car? says any existing manufacturer warranty on a Cat S car is likely invalidated. On a supercar, where a single gearbox or engine repair can run to five figures, this is a serious loss.
  • Resale. The write-off record can never be removed, and resale value is almost always lower. evo adds that the history puts some buyers off when you come to sell.
  • Unknown repair quality. The discount assumes a proper repair. A poor one can cost more to put right than you saved.

Checks before you buy

  1. A full history check to confirm the category, the damage recorded, outstanding finance and MOT history.
  2. An independent inspection by a specialist in that make, not just a general mechanic. What Car? notes inspections cost upwards of £100; a specialist supercar inspection will often cost more.
  3. Ask exactly what happened, which parts were replaced and who did the repair. Walk away if the answers are vague.
  4. Photos and invoices of the repair, ideally from a manufacturer-approved bodyshop.
  5. Get insurance quotes first, with the category declared, before you commit.
  6. Choose Cat N over Cat S if you want to avoid structural damage, as evo advises.

Example: is the discount worth it?

Ravi is choosing between two of the same model, both four years old with similar mileage. These are illustrative planning figures:

The Cat N car frees up £22,000 of cash on day one, but in this example it costs about £3,200 more over three years, because insurance is higher and the resale gap stays roughly the same in pounds. If Ravi could only afford the model as a Cat N, the question becomes whether the higher yearly cost still fits his budget. If he can afford the clean car, it is the cheaper one to own here.

Your numbers will differ, which is the point: compare yearly cost, not price.

FAQ

Is there a planner that ranks supercars by what they cost per year at my mileage?

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner ranks cars that fit your budget by yearly cost at your mileage, with editable running costs. Your own spreadsheet is the free alternative.

Is a Cat N supercar safe?

Cat N means the damage was not structural, but safety depends on the repair. Get a specialist inspection before buying.

How much cheaper is a Cat S car?

What Car? says up to about 50% cheaper than a clean equivalent, depending on the damage.

Does a Cat N car show on the logbook?

No. What Car? says the V5C does not state Cat N, so you need a paid history check to find it.

Can I get a warranty on a Cat S car?

The manufacturer warranty is likely to be invalid, but What Car? notes some used-car warranty providers cover Cat S and Cat N cars.
