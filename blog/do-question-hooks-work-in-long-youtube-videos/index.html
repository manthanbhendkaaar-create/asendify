Do question hooks work in long YouTube videos, or do they lose viewers?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Question hooks work in long YouTube videos when the question is specific, the viewer half-knows the answer, and the video answers it at a sensible point. They lose viewers when the question is vague ("Have you ever wondered...?"), when everyone already knows the answer, or when the answer is held back so long that people give up. Use a question to open a gap, then keep raising new ones as you close the old ones.

Key takeaways

  • In a study of trivia questions, Kang and colleagues (2009), Psychological Science, found curiosity was "an inverted-U-shaped function" of confidence, peaking when people were about 50% sure of the answer. Questions viewers half-know create the most curiosity.
  • The same study found higher curiosity was linked with better recall of surprising answers 1 to 2 weeks later, so a good question can make the answer more memorable too.
  • The paper describes curiosity as arising from an "information gap", and notes that when people already know a lot, curiosity falls. A question with an obvious answer does little.
  • Wistia's 2025 analysis found many viewers drop off around the halfway point, so one opening question is rarely enough to carry a long video.
  • YouTube's retention report shows dips where viewers abandon or skip. A dip just after the opening question often means it felt vague or the video went off topic.

"What's the best camera?"Too broad for one video"Is a £400 camera from 2019 still better than a new phone for YouTube?"
"Want to know a secret?"Feels like clickbait"What did the first iPhone prototype do that Apple removed before launch?"

How to use questions across a long video

  1. Open with one specific question the title already hints at. The viewer clicked for it.
  2. Give a partial answer early. This proves the video will deliver and keeps trust.
  3. Raise a second question before closing the first. "But that raises a stranger problem..." carries viewers into the next section.
  4. Answer each question on time. If a section ends without answering what it promised, viewers feel misled.
  5. Re-ask the main question near the middle. A one-line reminder helps viewers who are drifting.
  6. Close the main question clearly at the end, then finish quickly.

Questions and the title

The question in the first seconds should match what the title and thumbnail promised. If the title asks "Why did Britain drive on the left?", the opening should ask exactly that, not a broader question about road history. Viewers who see the same promise repeated feel they are in the right place; viewers who see a different question wonder whether to stay.

Testing your question hooks

Write three versions of the opening question for your next video before filming: one broad, one specific, and one that states a surprising fact instead of asking. Read them to someone in your audience, or post them as a community poll, and ask which video they would click and keep watching. Then film the winner. After publishing, check the retention graph for the first 30 seconds. If there is a sharp drop right after the question, it was probably too vague or the video took too long to start answering it. Keep a list of your best-performing opening questions; over time it shows which kinds of gaps your viewers care about most.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on question hooks for YouTube?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and open loops with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's analytics help pages are a free companion.

Should a YouTube video start with a question?

It can, if the question is specific and matches the title. A bold statement or a striking visual can work just as well.

How many questions should a long video ask?

One main question, plus a new smaller one in each section. Avoid having several unanswered at once.

Are rhetorical questions good hooks?

Usually not. If the answer is obvious, there is no gap to close, so curiosity stays low.

What if my topic does not suit a question?

Use a statement that creates the same gap, such as a surprising result you then explain.
