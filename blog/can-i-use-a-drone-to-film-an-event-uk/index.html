Can I use a drone to film an event in the UK, and how do I use the footage in the edit?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes, but within the CAA's Drone Code: never fly over crowds, stay below 120m, keep the drone in direct sight, keep the required distance from uninvolved people, and hold a Flyer ID and Operator ID for most camera drones. If you're filming for a client, you must have third-party insurance. In the edit, use aerials as short establishing shots or transitions, not long openers, because the first seconds are where viewers decide to stay.

Key takeaways

  • CAA Drone Code rule: "Never fly over people who are crowded together", whatever the size of drone; sports events, festivals, concerts and rallies are listed as crowded settings.
  • "Never fly more than 120m (400ft) above the earth's surface", and always keep the drone in direct sight.
  • Most camera drones of 100g or more need a Flyer ID and an Operator ID.
  • "If you fly commercially, you must have third party insurance", and you need the landowner's permission to take off and land.
  • Flying may be temporarily banned around some events, so check before the day.
  • In the edit, two to five seconds of aerial usually does more than thirty.

The rules that matter at events

The CAA's Drone Code (March 2026) is the main reference for most event flying in the open category.

Drones placed on the market from 1 January 2026 must carry a UK class mark (UK0 to UK6), and the CAA's updates page notes new Remote ID requirements from that date for UK1, UK2 and UK3 aircraft. The CAA's where you can fly guidance adds that you need the landowner's permission to take off and land, and that commercial flyers must have third-party insurance. Ask the venue and the event organiser in writing.

Using drone footage so viewers keep watching

Aerials look impressive, but a long, slow flyover at the start of a recap gives viewers nothing new each second. Use them for what they do best:

UseLengthWhy it works
Establishing shot of the venue2 to 4 secondsTells viewers where they are, fast
Transition between days or sessions2 to 3 secondsA natural reset that signals "new section"
Scale reveal (empty field to full site)3 to 5 secondsA before-and-after the ground can't show
Closing pull-back3 to 5 secondsA clean ending under the final line or logo

Open on people and moments, then cut to the aerial once viewers know why they're watching. Fast, varied shots and clear payoffs keep people watching; a minute of scenery rarely does.

Example: a two-day outdoor food festival

Example: an organiser hires a videographer to make a 90-second recap. The pilot holds a Flyer ID and Operator ID, has public liability and drone insurance, and gets written permission from the landowner. Before the gates open, she flies the empty site for a reveal shot. During the event, she flies only over a fenced-off back field, more than 50m from guests and never over the crowd at the main stage.

SecondsShot
0 to 3Close-up: chef flipping food, crowd cheering (ground camera)

70 to 74Aerial transition to the evening (drone)

Pre-flight checklist for event days

  • Flyer ID and Operator ID current, with the Operator ID label on the drone.
  • Written permission from the landowner or venue to take off and land.
  • Third-party insurance in place for commercial work, with a copy for the organiser.
  • Any temporary restrictions for the date and location checked.
  • A planned flight area away from guests, agreed with the organiser, and a spotter if needed.
  • A plan for shots that would need flying over the crowd: get them from the ground or a high vantage point instead.
  • Batteries charged, and timings agreed for flights before gates open or after the crowd leaves.

Tell guests in the event information that filming, including aerial filming, will take place. It helps with privacy expectations and with using the footage afterwards.

Is there a book that teaches how to hold attention in the first seconds of a video?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Free creator guides are an alternative.

Can I fly a drone over a festival crowd?

No. The Drone Code says never fly over people who are crowded together, whatever the drone's size.

Do I need a licence to film an event with a drone?

For most open-category flying you need a Flyer ID and Operator ID rather than a licence; some more complex flights need CAA authorisation.

Do I need insurance to film a client's event?

Yes. The CAA says commercial flyers must have third-party insurance.

How long should drone shots be in an event recap?

Usually a few seconds each, used as establishing shots or transitions.
