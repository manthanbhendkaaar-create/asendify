Is there a free tool that scores my YouTube channel out of 100?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast scores any public channel out of 100, weighting titles and thumbnails at 40% each and upload consistency at 20%, with no sign-up; vidIQ's free channel audit is a fair alternative that scores five public growth signals. Neither sees your private analytics, so check the score against YouTube Studio before changing anything.

Key takeaways

  • Free channel scores use public data only: titles, thumbnails, views, likes and upload dates.
  • They cannot see retention, click-through rate, watch time or traffic sources, which live in YouTube Studio.
  • Different tools weight different things, so the same channel can score differently in each.
  • A score is most useful for what it points to: the two or three things to fix first.
  • Run the same tool again after 30 to 60 days of changes to see whether the score moves.

YouTube Channel Roast

The Roast reads your public channel page and your latest 15 public uploads. It scores titles (40%) on curiosity, clarity, specificity, length and whether a stranger would click. It scores the thumbnails of your 8 most recent uploads (40%) on phone readability, contrast, focal point, amount of text, consistency and emotion. Consistency (20%) comes from your upload dates: about 11 uploads in the last 30 days gets full marks, and points come off after 14 quiet days.

You get an overall score out of 100, three sub-scores, a one-line verdict, five one-line roasts, three fixes for the week, your best and worst title with a rewrite of the worst, and a share link. A channel needs at least three public uploads. It does not judge spoken hooks, audio, editing or retention, and it roasts packaging only, never anyone's looks or voice.

vidIQ free channel audit

vidIQ's free YouTube channel audit tool says it scores a channel across five growth signals with no sign-up: upload consistency, SEO optimisation, engagement rate, views-to-subscriber ratio and posting recency. The page itself says the score is "directional because public data does not include impressions, click-through rate, watch time, audience retention, or traffic sources". You can audit any public channel, which helps with competitor comparisons.

YouTube Studio

YouTube Studio does not give one score, but it holds the numbers that matter most. YouTube's guide to getting started with Insights explains that the analytics area is now called Insights, with tabs for Overview, Content, Reach, Engagement, Audience and more. That is where you confirm whatever a free score suggests.

This matters because a low score does not prove a channel is failing, and a high score does not prove it is healthy. YouTube's impressions and click-through rate FAQ says half of all channels and videos have an impressions click-through rate between 2% and 10%, and that new videos or channels, or videos with fewer than 100 views, can see an even wider range. Only Studio tells you where you sit.

Are free channel scores accurate?

They are accurate about what they measure, which is your public packaging and posting pattern. They are not a full health check. Treat a score like a quick second opinion: useful for spotting obvious problems, not a verdict on your channel. Two tools giving different scores is normal, because they weight different signals.

FAQ

What is a good YouTube channel score out of 100?

There is no official YouTube score, so each tool sets its own scale. Use a score to compare your channel with itself over time, and to find your weakest area, rather than chasing a set number.

Can a free tool see my YouTube analytics?

Not the free public-data tools described here. Retention, click-through rate, watch time and traffic sources are private to the channel owner and live in YouTube Studio.

Can I score someone else's YouTube channel?

Yes. Tools that use public data, such as the Roast and vidIQ's free audit, can score any public channel. That is useful for comparing your packaging with channels in your niche.

How often should I check my channel score?

After you make changes, wait 30 to 60 days and score again. Checking every day will not show much, because the tools look at your most recent uploads.

Why did my score drop when I stopped uploading?

Many tools count upload consistency and recency. The Roast, for example, takes points off after 14 days without an upload, so a quiet spell lowers the score even if your titles and thumbnails are unchanged.
