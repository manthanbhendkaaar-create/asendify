How long can a YouTube Short be, and what format should it use?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A YouTube Short can be up to three minutes long. It should be a vertical video, and YouTube's help page notes a maximum resolution of 1080p for uploaded Shorts. You can record in the YouTube app or upload a vertical video from your phone or computer, add a title of up to 100 characters and publish. Being allowed three minutes doesn't mean you should use them: cut every Short to the length its idea needs.

Key takeaways

  • Shorts can be "up to three minutes long", according to YouTube's Shorts help page.
  • Upload vertical videos from your device or record in the YouTube app using the Short option.
  • The help page notes a "maximum resolution of 1080p" for uploaded Shorts.
  • Titles can be up to 100 characters; hashtags can be added while recording, and up to three may show by the title.
  • Use the same frame rate you recorded at, as YouTube advises for all uploads.

YouTube's Shorts basics

YouTube's help page on getting started with Shorts covers the essentials:

SettingYouTube's guidance
LengthUp to three minutes
OrientationVertical
Upload resolutionMaximum 1080p for uploaded Shorts
TitleUp to 100 characters
How to createRecord in the YouTube app (Create, then Short) or upload a vertical video from your device
Before publishingAdd a title, set privacy, then upload or save a draft

The page also links to a separate article on music eligibility for Shorts, which is worth checking if you use commercial music. YouTube's Audio Library is a safe source; see our page on where to get free music for YouTube videos.

Export settings for a Short

YouTube's general upload encoding settings still apply:

SettingRecommendation
ContainerMP4
Video codecH.264, High Profile, progressive scan, 4:2:0
AudioAAC-LC (or Opus), 48 kHz
Frame rateSame as recorded: "Content should be encoded and uploaded using the same frame rate that was used during recording"
Bitrate at 1080p8 Mbps at 24 to 30fps; 12 Mbps at 48 to 60fps
Aspect ratioVertical; the player "adapts automatically" to non-16:9 ratios

A common vertical canvas is 1080 x 1920 pixels, which keeps you at the 1080p maximum. Our page on what frame rate to use for YouTube explains frame-rate choices.

Hashtags on Shorts

YouTube's hashtags help page says creators can add hashtags to the title and description, including while recording a Short. Up to three "considered the most engaging" can show by the title, and a video with more than 60 hashtags has all of them ignored. Hashtags must relate directly to the content and can't contain spaces.

How long should your Short actually be?

YouTube sets the maximum, not the ideal. These are practical editing suggestions:

Type of ShortTypical length that fits the idea
Single tip or jokeAs short as it can be while landing the point
Quick tutorialLong enough to show each step clearly
Story or before-and-afterEnough for the setup and payoff
Clip from a long videoOne complete moment, not the whole segment

The test is simple: if a second doesn't add to the idea, cut it. A three-minute Short that could have been 40 seconds asks viewers for far more patience.

Shorts vs long videos: the technical differences

ShortsLong videos
Maximum lengthThree minutesMuch longer
OrientationVerticalUsually 16:9 horizontal
Upload resolutionUp to 1080p noted for uploaded ShortsUp to 4K and beyond
Title limit100 characters100 characters
ChaptersNot the usual formatTimestamps in the description, starting at 00:00
Frame rateMatch the recordingMatch the recording

Because Shorts are capped at 1080p, there is little point exporting a 4K vertical file for them; it adds upload time without adding quality on YouTube.

Editing checklist for Shorts

  1. Start on the point. No logo intro, no "hey guys".
  2. Keep key text and faces in the middle, away from on-screen buttons and captions.
  3. Use readable captions, checked for errors; see our page on whether to add captions to YouTube videos.
  4. Export vertical at up to 1080p, at your recording frame rate.
  5. Write a clear title within 100 characters, with two or three relevant hashtags.
  6. Check the music, preferably from the Audio Library.

Worked example: turning a tutorial into a Short

Example: Ben has a 12-minute video on sharpening kitchen knives and wants a Short from it.

DecisionFirst attemptRevised
Length2 minutes 50 seconds, three tips45 seconds, one tip: the angle trick
FormatHorizontal clip with black barsVertical 1080 x 1920 crop, hands centred
Opening"Hey everyone, today..."Close-up of the blade on the stone and the first instruction
Frame rateExported at 30fps from 25fps footageExported at 25fps to match the recording
Title"Knife sharpening tips #shorts #viral #fyp #cooking #kitchen...""The one angle that keeps knives sharp" + 2 relevant hashtags

The first attempt used most of the three-minute limit and crammed in three tips; the revision picks the single strongest moment and links back to the full video. Both are allowed by YouTube's rules; the second respects the viewer's time.
