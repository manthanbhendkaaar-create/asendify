Do YouTube cards make viewers leave mid-video, and when should you show them?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A YouTube card can send a viewer to another video before yours ends, so a badly timed card can cost you a little watch time on that video. Placed after a point has landed, and pointing to something the viewer genuinely wants next, a card usually keeps that person on your channel instead of losing them to the feed. The rule: never show a card in the hook or in the middle of a payoff.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help says "You can add up to 5 cards to one video" and that "they'll see a teaser for the time that you designate".
  • The same page says cards "are designed to complement videos and enhance the viewer experience with relevant info", and that cards aren't available on videos set as made for kids.
  • A card is a choice you hand the viewer. If it appears while they're still waiting for an answer, some will take it and leave.
  • YouTube's key moments report marks "dips" where viewers skipped or stopped watching. Check whether a dip lines up with a card teaser.
  • End screens, which appear in "the last 5–20 seconds of a video" according to YouTube Help, are the safer place for your main "watch next" push.

How a card can help or hurt retention

A card does two things at once. It tells the viewer "there's more over here", and it gives them a tap target to leave the video they're watching. Whether that is good or bad depends on timing and relevance.

Where to put cards: a simple checklist

  1. Keep the hook clean. No cards, no teasers, no pop-ups until the opening promise has been delivered.
  2. Place cards at section ends. If your video has chapters, the seconds after a chapter wraps are a natural break.
  3. Match the moment. Show a video card exactly when you say "I explain that in another video", not 40 seconds later.
  4. Use one or two, not five. Five is the limit, not a target. Each extra teaser is another interruption.
  5. Avoid stacking with sponsor reads or mid-roll ads. Too many interruptions in one stretch invite people to leave.
  6. Save the big push for the end screen. The end screen is designed for "watch next".

Why curiosity timing matters

Cards compete with the reason the viewer is still watching. Behavioural economist George Loewenstein's information-gap account of curiosity says awareness of specific missing information creates a strong desire to fill the gap. As Golman and Loewenstein summarise it, "awareness of specific pieces of missing information can prompt an unreasonably strong desire to fill these gaps."

In editing terms: while a question you raised is still open, the viewer is motivated to stay. Once you close it, attention is free, and that is the cheapest moment to offer them something else. A card during an open question competes with your own video. A card just after a closed question offers the next one.

How to check whether your cards cost you viewers

  1. Open YouTube Studio, choose the video and go to the retention graph.
  2. Note the timestamps of every card teaser you set.
  3. Look at the key moments report for dips that start within a few seconds of a teaser.
  4. Compare with a similar video that has no card at that point.
  5. If you see a sharp dip right at a teaser, move the card later, to the next natural break.

The Engagement tab help page also explains that "the end screen element click rate report shows you how often viewers clicked each end screen element", which lets you compare how many people take the end screen route versus leaving.

Example: moving one card

Example: a 14-minute tutorial on setting up a home studio has a video card at 0:45 pointing to "Best budget microphones", because the presenter says "I'll link my mic review". The retention graph shows a steeper-than-usual drop between 0:45 and 1:00.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on timing cards, prompts and pattern interrupts for retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops, pacing and pattern interrupts, with templates and a 30-day plan. YouTube's help pages on cards and end screens are the free starting point.

How many cards should I add to a YouTube video?

YouTube allows up to five. Most videos only need one or two, each placed where you naturally mention the linked video.

Do cards work on mobile?

Yes. YouTube Help says that on mobile viewers can see the card icon whenever the player controls are showing.

Are end screens better than cards for keeping viewers on my channel?

They do different jobs. End screens appear in the last 5-20 seconds, when the viewer is finishing anyway; cards work mid-video and are best kept for directly relevant links.

Can a card cause a dip in my retention graph?

It can, if it appears at a moment when viewers are deciding whether to stay. Compare dips in the key moments report with your teaser timestamps.
