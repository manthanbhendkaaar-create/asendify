Can a pinned comment improve YouTube retention, and what should it say?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Only indirectly. A pinned comment sits below the video, and on mobile viewers have to open the comments to see it, so it can't rescue a weak hook or slow middle. What it can do is set expectations, fix a mistake in the video, give timestamps that send skimmers back into the right section, and keep an open question alive. Treat it as support for a good edit, not a replacement.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help says a pinned comment appears at the top of the watch page on a computer, but on mobile viewers must expand the comment section to see it.
  • You can pin your own comment or a viewer's, and pinning a new one replaces the old one.
  • Retention is decided mostly inside the video: YouTube's content analytics tips note that "Audience numbers tend to decrease as videos progress, so try to catch their attention earlier."
  • A pinned comment is most useful for corrections, timestamps, answers to the most common question and a pointer to the next video.
  • Measure the video, not the comment: watch the key moments report before and after changing your pin.

Why it can only help indirectly

People who read comments are often already watching, so a pinned comment speaks to an engaged minority. It can make that minority feel the video was worth staying for, which matters for comments and return visits. It doesn't reach the viewer who swiped away at second 8.

That's why the order of work matters. YouTube's key moments report tells you what percentage of your audience "still watched your video after the first 30 seconds", and where the "dips" are: moments that "were either skipped or moments where viewers stopped watching". Fix those first. Then use the pinned comment to support what the edit already does well.

Five pinned comment templates

  1. Correction: "Correction: at 4:12 I said 2023, it's 2024. Thanks to everyone who spotted it." Stops the comment section filling up with the same point and reassures new viewers.
  2. Timestamps: "Jump to: 00:00 intro, 01:40 the setup, 06:20 the mistake most people make, 11:05 results." Note that real chapters need timestamps in the description; YouTube Help says "the first timestamp you list starts with 00:00", with "at least three timestamps", each chapter at least 10 seconds.
  3. The common question: "Most asked: does this work on a phone? Yes, see 7:30." Saves viewers from leaving to search.
  4. A light open question: "Stay to the end: the result at 12:40 surprised me." Only if it is true, and only if the payoff is worth it.
  5. Next step: "Part 2 is here: [link]." Works with the end screen.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on open loops and re-hooks that keep viewers watching?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops, re-hooks and pacing, with templates and a 30-day plan. YouTube's key moments help page is a free place to start finding where viewers leave.

Do pinned comments show on mobile?

Yes, but viewers have to expand the comment section to see them, according to YouTube Help. On a computer the pin appears at the top of the comments.

Should I pin my own comment or a viewer's?

Either. Pin whichever is most useful to a new viewer: a correction, timestamps, or a viewer's question with a clear answer.

Do timestamps in a pinned comment create chapters?

Chapters come from timestamps in the description, starting at 00:00, with at least three timestamps. Timestamps in a comment are clickable but are not the same as chapters.

How often should I change my pinned comment?

Whenever a better question or correction appears. Check comments in the first few days after upload, when most new viewers arrive.
