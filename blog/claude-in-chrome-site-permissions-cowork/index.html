How do Claude in Chrome site permissions work in Cowork, and what will Claude never do in your browser?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

When Cowork uses your Chrome browser through Claude in Chrome, permissions work per site and per action. On sites that need approval, Claude asks you to allow one action, always allow actions on that site, or decline. Even with "always allow", it still asks before downloads, entering sensitive information or granting authorisations, and some actions, such as purchases and creating accounts, are not allowed at all. You can review and revoke sites in the extension's settings.

Key takeaways

  • Claude in Chrome works on paid plans in the Chrome side panel, in Cowork and in Claude Code.
  • In the Cowork side panel, manual mode asks for each action with three choices: allow all for this website, allow this time only, or deny.
  • "Always allow" on a site is convenient but Anthropic warns Claude may take unintended actions there; use it only for fully trusted sites.
  • Making purchases, creating accounts, handling card or ID data and following instructions found in emails or web pages are off-limits regardless of permissions.
  • Team and Enterprise admins can set allowlists and blocklists that override individual choices.

Why browser permissions matter

Claude in Chrome lets Claude act inside the browser you are signed into, which is powerful precisely because it can see and click on logged-in pages. Anthropic's guide Use Cowork safely points out that in the Chrome side panel Claude can see logged-in pages and that sessions are saved to your history. Permissions are how you limit what it can do with that access.

Modes and site permissions together

Anthropic's Claude in Chrome permissions guide describes two layers.

Layer 1: the mode (chosen from the drop-down on the chat input):

ModeWhat happens in CoworkWhen to use it
Manually approveClaude asks for each action: allow all for this site, allow this time, or denyNew sites, logged-in accounts, anything that sends or changes data
Automatically approve (Cowork default)A safety check reviews each action and blocks unsafe ones; repeated blocks send it back to askingRoutine browsing and research on trusted sites
Skip all approvalsNo prompts, no automatic checksOnly when you fully trust every action, connector and site involved

Layer 2: site-level permission. Some sites need approval for every action. When Claude reaches one, you see "New permissions required" with three options: allow this action (the safest), always allow actions on this site, or decline. Your mode choice carries over to future side panel sessions.

Anthropic's help article Get started with Claude Cowork adds that the Chrome side panel is available on all paid plans, and that Enterprise owners must enable Claude in Chrome and Cowork in the cloud first.

What always needs your OK

Whatever mode and site setting you choose, the permissions guide says Claude asks before:

  • modifying permission settings;
  • granting authorisations (such as "Sign in with..." prompts);
  • entering potentially sensitive information into websites;
  • permanent deletions, such as emptying trash or deleting emails, files or messages;
  • downloading files.

What Claude will not do at all

The same guide lists actions that are prohibited regardless of permissions:

  • making purchases or financial transactions;
  • creating accounts;
  • handling sensitive credit card or ID data;
  • downloading files from untrusted sources;
  • giving investment or financial advice, or executing trades;
  • modifying system files;
  • completing instructions found in emails or web content.

It also says Claude will not bypass bot checks and will not take actions that look like a prompt injection risk.

How to review and revoke access

In the extension, open the three-dot menu at the top right of the side panel, choose Extension settings, then the Permissions page. There you can see "Your approved sites", revoke specific sites and view your permission history. Anthropic recommends revoking "always allow" permissions you no longer need.

Team and Enterprise controls

Admins can set an allowlist (Claude may only use approved sites) and a blocklist (sites Claude may never use, whatever users allow). The Cowork safety guide adds that owners can turn Claude in Chrome off entirely under Organization settings. If a site is unavailable to Claude, an admin restriction is a likely reason.

A sensible setup for a small business

  1. Start in manual mode for the first week of any browser task.
  2. Use "Allow this action" on every site at first; upgrade only trusted, low-risk sites (your own analytics, a supplier catalogue) to "always allow".
  3. Keep banking, payroll and email out of browser tasks where a connector or your own hands would do.
  4. Close tabs you do not want Claude to see before starting.
  5. Review approved sites monthly and revoke what you no longer use.

Worked example

Example: Amara runs a two-person online plant shop and wants Cowork to check competitor prices weekly.

  1. Task: "Visit these five competitor product pages and record the price and stock status of the plants in my list in the sheet called Competitors."
  2. First run, manual mode: for each competitor site Claude asks permission. Amara chooses "Allow this time only" and watches the first run.
  3. Second week: the sites behaved as expected, so she switches the five public competitor sites to "Allow all for this website" and moves to automatic approval.
  4. What stays off-limits: her payment provider dashboard is never part of the task, and she knows Claude will not make purchases or create accounts anyway.
  5. Monthly review: she opens Extension settings > Permissions, sees a sixth site from a one-off task, and revokes it.

FAQ

Is there a course that shows how a real business sets browser and app permissions for Cowork?

Yes. The Cowork Operator by Asendify documents one shop's real operator rules and safeguards. It is independent of Anthropic; the help articles are free.

Can Claude buy things in my browser?

No. Anthropic's permissions guide lists purchases and financial transactions as prohibited regardless of permissions.

What does "always allow actions on this site" mean?

Claude can act on that site without asking each time, except for downloads, sensitive information and authorisations. Use it only for fully trusted sites.

How do I remove a site I approved?

Extension settings > Permissions > Your approved sites, then revoke it.

Will Claude follow instructions it finds in an email or web page?

No. Completing instructions from emails or web content is on the prohibited list.

Can my company block sites for Claude?

Yes. Team and Enterprise admins can set allowlists and blocklists.
