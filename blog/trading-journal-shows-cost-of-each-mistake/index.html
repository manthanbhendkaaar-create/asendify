Is there a trading journal that shows how much money each mistake costs me?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 15 November 2026 · Last updated 15 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS is a Notion trading journal that lets you tag mistakes on each trade (such as moving a stop, chasing an entry or oversizing) and then totals the exact money each mistake type has cost you, alongside P&L, R-multiple and win rate. This page is for education only, not financial advice. Trading is risky, and no journal can promise a profit.

Key takeaways

  • A "mistake cost" report adds up the profit or loss of every trade tagged with the same mistake, so you can rank your costliest habits.
  • Most traders guess which mistakes hurt most; the numbers often point somewhere else.
  • Tagging only works if every trade gets an honest tag, including "no mistake".
  • The UK regulator says leveraged CFDs are high-risk, and firms must show the share of their retail accounts that lose money, which is why controlling mistakes matters.
  • Start with five or six mistake tags, not thirty, so the totals mean something.

How a mistake-cost report works

Each trade gets one or more tags. The journal then sums the P&L for each tag:

Notion makes this possible because its formulas can "run all kinds of calculations and functions based on other properties", and roll-ups and filters can total them. A template simply sets those formulas up for you.

Why mistakes matter more with leverage

The Financial Conduct Authority's page on contracts for difference describes CFDs as "high-risk products, which are not suitable for all retail consumers", and requires firms to tell customers the percentage of their retail client accounts that make losses. The FCA's policy statement PS19/18 sets retail leverage limits from 30:1 on major currency pairs down to 2:1 on cryptocurrencies, a 50% margin close-out rule and negative balance protection.

With leverage, a single oversized trade or a stop moved further away can wipe out weeks of careful trades. That is why the mistake that happens least often can still be the one that costs most.

Which mistakes to tag

Keep the list short and specific:

  1. Moved stop (further from entry after the trade opened).
  2. Oversized (risked more than your rule, for example over 1% of the account).
  3. Early entry (before your setup confirmed).
  4. Revenge trade (taken to win back a loss).
  5. Early exit (closed a winner before your target without a reason in your plan).
  6. No mistake (followed the plan, win or lose).

The "no mistake" tag matters. A losing trade that followed your plan isn't a mistake. Tagging it as one hides the difference between bad luck and bad behaviour.

Worked example

Example: Sana trades index CFDs part-time with a £10,000 account and a rule of risking 1% (£100) per trade. Over three months she logs 60 trades and tags each one honestly.

Her plan, followed, made money over this sample. Her mistakes turned that into a loss. She sets two rules for the next month: stops can only move towards profit, and position size is calculated before every entry. She keeps tagging so she can compare the next month's mistake costs with this one. Past results don't predict future ones, but the tags show clearly where her rules broke.

How to make the report honest

  • Tag every trade the same day. Memory softens mistakes.
  • One main tag per trade if possible, so the totals don't double-count.
  • Review weekly, not after every trade.
  • Look at cost and frequency together. A rare mistake can be the most expensive.
  • Don't delete bad trades. The report only works if it includes everything.

Common mistakes with journals themselves

  • Logging only wins, or skipping days after losses.
  • Too many tags, so each has two trades and nothing is clear.
  • Never reviewing. A journal you don't read is a diary.
  • Judging a rule on ten trades. Small samples swing wildly.

FAQ

Is there a trading journal that shows how much money each mistake costs?

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS, a Notion trading journal, totals the money lost or made on trades tagged with each mistake. A spreadsheet with a mistake column is a free alternative.

What are the most common trading mistakes to track?

Moving stops, oversizing, entering early, revenge trading and exiting winners early are common starting tags. Keep the list short.

Is a losing trade always a mistake?

No. A loss that followed your plan is part of trading. Tag it "no mistake".

How many trades do I need before the report is useful?

More is better. Thirty or more tagged trades gives a clearer picture than ten.

Does a journal make me profitable?

No. It shows where your results come from. What you change afterwards is up to you, and trading always carries risk.
