Can a supercar use E10 petrol, or does it need super unleaded?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Most modern supercars can physically use E10, because all new cars made since 2011 are compatible with it, but many high-performance engines are designed for higher-octane fuel, and in the UK the 97+ octane "super" grade is sold as E5. So the practical answer is to follow your handbook: if it specifies 97 or 98 RON, use super unleaded. Classic and older supercars may not be E10-compatible at all, and GOV.UK advises using E5 if in doubt.

This page is general information and planning estimates, not technical or financial advice. Your manufacturer's handbook is the authority for your car.

Key takeaways

  • E10 contains up to 10% renewable ethanol; it became the standard grade in Great Britain in 2021.
  • GOV.UK says all cars built since 2011 are compatible with E10, and most since the late 1990s are approved.
  • Classic, cherished and older vehicles, and some early-2000s models, may not be compatible.
  • E5 remains available as the "super" grade (97+ octane) at most filling stations.
  • Using the octane your engine is designed for matters more than the E number for most modern supercars.

What GOV.UK says

The government's guide, E10 petrol explained, says almost all petrol vehicles on the road can use E10 and all cars built since 2011 are compatible. Most cars and motorcycles made since the late 1990s are approved by their manufacturers. Vehicles that may not be compatible include classic, cherished and older vehicles and some specific models, particularly from the early 2000s. If your vehicle is not compatible, you can still buy E5 by choosing the "super" grade (97+ octane) at most filling stations. If in doubt, it says to keep using E5.

For classic cars, the guide notes that many older manufacturers are not listed in the government's vehicle checker, so owners should check the handbook, ask the manufacturer or an owners' club, or use E5.

You can check by make and model with GOV.UK's E10 vehicle checker.

Octane and E number are different things

TermWhat it means
E5 / E10Up to 5% or 10% ethanol in the petrol
RON (octane)Resistance to knocking; higher is needed by many high-compression or turbocharged engines
UK "regular" unleadedUsually 95 RON E10
UK "super" unleaded97+ RON, sold as E5

Many supercar handbooks specify 98 RON. Using a lower octane than specified can reduce performance and, in some cases, risk engine damage, depending on the car's knock control. That is a manufacturer question, not an ethanol question.

Classic supercars

For cars from the 1970s and 1980s, ethanol can affect some older rubber fuel hoses, seals and carburettor parts, and it absorbs moisture, which matters for cars that sit unused. That is why GOV.UK lists classic vehicles among those that may not be compatible. Owners' clubs often publish model-specific advice. If you store a classic over winter, see should you SORN a supercar over winter and do classic supercars need an MOT or road tax.

What to do

  1. Read the fuel section of your handbook or the label inside the fuel filler flap.
  2. Use GOV.UK's E10 checker for your make and model.
  3. If the handbook says 97 or 98 RON, use super unleaded.
  4. For classics, ask the manufacturer or owners' club, and use E5 if unsure.
  5. If you fill an incompatible car with E10 by mistake, GOV.UK's guide says a single tank is unlikely to cause damage; fill with E5 next time.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming "super" is only about ethanol. It is mainly about octane.
  • Using regular fuel to save money when the handbook specifies 98 RON.
  • Assuming all classics need E5 forever without checking; some later-1990s cars are approved.
  • Letting E10 sit for months in a stored classic.

Where to find your car's fuel requirement

PlaceWhat it shows
Owner's handbook, fuel sectionMinimum octane (RON) and approved fuels
Label inside the fuel filler flapOctane and sometimes E10 compatibility
GOV.UK E10 checkerCompatibility by make and model
Manufacturer or owners' clubAdvice for older or rare models

If these sources disagree, follow the manufacturer's handbook and use E5 super unleaded while you check.

Fuel and storage

If a car will sit for weeks, owners often fill the tank before storage to reduce space for condensation, and some use a stabiliser if the manufacturer approves it. For classics, using E5 before a long lay-up is a common precaution. Check your handbook or owners' club advice for your specific car.

FAQ

Is there a planner that includes fuel and other running costs in each supercar's yearly cost?

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner ranks cars by yearly cost with editable running costs. A spreadsheet is the free alternative.

Can my supercar use E10?

If it was built since 2011, it is compatible, but follow the handbook's octane requirement, which often means super unleaded.

Is super unleaded E5?

Yes. In the UK, the 97+ octane super grade is sold as E5.

Can classic cars use E10?

Some cannot. GOV.UK lists classic and older vehicles among those that may not be compatible; use E5 if unsure.

What happens if I put E10 in an incompatible car once?

GOV.UK's guidance says a single tank is unlikely to cause damage; go back to E5 for the next fill.

Does super unleaded improve performance?

Only if the engine is designed to use higher octane. In such cars, it lets the engine run as intended.
