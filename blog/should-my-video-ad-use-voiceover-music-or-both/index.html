Should my video ad use voiceover, music or both?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

For most video ads, use both: a voiceover (or a person speaking on camera) to carry the message, say the brand and make the call to action, and music underneath to set the pace and mood. Google reports that using sound, meaning music, voiceover or both, in YouTube Shorts ads has been shown to increase conversions by over 20%. Then make sure the ad still works muted with on-screen text, because many people watch without sound.

Key takeaways

  • Google: sound (music, voiceover or both) in Shorts ads "has been shown to increase conversions by over 20%".
  • Google's ABCDs: "Audio brand mentions enhance onscreen brand visuals' performance."
  • Reinforce the on-screen call to action with voiceover, and "Reinforce your message with audio and text. Avoid competing elements."
  • TikTok lists "using sound/ music" among the basics and points advertisers to its Commercial Music Library.
  • Only use music you're licensed to use in advertising; personal-use music isn't enough.

Mixing checklist

  1. Speech first. Set the voice level, then bring music up only until it supports, not competes.
  2. Duck the music under every spoken line.
  3. Say the brand once early, as Google suggests, and show it at the same time.
  4. Change the music where the story changes: problem to solution, solution to call to action.
  5. End on the voice, not on a music sting: the call to action should be the last thing heard.
  6. Check it muted. Add captions or text so the message survives without sound; see how to make video ads work with the sound off.

Music licensing basics

Music in an ad is commercial use. A track you can use in a personal video, or one you've bought to listen to, is not automatically cleared for advertising. Safer options:

  • Platform libraries meant for ads, such as TikTok's Commercial Music Library.
  • Production music libraries that sell licences covering paid ads.
  • Music you've commissioned with a written licence for advertising.

Check the licence terms for paid media, the platforms you'll use and how long you can run the ad.

Voiceover tips that keep people watching

  • Write for the ear. Short sentences, everyday words, one idea per line.
  • Open with the viewer's problem, not the company's name.
  • Use a real person where you can. Google advises featuring people to make the product story relatable.
  • Match the platform's tone. A polished announcer suits TV-style in-stream; a natural voice suits Shorts and TikTok.
  • Leave gaps. A beat of silence before the key line makes it land; see pattern interrupts in video ads.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches sound and pacing for video retention?

The Retention Editing Playbook covers pacing, pattern interrupts and openings for £1,000 one-time. Google's ABCDs and TikTok's creative guide are free.

Does music make video ads perform better?

Google reports that sound (music, voiceover or both) in Shorts ads has been shown to increase conversions by over 20%.

Should I say my brand name out loud?

Yes. Google says audio brand mentions enhance on-screen brand visuals.

Can I use any popular song in my ad?

No. Advertising needs music licensed for commercial use, such as a platform's commercial library.

Do I still need captions if I use voiceover?

Yes. Many people watch muted, so text keeps the message clear.
