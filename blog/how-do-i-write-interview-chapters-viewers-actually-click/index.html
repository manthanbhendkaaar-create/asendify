How do I write interview chapters viewers actually click?

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's help page says manual chapters must start at 00:00, list at least three timestamps in ascending order, and each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long (YouTube Help).
  • The same page says chapters give each section its own preview and make it easy for viewers to rewatch parts, and that manual chapters override automatic ones.
  • Wyzowl advises short titles, because hover titles are cut off after 49 characters, so the key idea should come first (Wyzowl). It also says to create a chapter only when it serves the viewer.
  • YouTube's retention report shows dips where "viewers are abandoning or skipping", so chapter points can follow those moments (YouTube Help).

What makes a viewer click a chapter

A chapter is a promise of a small answer. In an interview, viewers often arrive for one topic: a guest's story, a view on a news item, a tip. A good chapter title tells them where that topic is. A weak title, such as "Part 3" or "More discussion", gives them no reason to jump.

The best titles are specific and honest. They name the question or the claim, not the person's job or the vague theme. They also match what is said, because a viewer who clicks a chapter and does not find what the title promised leaves, and that hurts the next chapter too.

Step-by-step: write interview chapters

  1. Watch or read the transcript first. Chapters should follow what is actually said, not the plan you had.
  2. Find the topic changes. Mark each point where the question or the story moves on.
  3. Check the YouTube rules. Start at 00:00, have at least three timestamps and keep each chapter 10 seconds or more.
  4. Write each title as a question or a claim. "How did she fund the first year?" or "She paid herself nothing for 8 months".
  5. Put the key words first. Hover titles are cut off after 49 characters.
  6. Keep it honest. Do not promise a reveal the section does not contain.
  7. Skip empty chapters. Create a chapter only when it helps the viewer, as the Wyzowl guide says.
  8. Give the intro its own chapter. Many viewers want to skip it, and a clear label helps them.
  9. Order for the viewer. If the strongest topic is near the end, it is still fine to label it clearly. Do not reorder the interview just for the list.
  10. Add the list to the description. In YouTube Studio, put the timestamps in the description and save. Manual chapters override automatic ones.
  11. Check the retention graph. Look for dips and spikes, and see whether viewers are using the chapters you wrote.
  12. Revise after a week. Rename weak chapters and compare which are clicked.

Chapters and retention

Chapters are not only for clicking. They also show the viewer the shape of the video. A viewer who sees five clear promises is more likely to stay than one who sees a single block. YouTube's retention report can show whether it works: look for the dips where viewers skip ahead and check whether a better chapter title, placed just before, would have kept them.

Auto-chapters can help as a starting point, but YouTube says they are not generated for every video, and manual chapters override them. Write your own for interviews, because the model does not know what is surprising in the conversation.

When not to add chapters

A short interview of a few minutes may not need them. If you cannot find three honest topic changes of at least 10 seconds each, YouTube will not show the list anyway. Do not split a flowing conversation just to reach the minimum.

What are YouTube's rules for chapters?

Start at 00:00, list at least three timestamps in ascending order, and make each chapter at least 10 seconds long.

How long should a chapter title be?

Short. Hover titles are cut off after 49 characters, so lead with the key idea.

Should I use automatic chapters?
