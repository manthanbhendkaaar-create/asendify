Is there a tool that gives each YouTube video a retention score out of 100?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 18 November 2026 · Last updated 18 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Some tools turn a video's retention data into a single score so you can compare uploads at a glance. Asendify's Retention Doctor gives each video a Retention Score from 0 to 100, with where viewers left and a fix for each drop; it suits creators on one channel with a few hundred views per video, while YouTube Studio's free "typical retention" comparison gives a rougher version of the same signal.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube already compares your retention with "all YouTube videos of similar length" and with your own 10 latest videos of similar length.
  • Its Analytics API has a metric, relativeRetentionPerformance, scored from 0 to 1, where 0.5 is the median for similar-length videos.
  • A single score is useful for spotting which videos to study, not for explaining why they did well or badly.
  • Retention data takes one to two days to process, so judge a score after that, not on upload day.
  • You can build a rough free score yourself from YouTube Studio numbers in a spreadsheet.

What YouTube already measures

YouTube's help page on audience retention key moments explains the report every creator has in Studio. It shows how well each moment of a video held viewers, flags the intro (share still watching at 30 seconds), top moments, spikes and dips, and lets you compare with "all YouTube videos of similar length" or "your 10 latest videos of similar length". Two limits: the data can take one to two days to process, and key moments only appear on videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views.

Under the hood, the YouTube Analytics API metrics include three numbers that matter here:

MetricWhat it measuresScale
audienceWatchRatioShare of viewers watching at each point of the videoRatio, can exceed 1 where people rewatch
relativeRetentionPerformanceRetention at each point compared with similar-length YouTube videos0 to 1; 0.5 = median
averageViewPercentageAverage share of the video watched per playbackPercentage

Google defines relativeRetentionPerformance this way: a value of 0.5 means "half of the videos of similar length retain viewers better". That is the closest thing YouTube publishes to an official retention score.

Why a score out of 100 helps (and where it stops)

A curve per video is hard to compare across 20 uploads. One number lets you sort them. Common uses:

  • Pick what to study. Rewatch your three highest and three lowest scorers side by side.
  • Spot drift. If scores fall over a month, something in your format changed.
  • Test one change. Change the intro style, then compare scores of the next three uploads with the three before.

What a score cannot do:

  • Explain causes. A 42 tells you the video lost people; only watching the dips tells you why.
  • Replace click-through rate. A high score on a video nobody clicks still means few views. YouTube's guide to impressions and watch time covers how the two fit together.
  • Work on tiny samples. Under about 100 views, the curve is too noisy to score fairly.

How read-only access works

Retention tools connect through Google sign-in. The YouTube Analytics authorisation guide lists the read-only scopes: yt-analytics.readonly ("View YouTube Analytics reports for your YouTube content") and youtube.readonly ("View your YouTube account"). A tool that asks only for these can read your numbers but cannot upload, edit or delete videos. Check the permissions screen before you approve, and remove access from your Google account settings whenever you like.

  • Video B: (48 x 0.4) + (29 x 0.4) + (30 x 0.2) = 19.2 + 11.6 + 6 = 36.8, so about 37.

Video B is the one to study first. Open it at the biggest dip, watch the 15 seconds before, and write one rule for the next edit. Keep the same formula for every video, or the scores will not be comparable.

How to use any retention score well

  1. Wait 48 hours. YouTube says retention data can take one to two days to process.
  2. Compare like with like. A 60-second Short and a 20-minute tutorial will never score the same. Compare within formats.
  3. Look at the curve behind the number. A score is a signpost to the dips, not the answer.
  4. Change one thing at a time. Otherwise you cannot tell which change moved the score.
  5. Review monthly. A trend over 8 to 12 videos means more than any single result.

Common mistakes when reading a retention score

  • Chasing the score instead of the viewer. Cutting every pause can raise a number while making a video harder to follow. Check that the edit still makes sense.
  • Ignoring spikes. YouTube notes that spikes are moments that were rewatched or shared. They can mean a strong section, or a confusing one people had to replay. Watch them before deciding.
  • Judging on day one. Early viewers are often subscribers who behave differently from viewers who arrive later from search and suggestions.
  • Mixing formats in one league table. Keep Shorts, tutorials and long vlogs in separate lists, or the comparison is meaningless.

FAQ

Is there a tool that gives each YouTube video a retention score out of 100?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor gives each video a Retention Score from 0 to 100 with drop-off timestamps and fixes. YouTube Studio's typical-retention comparison is the free alternative.

Does YouTube have its own retention score?

Not as a single number in Studio, but the Analytics API's relativeRetentionPerformance scores retention against similar-length videos on a 0 to 1 scale, with 0.5 as the median.

What is a good retention score?

It depends on the formula and the format. Use any score to compare your own videos of similar length, not to compare with other channels.

Why is my video's retention data missing?

YouTube says data can take one to two days to process, and key moments need a video of at least 60 seconds with at least 100 views.

Can a retention tool change my videos?

Not if it only requests the read-only scopes yt-analytics.readonly and youtube.readonly. Check the Google permissions screen.
