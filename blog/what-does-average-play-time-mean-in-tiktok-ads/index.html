What does "average play time per video view" mean in TikTok Ads Manager, and why does it include rewatches?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

"Average play time per video view" in TikTok Ads Manager is the average number of seconds your ad was played each time someone viewed it, and TikTok includes time spent replaying the video. "Average play time per user" is the same idea per person. Because rewatches are included, a short looping ad can show an average play time close to, or even above, its own length, while view counts such as 2-second views and 25% to 100% views leave replays out. Read play time together with those checkpoints: the checkpoints show where people leave on the first watch, and play time shows whether anyone came back for more.

Key takeaways

  • TikTok defines average play time per video view as the "average time your video was played per single video view, including any time spent replaying the video".
  • Average play time per user is the same, measured per person.
  • Video views, 2-second views and the 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% views exclude replays.
  • So play time can rise because people rewatch, even if fewer people reach the end on their first watch.
  • Compare play time with the ad's length and with your own earlier ads, never on its own.

The play-time metrics, side by side

From TikTok's help page on video play metrics:

MetricWhat it measuresIncludes replays?
Video viewsTimes your video started to playNo
2-second video viewsPlays of at least 2 seconds in an impression sessionNo
6-second video viewsPlays of at least 6 seconds, full plays of shorter videos, or an engagement in the first 6 secondsNot stated
Video views at 25% / 50% / 75% / 100%Plays that reached that share of the video's lengthNo
Average play time per video viewAverage seconds played per viewYes
Average play time per userAverage seconds played per personYes

TikTok also notes that if someone swipes away and comes back, the return is a new impression and a new view.

Why play time and completion can disagree

Imagine two 12-second ads:

  • Ad A loses half its viewers by second 4, but the people who stay often watch it twice, because the ending loops back to the start.
  • Ad B holds people steadily to the end, but almost nobody replays it.

Ad A may show a similar or higher average play time than Ad B, while Ad B has far more 75% and 100% views. Neither number is wrong. They answer different questions:

QuestionLook at
Does my opening hold people?2-second and 6-second views divided by impressions
Where do first-time viewers leave?Drop between 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% views
Is anything worth watching twice?Average play time vs ad length
Is the whole audience watching longer than before?Average play time per user, version vs version

How to use it in an edit review

  1. Write the ad length next to the play time. A 9-second average on a 10-second ad tells a different story from a 9-second average on a 45-second ad.
  2. Check the checkpoints first. If 25% views are low, fix the hook before thinking about rewatches.
  3. Look for rewatch value. If play time is high relative to completion, something in the ad rewards a second look: a fast reveal, dense text, a satisfying loop. Find it and use it again.
  4. Watch out for unreadable text. Some rewatching happens because people couldn't read the text in time. That inflates play time for a bad reason. Slow the text down.
  5. Compare versions with the same length. Play time across ads of different lengths is hard to compare. Test edits of the same cut length.

How editing raises play time honestly

  • A stronger first second. More people past the opening means more seconds played overall.
  • No dead air. Trim pauses, slow intros and repeated points.
  • A reveal near the end. Hold back the result, price or finished product until late, and say early that it is coming.
  • A clean loop. Match the last frame to the first so the replay feels natural on TikTok.
  • Readable text. Fewer words per card, on screen long enough to read.

Worked example

Example: a skincare brand tests two 15-second TikTok ads with the same offer.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches loops and reveals for short video?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) covers open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. TikTok's creative guides are free alternatives.

Can average play time be longer than my video?

It can happen in principle, because TikTok includes replay time. If it does, people are rewatching a lot.

Do replays count as extra views?

No. TikTok excludes replays from video views and from the 2-second and percentage view counts.

Which matters more, play time or completion?

For most ads, getting people to the offer on the first watch matters more. Play time is a useful second signal.

Is there a "good" average play time?

TikTok does not publish one. Compare against the ad's length and your own past ads.
