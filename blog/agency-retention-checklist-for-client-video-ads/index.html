What retention checklist should an agency give its editors for client video ads?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

An agency should give every editor one short, yes-or-no checklist that is run on the rough cut of each client ad before it goes to review: the hook lands in the first 3 seconds, the offer is clear early, the ad works with the sound off, every shot earns its place, the call to action is said and shown, and the cut fits each platform's specs. One shared list makes quality the same whichever editor cuts the job.

Key takeaways

  • Run the checklist on the rough cut, before colour, graphics and the client sees anything.
  • Front-load the hook: TikTok advises putting the hook in the first 6 seconds and the main proposition in the first 3.
  • Assume sound off. Facebook's own guidance says ads should work without audio because feeds autoplay muted.
  • Keep one idea per ad and one call to action, said and shown on screen.
  • A checklist only works if a second person signs it off. Editors marking their own homework miss things.

Why agencies need a shared checklist

Client ads fail in predictable ways: a logo sting eats the first two seconds, the offer arrives at second 20, captions sit under the platform's buttons, or the call to action is only spoken. None of these are skill problems. They are process problems, and they get worse as more editors and more clients come on board.

Poor inputs make it harder. In a UK study by BetterBriefs with the IPA, 73% of agencies said the briefs they receive aren't good enough. A checklist cannot fix a weak brief, but it stops a weak brief turning into a weak edit, because the editor checks the cut against the same standards every time.

The platforms also tell you what they reward. Google's ABCDs of effective video ads put Attention first ("hook and sustain attention with an immersive story"), then Branding, Connection and Direction. Google says applying them can deliver "as much as a 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood", citing a 2021 Google/Kantar study. That is Google's figure for its own framework, not a promise for one ad, but it shows the order to check things in.

The client-ad retention checklist

Work through it in order. Any "no" goes back to the editor before the client review link is sent.

Opening (0 to 3 seconds)

  1. Cold open. Does the first frame show the product, a face mid-sentence, a result or a problem, not a logo animation or a greeting?
  2. Proposition by second 3. Can a viewer say what is on offer by the third second? TikTok's creative best practices recommend introducing the content proposition in the first 3 seconds and prioritising the hook in the first 6.
  3. Brand inside the hook. Is the brand visible early, inside an interesting frame rather than as a separate intro?
  4. Movement. Is something already happening in frame one?

Message (3 seconds to the call to action)

  1. One idea. Does the ad make one promise, not three?
  2. Proof. Is there a reason to believe it on screen: a demo, a before-and-after or a real customer with permission?
  3. No dead air. Pauses, false starts and repeated lines are cut.
  4. Each shot has a job. Every insert shows, proves or explains something.
  5. A reason to keep watching. Is there an open question the ad answers before it ends?

Sound off and text

  1. Works muted. Watch it once with the sound off. Is the message still clear?
  2. Readable text. TikTok recommends showing 5 to 10 words per second when you use text. Is every line on screen long enough to read on a phone?
  3. Captions accurate. Have auto-captions been checked word by word? The W3C notes that automatically generated captions don't meet accessibility needs unless they are fully accurate.

Close and delivery

  1. Call to action said and shown. Google's guidance is to reinforce the on-screen call to action with voice-over.
  2. Safe zones. Are text and logos clear of the app's buttons and captions on each placement?
  3. Right versions. Vertical, square and horizontal cuts exist where the media plan needs them.
  4. Compliance. Testimonials are genuine and approved, and any creator content is labelled as an ad where needed.

Worked example

Example: an agency is cutting a 20-second Instagram ad for a dental clinic's teeth-whitening offer. The rough cut opens with a 2-second animated logo, then the dentist says "Hi, we're Smile Studio" and the price appears at second 9.

Run through the list. Line 1 fails (logo first), line 2 fails (no proposition by second 3), and line 10 fails (muted, the first 4 seconds show only a logo and a talking head with no text). The editor rebuilds the opening: frame one is a close-up before-and-after with the text "Whiter teeth in one visit?", the dentist's first line becomes the answer, and the logo moves to a small corner mark. The cut is still 20 seconds. Nothing new was filmed; the checklist only changed the order.

Whether the new version performs better is for the test to decide. The checklist's job is to make sure no version goes out with an avoidable fault.

How to roll it out across a team

  • Put it where the work happens. A pinned note in the project template or the review tool, not a document nobody opens.
  • Use line numbers in feedback. "Line 11 fails at 0:06" is faster to act on than "the text feels rushed".
  • Second pair of eyes. A producer or another editor ticks the list, not the person who cut the ad.
  • Review after launch. When results come in, look at where viewers dropped and add a line if the same fault keeps appearing.

FAQ

Is there a retention checklist an agency can give editors for client ads?

Yes. Use the 16-line list above for free, or a full system such as Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook if you want shared rules and templates for the whole team.

When should the checklist be run?

On the rough cut, before colour, graphics and the client review. Fixing the order of shots is cheap early and expensive after sign-off.

Who should tick the checklist?

Someone other than the editor who cut the ad, such as a producer or another editor. A second person catches faults the editor no longer sees.

Does the checklist change by platform?

The core lines stay the same. Specs, safe zones and ideal length change, so keep a short platform section at the end of the list.

How long should the checklist take?

Two watches of the ad, one with sound and one muted, plus a few minutes to tick the lines. On a 15 to 30-second ad that is usually under 10 minutes.
