Key takeaways

  • Start with substance. Meta's video best practices say "The first few seconds of every video are critical" and suggest a 15-second trailer of the best moments at the start of longer videos.
  • Viewers dislike preamble. Nielsen Norman Group found frequent complaints that videos "include lengthy introductions and don't get to the point quickly enough."
  • Talking heads can hold attention. The edX study of 6.9 million viewing sessions found "informal talking-head videos are more engaging."
  • Find the exact moments people leave. YouTube's key moments help page says "Dips mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video."
  • Chapters help long interviews: NN/g notes time markers "help users jump to the appropriate moment."

A retention edit for a long interview

  1. Cold open (0 to 30 s): the guest's most surprising or useful line, possibly a quick montage of two or three.
  2. Context (30 to 45 s): who they are and why this conversation matters, in one or two sentences.
  3. Reorder if needed. You don't have to keep the recorded order; lead with the strongest topic.
  4. Trim answers, not meaning. Keep the guest's point intact; never cut so an answer means something else.
  5. Open loops. "Later, she explains why she turned down the acquisition" near the start.
  6. Chapters on YouTube for each topic.
  7. Visual variety: switch camera on emphasis, punch in on key lines, add text for names, numbers and sources.
  8. End cleanly with a final strong answer and one call to action.

Ethics of interview editing

Tightening answers is normal. Changing what someone meant is not. Keep context for strong opinions, don't splice sentences from different answers into one claim, and if a guest asks to see the edit before publishing, agree that in advance. Viewers trust interviews because they hear the guest in their own words.

Common mistakes

  • Keeping the first five minutes because "that's how it started".
  • Trimming so hard that the guest sounds rushed or loses nuance.
  • No chapters on long interviews.
  • A thumbnail and title promising a topic that appears an hour in.
  • Ignoring the audio: poor sound loses viewers faster than plain visuals.

FAQ

Is there a guide on editing interview videos for retention?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks, open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's analytics help pages are free.

Should an interview start with the guest's introduction?

Usually not. Open on a strong answer, then introduce the guest briefly.

Can I change the order of questions?

Yes, as long as answers keep their meaning and context.

How much should I cut from a long interview?

Cut warm-ups, repetition and tangents; keep anything that adds substance. There's no fixed percentage.

Do interviews need B-roll?

Not always. Camera switches, punch-ins and on-screen text can be enough; B-roll helps when the guest describes something visual.
