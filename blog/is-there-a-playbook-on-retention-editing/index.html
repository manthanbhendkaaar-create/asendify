Key takeaways

  • Retention editing means cutting a video so more viewers stay to the end. YouTube's retention report shows "how well different moments of your video held viewers' attention".
  • A playbook is a method plus tools: rules, templates and a plan for practising them.
  • The core ideas are backed by platform guidance. Google tells advertisers to "hook and sustain attention with an immersive story".
  • Curiosity is a real driver: a 2009 Psychological Science study linked higher curiosity to better recall of surprising answers.
  • No playbook guarantees views. Your topic, title and thumbnail still matter.

What "retention editing" covers

Retention editing is the part of editing focused on keeping viewers watching. It includes:

  1. Hooks. The first seconds, where viewers decide to stay.
  2. Open loops. Questions raised early and answered later.
  3. Pacing. How often something new happens.
  4. Pattern interrupts. Changes in shot, sound or format that reset attention.
  5. Re-hooks. Fresh reasons to stay, placed before known drop-off points.
  6. Endings. Paying off the promise and moving viewers to the next video.

YouTube's analytics show the result. The intro metric reports "what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds", and "dips mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping".

Who it doesn't suit

If you want someone else to edit for you, hire an editor instead. If you post rarely, the 30-day plan will be hard to follow. And if you need a cheap introduction, start with YouTube's free help pages and a general storytelling book.

Questions to ask before buying

Before buying any retention resource, including this one, check three things. First, look at your last five retention graphs: if viewers leave mostly in the first 30 seconds, you may only need help with hooks, and free resources might cover it. Second, be honest about time: a playbook with a 30-day plan only helps if you can apply something to most videos in that month. Third, decide how you'll measure change, such as intro retention compared with typical retention. If you can answer all three, you'll get far more from any guide you choose.
