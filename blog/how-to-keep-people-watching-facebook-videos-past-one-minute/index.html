How do I get people to watch my Facebook videos for longer than a minute?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Facebook has said it gives more weight to videos that hold people for at least one minute, and more still to longer videos that keep people engaged. To get there, open with a short preview of the best part, burn in captions for sound-off viewers, frame it 4:5 or vertical for the feed, cut long footage to a tight "Facebook cut" of around 3 minutes, and only post original material.

Key takeaways

  • Meta's 2019 video ranking update says it is "important to ensure that videos capture viewers' attention for at least one minute".
  • It gives extra weight to videos that keep people engaged, "especially on videos that are at least three minutes long", and to videos people come back for week after week.
  • Since 2017 Facebook has weighted percent completion more heavily the longer a video is, so long videos are not penalised for lower completion.
  • Meta's creator tips include a 15-second preview at the start, burned-in subtitles, a 4:5 frame and a 3-minute cut of longer videos.
  • Unoriginal or reposted video gets reduced reach, and adding a watermark or stitching clips does not count as original.

What Facebook says it rewards

Meta's post Updates to video ranking (May 2019, updated November 2019) names three factors: loyalty and intent (videos people seek out and return to), video and viewing duration, and originality. On duration it says creators should make sure videos hold attention for at least a minute, and that Facebook will weight videos that keep people engaged, especially those of at least three minutes.

An earlier post, Updating how we account for video completion rates, explains why long videos are not unfairly punished: Facebook decided to "weight percent completion more heavily the longer a video is". Finishing 80% of a 10-minute video says more than finishing a 15-second clip.

In July 2025 Meta's creator team said it would cut the reach of unoriginal content: accounts that repeatedly reuse other people's videos can lose monetisation and reach, and "simply stitching together clips or adding your watermark does not qualify as meaningful enhancement".

The edit that gets past one minute

Meta's video best practices for creators give a practical template:

  1. A 15-second preview first. Meta says the first few seconds are critical and that 15-second trailers have driven better engagement than five-second ones. Show the most interesting moments, then start.
  2. Burned-in subtitles. Captions catch people watching with the sound off; subtitle the preview too.
  3. Frame for the feed. Meta calls 4:5 the recommended aspect ratio for video on Facebook.
  4. Make a "Facebook cut". For long footage, cut a version of about 3 minutes.
  5. Keep a promise open. Tell people early what they will get by the end, and pay it off late.
  6. Write one sentence. Meta suggests a one-sentence description over three or more.

Build return viewers, not just one hit

Loyalty is one of Meta's three factors. Post a recognisable series ("Saturday restorations"), at a regular time, and tell viewers when the next part lands. Meta also suggests resharing a video within two hours of publishing to your timeline and Stories, and testing thumbnails and headlines with Post Testing, which can compare up to four versions on a metric such as one-minute views.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches previews and open loops for longer videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) covers openings, open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Meta for Creators' guides are a free alternative.

Does Facebook still favour 3-minute videos?

Meta's 2019 update said it gives more weight to engaging videos of at least three minutes. Meta has not published a newer replacement, but its later creator tips still recommend a 3-minute cut of long videos.

Should Facebook videos be vertical or square?

Meta's creator best practices call 4:5 the recommended ratio for feed video. Reels are full-screen vertical.

Will reposting my own TikToks hurt my reach?

Posting your own work is fine, but Meta advises against visible third-party watermarks and repeatedly recycled content.

What should I measure?

One-minute views and average watch time, plus whether the same people come back to your next video.
