How do I make a video ad that works with the sound off?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

To make a video ad work with the sound off, tell the whole story in pictures and on-screen text: start on a clear visual, put the key line on screen in large, high-contrast words, burn in captions for anything spoken, show the product and brand on screen rather than only saying them, and end with a written call to action. Then add sound that makes the ad better for people who do turn it on. Design for both, because some placements autoplay muted and others are sound-on.

  • Facebook has said its feed autoplays video ads muted, and its 2021 advice was that ads should work without audio.
  • In 2016 Facebook reported that 41% of videos were "effectively meaningless" without sound, and that captioned video ads raised average view time by 12% in its internal tests.
  • RNID says over 18 million people in the UK are deaf, have hearing loss or have tinnitus, so captions also widen your audience.
  • TikTok recommends 5 to 10 words per second when you use on-screen text.
  • Sound-on still matters: Google's Shorts ads guide says to use music, voiceover or both, and Meta calls audio a key driver of engagement in Reels.

Why sound-off design still matters

Search Engine Journal's write-up of Facebook's video ad tips (2021) lists "sound off" as a must-do, because Facebook autoplays ads muted. Earlier, Social Media Today reported Facebook's 2016 finding that 41% of videos made no sense without sound, and its internal test showing captions lifted average view time by 12%. These figures are old and come from Facebook itself, so treat them as direction, not a forecast for your ad. The principle hasn't changed: if the first seconds rely on a spoken line, a muted viewer sees nothing worth stopping for.

Captions also matter for access. RNID puts the number of people in the UK who are deaf, have hearing loss or tinnitus at over 18 million, about one in three.

Text speed. TikTok's creative best practices suggest "displaying 5-10 words per second when using text". Slower is safer for longer lines; nobody can read a full sentence in half a second.

Safe zones. Platform buttons and captions cover parts of vertical video. Meta's Reels ads documentation says to "keep the bottom 35% of your 9:16 creative free of text, logos, and other key elements". TikTok asks you to keep key content within the UI safe zone.

Contrast. Google's ABCDs advise bright, high-contrast visuals. Use a solid or semi-transparent box behind text that sits over busy footage.

Then design for sound-on

Muted-first doesn't mean silent. Google's guide to YouTube Shorts ads says to use sound (music, voiceover or both) and claims sound has been shown to lift conversions by over 20%, though it doesn't give a source. Meta's Reels documentation calls audio "a key driver of engagement". Google's ABCDs also recommend reinforcing your on-screen brand and call to action with audio. So make the ad complete without sound, then let sound add energy and emphasis for people who hear it.

Worked example: fixing a talking-head ad

An illustrative 20-second ad shows a physiotherapist talking to camera about back pain. Muted, it's a person moving their lips for four seconds.

Fixes:

  1. Open on the movement itself, someone bending to pick up a child, with the text "Back hurts when you lift?"
  2. Burn in captions for every line, broken into short chunks of 3 to 6 words.
  3. Put the clinic name on screen at second two, inside the frame, not as a separate logo card.
  4. Show the stretch being done, with a text label for each step.
  5. End on text: "Book a 30-minute assessment" plus the button.

Muted viewers now get the problem, the proof and the offer. Sound-on viewers get the same, plus the physio's voice.

Testing sound-off performance

You can check how well an ad works muted before and after launch.

Before launch. Watch the ad on your phone with the sound off, ideally after a day away from it. Ask: could a stranger tell what the product is, what it does and what to do next? Then show it muted to someone who hasn't seen it and ask them to explain it back.

After launch. Compare early-attention metrics (hook rate, 2-second or 3-second views) between a version with strong on-screen text and one that relies more on voiceover. If the text-led version stops more people, sound-off design is helping.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches editing for sound-off viewing?

The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, text and pacing at £1,000 one-time. Google's ABCDs and TikTok's creative guide are free alternatives.

Should I burn captions in or upload a caption file?

For feed ads, burned-in captions show for everyone. Caption files help accessibility and search, so many advertisers use both.

Do TikTok ads need sound?

TikTok recommends using sound or music, but add text too so the message survives for anyone scrolling muted.

How much text is too much?

If viewers can't read it in the time it's on screen, it's too much. TikTok suggests 5 to 10 words per second at most.
