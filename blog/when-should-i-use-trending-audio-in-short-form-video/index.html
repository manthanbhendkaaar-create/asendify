When should I use trending audio in short-form video?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use trending audio when you post from a personal or creator account, the sound genuinely fits the idea, and you can add your own twist in the first second. Don't use it on a business account or in an ad unless the track is cleared for commercial use: on TikTok that means the Commercial Music Library. And don't let the sound do the work; viewers stay for the idea, not the song.

Key takeaways

  • Trending sounds are a format, not a strategy. They help when the audio sets up a joke, a reveal or a rhythm your edit can follow.
  • TikTok says "Businesses cannot use the general music library for commercial usage" and points them to the Commercial Music Library, a pre-cleared library of about one million tracks.
  • YouTube warns that music that is safe in a Short under 60 seconds may not be safe in a longer video, so don't reuse a Shorts sound in a long upload or a compilation.
  • UK law is simple at the core: you cannot use copyright material without permission, and a licence covers only the uses it names.
  • Cross-posting is where most problems start: a sound licensed inside one app is not automatically licensed for another app, your website or an ad.

How to use a trending sound well

  1. Start with your idea, then find the sound. If you search the trending list first, you end up making the same video as everyone else.
  2. Put your twist in frame one. The viewer has heard the audio many times. The first image or caption must tell them this version is different.
  3. Cut to the beat. Trending sounds often have a clear drop or punchline. Land your reveal on it.
  4. Keep voice clear. If you talk over the sound, lower it well under your voice and caption what you say.
  5. Use it early in a trend's life. A sound used by everyone for weeks feels stale; a fresh one feels current. There's no reliable public number for when a trend peaks, so watch your own results.
  6. Check your analytics, not the trend. If the sound version holds viewers no better than your voice-only version, drop the habit.

Example: a café owner's Reel and TikTok

Example: a café owner wants to post a 12-second clip of latte art set to a sound that is trending this week. Her TikTok is set up as a business account, so she picks a similar upbeat track from the Commercial Music Library instead, and cuts the pour to land on the drop at second 6. On her personal creator account, where she shares behind-the-scenes clips, she uses the trending sound itself and opens with a caption: "Rating my worst latte art". When she later wants the clip on her website, she swaps the audio for a track she has licensed for web use, because neither app's library covers her site. Details are illustrative.

When trending audio is a bad idea

  • Serious or sensitive topics. A comedy sound on a health or money message reads as flippant.
  • Talking-head explainers. If your value is what you say, music fights your voice.
  • Ads. You need commercial rights, and a paid view gives you less time to win attention; a sound viewers have heard 50 times rarely helps.
  • Long-form. Shorts music rules don't carry over, and a claimed song in a long video can lead to muted audio or blocked countries.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks that work without a trending sound?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and open loops for YouTube and Shorts. Testing your own hooks with trial Reels is a free alternative.

Can my business account use trending sounds on TikTok?

Not for commercial use. TikTok tells businesses to use the Commercial Music Library for organic posts, ads and branded content.

Does trending audio boost reach?

There's no published guarantee. It can help a video fit a format people are watching, but the hook and idea decide whether viewers stay.

Can I reuse a Shorts sound in a long YouTube video?

Not safely. YouTube says music safe in a Short under 60 seconds may not be safe in a longer video.

Can I download a TikTok with its sound and post it on my website?

The in-app licence doesn't cover your site. Replace the audio with music you have licensed for that use.
