Key takeaways

  • Video supports photos; it doesn't replace them. In the National Association of Realtors' 2025 Home Buyers and Sellers Generational Trends report, 83% of US buyers who searched online rated photos "very useful", 41% virtual tours and 29% videos.
  • That means a video has to earn its time. Lead with what photos can't show: flow, light, space and the view.
  • Get to the point. Nielsen Norman Group found viewers complain when videos "include lengthy introductions and don't get to the point quickly enough."
  • Social versions need a hook in seconds: TikTok advises introducing your proposition "in the first 3 seconds".
  • Measure drop-off and fix the room or moment where viewers leave.

Retention edits for a listing tour

  1. Open on the hero shot. The kitchen-diner, the garden or the view, for two or three seconds, then go to the entrance. This is an open loop: viewers stay to see how they get there.
  2. Walk in a logical route. Jumping between floors confuses viewers and makes them leave.
  3. One shot per room unless a room is a selling point. Cut slow pans in half.
  4. Show scale and light: a person walking through, a window with daylight, a doorway framing the next room.
  5. On-screen text for facts: bedrooms, garden orientation, parking. Many people watch muted.
  6. End with the action: book a viewing, call, or see the floor plan.
  7. Be accurate. Don't use lenses or edits that make rooms look larger than they are, and keep claims within what the listing details support.

Common mistakes

  • Long drone intros before the viewer has seen inside.
  • Every room given equal time, including cupboards.
  • Music with no captions on portals that autoplay muted.
  • Wide-angle distortion that makes rooms look bigger than they are.
  • Ending on the agency logo with no next step.

What a paid guide adds over free tips

Free resources, including portal and platform guidance, tell you to keep videos short and show the best features. A paid playbook is only worth it if you make many videos, for your own agency or clients, and want a repeatable method for hooks, structure and pacing across tours, market updates and social clips. It will not sell a property on its own; price, location and condition matter far more than editing.

FAQ

Is there a guide on retention editing for property videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks, open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Free video guidelines from Nielsen Norman Group cover the basics.

How long should a listing video be?

Long enough to show the layout and best features, usually a few minutes at most for a tour and much shorter for social teasers.

Should a property video start with a drone shot?

Only if the setting is the main selling point. Otherwise open on the best interior shot.

Do buyers watch listing videos?

Some do. In NAR's 2025 US report, 29% of online searchers rated videos very useful, compared with 83% for photos.

Should the price be in the video?

Follow your portal's and local rules; many agents put the price and key facts on screen near the start or end.
