Do countdown hooks ("top 10" lists) keep viewers watching longer?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

They can. A countdown ("10 mistakes, number 1 is the worst") tells viewers how far they've come and promises a payoff at the end, which gives them a reason to stay. It backfires when the early items drag, so progress feels slow, or when number one doesn't live up to the build-up. Keep early entries short, make the final entry genuinely the best, and re-hook along the way.

Key takeaways

  • A countdown is an open question ("what's number one?") plus a progress bar ("we're at 6 of 10").
  • George Loewenstein's information-gap account says awareness of specific missing information creates a strong pull to fill it; Golman and Loewenstein describe "an unreasonably strong desire to fill these gaps".
  • A meta-analysis of web-survey experiments by Villar, Callegaro and Yang (2013) found "fast-to-slow indicators reduced drop-offs" while "slow-to-fast indicators increased drop-offs". Surveys aren't videos, but the lesson transfers: slow early progress loses people.
  • Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng (2006) found people bought coffee "more frequently the closer they are to earning a free coffee": effort rises near a goal.
  • Check the result in YouTube's key moments report: dips show where viewers "stopped watching" or skipped.

Why countdowns can work

Three things work in a countdown's favour:

  1. A clear open question. "Number one" is a specific missing piece of information. That is exactly the kind of gap the information-gap account describes.
  2. Visible progress. Viewers always know how much is left. Research on progress indicators shows that how fast progress feels matters, not just whether it is shown.
  3. A goal that gets closer. The goal-gradient research found that people push harder as a reward gets closer. In a video, the last few entries are where viewers are least likely to leave, if they've got that far.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on hooks and re-hooks for countdown videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops, re-hooks and pacing, with templates and a 30-day plan. YouTube's key moments report is the free way to see where your list loses people.

Should number one always come last?

Usually, because it's the payoff viewers are waiting for. But entry two and the first entry shown must also be strong, or viewers won't stay long enough to reach it.

How many items should a countdown have?

Enough that every entry earns its place. Five strong items hold better than ten with filler.

Do chapters hurt countdown videos?

They let some viewers jump straight to number one. Naming chapters by number rather than by answer keeps some curiosity.

Do countdowns work in Shorts?

They can, with 3 to 5 very short entries. In a fast feed, progress has to feel quick from the first second.
