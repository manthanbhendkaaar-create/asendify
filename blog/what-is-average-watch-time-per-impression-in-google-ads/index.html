What does "average watch time per impression" mean for YouTube ads in Google Ads?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

"Avg. watch time/impr." in Google Ads is the average number of seconds people watched your YouTube video ad each time it was shown. Google times each impression from the moment the ad starts until it finishes, is skipped or stops for any other reason, so skips are included rather than thrown away. That makes it the closest thing Google Ads gives you to an average view duration for ads, and a fair way to compare two edits: the one with the higher average watch time per impression held the same kind of audience longer. It is reported for skippable in-stream, non-skippable in-stream, bumper and Shorts ads.

Key takeaways

  • Google defines watch time as "the total amount of time people spend watching your video ads over a given time period", in seconds.
  • Average watch time per impression is that total divided across impressions.
  • It counts time until the ad completes, is skipped, or stops for any reason.
  • It is available for in-stream, non-skippable in-stream, bumper ads and Shorts.
  • Add it in your video campaign's columns under "Performance". It shows "--" until an ad has enough measured impressions.

Where it sits among YouTube ad metrics

From Google's pages on watch time for video campaigns and YouTube ads and view metrics:

MetricWhat it tells youLimitation
Views (TrueView)People who watched 30 seconds or to the end (or interacted) on skippable in-streamSays nothing about people who skipped
View rateViews divided by impressionsA yes/no threshold
Video played to 25/50/75/100%Share of people who started the player and reached each pointFour checkpoints only
Watch timeTotal seconds watchedGrows with spend, so hard to compare
Avg. watch time/impr.Seconds watched per impressionDepends on ad length and format

The advantage of watch time per impression is that it gives credit for partial attention. A viewer who skips at 12 seconds of a 60-second ad adds 12 seconds, whereas for views they count as zero.

How Google measures it

Each time the ad starts, Google measures until it completes, is skipped or stops. If some viewing can't be measured (Google gives a browser crash as an example), it estimates the missing time from the average of measured impressions. Data only appears once an ad has a minimum number of measured impressions, and segmented totals may not add up exactly to the campaign total because that minimum is applied to each segment separately.

How to use it to judge edits

Google itself suggests three uses: compare ads or ad groups to see which content holds attention, test the same creative in different setups to see which audiences watch longer, and use the average seconds watched to shape the length and structure of future ads.

In practice:

  1. Compare like with like. Same length, same format, same audience. A 6-second bumper and a 90-second in-stream ad can't be compared directly.
  2. Set it against the ad's length. If the average is 9 seconds on a 60-second ad, most people are leaving well before your offer. Move the offer earlier or cut a shorter version.
  3. Pair it with the quartiles. Watch time per impression tells you how much attention you got; the 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% checkpoints show where you lost it.
  4. Use it for audience tests. If the same ad gets much longer watch time with one audience, that audience may be the better fit for the message.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches pacing for YouTube?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) covers pacing and hooks for £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCD guidance is a free alternative.

Is average watch time per impression the same as average view duration?

It's similar in spirit, but it's an ad metric in Google Ads, measured per impression. Average view duration is a YouTube Studio metric for your own videos.

Does it include people who skipped?

Yes. Google measures until the ad completes, is skipped or stops.

Why does it show "--"?

The ad hasn't reached Google's minimum number of measured impressions yet.

Is it available for in-feed ads?

Google lists in-stream, non-skippable in-stream, bumper ads and Shorts as the supported formats.
