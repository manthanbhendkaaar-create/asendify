How do you make a brand activation or pop-up video that people watch past the first few seconds?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Open on a real person's reaction to the experience, not on the logo or the set build, because that reaction is what makes a stranger want to see more. Show what the activation actually is within the first few seconds, keep the brand visible throughout, cut vertical versions for social, get consent from people on camera, and label any paid or creator posts clearly as ads.

Key takeaways

  • Most people who see an activation video weren't there, so the video has to explain the experience fast.
  • Google's ABCDs advise brands to "Hook and sustain attention with an immersive story" and "Brand early, often, and richly."
  • TikTok recommends vertical 9:16, at least 720p, and putting the hook in the first 6 seconds.
  • If an influencer is paid or gifted to attend and post, the ASA expects the content to be obviously identifiable as an ad from the start.
  • Plan capture of genuine reactions; staged crowds or invented reactions are misleading.

Hook with a reaction

Google's guidance on the ABCDs of effective video ads tells advertisers to "get to the heart of the story faster, and use engaging pacing and tight framing to draw viewers in." For an activation, the heart of the story is usually a visitor's reaction: someone stepping into the installation, tasting the product, winning the prize. Start there. The wide shot of the set build can come later, or not at all.

The same guide says "Brand early, often, and richly" and suggests reinforcing the message with audio and text. Put the brand on the set, on staff clothing and in an on-screen line in the opening seconds so viewers aren't left guessing.

Cut for social

Most activation videos live on Reels, TikTok and Stories. TikTok's creative best practices recommend vertical 9:16 at 720p or higher, putting the hook in the first 6 seconds, and using captions or text overlays (about 5-10 words per second if you use on-screen text). Keep text inside the safe zone so app buttons don't cover it.

Film vertically on the day with at least one dedicated camera or phone, rather than cropping landscape footage afterwards, which often cuts off faces and products.

Labelling and consent

If you pay or gift creators to attend and post, their content is advertising. The ASA's guidance on recognising ads on social media says content from influencers with a commercial connection to a brand must be "obviously identifiable" as advertising, and that disclosure won't be enough if the audience has to watch the whole thing to understand it. A clear label such as "#Ad" up front is the usual approach. Posts from the brand's own recognisable account are generally identifiable as ads already.

For visitors, use clear signs that filming is taking place, get signed releases for anyone featured prominently, and offer a way to opt out.

Example: a two-day pop-up for a drinks brand

Example: a soft-drinks brand runs a two-day pop-up in a shopping centre where visitors try a new flavour in a mirrored room.

  • Before: a shot list with three reaction set-ups at the mirrored room entrance, a vertical phone operator, release forms and signage.
  • Day one: 40 visitors filmed reacting; 12 sign releases for prominent use.
  • The 20-second vertical opens on a visitor's double take as the mirrors light up (0-2 s), then shows the tasting (3-8 s), the branded can and flavour name on screen (from 1 s), the queue outside (9-15 s) and "Here until Sunday, level 2" (16-20 s).
  • Three paid creators post their own versions labelled "#Ad" in the first line of the caption and on screen.
  • A 45-second landscape version goes on the brand's YouTube and website after the event.

Measuring an activation video

For organic posts, look at how many viewers watched past the first few seconds and how many shared or saved the clip. For paid posts, compare cost per completed view and clicks to the location or product page against your other ads. If the opening reaction is strong but people drop off in the middle, the cut is too long: trim the atmosphere shots first.

Brief the crew before the doors open

Give the camera team a one-page brief: the three reaction spots to cover, the busiest times, which creators are attending, where the release forms are, and the deadline for the first social cut. A brief that fits on one page actually gets read on the day.

Common mistakes

  • Opening on the logo or a timelapse of the build.
  • Landscape-only footage cropped badly for social.
  • Unlabelled creator posts.
  • Using visitors' faces without consent.
  • Forgetting the practical call to action: where, when, until when.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks and pacing for short branded videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches cold opens and pacing for YouTube, which carries over to activation clips. Google's ABCDs guide is a free, ad-focused alternative.

How long should a brand activation video be?

For social, often 15-30 seconds. A longer landscape version for the website can run 45-90 seconds.

Do creators at my activation need to label their posts as ads?

If you paid or gifted them, yes, under ASA guidance the content must be obviously identifiable as advertising from the start.

Should I film vertical or horizontal?

Both, if you can. Film vertical for social on a dedicated device and landscape for the website.

Can I use visitors in the video?

Yes, with clear filming notices and signed releases for anyone featured prominently, plus a way to opt out.

What makes a good opening shot?

A genuine reaction from a visitor at the moment the experience surprises them.
