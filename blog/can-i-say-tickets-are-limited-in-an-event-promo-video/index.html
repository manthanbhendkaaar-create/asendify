Can I say "only a few tickets left" in my event promo video?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Only if it is true and you can prove it. UK advertising rules say marketers must make a reasonable estimate of demand and must not falsely claim an offer is available only for a very limited time. The ASA has ruled that a ticket ad saying "Only 14 tickets remaining" was misleading. Honest urgency, such as a real early-bird deadline or a genuinely small venue, works well in promo videos and keeps you on the right side of the rules.

Key takeaways

  • CAP Code rule 3.27: "Marketers must make a reasonable estimate of the demand for advertised products."
  • Rule 3.30 bans falsely claiming something "will only be available for a very limited time".
  • The ASA found "Only 14 tickets remaining" misleading because consumers would think most tickets had already sold.
  • "Hurry! Limited stock" was ruled misleading where the advertiser had no sales history to support it.
  • The UK government's Competition and Markets Authority has also warned businesses about pressure-selling tactics online.

What the rules say

The ASA's advice on availability claims summarises the rules in the CAP Code, which covers non-broadcast ads including social video and online promos:

Rule or rulingWhat it means for your promo
Rule 3.27: reasonable estimate of demandYou need evidence for how many tickets you have and how fast they're selling
Rule 3.30: no false time limits"Ends Friday" must actually end Friday
"Only 14 tickets remaining" ruled misleadingA count must reflect reality and not imply most have sold if they haven't
"Hurry! Limited stock" ruled misleadingYou need sales history to support scarcity claims
"Subject to availability" may not be enoughSmall print doesn't fix a misleading headline

The Competition and Markets Authority's open letter on online choice architecture also warned businesses about online sales tactics that pressure people unfairly.

Why honest urgency works better anyway

Fake scarcity has a retention problem as well as a legal one. Attendees who see "Last 10 tickets" in March and still see it in June stop believing your videos. Real urgency is more persuasive because it is specific and checkable.

Honest urgency you can use

  • Real deadlines: "Early-bird price ends 31 March" (and it does).
  • Real capacity: "120 seats. That's the room." If the room really holds 120.
  • Real progress: "Half of all tickets sold" when you've sold half.
  • Real tiers: "First 200 tickets at £49" with an accurate count.
  • Time-bound content: "Speakers announced Friday" as a reason to follow.

Putting urgency into the edit

Urgency belongs where it helps, not as a constant shout. In a 30-second promo:

  1. 0-3 s: cold open on the most striking moment. The Retention Editing Playbook's rule: start on the most interesting thing.
  2. 3-20 s: what the event offers, with proof.
  3. 20-27 s: the honest urgency line as on-screen text: "Early-bird ends Sunday".
  4. 27-30 s: the link.

Google's ABCDs of effective video ads say to "add a CTA to drive a specific objective. Say what you'd like viewers to do." A clear, true deadline gives that call to action a reason.

Example: a festival's ticket campaign

Example: a 3,000-capacity food festival runs video ads from January to June.

  • January: "Early-bird: first 500 tickets at £30." The team tracks sales daily; the line comes down the day the 500th sells.
  • March: "1,500 sold. Standard tickets £38." True on the day the ads go live; updated weekly.
  • May: "Saturday is 90% sold." Only after the ticketing system shows it.
  • June: "Sunday only. Saturday sold out." Saturday really is sold out.

Each claim matches the ticketing data on the date it runs. The team keeps screenshots as evidence.

Keep evidence

If you make a scarcity or deadline claim, keep a dated record of the data behind it: ticket sales reports, venue capacity, the date a price changed. If someone complains, that's what you'll need.

Quick checklist before a promo goes live

  • Check every scarcity or deadline line against today's ticketing data.
  • Save a dated screenshot of the data that supports each claim.
  • Make sure deadlines in the video match the deadline on the ticket page.
  • Set a reminder to update or pause ads when a tier sells out or a price changes.
  • Avoid vague pressure lines such as "selling fast" unless sales data supports them.
  • Make the main limitation clear in the video itself, not only in small print.
  • Have someone outside the marketing team read the claims and ask, "Is this obviously true?"
  • Review complaints or comments that question a claim, and fix the ad quickly if needed.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving an "early-bird ends soon" line running after the early-bird has ended.
  • Showing a ticket count that was true at launch but not now.
  • Using a countdown timer that resets.
  • Saying an event is "nearly sold out" based on a feeling rather than ticket data.
  • Relying on small print to correct a misleading headline.
  • Running the same scarcity line in every video, so it stops meaning anything.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches cold opens and calls to action for video?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches cold opens and endings that lead to action. It doesn't cover advertising law; use the ASA's guidance for that.

Can I say "selling fast"?

Only if sales data supports it. The ASA expects a reasonable basis for demand claims.

Is a countdown timer allowed in a promo?

Yes, if the deadline is real and the offer actually ends when the timer does.

Does "subject to availability" protect me?

Not always. The ASA says such phrases may not be enough to make a limitation clear.

Do these rules apply to organic social posts?

The CAP Code covers marketing communications, which can include your own social posts promoting ticket sales.

What if tickets sell out faster than expected?

Update your ads promptly. Ads saying tickets are available when they aren't can also mislead.
