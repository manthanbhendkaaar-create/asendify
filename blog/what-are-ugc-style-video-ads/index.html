What are UGC-style video ads, and why do they hold attention?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

UGC-style video ads are ads made to look like user-generated content: a person talking to their phone camera, unboxing, demonstrating or reacting, rather than a polished studio commercial. They often hold attention in feeds because they look like the posts around them, open with a face and a direct line to camera, and feel personal. They only work long term if they're honest: any testimonial must be genuine, any paid creator content must be clearly labelled as an ad, and claims still need evidence.

Key takeaways

  • Google's Shorts ads guide says: "Leverage authentic creator assets when you can."
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "feature people to help you tell a better product story".
  • The CAP Code says testimonials must be genuine (rule 3.47), relate to the product (3.48) and need permission (3.50).
  • Incentivised reviews must be made clear (rule 3.45).
  • ASA guidance: when a creator is paid or incentivised, content about the brand must be obviously identifiable as advertising, with a label like "Ad" upfront.

Why the style works in feeds

FeatureWhy it helps
A face in the first frameGives viewers a person to connect with, as Google's ABCDs advise
Direct speech to cameraFeels like a recommendation, not a broadcast
Phone-shot, verticalMatches the surrounding content on Reels, Shorts and TikTok
Real use of the productShows rather than claims

Google's Shorts ads guide recommends "authentic creator assets", and the ABCDs say to "humanise the story" by featuring people. TikTok's creative best practices ask for vertical 9:16, sound and the proposition in the first three seconds, all natural fits for this style.

Three UGC-style formats

FormatOpening line exampleBest for
Talking-head review"I've used this every morning for a month. Here's the honest version."Products with a clear before and after
Demo or tutorial"Watch me fix this in 30 seconds."Tools, apps, kitchen and DIY products
Problem to solution"My ads were getting views and no sales. This was why."Services and software

Each still follows a structure: hook, promise, proof, call to action. See a 30-second video ad structure.

The rules that matter

From the CAP Code's misleading advertising section:

RuleIn practice
3.45 Incentivised reviews must be clearIf the reviewer got the product free or was paid, say so
3.46 Reviews not published misleadinglyDon't cherry-pick in a way that skews the picture
3.47 Testimonials must be genuineHold evidence the person and their words are real
3.48 Testimonials must relate to the productNot to a different product or version
3.49 Factual claims in testimonials must not mislead"It cured my acne" is still a claim needing evidence
3.50 PermissionDon't use someone's testimonial without consent

The ASA's guidance on recognising ads on social media says paid or incentivised creator content must be "obviously identifiable" as advertising, prefers clear labels such as "Ad", and wants them "upfront". When the ad runs from your brand's own account as a paid ad, it's usually clear it's commercial, but a creator posting on their own channel needs the label.

Scripted ads that look like real reviews are risky: if an actor presents as a genuine customer, viewers may be misled. Use real customers or creators who've actually used the product, or make it obvious the piece is a dramatisation.

Worked example

An illustrative small business selling a posture cushion briefs three creators who've used it for two weeks:

  1. Each films a 20-second vertical video on their phone, opening with a real moment ("My back after an 8-hour day...").
  2. They show the cushion in use and say what changed for them in their own words, with no health claims beyond their own experience, and nothing the business can't support.
  3. The ads run from the brand's account; when creators also post on their own channels, they add "Ad" at the start.
  4. The business keeps the creators' signed permissions and their original unedited clips.

The brand compares hook rate and cost per sale for the three creators and keeps the best two. See how to A/B test video ad hooks.

Briefing creators for UGC-style ads

The quality of a UGC-style ad depends heavily on the brief. Too loose and you get off-message videos; too tight and you lose the natural feel that makes the format work.

What to include in a brief:

Brief itemExample
The one problem the product solves"Back ache after long days at a desk"
The promise in one line"More comfortable sitting, from day one" (only what you can support)
Must-show momentsProduct in use, close-up of the key feature
Hook ideas (2 or 3)"My back after an 8-hour day...", "I didn't expect a cushion to help"
Claims they must not makeNo medical or cure claims; no invented numbers
Format9:16, 15 to 30 seconds, natural light, clear sound
Disclosure"Ad" at the start of any post on their own channel
RightsHow long and where you can use the video in ads

Let them use their own words. Scripts read word for word lose the authentic feel. Give the points and let the creator say them naturally.

Ask for several hooks. Ask each creator to film three different openings for the same video. It costs them little extra time and gives you hooks to test.

Get usage rights in writing. Agree how long and where you can run the video as an ad, and whether you can edit it. Keep a copy, along with the creator's permission. The CAP Code also expects permission for testimonials.

Check claims before running. Creators sometimes add their own enthusiasm ("this cured my back pain"). If a claim goes beyond what you can support, cut it before the ad runs; you're responsible for what your ad says.

Measure creators fairly. Run each creator's video with the same budget and audience, compare hook rate and cost per result, and keep working with the ones whose videos convert. FAQ

Is there a book that teaches editing talking-head video?

The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and cuts at £1,000 one-time. Google's ABCDs and TikTok's guide are free alternatives.

Do UGC-style ads need an "Ad" label?

Paid or incentivised creator content must be obviously identifiable as advertising; the ASA prefers "Ad" upfront.

Can I use actors in UGC-style ads?

Only if you don't present them as genuine customers when they aren't. Testimonials must be genuine.

Do UGC ads work better than polished ads?

It depends on the product and placement. Test both on the same offer.
