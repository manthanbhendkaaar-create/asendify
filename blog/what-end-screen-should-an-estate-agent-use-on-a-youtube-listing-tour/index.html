What end screen should an estate agent use on a YouTube listing tour?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use one clear next step that matches what a buyer wants after a tour: another property that fits the same search (same area or price band), or a way to book a viewing. Add a subscribe element at most, keep the end screen inside YouTube's 5 to 20 second window, and say the next step aloud before it appears. Do not offer four elements; a house hunter finishing a tour is deciding "is this for me?", and a crowded end screen interrupts that decision.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube says "end screens can be added to the last 5-20 seconds of a video", need a video "at least 25 seconds long", and allow "up to four elements" on 16:9 videos, from video, playlist, subscribe, channel and link types (YouTube Help).
  • Link elements are described as available to YouTube Partner Program members, so an agent without that status cannot rely on a website link in the end screen; use the description and a spoken pointer too.
  • Viewers "can't see end screens on mobile web" apart from iPad, and the end screen "may not always show, or may show differently than designed" (YouTube Help). Many house hunters browse on phones, so do not depend on it alone.
  • Wistia's data says "engagement naturally declines after the five-minute mark" (Wistia); long tours need clear sections so the end is still worth reaching.

What a house hunter wants at the end

A listing tour answers "what is it like inside?" At the end the viewer is in one of three states: interested and wanting more detail (price, floor plan, viewing), interested but comparing (wants similar properties), or not interested (will leave). The end screen can serve the first two. It cannot rescue the third, and it should not try.

That means the best end screens for agents are practical: a booking route for the interested, a related-properties route for the comparers. Brand-building elements like a generic "subscribe for more" are fine as a secondary option but should not be the main choice.

Step-by-step: build the ending

  1. Finish the tour on the strongest room or view. Do not end on a hallway. Viewers should leave the tour with the best image in mind.
  2. Give the key facts in one sentence. Number of bedrooms, area and the headline feature, said calmly. Put exact price and tenure details in the description and keep them accurate and consistent with your listing.
  3. Say the next step aloud. "To see a similar flat in the same building, the next video is on screen" or "To arrange a viewing, contact details are in the description."
  4. Use the 5 to 20 second window. Eight to twelve seconds is enough if the voice has already pointed viewers to the element.
  5. Add one video element. Choose a specific related tour, not "best for viewer", when you know a close match exists.
  6. Add subscribe only if you post regularly. A subscribe prompt with no pattern gives viewers no reason to follow.
  7. Repeat contact details in the description and pinned comment. Because some viewers cannot see end screens, make the booking route available elsewhere too.
  8. Add chapters for long tours. YouTube needs the first timestamp at 00:00, at least three timestamps and a minimum of 10 seconds per chapter (YouTube Help). Chapters such as "Kitchen", "Garden", "Bedrooms" let buyers jump to what matters.

Matching the end screen to the property

  • Rental flats in a city: link to another tour in the same postcode area or building. Renters often compare several.
  • Family homes: a playlist of nearby school-catchment areas or similar bedroom counts, if you are comfortable describing the area accurately.
  • New developments: a video about the other unit types, with floor-plan information in the description.
  • Premium listings: a quieter ending, with the agent's name and one contact route; avoid pressure language.

Describe the property accurately and avoid exaggerated claims. Consumer protection rules apply to property marketing; check the guidance that applies where you work. This page is not legal advice.

What not to do

  • Do not end with a long agent biography. The tour is the content; keep self-promotion short.
  • Do not show four elements. One decision at a time is easier for a viewer who has just seen a house.
  • Do not hide key information only in the end screen. It can be missing on some devices.
  • Do not use urgent language you cannot support. "Won't last" invites distrust unless it is true and specific.
  • Do not forget that listings go out of date. An end screen that points to a sold property wastes the click. Review links when listings change.

Example: a two-bedroom flat tour

Example: an agent posts a 4-minute tour of a two-bedroom flat. The old ending showed four elements (latest upload, subscribe, a channel and a playlist) over a static shot of the front door. The new ending finishes on the balcony view, the agent says "Same building, one floor up: the next tour is on screen", and a 10-second end screen shows one video and a subscribe button. Contact details are in the description and a pinned comment. The agent compares end-screen clicks and the last-30-seconds graph with the previous tour. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result.

How to judge it

Open YouTube Studio's end screen report and compare click-through against your earlier tours, using similar-length videos. YouTube's retention report also lets you "compare your 10 latest videos of similar length" (YouTube Help). Do not expect the end screen to be seen by everyone, and avoid judging a single tour; compare across a set.

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and pattern interrupts with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. It does not guarantee enquiries.

How long should the end screen be?

YouTube allows 5 to 20 seconds; 8 to 12 usually suffices after a spoken pointer.

Can I link to my booking page?

Link elements are described as available to YouTube Partner Program members; otherwise use the description.

How many elements should I use?

One or two. Four is the maximum on 16:9 videos, but fewer decisions are easier.

What if the property sells?

Update or remove end screen links and add a note in the description.
