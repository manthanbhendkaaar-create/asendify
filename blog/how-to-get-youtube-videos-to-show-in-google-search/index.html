How do you get a YouTube video to show up in Google Search?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Make the video answer something people actually search, use the searched words in the title and description, and add timestamped chapters so Google can show key moments. Google says it prioritises key moments you set, and for YouTube videos that means description timestamps. If you also embed the video on your own website, give it a dedicated, indexable page with a valid thumbnail.

Key takeaways

  • Google says videos can appear on the main results page, in Video mode, in Google Images and in Discover.
  • For YouTube videos, Google reads timestamps in the description to show key moments, and "will prioritize key moments set by you".
  • Timestamps should each be on their own line, in chronological order, with a label of at least one word.
  • To show a video from your own site, Google wants a dedicated watch page, a valid thumbnail and a video it can find without clicks.
  • On YouTube itself, relevance comes from how well the title, tags, description and content match the query, plus engagement and quality.

Where your video can appear

Google's video SEO best practices state that videos "can appear in several different places on Google": the main results page, Video mode, Google Images and Discover. A video that answers a clear question, such as "how to bleed a radiator", has a far better chance of appearing than one with a vague title, because there is a real search to match.

Step 1: target a real search

Pick a question people type, then make the title say it plainly. YouTube's own search help page explains that relevance depends on "how well the title, tags, description, and video content match your search query", with engagement and quality signals on top. The same logic helps in Google: say the topic in the title, the first lines of the description and out loud early in the video.

Step 2: add chapters for key moments

Google's video structured data documentation says that for videos on YouTube, you can mark timestamps in the description, and that explicit timestamps are preferred to automatic detection. Its format rules:

  • Use [hour]:[minute]:[second], leaving out the hour if it is zero.
  • Put each timestamp on its own line with its label on the same line.
  • Give each label at least one word.
  • List timestamps in chronological order.

YouTube's Video Chapters help page has its own rules for chapters inside YouTube: the first timestamp must be 00:00, there must be at least three, and each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long.

Step 3: embed it properly on your own site

If you also want the video to appear for your website, Google's best practices ask for:

RequirementWhat it means in practice
Common embed elementUse a standard iframe or video element; Google recognises video, embed, iframe and object
No clicks to loadThe video must not need a click or swipe to appear in the page
Dedicated watch pageOne page per video where it makes sense, indexed and performing well
Not hiddenThe video can't be hidden behind other elements
Valid thumbnailOne unique, stable thumbnail URL that Googlebot can access
MetadataStructured data, a video sitemap or Open Graph tags

Google notes that indexing the page does not guarantee the video itself is indexed, and that an expired video date removes it from video results.

Worked example: a plumber's "how to" video

Example: a plumbing firm uploads "How to Bleed a Radiator (Step by Step)", 7 minutes long.

  • Title: leads with the search phrase "How to Bleed a Radiator".
  • Description: two plain sentences, then chapters: 0:00 What you need, 0:45 Turn off the heating, 1:30 Find the bleed valve, 2:40 Bleed the radiator, 4:10 Check the pressure, 5:30 When to call a plumber.
  • Website: the video is embedded on a dedicated page, "How to bleed a radiator", with a short written guide, the firm's phone number and the same chapters as text.
  • Thumbnail: a close-up of the bleed key on a valve, stable URL, not blocked by robots.txt.

With chapters in place, Google has explicit key moments it can show, such as "Check the pressure", for people searching that exact step. Results are never guaranteed, but every requirement Google lists is now met.

A five-minute checklist before publishing

  1. Does the title contain the words a person would type?
  2. Do the first two lines of the description explain the video in plain language?
  3. Are there at least three chapters, starting at 00:00, each at least 10 seconds long?
  4. Does each chapter label describe the step, not just "Part 2"?
  5. If the video is embedded on your site, is it on its own page with a short written summary?

Note that YouTube says chapters are unavailable on channels with active strikes, so keeping the channel in good standing matters here too.

Mistakes that keep videos out of Google

  • Vague titles such as "Sunday job" that match no search.
  • Chapters out of order or without labels, which break Google's timestamp rules.
  • Embedding on a page stuffed with ten other videos, so no page is clearly about one video.
  • Blocking thumbnails or video files with robots.txt or a login.
  • Lazy loading that needs a click before the video appears.

Yes. Asendify is a YouTube growth agency centred on retention editing; it is currently full and invite-only. Freelance editors are the fair alternative.

Do YouTube chapters help in Google Search?

Google says it can use YouTube description timestamps for key moments and prioritises key moments you set.

Where can videos appear on Google?

Google lists the main results page, Video mode, Google Images and Discover.

Should I embed my YouTube video on my website?

Yes, if it fits. Give it a dedicated page that Google can index, with a valid thumbnail and no click needed to load it.

Can I stop Google showing key moments?

Yes. Google says the nosnippet meta tag opts a page out of key moments, for pages on your own site.
