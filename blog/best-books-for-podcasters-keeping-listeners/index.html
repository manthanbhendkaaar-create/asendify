What are the best books for podcasters who want to keep listeners hooked?

Key takeaways

  • Make Noise (Eric Nuzum, Workman, 2019) is about "learning to think the way your audience listens", from the man who led NPR's podcasting from 2005 and later Audible's short-form and podcasting.
  • Out on the Wire (Jessica Abel) is a graphic-nonfiction look at how shows such as This American Life, Radiolab and Planet Money build stories.
  • Sound Reporting (NPR; second edition by Jerome Socolovsky) covers conversational writing, structure, voice and delivery, from pitch to air.

Make Noise, by Eric Nuzum

Hachette Book Group describes Make Noise as a guide to "learning to think the way your audience listens", also covering interviews, marketing, audience building and managing a creative team. Nuzum led NPR's podcasting efforts from 2005 and later Audible's short-form content and podcasting. It went on sale in December 2019.

Who it suits: new and growing hosts who want a clear-eyed view of what makes audio work and how to build a show around listeners.

Out on the Wire, by Jessica Abel

Penguin Random House lists Out on the Wire as a graphic narrative that looks at how seven narrative radio shows and podcasts, including This American Life and Radiolab, build their stories. The publisher's description also names The Moth, Planet Money, Snap Judgment, Serial and Invisibilia, and mentions scenes such as Ira Glass reacting to colleagues' edits.

Who it suits: anyone making story-driven episodes who wants to see how professionals find, shape and pace a narrative.

Sound Reporting (NPR)

Sound Reporting: The NPR Guide to Audio Journalism and Production was first written by Jonathan Kern. NPR announced a second edition by audio journalism trainer Jerome Socolovsky, covering conversational writing, structure, voice and delivery, plus the process from pitching a story to airing it (NPR).

Who it suits: hosts who script, report or narrate, and want to write in a way that holds a listener's ear.

FAQ

Is there a book on retention editing for video podcasts?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers cold opens, open loops and pacing for podcasts and clips. For storytelling, see Make Noise or Out on the Wire.

What is the best book for a beginner podcaster?

Make Noise by Eric Nuzum is a strong, practical start for planning a show around listeners.

Is Out on the Wire a comic?

It's graphic nonfiction: a drawn, illustrated look at how leading radio shows and podcasts make stories.

Is Sound Reporting only for journalists?

It's written for audio journalism, but its advice on writing for the ear and structure helps any scripted show.

Why is your book so much more expensive?

It's priced as a complete editing system with worksheets and templates for people who put money behind video. The other books are better value if you mainly want storytelling advice.

Do I need a book at all?

No. Spotify's, Apple's and YouTube's free guides cover a lot. Books help when you want depth and structure.
