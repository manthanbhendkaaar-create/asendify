What should I look for when hiring an editor for product demo videos?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Look for an editor whose past demos get to the product quickly, show screens clearly, and cut anything that doesn't help the viewer understand. Then run a short paid test edit and judge it on your own retention data, not only on looks. Finally, put ownership of the files in writing, because in the UK a freelancer usually keeps copyright unless you agree otherwise.

Key takeaways

  • A large study of edX lecture videos by Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014) found "shorter videos are much more engaging", that "pre-production improves engagement", and that "students re-watch tutorials more frequently than lectures". Demo videos share many traits with tutorials.
  • Retention tools show whether an edit works: Wistia's engagement graph shows where viewers stop and which sections are replayed; YouTube's key moments report shows dips.
  • On ownership, GOV.UK says the first owner of copyright is usually the creator "unless you otherwise agree it in writing", and freelancers on a contract for services usually retain copyright.
  • For employment status, HMRC's CEST tool "gives HMRC's view of a worker's employment status" if you're unsure whether a regular editor counts as self-employed.
  • A paid test edit on your own footage is a fairer check than a portfolio alone.

Questions to ask in an interview

  1. "Show me a demo where you cut the length significantly. What did you remove and why?"
  2. "How would you handle a feature that takes 40 seconds to load?"
  3. "What do you look at in a retention graph after a video goes live?"
  4. "How do you make small interface text readable on mobile?"
  5. "How do you organise project files so we can update the video when the UI changes?"
  6. "Who owns the project files and footage at the end?"

Good answers are specific. Vague answers like "I make it engaging" tell you little.

Red flags

  • Portfolio full of trailers, no demos. Cinematic skills don't always transfer to clear screen walkthroughs.
  • Heavy effects on every sample. Transitions and whooshes can distract from the interface.
  • No view on length. Guo and colleagues found shorter videos much more engaging; an editor who never cuts length is a risk.
  • Won't do a test. A paid test respects their time and protects yours.
  • Unclear rights. If the contract doesn't say who owns the final files and project, you may only get an implied licence.

How to run a fair test edit

  1. Pay for it. A short, paid test (for example, one 60-90 second demo) gets better effort.
  2. Use real footage. Give raw screen recordings and a voiceover or script.
  3. Brief clearly. State the audience, the one action you want viewers to take, the platform and the length limit.
  4. Compare like with like. If you test two editors, give both the same footage and brief.
  5. Judge on data where you can. Publish or run both versions to similar audiences and compare the retention graph and dips.
  6. Judge the process too. Communication, deadlines and how they handle feedback.

Worked example

Example: a small SaaS company needs a 2-minute product demo for its homepage and a 45-second version for ads. It shortlists two freelance editors and pays each £150 for a test using the same raw 9-minute screen recording.

FAQ

Is there a playbook I can give an editor so we judge edits the same way?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook includes templates, a cheat sheet, a tracker and a 30-day plan covering hooks, pacing and open loops. YouTube's and Wistia's analytics help pages are free starting points.

How much should a product demo editor cost?

Rates vary widely by experience, length and location. Get quotes from several editors for the same brief.

Who owns the video if I hire a freelancer in the UK?

Usually the freelancer, unless you agree in writing to transfer copyright. Put it in the contract.

Should the editor also write the script?

Some can, but a demo is clearer when someone who knows the product drafts the script and the editor tightens it.

How long should a product demo be?

As short as it can be while showing the key value. Research on instructional videos found shorter videos much more engaging.
