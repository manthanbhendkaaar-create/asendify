Where can I watch anime for free legally in 2026?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 14 November 2026 · Last updated 14 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Since Crunchyroll ended its free, ad-supported plan at the end of 2025, the main legal ways to watch anime for free are ad-supported services such as Tubi, RetroCrush and Pluto TV, and official YouTube channels run by rights holders, such as the Pokémon TV channel. Free choices are narrower than before, so for the newest series you will usually need a paid plan, but there is still plenty to watch legally without paying.

Key takeaways

  • Crunchyroll's ad-supported free plan ended on 31 December 2025, according to reports of an in-app notice.
  • Free, legal, ad-supported options include Tubi (general library with some anime), RetroCrush (classic anime from the 1970s to 1990s) and Pluto TV.
  • Official YouTube channels from studios and licensors post full episodes for free in some regions.
  • Free libraries skew towards older or less-current titles; brand-new simulcasts are mostly behind paid plans.
  • Pirate sites are not legal, often carry malware-laden ads, and take money away from the people who make the shows.

What changed with Crunchyroll

Android Authority reported that Crunchyroll's free plan would end on 31 December 2025, after users saw an in-player message in place of an ad, and noted that the free plan had only offered select shows. GamesRadar quoted the notice: "Ad-supported streaming ends December 31, 2025." Crunchyroll's paid tiers are Fan, Mega Fan and Ultimate Fan; BGR listed them at $7.99, $11.99 and $15.99 at the time. Check current prices in your country before subscribing.

FAQ

Is Crunchyroll still free?

No. Reports say Crunchyroll ended its free, ad-supported plan on 31 December 2025. It now offers paid plans: Fan, Mega Fan and Ultimate Fan.

Is watching anime on pirate sites illegal?

Streaming from unlicensed sites infringes copyright in many countries, and these sites often carry harmful ads and malware. Licensed free services are the safer choice.

Is there free anime on YouTube?

Yes. Some rights holders post full episodes on official channels, such as the Pokémon TV channel, and independent creators publish original anime. What is available depends on your region.

What is the best free anime streaming service?

It depends on what you like. RetroCrush is strong for classic anime, Tubi for a mixed library, and official YouTube channels for specific franchises.

Can I watch new anime episodes for free?

Usually not on the day they air. New simulcasts are mostly behind paid plans, though some older seasons are free on ad-supported services.
