How do I use Premiere Pro markers to plan re-hooks across a long video?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Add a sequence marker at every point where you expect attention to sag, label each with the re-hook you plan to use there, and check the spacing before you start trimming. In a long video the markers become a map: you can see at a glance whether a ten-minute stretch has no reason to keep watching. Adobe describes markers as a way of organising your editing workflow, so they cost nothing to add and nothing to remove, and they can double as chapter points later.

Key takeaways

  • Adobe's help pages cover "different marker types and how to use the Markers panel" for "organizing your video editing workflow" (Adobe Premiere Pro help). Check the current shortcut and marker types in your version.
  • YouTube's retention report says "dips mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video" (YouTube Help). Markers let you plan against likely dips before you publish.
  • Wistia says "engagement naturally declines after the five-minute mark" (Wistia), so a long video needs more than one planned re-hook.
  • YouTube chapters need the first timestamp at 00:00, at least three timestamps and a minimum of 10 seconds each (YouTube Help); markers placed at section starts can be copied straight into your description.

What a re-hook is, and why plan it

A re-hook is a short moment that gives viewers a fresh reason to keep watching: a question you will answer, a result you will show, a change of format, a promise of what is next. The first hook gets people past the opening. In a long video, attention has to be earned again several times.

Planning matters because re-hooks are easy to forget in the edit. You are busy with cuts, sound and graphics, and by the end the timeline has twenty minutes of material and one hook. A marker is a prompt that sits on the timeline until you deal with it.

Marker types and what to use them for

Premiere Pro offers several kinds of marker and colours; the names and shortcuts differ by version, so open the Markers panel in yours. A simple scheme works with any set of colours.

Marker colour or labelUseExample text
GreenSection start (also a chapter)"S2: Why the price fell"
RedLikely dip, needs a re-hook"Dip risk: 3 min of setup"
BlueRe-hook planned and placed"Re-hook: question at 4:05"
YellowCheck on a second pass"Is this example needed?"
PurpleSpike candidate, give room"Best moment, keep full length"

Use whatever colours you like, but write the scheme in your project notes so a second editor understands it.

Step by step: plan the re-hooks

  1. Mark the sections first. Put a marker at the start of each section from your script or outline. Name each with the question it answers.
  2. Measure the gaps. Read the time between markers. A section longer than your usual attention span (check your own graph) gets a second marker in the middle.
  3. Mark likely dips. Use the list from your earlier videos: long setups, repeated points, slow demos, talking over static visuals, a recap before the main point.
  4. Choose a re-hook for each. Pick one device per marker: an open question, a result teaser, a change of visual format, a short story, a poll in the comments, a promise of a section. Write it in the marker comment.
  5. Vary the device. If every marker says "question", the video will feel mechanical. Alternate.
  6. Place the re-hook just before the dip risk. A re-hook after viewers have left does nothing; place it as the sag begins.
  7. Check the opening and the end. The first 30 seconds and the final minute need separate notes; do not use re-hook markers for them.
  8. Edit to the markers. As you cut, move through the red markers and swap them to blue as you place the re-hook.
  9. Run a second pass. Scroll the timeline; any red marker left is a gap.
  10. Export chapter times. Copy section markers to the description in the format YouTube asks for: first timestamp at 00:00, at least three entries, at least 10 seconds each.
  11. Compare with the graph after publishing. Look for dips at the red markers and note whether the re-hook helped.

How many re-hooks does a long video need?

There is no fixed number. A rough rule many editors use is one every few minutes, but your own graph is a better guide. The table below is a planning aid, not a measured result.

LengthSectionsRe-hooks to plan (starting point)

30 min or more8 or more6 to 10, plus chapter markers

Wistia reports an 11% drop in engagement for videos past 30 minutes, so very long videos benefit from visible structure and a reason to stay at each chapter (Wistia).

Example: a 20-minute explainer

Example: an editor has a 20-minute explainer with six sections. They add six green markers, then measure gaps: section 3 is 5 minutes 40 seconds long. They add a red marker at 9:30 for a mid-section dip risk, and a second red marker at 14:10 where the old graphs showed a dip during a long demo. For each they write a re-hook: a question at 9:25 ("what does this cost in year two?"), a results preview at 14:00. They also add a purple marker at 17:20 where a previous video had a spike. On the second pass they change red markers to blue as each is done. After publishing, they look at the graph at each position and write the result in their log. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Too many markers. If every second has a marker, none stands out.
  • Using the same device each time. Vary the re-hooks.
  • Promises you do not keep. A re-hook that teases something the next section does not deliver damages trust.
  • Forgetting to delete or hide. Markers do not export in the video file, but check your project before sharing it with a client.
  • Planning only from the outline. Check the cut as well, since timing changes in the edit.

After publishing

Compare the graph with your markers. If there is a dip at a red marker you handled, try a different device next time. If a section with no marker held up, note why. Keep the marker scheme between projects so your notes build up.

Do markers appear in the exported video?

No, they stay in the project, though check your export settings if you embed chapter data.

Can I reuse markers as YouTube chapters?

Yes. Copy the section times to the description, with the first at 00:00 and at least three entries.

What if I use DaVinci Resolve or Final Cut?

Both have marker tools; the same plan works with whatever colours your editor offers.

How many markers should I use?

Enough for each section and each planned re-hook, and no more than you can act on.
