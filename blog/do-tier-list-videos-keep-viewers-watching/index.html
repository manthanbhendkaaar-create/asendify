Do tier list videos keep viewers watching longer?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Tier list videos can hold viewers well because every item is a small open question: where will this one land, and will the creator agree with me? The format breaks a long video into short, clear segments, which learning research links to better understanding. They lose viewers when each placement takes too long, the reasoning repeats, or the best items are obvious from the start.

Key takeaways

  • A tier list ranks items into groups (often S, A, B, C, D). It is popular for games, products, tools, food and creators.
  • Research on rankings by Isaac and Schindler in the Journal of Consumer Research found people treat ranked lists as categories, so crossing a category border (like 11th to 10th) feels bigger than it is. Tiers create these borders on purpose.
  • A meta-analysis of 56 investigations by Rey and colleagues (2019) found presenting material in "meaningful and coherent" segments helps learning, with "small to medium effects". Each tier placement is a natural segment.
  • YouTube chapters let viewers jump between sections, which suits tier lists but also makes skipping easy.
  • Use YouTube's key moments report to see which items cause "dips" where viewers abandon or skip.

What the research suggests

Categories make rankings matter. Isaac and Schindler found that people mentally group ranked items, and that moving across a round-number border changes perceptions more than other moves of the same size. In a tier list, the S/A border is the whole drama: viewers care whether an item "made S tier".

Segments help people follow. Rey and colleagues' review found that splitting material into meaningful segments improved learning. "Retention" in that research means remembering, not watch time, but a format people can follow easily gives fewer reasons to leave.

Chapters cut both ways. Chapters help viewers navigate a long list, and YouTube requires at least three chapters starting at 00:00. But viewers can jump straight to the item they care about. Some creators accept that trade; others keep the S tier reveal to the end and name chapters without giving away results.

Common reasons viewers leave a tier list

  • Slow placements. Two minutes on every item adds up. Many items need only a sentence or two.
  • Front-loaded favourites. If everyone knows what goes in S tier and you place it first, there's less reason to stay.
  • Same reasoning each time. "It's good but not great" ten times in a row turns into background noise.
  • No stakes. If nothing surprising happens, viewers stop wondering.
  • Long intro. Explaining the rules for a minute before the first item delays the payoff.

How to pace a tier list video for retention

  1. Start with a contested item. Open on a placement viewers will argue about, not the least interesting one.
  2. Vary the length per item. Quick placements for obvious items; more time for controversial ones.
  3. Hold back a few big ones. Tease them early ("and yes, we'll get to the one everyone asks about") and place them late. That is an open loop.
  4. Show the board often. Cut back to the full board so viewers see progress.
  5. Add proof, not repetition. Use a clip, stat or example to justify a placement instead of restating opinions.
  6. Use chapters carefully. Name them by item or group without revealing the tier if you want people to watch through.
  7. Check dips. If one item causes a dip, shorten that section next time or cut it.

Worked example

Example: a productivity channel makes a 22-minute tier list of 20 note-taking apps. The first edit spends about a minute per app in alphabetical order. The key moments report shows a steady decline with dips at several minor apps.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on open loops for list and ranking videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops, hooks and pacing, with templates and a 30-day plan. YouTube's analytics help is a free starting point.

How long should a tier list video be?

Long enough to place every item with a reason, and no longer. Shorter placements for obvious items keep the length down.

Should I put chapters on a tier list video?

Chapters help navigation but make skipping easy. Name them without revealing placements if you want viewers to watch through.

How many tiers should I use?

Many creators use five (S to D). Fewer tiers make each border feel bigger; too many blur the differences.

Do tier lists work as Shorts?

Yes, with only a few items and very fast placements. Save the full ranking for a long video.
