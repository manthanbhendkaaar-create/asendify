How do I A/B test YouTube thumbnails?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use YouTube Studio's built-in Test & compare feature. On a computer, open the video's details, choose to test thumbnails, upload up to three versions, and YouTube shows them to different viewers and keeps the one with the highest watch time. It is free, but it does not work on Shorts, and tests can take up to two weeks.

Before you test, you can also take the free test on the Asendify AI site to see how the tool works with no payment.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's Test & compare lets you test up to three thumbnails, three titles, or combinations of both.
  • The winner is chosen on watch time, not click-through rate, so a "clickbait" thumbnail does not automatically win.
  • Make the versions clearly different. Near-identical designs rarely produce a clear winner.
  • Do not edit the title or thumbnail during the test; that stops it.
  • Third-party tools offer A/B testing too, but often judge on click-through rate instead.

How YouTube's Test & compare works

YouTube's help page on A/B testing titles and thumbnails says creators "can test and compare up to 3 different titles and thumbnails". You can test titles only, thumbnails only, or titles and thumbnails together.

The key detail is how a winner is picked: "the title or combination of title and thumbnail with the highest watch time will be shown to all viewers." Results are labelled Winner, Performed same, or Inconclusive. If there is no clear winner, the first option you uploaded stays as the default.

A small control group sees only your default version, and its data is left out of the results.

Step by step: running a thumbnail test

  1. Choose the right video. A new upload or a video still getting steady impressions works best. Tests need impressions to reach a result.
  2. Design two or three real alternatives. Change one big thing per version: the face or no face, the object shown, the text or no text, or the background colour.
  3. Open YouTube Studio on a computer. Go to Content, open the video's details and choose the option to test and compare in the thumbnail area.
  4. Upload your versions. Use full 1280 x 720 resolution so YouTube does not downscale all of them.
  5. Leave it alone. Changing the title or thumbnail during the test stops it, and you would need to restart.
  6. Read the result. If one version wins, YouTube applies it. If the result is inconclusive, your versions may have been too similar or the video may not have had enough impressions.
  7. Write down the lesson. "Close-up of the result beat my face" is useful for every future upload.

Why watch time, not clicks, decides

It is tempting to judge thumbnails purely on click-through rate. YouTube explains in a post on its recommendation system that it added watch time partly because clicks alone did not reliably show that a viewer was satisfied. A thumbnail that promises something the video does not deliver can win clicks and lose viewers.

That is why Test & compare scores on watch time. A thumbnail that brings in the right viewers, who then keep watching, beats one that only attracts curiosity.

How to read click-through rate alongside a test

Click-through rate is still useful context. YouTube's page on impressions and click-through rate says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%, and that videos with many homepage impressions tend to have lower rates. So a test that runs mostly on homepage traffic may show lower click-through rates for every version, which is normal.

Test bold differences first. Once you know your audience prefers, say, close-ups without text, you can test finer details.

Third-party A/B testing tools

Some browser extensions and creator tools also run thumbnail tests. TubeBuddy, for example, describes on its website a tool that "automatically rotates variants, measures real click-through rate, and declares a winner". That approach judges on clicks rather than watch time, so results can differ from YouTube's own tool. If you use one, keep YouTube's watch-time view in mind.

Example: a two-week thumbnail test

Example: Ravi runs a home DIY channel. His video "Fixing a squeaky door in 2 minutes" gets about 3,000 impressions a day. He tests three thumbnails:

YouTube applies version B. Ravi's lesson is that his audience wants to see the fix, not him, and he uses close-up "object" thumbnails for his next three videos. These figures are illustrative; the method is what matters.

Common mistakes

  • Testing tiny tweaks. A slightly brighter version of the same image rarely produces a clear result.
  • Testing on a video with few impressions. Without traffic, tests end inconclusive.
  • Changing the title mid-test. It stops the test.
  • Uploading low-resolution images. One small image downgrades all of them.
  • Only reading click-through rate. The point is watch time from the right viewers.

FAQ

Is YouTube thumbnail testing free?

Yes. Test & compare is built into YouTube Studio at no cost. You need a computer and advanced features enabled.

How many thumbnails can I test on YouTube?

Up to three thumbnails, three titles, or three title and thumbnail combinations per test.

How long does a YouTube thumbnail test take?

YouTube says a test should be completed within two weeks. It can finish in a few days if the video gets plenty of impressions.

Can I A/B test thumbnails on Shorts?

No. YouTube says Shorts cannot be tested with Test & compare.

Why was my thumbnail test inconclusive?

Usually because the versions were too similar or the video did not get enough impressions. Try bolder differences on a video with steady traffic.

Does the winning thumbnail always get more clicks?

Not necessarily. YouTube picks the winner on watch time, so the winner might get slightly fewer clicks but keep viewers watching longer.
