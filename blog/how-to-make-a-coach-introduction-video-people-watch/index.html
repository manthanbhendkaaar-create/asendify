How do I make a coaching introduction video for my website that people watch?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open with who you help and the problem they're stuck on, said in their words, not with "Hi, I'm Sam and I've always been passionate about…". Then show briefly how working with you goes, add one or two credentials that matter to that client, and finish with one next step, such as booking a free call. Keep the main version to about 60 to 90 seconds. People watching a coach's video are asking "do they get me, and can I trust them?", and they decide in the first few seconds whether it's worth finding out.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia's 2026 data reports that videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate and that most viewers leave around the halfway point.
  • Nielsen Norman Group's research on video found people dislike videos with "lengthy introductions" that "don't get to the point quickly enough".
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "humanize the story" and "keep messaging and language focused and simple".
  • The client is the hero of the video; you are the guide.
  • Don't promise outcomes you can't guarantee; show your process instead.

A 75-second structure

TimeSectionWhat to say
0 to 8 sWho it's forThe client and their situation, in their words
8 to 20 sThe problemWhat it costs them now: stress, time, missed chances
20 to 40 sHow you workWhat a session or programme looks like, concretely
40 to 55 sWhy youOne relevant credential or experience
55 to 70 sWhat to expectWhat clients typically work on, honestly framed
70 to 75 sNext stepOne call to action

Naming the client's exact situation in the first seconds does the hook's job: the right people lean in, and the wrong ones leave early, which is fine.

Filming and editing

  1. Talk to one person. Imagine a specific past client and speak to them.
  2. Use a script outline, not a script. Bullet points keep it natural; record several takes and keep the best lines.
  3. Good audio first. A clip-on mic in a quiet room matters more than the camera.
  4. Natural light from the front or side, a tidy real background.
  5. Cut every pause and repeated sentence. Jump cuts are fine; add a little B-roll of you working with a client (with their consent) or writing notes to cover them.
  6. Caption it. Many visitors browse with sound off.
  7. Show the length near the player or in the thumbnail; NN/g recommends making duration visible before people press play.

Stay honest

Coaching videos often slide into promises. Avoid guaranteed outcomes ("double your income", "cure your anxiety"), unverifiable before-and-after claims and client stories you don't have permission to share. If you use a testimonial, it must be genuine and shared with consent. Describe what you do and what clients typically work on; let your process build trust.

Worked example

Example: a career coach for new managers.

  • 0 to 8 s: "You got the promotion. Now you're managing people who were your friends last month, and it's awkward."
  • 8 to 20 s: "Most new managers either avoid hard conversations or overcorrect and become someone they don't like."
  • 20 to 40 s: "We meet weekly for eight weeks. Each session we take one real situation from your week, a one-to-one, a missed deadline, a difficult email, and plan exactly what you'll say."
  • 40 to 55 s: "I managed teams in retail operations for twelve years before I started coaching."
  • 55 to 75 s: "If that sounds useful, book a free 20-minute call below."

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to open a talking-head video?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCDs guidance is free.

How long should a coach introduction video be?

About 60 to 90 seconds for the website version, with a shorter cut for social.

Should I tell my life story?

Only the part that explains why you help this kind of client, in a sentence or two.

Can I include client results?

Only genuine ones, shared with permission, and without implying guaranteed outcomes.

Do I need professional filming?

No. A phone, good light and a clip-on mic are enough for most coaches.
