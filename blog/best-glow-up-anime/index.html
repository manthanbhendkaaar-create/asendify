What are the best glow up anime?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The best-known glow-up anime are Lookism, where a bullied student wakes up in a new body, Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki, where a gamer learns to "win" at real life, Kiss Him, Not Me, and the older classic The Wallflower. For a series built around practical, step-by-step glow-up habits, ASCEND is a free original anime by Asendify on YouTube and anime.asendify.co.

Key takeaways

  • "Glow-up anime" covers two kinds of story: sudden transformations and slow, earned self-improvement.
  • Lookism uses a body-swap premise to explore how differently people are treated by looks.
  • Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki is the most practical: the hero learns social skills step by step.
  • Some glow-up stories show unhealthy shortcuts, such as starving. Enjoy the story, not the method.
  • If you want habits you can copy, pick shows where the change comes from practice.

Lookism

Netflix's Lookism is an animated series based on the Korean webtoon and made by Studio Mir. Netflix describes it as the story of "A high school student [who] awakes one morning to discover that he now has a handsome face and a perfect body", and says it explores appearance-based prejudice and materialism. It is less a how-to and more a story about how unfairly people can be treated based on looks.

Why people like it: the contrast between how the same person is treated in two bodies makes the glow-up theme hard to ignore.

Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki

This is the pick for viewers who want a glow-up that feels earned. A review on Anime Feminist describes Tomozaki as "one of Japan's best gamers" who "can't seem to beat the game of 'real life'", until a skilled classmate, Aoi Hinami, offers to teach him how to win at it. Her lessons focus on behaviour and social skills, and the reviewer notes that she even "taught him how to smile."

Why people like it: it treats self-improvement as a set of small skills you can practise, like levelling up in a game.

Kiss Him, Not Me

In this comedy, Kae Serinuma's look changes dramatically, and four popular boys suddenly notice her, though she would rather imagine them paired with each other. It is fun, but the premise is worth a warning. In its review of the first episodes, Anime News Network says "The way Kae sheds her excess pounds is unhealthy to the extreme", because she stops eating for a week. The reviewer still gave the show a B.

In real life, the NHS advises against this. Its weight loss tips say "do not skip meals" and suggest aiming to lose 0.5 to 1kg a week. This is general information, not medical advice; see a GP if you are worried about your weight or eating.

The Wallflower

The 2006 anime of Yamato Nadeshiko Shichihenge is a classic makeover comedy. Its LiveChart listing explains the setup: four boys rent a house for free on the condition that they turn the girl who lives there, Sunako, into a "lady". She was once called ugly by a boy she liked, and now avoids anything "beautiful". The 25 episodes play the makeover for laughs, and Sunako resists it at every turn, which is part of the charm.

How to watch glow-up anime in a helpful way

  • Copy behaviours, not bodies. Pick a habit, not a look.
  • Ignore shortcuts. Starving, extreme training or instant fixes are story devices.
  • Watch one or two episodes, then act. Motivation fades fast if you only binge.
  • Be kind to yourself. Real glow-ups are slow and uneven.

If you are struggling with how you feel about your appearance for more than a couple of weeks, talking to a GP is a better first step than any show. The NHS lists where to get urgent help for mental health in the UK.

FAQ

What is a glow-up anime?

An anime where a character noticeably improves their looks, confidence or social life. Some show sudden transformations; others show slow, practised change.

Is Lookism a good glow-up anime?

It is popular and thought-provoking, but its glow-up is a sudden body swap rather than self-improvement. It works best as a story about how looks affect the way people are treated.

Which glow-up anime is the most realistic?

Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki is often the most practical, because the hero improves through coaching and practice. ASCEND also focuses on one real technique per episode.

Is Kiss Him, Not Me a healthy glow-up story?

Not in how the weight loss happens. Anime News Network called it "unhealthy to the extreme". Enjoy it as comedy and follow NHS advice for real weight loss.
