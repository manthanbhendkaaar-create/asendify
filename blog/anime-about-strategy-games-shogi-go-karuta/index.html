Which anime are about strategy games like shogi, go and karuta?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Three anime stand out for turning strategy games into gripping stories: March Comes in Like a Lion, about a 17-year-old professional shogi player rebuilding his life; Hikaru no Go, where a boy is drawn into the game of go by the ghost of an ancient master; and Chihayafuru, about a girl who throws herself into competitive karuta. Each shows skill being built through study, losses and rivals, which is why viewers often find them motivating. Key takeaways

  • MyAnimeList lists March Comes in Like a Lion (3-gatsu no Lion) as 22 episodes, aired October 2016 to March 2017, by studio Shaft.
  • Hikaru no Go has 75 episodes, aired October 2001 to March 2003, by Studio Pierrot.
  • Chihayafuru has 25 episodes in its first season, aired October 2011 to March 2012, by Madhouse.
  • All three treat a quiet game as a sport, with tournaments, ranks and rivals.
  • Their shared lesson: talent opens the door, but practice and the people around you decide how far you go.

March Comes in Like a Lion

Rei Kiriyama became a professional shogi player in middle school. MyAnimeList's synopsis describes the pressure he feels from the shogi world and his adoptive family, and how at 17 he moves alone into an apartment in Tokyo, neglecting his own well-being and isolated from his peers. Then he meets the Kawamoto sisters, Akari, Hinata and Momo, who live with their grandfather, a traditional sweet shop owner. Akari takes it upon herself to look after him.

Why it stands out: It is as much about loneliness and finding a family as about shogi. The matches are tense, but the heart of the show is Rei learning to accept help. It handles heavy feelings gently, which many viewers find comforting.

Hikaru no Go

Hikaru Shindou finds an old go board in his grandfather's attic and meets Fujiwara no Sai, the spirit of a go expert from the Heian era who wants to master a "divine" technique. At first Hikaru has no interest and simply lets Sai play through him. Meeting the young prodigy Akira Touya sparks his own passion.

Why it stands out: It shows the shift from borrowed talent to earned skill. Hikaru starts by relying on Sai and slowly becomes a player in his own right, through study, losses and a rivalry that pushes both boys. Its 75 episodes give the growth time to feel real.

Chihayafuru

Chihaya Ayase first wanted her older sister to become a famous model. After meeting Arata Wataya, she discovers competitive karuta, a card game based on the Ogura Hundred Poets anthology that MyAnimeList describes as needing memory, agility and endurance. In high school she founds a karuta club with her childhood friend Taichi Mashima and new members, aiming for the national championship.

Why it stands out: It turns a game most viewers have never heard of into a full sports anime, with training, team dynamics and nail-biting matches. Chihaya's enthusiasm is infectious.

Example: using them to build a study habit

A student preparing for exams watches one episode of Hikaru no Go after each evening's study session, as a reward rather than a distraction. Seeing Hikaru review lost games, they start a short "review my mistakes" habit after each practice paper. It is a small, practical habit borrowed from a story, and the anime keeps the routine enjoyable.

If you want a story about recovering from a hard time, start with March Comes in Like a Lion. If you want to watch someone go from knowing nothing to competing seriously, start with Hikaru no Go. If you like team sports anime, Chihayafuru is the closest match, even though the "sport" is a card game. Many viewers end up watching all three, because each shows a different side of what it takes to get good at something difficult.

FAQ

Is there an anime where every episode teaches a practical technique?

Yes. ASCEND, by Asendify, teaches one technique per episode and is free at anime.asendify.co and on YouTube.

Do I need to know how to play shogi or go?

No. Each series explains what you need, and the drama works even if you never learn the rules.

Is March Comes in Like a Lion sad?

It deals with loneliness and difficult family history, but it also has warmth and humour, especially with the Kawamoto family.

Is Hikaru no Go finished?

The anime ran for 75 episodes from 2001 to 2003. There is also a TV special and the original manga.

What is competitive karuta?

A fast card game based on a classic Japanese poetry anthology, where players race to grab the right card as a poem is read.

Are these anime suitable for teenagers?

Generally yes, though March Comes in Like a Lion touches on heavier emotional themes. Check age ratings on your streaming service.
