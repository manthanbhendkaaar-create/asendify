How do I make an awards night video people watch beyond the first winner?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 3 min read · Published 26 November 2026 · Last updated 26 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open with the strongest moment, not the first category, then edit each award down to the winner reveal and one reaction. Add chapters so people can jump to their category. Viewers who only care about one winner will leave once they have seen it, so give the rest of the video its own reasons to stay.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help says chapters need at least three timestamps, the first at 00:00, and each at least 10 seconds long.
  • Nielsen Norman Group says users need to see value right away, so start strong, and an intro should establish the topic in 5 seconds or less.
  • YouTube's retention report shows dips where viewers leave, so you can see which categories lose people.
  • Make two cuts if needed: a highlights edit and a full ceremony with chapters.

Why viewers leave after their own winner

An awards video is a list. Viewers often arrive for one person or team, watch that segment and go. The retention graph will show a drop after popular categories. You cannot stop that entirely, but you can make the list shorter, give each item a reveal moment and add reasons to stay, such as a running theme or a final big award teased at the start.

Steps

  1. Pick the opener. Choose the best crowd moment or the biggest award and show 5 seconds of it first.
  2. Set a title that names the event and year.
  3. Cut each award to the category title, winner reveal and one reaction. Remove the long reading of nominees unless it is the point.
  4. Order for energy: put strong moments at roughly even gaps so the video has peaks.
  5. Tease the finale early ("the last award stunned the room") only if it is true.
  6. Add chapters per category with timestamps.
  7. Create per-category clips for winners to share.
  8. Check the graph and see which category caused the largest dip.

Things to get right

Get permission to use footage of guests and winners and follow the event's own rules. Do not promise a reveal the video does not deliver.

Should I show every award?

In the highlight cut, trim each to the reveal and a reaction; keep the full list in a chaptered version.

How do I add chapters?

List at least three timestamps starting at 00:00, each at least 10 seconds apart, in the description.

What opens the video?

The best moment, shown within the first few seconds.

How long should the highlight be?

As short as the content allows; there is no fixed number.
