Is crypto trading regulated in the UK?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Mostly not. The FCA describes crypto as "largely unregulated in the UK", says investors should be prepared to lose all their money, and warns it is highly unlikely the Financial Services Compensation Scheme would cover losses. Since 6 January 2021, firms cannot sell crypto CFDs, futures or options to retail customers. Crypto marketing is regulated, though, and HMRC taxes gains when you sell, swap, spend or give away tokens.

This page is education only, not financial or tax advice. Cryptoassets are high risk and you could lose everything you put in.

Key takeaways

  • FCA: crypto is "largely unregulated in the UK", so there is no safety net if things go wrong.
  • Bitcoin fell 30.44% from its October 2025 peak to 1 December 2025, and Ether 42.49% from its August 2025 peak, according to the FCA.
  • Crypto CFDs, futures and options have been banned for UK retail customers since 6 January 2021; firms still offering them to retail are likely scams.
  • Genuine crypto adverts must carry prominent risk warnings and should not offer free gifts or refer-a-friend bonuses.
  • Capital Gains Tax can apply when you sell, swap, spend or give away crypto, and exchange reports are not tax calculations.

The FCA's consumer guide Crypto: the basics (updated January 2026) is unusually blunt:

It adds that getting money back into a bank account can be difficult, that firms may not keep customer assets properly separated, and that scammers actively target crypto investors.

The retail ban on crypto derivatives

The FCA's press release announcing that it banned the sale of crypto derivatives to retail consumers explains the rule. From 6 January 2021, firms acting in or from the UK cannot sell, market or distribute to retail consumers CFDs, options, futures or exchange traded notes that reference unregulated transferable cryptoassets such as Bitcoin or Ether.

The FCA's reasons: retail investors cannot reliably value these products, the underlying markets have significant market abuse and financial crime, prices are extremely volatile, and there is no legitimate investment need for retail customers. It estimated the ban would save retail consumers around £53 million, and said any firm still offering these products to retail after the ban is likely to be a scam. The 2021 ban also covered crypto exchange traded notes; the FCA has since reviewed its approach to ETNs, so check its current position before relying on either.

Tax on crypto trading

HMRC's guidance on paying tax when you sell cryptoassets says you may owe Capital Gains Tax when you dispose of crypto, which includes:

  • selling it for money
  • exchanging one token for another
  • using it to pay for goods or services
  • giving it away (except to a spouse, civil partner or charity)

Each token type goes into a "pool" with an average cost, with special rules for tokens bought on the same day or within 30 days of a sale. Allowable costs include transaction fees. HMRC stresses that exchange reports are not tax calculations and do not track pooled costs, so you must keep your own records: token type, dates, amounts, sterling values and pooled costs. Our page on how trading profits are taxed in the UK covers other markets, and what trading records to keep for HMRC covers record keeping.

Worked example: swaps count

Example (illustrative): Leah buys 1 ETH for £2,000. Later she swaps it for a different token worth £3,200 at the time, and then sells that token for £2,500.

EventDisposal?Gain or loss
Buys 1 ETH for £2,000Non/a
Swaps ETH for token worth £3,200Yes, exchange of crypto£3,200 - £2,000 = £1,200 gain
Sells token for £2,500Yes£2,500 - £3,200 = £700 loss
Net for the year (before fees)£500 net gain

Leah may think she made only £500 in cash terms, which is right overall, but the swap was a disposal in its own right, and the loss on the second trade needs to be reported to be used. Whether tax is due depends on her total gains for the year and the allowance; logging every swap with its sterling value is the only way to get this right.

Safer habits if you trade crypto

  • Only use money you can afford to lose, as the FCA advises.
  • Be wary of leverage offers to UK retail customers; regulated firms cannot sell crypto derivatives to you.
  • Check firms on the FCA Firm Checker and Warning List; see are trading signal groups and finfluencers legal.
  • Record every trade and swap in pounds at the time it happens.

Is my crypto protected if an exchange goes bust?

The FCA says it is highly unlikely you would be covered by the FSCS.

Can I trade crypto CFDs in the UK?

Not as a retail customer. The FCA banned crypto derivatives for retail from 6 January 2021.

Do I pay tax when I swap one crypto for another?

Yes, a swap is a disposal and can create a capital gain or loss.

Are crypto adverts regulated?

Yes. Genuine adverts must carry prominent risk warnings and should not offer free gifts or refer-a-friend bonuses.

Are stablecoins safe?

The FCA warns they are not guaranteed; some lack backing assets, and TerraUSD collapsed in 2022.
