How long before bed should I avoid pre-workout?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A standard caffeinated pre-workout should be taken at least 13.2 hours before bed to avoid losing sleep, according to a 2023 meta-analysis that used a serving with about 217 mg of caffeine. For an 11 pm bedtime, that means by about 9:45 am. If you train in the evening, a caffeine-free (stim-free) pre-workout is the safer choice for sleep.

Key takeaways

  • Pre-workouts often contain about twice the caffeine of a standard coffee, so they need a much earlier cut-off.
  • The meta-analysis put the cut-off at 8.8 hours for a coffee with about 107 mg and 13.2 hours for a pre-workout with about 217 mg.
  • Evening training plus caffeinated pre-workout is one of the most common reasons for poor sleep in people who exercise.
  • Stay within 200 mg per dose and 400 mg per day from all sources, the levels EFSA treats as safe for healthy adults.
  • Check the label: caffeine per scoop varies a lot between products.

This page is general information, not medical advice. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, anxiety or sleep problems, or take medication, see a doctor or GP before using pre-workout.

Where the 13.2 hour figure comes from

Gardiner and colleagues pooled 24 studies of caffeine and sleep for Sleep Medicine Reviews. Across those studies, caffeine reduced total sleep time by about 45 minutes, cut deep sleep by about 11 minutes and added about 9 minutes to the time it took to fall asleep (Gardiner et al., 2023).

From that data they worked out how far before bed caffeine needs to be taken to avoid reducing total sleep time:

DrinkCaffeineTake it at least this long before bed
Coffee, 250 mlabout 107 mg8.8 hours
Standard pre-workout serveabout 217.5 mg13.2 hours

The bigger the dose, the longer it takes to fall to a level that does not disturb sleep. That is why a pre-workout needs about four and a half more hours than a coffee.

Times are rounded. For most people who train after work, a full caffeinated pre-workout is simply too late in the day.

Why pre-workout hits sleep harder than coffee

  1. Higher dose. Many pre-workouts contain 150 to 300 mg of caffeine per scoop, and some more. A standard serve in the meta-analysis had 217.5 mg.
  2. Taken later. People usually take it right before training, which for many is 5 pm to 8 pm.
  3. Fast to drink. A shot of pre-workout delivers the whole dose in seconds, unlike a coffee sipped over half an hour.
  4. Stacking. Some people have coffee in the morning, an energy drink after lunch and pre-workout in the evening. The total can pass 400 mg.

Caffeine also clears slowly in many people. The International Society of Sports Nutrition reports an average half-life of 4 to 6 hours, with a range of 1.5 to 10 hours in adults.

How much caffeine you actually need to train well

The ISSN position stand says caffeine consistently improves exercise performance at 3 to 6 mg per kg of body weight, most often taken about 60 minutes before exercise. It also notes that lower doses can help.

Food safety guidance sets a ceiling. The European Food Safety Authority says single doses up to 200 mg and up to 400 mg a day do not raise safety concerns for healthy adults. It also notes that even 100 mg close to bedtime may affect sleep in some adults.

So a 70 kg person at 3 mg per kg would need about 210 mg, right at the single-dose guidance. Many people do well on less, especially if they are not heavy caffeine users. Our guide to how much caffeine to take before a workout goes into dosing in more detail.

Example: the 7 pm gym session

Example: Dan, 80 kg, goes to bed at 11 pm and trains at 7 pm. He takes one scoop of a pre-workout with 220 mg of caffeine at 6:30 pm.

That is about 4.5 hours before bed, far short of the 13.2 hour guide. Using simple half-life maths with an average 5 hour half-life, about 120 mg of the dose would still be in his body at 11 pm. That is more than a whole coffee, at bedtime. It is no surprise he lies awake until 1 am.

Safety notes

  • Read the label. Check the caffeine per scoop and do not double-scoop.
  • Never use caffeine powders. The US Food and Drug Administration warns that pure and highly concentrated caffeine products can cause serious harm, including death.
  • Count all sources. Coffee, energy drinks, cola and some supplements all add up. Our guide on how much caffeine is in pre-workout covers label reading.
  • Under 18s should not use caffeinated pre-workout.

FAQ

Is it bad to take pre-workout at night?

A caffeinated pre-workout at night is likely to reduce sleep for many people. A stim-free product avoids the caffeine problem.

Can I take pre-workout 6 hours before bed?

For a standard serve with about 217 mg of caffeine, 6 hours is well short of the 13.2 hours the meta-analysis suggests. Expect it to affect your sleep.

What is a stim-free pre-workout?

A pre-workout without caffeine or other stimulants. Check the label, as some products marketed as low-stim still contain caffeine.

Does a half scoop of pre-workout still affect sleep?

It can. Half a standard scoop is still about the same caffeine as a coffee, and the meta-analysis put the cut-off for a coffee at 8.8 hours before bed.

Is caffeine worth it for evening training?

Often not, if it costs you sleep. Poor sleep can hurt the next day's training and recovery more than the caffeine helped the session.
