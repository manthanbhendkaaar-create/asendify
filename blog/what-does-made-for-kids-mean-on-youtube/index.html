What does "made for kids" mean on YouTube, and what do you lose if you set it?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

"Made for kids" is an audience setting every YouTube creator must choose for their channel or each video, introduced after the US Federal Trade Commission's 2019 children's privacy settlement with Google. If a video is made for kids, YouTube switches off features that rely on personal data, including comments, notifications, end screens, the miniplayer and personalised ads. Set it honestly based on who the video is for: getting it wrong can bring consequences on YouTube and under the law.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's help page on deciding if content is made for kids says: "Regardless of your location, we require you to tell us whether or not your videos are made for kids."
  • The FTC's September 2019 settlement had Google and YouTube pay $170 million ($136 million to the FTC, $34 million to New York) and build a system "that permits channel owners to identify their child-directed content."
  • YouTube's list of features affected by COPPA includes comments, notifications, cards or end screens, channel memberships, Super Chat, the miniplayer, "Save to playlist" and personalised advertising.
  • YouTube's factors include subject matter, whether children are the intended audience, child actors, characters or toys that appeal to children, simple songs or games, and whether the content is advertised to children.
  • A video featuring a child or using animation is not automatically made for kids; the test is who it is aimed at.

At channel level, YouTube says made-for-kids channels also lose posts, memberships, notifications and comments. For a channel that grows through community and end screens, that is a big change, which is why the setting should match the real audience rather than be chosen for convenience.

How to decide

YouTube lists these factors. Ask each as a question about your video:

YouTube factorQuestion to askLeans "made for kids" if...
Subject matterIs it, for example, educational content for preschoolers?The topic is aimed at young children
Intended audienceDo my title, description and tags target children?Yes
Child actors or modelsAre children featured as the main focus?Yes, and the video is for children
Characters, celebrities, toysDoes it centre on things that appeal to children?Yes
LanguageIs it pitched so young children can understand?Yes
ActivitiesPlay-acting, simple songs or games?Yes
Songs, stories, poemsNursery rhymes or children's stories?Yes
AdvertisingIs the content promoted to children?Yes

A gaming video about a game adults also play, a parenting vlog aimed at parents, or an animated explainer for adults is usually not made for kids, even if children might watch. A nursery-rhyme compilation, toy-unboxing for young children or a cartoon for preschoolers usually is.

If you are unsure, YouTube also notes legal obligations under COPPA and other local laws; for real uncertainty, get legal advice rather than guessing.

FAQ

Is there an agency that edits YouTube videos for retention for established channels?

Yes. Asendify does retention editing for established channels and is currently full and invite-only. Freelance editors and other agencies are alternatives.

Do I have to set made for kids if I'm not in the US?

Yes. YouTube requires every creator, regardless of location, to say whether their videos are made for kids.

Can made-for-kids videos earn money?

They can still show ads, but personalised advertising is off, and features such as memberships and Super Chat are unavailable.

Does having children in my video make it made for kids?

Not on its own. The question is whether the video is aimed at children.

Can I change the setting later?

Yes, in YouTube Studio for the channel or each video. Set it based on the content's real audience.

Why did the setting come in?

After the FTC's 2019 COPPA settlement, YouTube had to build a way for channel owners to identify child-directed content.
