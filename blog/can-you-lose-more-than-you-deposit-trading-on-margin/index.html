Can you lose more than you deposit when trading on margin?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

It depends on the account. In a US-style margin account you borrow from your broker, and FINRA warns you can lose more than your original investment. UK retail clients trading CFDs or spread bets have negative balance protection, so the FCA's rules mean they cannot lose more than the money in their account. That protection disappears if you agree to be treated as a professional client.

This page is education only, not financial advice. Leveraged trading is high risk and you can lose money quickly.

Key takeaways

  • FINRA: margin trading "can mean losing more than your original investment", and a US margin account needs at least $2,000 equity.
  • FINRA margin requirements apply during the trading day, and a shortfall must be met "as promptly as possible" by adding funds or selling positions.
  • FCA rules for UK retail CFDs: leverage capped between 30:1 and 2:1, positions closed out at 50% of required margin, and negative balance protection.
  • The FCA says retail protections stop nearly 400,000 people a year from risking more than their original stake.
  • In October 2025 the FCA warned firms not to push retail clients into "elective professional" status, which removes these protections.

How a US margin account can go negative

FINRA's page on day trading and margin (June 2026) explains that you must keep at least $2,000 equity in a margin account, and firms can set higher "house" requirements. Requirements are calculated on positions held during the day, not just at the close, and a deficit must be met "as promptly as possible". Repeated deficits can lead a firm to freeze margin trading for 90 days.

Because you borrowed money, a sharp fall can wipe out your equity and leave you owing the broker. FINRA states plainly that margin trading can mean losing more than your original investment, and that frequent trading on margin is generally not appropriate for people with limited resources, limited experience or low risk tolerance: "never fund this type of trading with essential assets".

The UK retail protections

The FCA's page on rules for contracts for difference sets out what firms must do for retail clients, under rules published in July 2019:

  • limit leverage to between 30:1 and 2:1, depending on the underlying asset
  • close out a client's position when their funds fall to 50% of the margin needed
  • guarantee that a client cannot lose more than the total funds in their account
  • stop offering cash or other incentives to trade
  • show a standardised warning with the percentage of the firm's retail accounts that lose money

Our page on why most CFD traders lose money covers what those loss percentages show.

The "professional client" trap

In its October 2025 press release, the FCA warned that CFD investors risk losing out on protections. It was concerned that firms use high-pressure tactics to get people to opt up to "elective professional" status, which removes retail protections such as leverage limits and negative balance protection. It said these protections prevent nearly 400,000 people a year from risking more than their original stake, and that "firms must not push elective professional or redirection promotions onto their retail clients". It also noted that over 90,000 people lost around £75 million over four years at one firm linked to finfluencer promotions.

If a firm offers you higher leverage in exchange for professional status, that is the trade-off: more leverage, and the possibility of owing more than you put in.

Worked example: the same fall, two accounts

Example (illustrative): Two traders each put in £5,000 and take a position worth £50,000 (10:1 leverage). The market falls 15% overnight.

In practice, the UK retail account would usually have been closed out before reaching zero, at 50% of required margin, unless the price gapped past that level. The bigger lesson is position size: at 10:1, a 10% move wipes out the whole deposit. Sizing each trade so that a stop-out costs a small, fixed share of the account, as in our guide to stop-loss orders, matters more than which protection you have. The free position size calculator helps with that step.

Before using leverage

  • Check your client status. Stay a retail client unless you genuinely understand what you give up.
  • Use leverage you choose, not leverage offered. 30:1 is a maximum, not a target.
  • Know the close-out level and how your firm calculates it.
  • Write your risk rules down in a plan; see how to build a trading plan.

Can I lose more than I deposit with CFDs in the UK?

Not as a retail client. FCA rules require negative balance protection, so you cannot lose more than the funds in your account.

Can I lose more than I invest on a margin account?

Yes. FINRA warns that margin trading can mean losing more than your original investment.

What is the margin close-out rule?

UK firms must close a retail client's CFD positions when their funds fall to 50% of the margin needed to keep them open.

What happens if I become a professional client?

You lose retail protections such as leverage limits and negative balance protection. The FCA has warned firms not to push clients into this.

What is the maximum leverage for UK retail CFD traders?

Between 30:1 and 2:1, depending on the underlying asset.
