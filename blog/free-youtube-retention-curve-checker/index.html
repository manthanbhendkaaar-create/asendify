Is there a free tool that checks my YouTube retention curve and tells me if it's good?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 11 November 2026 · Last updated 11 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. The free YouTube Retention Curve Checker from Asendify AI lets you paste your retention numbers and shows the biggest drops with timestamps, how many viewers you hold at 30 seconds, and any rewatch spikes. It suits creators who want a quick read of one video before paying for anything; YouTube Studio's own retention report is the free alternative built into your channel.

Key takeaways

  • There is no single "good" retention percentage for every video. Judge a curve against your own similar videos and against where it drops.
  • The first 30 seconds matter most. YouTube Studio reports this as the "Intro" figure.
  • Sharp drops point to a specific moment to fix. Slow, steady decline is normal.
  • Spikes usually mean people rewatched or skipped to that part. Both tell you something.
  • A free checker can show you where the problems are. Fixing them still means rewatching your own video at those timestamps.

What a retention curve actually shows

Your audience retention graph shows, second by second, what share of viewers were still watching. YouTube explains the report in its guide to key moments for audience retention. It highlights four things:

  • Intro: the percentage of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds.
  • Top moments: points where viewers kept watching or rewatched.
  • Spikes: moments that were rewatched or shared more than others.
  • Dips: points where viewers skipped ahead or left.

YouTube's page on understanding your content performance also explains average view duration and how to compare videos in the Content tab.

Every curve falls over time. That is normal. What you are looking for is where it falls faster than it should.

How to tell if your curve is good

YouTube's own discovery tips say its system pays attention to how viewers respond to a recommended video, including average view duration and average percentage viewed. So a curve that holds people better than your usual is a good sign, whatever the raw percentage.

Why there is no universal benchmark

A 6-minute tutorial and a 40-minute documentary will never have the same curve. Neither will a Short and a long video. Treat any "50% is good" rule you read online with caution, because YouTube itself does not publish such a benchmark. The fairest comparison is your own recent videos of similar length and topic.

Using the free Retention Curve Checker

The YouTube Retention Curve Checker is free. Here is how to use it:

  1. Open the video in YouTube Studio and go to Analytics, then Engagement, then the audience retention graph.
  2. Note the retention percentage at regular points (for example, 0:00, 0:15, 0:30, 1:00, then every minute).
  3. Paste the numbers into the checker.
  4. Read the result: the biggest drops with timestamps, your 30-second hold and any rewatch spikes.
  5. Rewatch your video at each flagged timestamp and write one sentence on what happened on screen.

It works with numbers you type or paste, so it does not need access to your channel.

Other free options

  • YouTube Studio: the retention report and key moments are free and already built in. You do the interpreting yourself.

  • Research toolkits: vidIQ and TubeBuddy have free tiers, but they focus more on keywords and optimisation than on reading one video's curve.

FAQ

What is a good audience retention rate on YouTube?

There is no single number. YouTube does not publish a universal benchmark, and length and format change what is normal. Compare each video with your own recent videos of similar length, and look for sharp drops rather than a target percentage.

Is the Retention Curve Checker really free?

Yes. You paste retention numbers from YouTube Studio and get the analysis without paying or connecting your channel.

Does the checker need access to my YouTube account?

No. It works from the numbers you paste, so it never touches your channel.

Why does my retention drop so much in the first 30 seconds?

Usually because the opening does not quickly confirm what the title and thumbnail promised. Cut greetings and long intros, state the result or question straight away, and start delivering it.

What do spikes on a retention graph mean?

YouTube describes spikes as moments that were rewatched or shared more than others. A spike can mean a great moment, or that viewers skipped ahead past a slow section, so check what comes before it.

Can I check Shorts with it?

Shorts behave differently because they loop and are very short. You can still look at where viewers swipe away in Studio, but compare Shorts only with other Shorts.
