How should a short-form video editor use retention graphs in a portfolio case study?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use retention graphs in a case study when you have the client's permission, a fair before-and-after comparison and a clear story of what you changed. Show the whole graph, not a cropped peak; pair it with the Shorts "stayed to watch" figure and the edit decisions that explain the difference. One honest, well-explained result wins more clients than a wall of screenshots with big percentages.

Key takeaways

  • For Shorts, YouTube's key early number is stayed to watch: "the percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short". It is the clearest single figure for an editor's hook work.
  • YouTube Studio lets channels compare against typical retention for their 10 latest videos of similar length, which gives you a fair baseline instead of a cherry-picked one.
  • A case study on your site or social profile is marketing. The ASA says the CAP Code applies to a company's own website and channels it controls, and that marketers must hold documentary evidence to prove claims.
  • Get written permission before you show a client's analytics, channel name or footage.
  • Explain the edit, not just the number. Prospects hire the thinking.

How to build it

  1. Ask first. Agree in writing what you can show: graphs, channel name, clips, numbers. Many clients prefer an anonymised version.
  2. Pick a fair window. Ten Shorts before and ten after is better than one viral hit. Use the same length band where you can.
  3. Screenshot the full graph. Include the axis and the date range. A cropped spike looks like hiding something.
  4. Annotate the moments. Mark where you changed the hook, added a pattern interrupt or cut a slow beat. Link the dips and rises to edit decisions.
  5. Show one clip side by side. The old opening next to your opening, three seconds each. This does more than any chart.
  6. State what else changed. New topic, more uploads, a trend. If you can't separate your effect, say so.
  7. Keep the evidence. Save the original exports with dates, so you can back up the claim if anyone asks.

Example: an editor's cooking-channel case study

Example: an editor took over a cooking channel's Shorts. Before: the 10 previous Shorts averaged 58% stayed to watch, and most retention graphs fell sharply in the first two seconds, while the presenter said hello and introduced the dish. The editor's change: open on the finished dish being cut, caption the dish name in the first frame, move the greeting to the end. After: 71% across the next 10 Shorts of similar length. The case study shows both graphs uncropped, a 3-second side-by-side of the old and new openings, and a note that posting frequency stayed the same but two of the new Shorts used a trending format. The client approved the text and asked for the channel name to be left out. Figures are illustrative.

Where to put it

  • Your portfolio page: one or two deep case studies beat ten thin ones.
  • Proposals: the matching case study for the prospect's niche.
  • LinkedIn or X: the side-by-side clip plus one line on what changed, with the full write-up linked.

FAQ

Is there a book that helps editors explain their retention decisions?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook includes a tracker and spreadsheet for logging edits against results. YouTube Studio's reports are the free alternative.

Can I share a client's YouTube analytics in my portfolio?

Only with their permission. Agree in writing what you can show, and anonymise if they prefer.

Which number should a Shorts case study lead with?

Stayed to watch, because it reflects the hook, plus the full retention graph for context.

How many videos should I compare?

Around ten before and ten after, of similar length, is fairer than a single video.

Do advertising rules apply to my portfolio claims?

In the UK, the CAP Code can apply to marketing on your own site and social profiles, so keep evidence for any number you quote.
