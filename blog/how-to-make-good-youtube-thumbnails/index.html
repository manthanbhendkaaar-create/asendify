How do I make good YouTube thumbnails?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 7 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A good YouTube thumbnail shows one clear idea that still reads at phone size: a single focal point, strong contrast and only a few large words. It should work with the title rather than repeat it, promise only what the video delivers early, and be tested in YouTube Studio on watch time, not clicks alone.

Key takeaways

  • Design for a small screen first. If you cannot tell what the thumbnail is about at the size of a stamp, simplify it.
  • One subject, one idea. A face, an object or a result, not all three fighting for attention.
  • Use contrast and fewer words. Three or four large words usually read better than a sentence.
  • Pair it with the title. The thumbnail shows, the title tells, and together they make one promise.
  • Follow YouTube's rules and test. Custom thumbnails need a verified account, and Studio can compare up to three versions.

What YouTube says about thumbnails

YouTube's help page on adding custom thumbnails explains that you can pick an auto-generated option or upload your own if your account is verified. It recommends making the image as large as possible, because it is also used as the preview in the embedded player. For videos it suggests:

RequirementYouTube's recommendation
Resolution3840 x 2160 pixels for videos (2160 x 3840 for Shorts)
Minimum sizeAt least 640 pixels wide for videos
Aspect ratio16:9 for videos, 9:16 for Shorts, 1:1 for podcast playlists
File formatImage formats such as JPG or PNG
File size2 MB when uploading from mobile; 50 MB from desktop
AccountMust be verified to upload custom thumbnails

Thumbnails also have to follow the rules. YouTube's thumbnails policy says thumbnails and other images that violate the Community Guidelines are not allowed, and its spam policy lists misleading thumbnails as part of "malicious clickbait".

Why thumbnails matter

Your thumbnail competes with every other video on the screen. YouTube's impressions and click-through rate FAQ says thumbnails are always competing with other videos on the home page, in "Up next", in search and even in subscription feeds. It also says half of all channels and videos have an impressions click-through rate between 2% and 10%, and that videos shown widely, such as on the home page, naturally get a lower rate.

So there is no single "good" number to chase. The useful question is whether a thumbnail does better than your own recent ones for similar videos.

Focal point

Decide what the viewer should see first. In a tutorial it might be the finished result. In a story video it might be your face reacting to the outcome. Crop tight so the subject fills a good share of the frame.

Readability

Most people browse YouTube on small screens. Shrink your thumbnail to the size it appears in a suggested list and look at it from arm's length. If the idea is not clear in a second, remove things until it is.

Contrast and colour

Bright subjects on dark backgrounds, or the other way round, stand out in a feed. Avoid placing important text over a busy part of the image. A simple outline or shadow behind the subject often helps it separate from the background.

Text

Text on a thumbnail should add something the title does not say. If the title is "I Tried Living on £15 of Food for a Week", the thumbnail text might be "DAY 7?" rather than repeating the full line. Keep it large and short.

Emotion and stakes

Faces showing a clear reaction can help, but they are not required. A clear before/after, an unexpected object or a striking result can do the same job. The aim is to show why the video is worth watching.

Consistency

Viewers who like your videos should be able to spot your next one. Keep fonts, colours or layout broadly consistent, while changing the main image each time.

Example: fixing a thumbnail that does not stand out

Example: Tom runs a 3,000-subscriber channel reviewing budget camping gear. His video on a cheap tent has a thumbnail showing the whole campsite at dusk, with the words "Budget Tent Review – Is It Any Good?" in small white text. His Studio shows a click-through rate of 2.1% for the video, lower than his recent uploads.

ChangeBeforeAfter
SubjectWhole campsite, tent small in the frameTent fills two-thirds of the frame
Text6 small words repeating the title"£30 TENT?" in large yellow letters

StoryNo reason to clickRain on the tent, Tom's face reacting inside

He tests the new version against the old one with YouTube's test and compare feature. The figures here are illustrative; the point is that every change makes the idea clearer at phone size and gives the viewer a reason to care.

How to test thumbnails

YouTube's A/B testing help page says creators can test and compare up to three titles and thumbnails. At the end, the option with the highest watch time is shown to all viewers; if there is no clear winner, the first option you uploaded is used. The feature runs in YouTube Studio on a computer, needs advanced features enabled, and does not work on Shorts.

Because the winner is chosen on watch time share, a thumbnail that gets lots of clicks from people who then leave will not win. That is a good thing: it rewards honest packaging.

Check the thumbnail against the opening

A thumbnail makes a promise. The opening of the video has to keep it. YouTube's audience retention help page says a high intro percentage can mean the first 30 seconds "matched the viewer's expectation of the video's thumbnail and title". If many viewers leave in the first 30 seconds, it suggests changing the thumbnail and title to better reflect the video, or changing the opening.

FAQ

What size should a YouTube thumbnail be?

YouTube recommends 3840 x 2160 pixels for videos, with a minimum width of 640 pixels and a 16:9 aspect ratio. Files can be up to 2 MB from mobile or 50 MB from desktop.

Do I need a face on my YouTube thumbnail?

No. Faces showing a clear reaction can help viewers connect, but a strong result, object or before/after image can work just as well. What matters is one clear idea.

How much text should a thumbnail have?

Usually very little. Two to four large words that add to the title tend to read better on phones than a full sentence or a copy of the title.

Why can't I upload a custom thumbnail?

YouTube requires your account to be verified before you can upload custom thumbnails. Until then, you can choose from the auto-generated options.

Can I change a thumbnail after the video is live?

Yes. You can change it at any time in YouTube Studio, or run a test with up to three versions to see which keeps the most watch time.
