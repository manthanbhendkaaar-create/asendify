What are YouTube cards, and how should you use them?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

YouTube cards are small clickable prompts you can add at any moment in a video to point viewers to another video, a playlist, a channel or, for Partner Program members, an external link. You can add up to five per video in YouTube Studio's editor. Unlike end screens, which only appear in the last 5-20 seconds, cards can appear wherever they're most relevant, so use them when you mention something viewers might want next.

Key takeaways

  • There are four card types: video, playlist, channel and link; link cards need the YouTube Partner Program.
  • YouTube Help: "You can add up to 5 cards to one video."
  • Viewers see a short teaser at the time you choose, or open cards from the card icon on the player.
  • "Cards aren't available on videos set as made for kids."
  • Cards launched in 2015 as the successor to annotations, partly because annotations didn't work on mobile.

The four card types

YouTube's help page on info cards lists:

Card typePoints toNotes
VideoA public YouTube videoGood for "I explain this in another video"
PlaylistA public playlistGood for series and "start here" lists
ChannelA YouTube channelMessage and teaser text are required
LinkAn external websiteOnly for YouTube Partner Program members; the site must follow YouTube's policies

How to add cards

YouTube Help gives the steps:

  1. Sign in to YouTube Studio.
  2. Select Content and click the video.
  3. Select Editor, then Info cards, and choose a card type.
  4. Set the start time.
  5. Add an optional custom message and teaser text (required for channel cards).
  6. Click Save.

Cards are shown below the video description, and viewers can scroll through them during playback. YouTube also notes that cards won't show on videos claimed through Content ID where the content owner has set up a campaign, and that videos with cards don't display a call-to-action overlay.

These end screen figures come from YouTube's end screens help page, which also says card teasers are suppressed while the end screen is showing. Our page on whether end screens hurt retention covers that side.

A short history

YouTube introduced cards in March 2015. Its launch announcement said cards would "eventually replace annotations" and that they "finally work on mobile", which annotations never did well. Annotations have since gone; cards and end screens are the interactive tools that remain.

How to use cards well

DoAvoid
Add a card the moment you mention a related videoStacking all five cards in the first minute
Use a playlist card for series ("Part 2 is in this playlist")Cards that pull viewers away before your main point
Write a short, specific teaser ("Full sourdough starter guide")Vague teasers like "Watch this!"
Point to your strongest related videoLinking to old videos you'd rather people didn't see
Check timing against your retention graphPlacing cards in the first seconds of the hook

A card is an invitation to leave this video for another one, so timing matters. Placing it right after you've delivered the promise of the current section, rather than during your hook, respects the viewer and your own watch time. Playlists make good card targets; see do YouTube playlists help your channel. Chapters help viewers navigate within a video; see how to add chapters.

Worked example: a fitness channel's cards

Example: Jas runs a home-workout channel. Her 12-minute "Beginner full-body workout" video mentions her stretching routine at 1:30 and her "30-day plan" playlist at 10:45.

TimeCardTeaser
1:30Video card: "10-minute stretching routine""My warm-up stretches"
6:00None (middle of the workout, viewers are busy)
10:45Playlist card: "30-day beginner plan""Day-by-day plan"
Last 20sEnd screen: next video + subscribeCard teasers are suppressed here anyway

She keeps two cards, both tied to what she's saying at that moment, instead of filling all five slots. Whether the cards get clicked is something she can check in YouTube Studio's analytics. The example is illustrative.

FAQ

Is there a free tool that roasts my YouTube channel's packaging and tells me what to fix?

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast scores your latest public uploads out of 100 and gives three fixes, with no sign-up. YouTube Studio analytics are a fair free alternative.

How many cards can a YouTube video have?

Up to five.

Can I add link cards to my website?

Only if you're in the YouTube Partner Program, and the site must follow YouTube's policies.

Do cards work on made for kids videos?

No. Cards aren't available on videos set as made for kids.

What's the difference between cards and end screens?

Cards can appear any time (up to five); end screens appear only in the last 5-20 seconds (up to four elements on 16:9 videos).

Do cards hurt watch time?

They invite viewers to leave, so place them after you've delivered on your current point, not during your hook.
