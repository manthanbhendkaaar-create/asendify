Can I use an AI avatar or AI voice in a client's video ad?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Yes, but the same rules apply as to any other ad, and some platforms add their own. In the UK there's no blanket legal duty to disclose AI in ads, but an AI avatar can't pose as a real customer or celebrity, an AI image can't exaggerate what a product does, and the advertiser stays responsible for whatever the tool produced. TikTok goes further: it rejects or restricts ads with significant AI content that isn't labelled. Label when in doubt, and never clone a real person without written permission.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Personality rights, copyright and data protection can also apply; take legal advice for cloned voices or likenesses.

Key takeaways

  • The ASA says "There is no blanket legal requirement in the UK to disclose the use of AI in ads", but "disclosure alone is very unlikely to mitigate the harm caused by a fundamentally misleading message".
  • The ASA gives the example that an AI image showing a cosmetic's effect that doesn't reflect real results is likely to be materially misleading.
  • In its 2026 deepfakes guidance, the ASA says "the advertiser remains responsible for any output used in their ads", even when automated tools made it.
  • TikTok's ad policy says undisclosed AI-generated content means "your ad will be rejected or restricted", and bans misusing a public figure's likeness without permission.
  • AI avatars can help with versions and languages; the retention work (hook, pacing, story) still has to be done by an editor.

Why AI doesn't do the retention work for you

AI avatars can produce many versions quickly, but a flat, synthetic delivery often loses viewers in the first seconds. The things that hold attention are still editorial choices:

  • The hook: a specific situation or result in the first two seconds.
  • Pacing: cuts, B-roll and on-screen text every few seconds.
  • Real proof: actual product footage, not generated imagery of results.
  • A human moment: many ads mix a short real clip with AI-assisted versions.

Use AI for the scalable parts (versions, languages, placeholder voiceovers) and keep humans for the parts viewers judge.

A pre-launch AI checklist for client ads

  1. List every AI element: voice, avatar, images, background, translation.
  2. Ask "would a viewer be misled without a label?" If yes, label it, or change the content.
  3. Check the platform's rules. TikTok needs AIGC labels for significant AI content; other platforms have their own settings.
  4. No AI results or demos. Show the real product working.
  5. No AI testimonials. Real customers only, with permission.
  6. Written permission for any real person's likeness or voice, including the client's own staff.
  7. Review for bias and stereotypes. The ASA says the advertiser is responsible for problematic output.
  8. Keep records of the tools, prompts and source footage used.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks that work even with AI presenters?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. The ASA's AI guidance is free.

Do I have to label AI in UK ads?

There's no blanket legal requirement, but you must not mislead. Platforms like TikTok require labels for significant AI content.

Can I use an AI voice for the voiceover?

Usually yes, if it's a narrator and doesn't imitate a real person without permission.

Can I make an AI testimonial?

That's high risk: it implies a real customer's experience. Use real customers with permission.

Who is responsible if an AI tool makes a misleading ad?

The advertiser. The ASA says automation doesn't transfer responsibility.
