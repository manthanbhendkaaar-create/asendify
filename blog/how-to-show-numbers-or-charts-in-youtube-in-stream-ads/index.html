How do I show numbers or charts in a YouTube in-stream ad without losing attention?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Show one number at a time, large, on screen for long enough to read and said out loud as it appears, then move on. Lead with the single most striking figure inside the first five seconds, before the skip button appears, and keep any chart to one simple comparison with a clear label. In the UK every number in an ad must be backed by evidence you hold before the ad runs.

Key takeaways

  • On skippable in-stream ads, viewers can skip after five seconds, and with CPV bidding you pay when someone watches 30 seconds (or the whole ad if shorter) or interacts. A number has to earn its place in those first seconds.
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to reinforce your message with audio and text and avoid competing elements. Say the number and show it.
  • ASA guidance: marketers must hold documentary evidence to prove claims that consumers would see as objective. Every percentage, price and "x times faster" counts.
  • Readability: WCAG asks for text contrast of at least 4.5:1, or 3:1 for large text. On a busy video background, add a solid panel behind numbers.
  • One number per shot, one chart per ad. More than that and viewers stop reading.

How to show a number so people take it in

  1. Say it as it appears. Voice and text together; the ear catches it if the eye misses it.
  2. Make it big. The number should be the largest thing on screen, filling a good share of the frame on a phone.
  3. Hold it. Give viewers time to read it; a short label and number usually need 2 to 3 seconds. Cut away too soon and it's wasted.
  4. Round sensibly. "Nearly 3 in 4" is easier than "73.6%", as long as it is still accurate.
  5. Give it meaning. "£40 a month" needs a comparison: "less than your phone bill".
  6. Add the source line. Small, but readable, for example "Survey of 1,000 UK users, March 2026".
  7. Animate only to help. A bar growing to its value can help; spinning digits and particle effects don't.

Example: an accounting software ad

Example: a 30-second skippable ad for accounting software. The old version opened on a logo, then showed a chart with five bars comparing time spent on invoicing across company sizes, plus three other statistics. The new version opens on a freelancer closing a laptop at 5pm with the text "Invoices done in 4 minutes", spoken at the same time. At 15 seconds, a two-bar chart compares "Spreadsheet: 25 min" with "App: 4 min", labelled "Average time to send an invoice, in-house test of 50 invoices, 2026". The company keeps the test results on file in case the claim is challenged. The other three statistics move to the landing page. Figures and details are illustrative.

Mistakes that cost viewers

  • A dense chart in the first five seconds, so viewers skip before understanding it.
  • Several numbers on screen at once.
  • White numbers over bright footage with no panel.
  • Claims with no evidence, or comparisons to an unnamed "leading brand".
  • Tiny source lines no one could read.

FAQ

Is there a book on hooks for the first seconds of a video?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and open loops with templates. Google's free ABCDs are an alternative.

Should a number be the hook of my YouTube ad?

It can be, if it's surprising and easy to grasp. Show it with the product, inside the first five seconds.

How long should a number stay on screen?

Long enough to read comfortably; about 2 to 3 seconds for a number and a short label.

Do I need evidence for statistics in my ad?

Yes. In the UK the ASA says marketers must hold documentary evidence for objective claims before the ad runs.

Can I use a chart in a short ad?

Yes, if it's one simple comparison with two or three bars and a clear label.
