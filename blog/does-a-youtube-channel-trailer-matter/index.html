Does a YouTube channel trailer matter, and what should it include?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A channel trailer matters most for channels that get visitors to their Home tab who haven't subscribed yet. YouTube describes it as a preview "so that viewers can learn more and subscribe", and it is shown only to non-subscribers, and not again once a viewer has watched it. It won't fix weak videos, but a short, clear trailer can turn curious visitors into subscribers. Most viewers find you through videos, so it is a finishing touch, not a growth strategy.

Key takeaways

  • The trailer appears on your Home tab for people who aren't subscribed; returning subscribers can see a separate featured video.
  • Once someone has watched the trailer, YouTube doesn't show it to them again.
  • Ads aren't shown on the trailer by default, unless it contains third-party claimed content.
  • You set it in YouTube Studio under Customisation, Home tab, Add section, Channel trailer.
  • Your Home tab can have up to 12 sections, so the trailer sits alongside playlists, uploads and Shorts.

What YouTube says the trailer is for

YouTube's help page on customising your channel layout says: "Your channel trailer offers a preview of your channel so that viewers can learn more and subscribe." It notes that ads aren't shown on the trailer by default, unless the video includes third-party claimed content, and that once a viewer has watched it, it won't be shown to them again.

To set one: in YouTube Studio, go to Customisation, then Home tab. Under Layout, click Add section, choose Channel trailer, pick a video and click Publish.

By default four sections are pre-filled: Shorts, uploads, created playlists and public subscriptions. YouTube's manage your channel page covers the name, handle, description and links, and the channel branding page covers the banner and profile picture. Our page on what size a YouTube banner should be gives the image specifications.

What to put in a channel trailer

YouTube doesn't set a required length or script. These suggestions follow from what the trailer is for, a first-time visitor deciding whether to subscribe:

PartWhat to saySuggested time
HookWho the channel is for and the result they'll getFirst 5 seconds
ProofA few quick clips from your best videos10 to 20 seconds
What to expectTopics and upload schedule5 to 10 seconds
Ask"Subscribe if you want..." in plain wordsLast 5 seconds

The timings are practical suggestions, not YouTube requirements. Keep it short: a visitor on your Home tab has not committed to anything yet. If your channel's best video already does this job, you can use it as the trailer rather than filming something new.

When a trailer matters most, and least

SituationHow much the trailer matters
Many visitors reach your Home tab (from search, links or collaborations)More: it's the first thing they see
Most views come from Shorts or the home feed, and few visit your channel pageLess: work on the videos themselves
Channel focus has changedMore: explain the new direction
Brand new channel with few videosSome: but the videos matter more

YouTube Studio's analytics can show how many viewers reach your channel page. If that number is small, your titles and thumbnails, which decide whether people click on your videos at all, are a better use of time. Our guides to how to write good YouTube titles and how to make good YouTube thumbnails cover those.

Worked example: a fitness channel

Example: Dana runs a home-workout channel. Her Home tab currently opens with her newest upload, a 25-minute live stream recording, for every visitor.

BeforeAfter
Newest upload autoplay for everyone45-second trailer for non-subscribers
No explanation of the channelOpens with "15-minute workouts for busy parents, no equipment"
Generic sectionsSections: "Start here" playlist, 15-minute workouts, Shorts
No upload schedule mentioned"New workout every Monday and Thursday" in the trailer and banner
Subscribers see the same live streamFeatured video for subscribers: her latest workout

Dana keeps the trailer short, shows clips from her three most-watched workouts, and ends with one clear ask. Because YouTube shows the trailer only once per viewer, she doesn't worry about subscribers seeing it repeatedly. She checks in YouTube Studio a month later to see whether more channel-page visitors subscribe.

How long should a channel trailer be?

YouTube doesn't set a length. Short is usually better: enough to say who the channel is for, show a few clips and ask for the subscribe.
