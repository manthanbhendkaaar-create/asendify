How do I make an equity crowdfunding pitch video that investors watch, without breaking UK promotion rules?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Keep it to roughly one to three minutes, show the product or service working in the first few seconds, then cover the problem, why your team, the evidence of traction, and the investment ask, and end with a clear call to action. Republic Europe (formerly Seedrs) tells founders "your video should last between 1 - 3 minutes" and warns that "the video is a financial promotion, and will need to be verified in the same way as the written campaign". So retention and compliance pull in the same direction: say fewer things, say them clearly, and only say what you can prove.

This page is general guidance, not financial or legal advice. Your platform's campaign team and an FCA-authorised approver have the final say on what your video can include.

Key takeaways

  • Republic Europe's guidance: one to three minutes, and the video "shouldn't aim to cover content not described in the written pitch".
  • It is a financial promotion; "any factual content needs to be supported with evidence".
  • Show the product first: a guide on Seedrs' blog suggests "ideally the first 10 seconds should show your product".
  • Then why (team and story), how (the product solving the problem), what (the investment, market, traction) and a call to action.
  • Write a script or detailed outline and get it reviewed before filming; reshoots after compliance review are expensive.
  • The FCA expects financial promotions to be "fair, clear and not misleading", with "a balanced view of the benefits and risks".

What the platforms and the regulator say

SourceGuidance
Republic Europe: what to include in your video pitch"Between 1 - 3 minutes"; feature the team; cover problem, market, why you, why now; "finish the video with a strong call to action"; the video is a financial promotion and "any factual content needs to be supported with evidence"; recommends creating a script for review before filming
Seedrs blog: perfecting your crowdfunding video pitchFive sections: product demo, why, how, what, call to action; "ideally the first 10 seconds should show your product"; the video "doesn't need to cover every detail of the business" (written by a crowdfunding video studio)
FCA FG24/1Financial promotions "should be fair, clear and not misleading, and support consumer understanding"; firms should give "a balanced view of the benefits and risks"

FG24/1 is written about social media, but the "fair, clear and not misleading" standard applies to financial promotions on every channel, which is why platforms review pitch videos.

Why investors stop watching

  • A long brand film with music and drone shots before anyone explains the business.
  • Founder backstory first, with the product appearing at minute two.
  • Everything at once: market size, five revenue streams, the roadmap and the exit plan in three minutes.
  • Unsupported superlatives: "the next unicorn", "guaranteed growth", which reviewers will strike out anyway.
  • Reading a script to camera with no energy.

A 2-minute structure

TimeSectionWhat to show
0:00 to 0:10ProductThe product or service in use, clearly, with a one-line caption
0:10 to 0:35Problem and whyA founder states the problem in a concrete example and why the team started
0:35 to 1:05HowHow it works, a short demo, a real customer if you have permission
1:05 to 1:40WhatEvidence: revenue, users, retailers, partners, as stated in your written pitch and supported by evidence
1:40 to 2:00The askWhat you're raising and what it's for, then direct viewers to read the full pitch and risk warnings

Timings are an example. Every number on screen should match the written pitch.

What not to say

  • Forecasts presented as certain ("we will be worth £50m by 2029").
  • Return promises or implied returns ("investors will double their money").
  • Comparisons with famous companies that imply similar outcomes.
  • Claims not in the written pitch or not supported by evidence.
  • Anything that plays down risk ("there's very little risk because...").

The written pitch will include the platform's risk warnings; the video should never contradict them.

Example: re-cutting a 4-minute pitch video

Example: a craft drinks brand films a 4-minute video: 40 seconds of brewery scenery, two minutes of the founders' story, then a list of claims including "set to become the UK's favourite low-alcohol beer".

The re-cut is 2 minutes 10 seconds. It opens on a bottle being poured with a caption naming the product and where it's stocked. A founder explains the problem in one sentence. A stockist's buyer (with written permission) says one line about reorders. Three supported numbers appear on screen, each matching the written pitch. "UK's favourite" is cut because it can't be evidenced. It closes on the raise amount and "Read the full pitch, including risks". Brand and details are illustrative.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to hold viewers to the end of a short video?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Your crowdfunding platform's guidance covers what the video must and mustn't say.

How long should an equity crowdfunding video be?

Republic Europe recommends between one and three minutes.

Is a crowdfunding pitch video a financial promotion?

Republic Europe says yes: it must be verified like the written campaign, and factual content needs evidence.

Can I include revenue forecasts in my video?

Only if your platform's review allows it and they are presented fairly with their basis; never as guarantees.

Do I need a professional video?

Not necessarily. Clear sound, real footage and a tight structure matter more than budget.
