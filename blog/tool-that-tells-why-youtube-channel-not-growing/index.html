Is there a tool that tells me why my YouTube channel is not growing?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 October 2026 · Last updated 23 October 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Partly. No tool can tell you for certain why a channel is not growing, because the cause can sit in one of four places: YouTube is not showing your videos, people see them but do not click, people click but leave early, or viewers watch once and never come back. YouTube Studio (free) shows which of those four is failing, and a free outside tool such as the YouTube Channel Roast checks the part a stranger sees first: your titles, thumbnails and upload rhythm.

The four places growth usually stalls

Think of growth as a funnel. Every view passes through the same four stages, and a channel stops growing when one of them is weak:

  1. Reach. Is YouTube showing your thumbnails to people at all? This is measured as impressions.
  2. Click. Of the people who see a thumbnail, how many click? This is the impressions click-through rate (CTR). YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10% (YouTube Help).
  3. Watch. Once people click, do they stay? This is audience retention.
  4. Return. Do viewers come back? YouTube splits your audience into new, casual and regular viewers; regular viewers are those who have returned to watch for at least six months in the past year (YouTube Help).

The fix for each stage is different, which is why "why am I not growing?" has no single answer. The first job is to find the stage.

Step 1: find the weak stage in YouTube Studio (free)

YouTube Studio is the only place with your private numbers. Open Analytics from the left menu for the whole channel, or go to Content, point to a video and choose Analytics for one video (YouTube Help). Then check:

  • Reach tab: impressions and impressions CTR. Few impressions points to a reach problem; plenty of impressions with a low CTR points to a packaging problem.
  • Engagement tab: watch time and average view duration. A steep fall in the first 30 seconds points to a hook problem.
  • Audience tab: the mix of new, casual and regular viewers. Lots of new viewers but very few returning ones points to a "one-off video" problem.

Write down one number per stage for your last five videos. The stage that is weak across most of them is your bottleneck.

Step 2: check what a stranger sees with the YouTube Channel Roast

Studio tells you that CTR is low. It does not tell you what is wrong with your titles and thumbnails. That is where an outside view helps.

The YouTube Channel Roast is a free tool: you paste a public channel link and it reviews your latest public uploads. No sign-up, no card, 3 roasts a day. It scores three things:

  • Titles (40%): curiosity, clarity, specificity, length and clickability.
  • Thumbnails (40%): readability on a phone, contrast, focal point, amount of text, consistency across uploads and emotion, based on your 8 most recent uploads.
  • Consistency (20%): uploads in the last 30 days, days since your last upload, your average gap and your longest gap. Long gaps cost points.

You get an overall score out of 100, separate scores for each part, a one-line verdict, 5 one-line roasts, 3 fixes to make this week, and your best and worst title with a rewrite of the worst one. Your channel needs at least 3 public uploads.

Be clear about its limits: it uses public information only, so it cannot hear your spoken hook, judge your editing, or read your retention graph or private analytics. It covers the click stage and part of the return stage (upload rhythm), not the others.

Step 3: check the watch stage with your retention data

If Step 1 pointed to retention, open the audience retention report for your weakest recent video. YouTube shows the share of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds, plus spikes and dips through the video (YouTube Help). If you prefer a quick summary, paste the numbers into the free Retention Curve Checker, which lists the biggest drops with timestamps and the 30-second hold.

Then watch each big drop with the sound on and ask: what made someone leave here?

A worked example

Imagine a cooking channel with 40 videos whose subscriber count has been flat for three months. The creator runs the steps above:

  • Studio: impressions are steady, but CTR on the last five videos sits around the low end of YouTube's typical range. Retention after 30 seconds is in line with their older videos. Returning viewers are few.
  • Roast: the consistency score is low because of a three-week gap, and the worst title is a vague "Sunday cooking vlog". The fixes suggest naming the dish and the benefit in the title, and making thumbnail text readable on a phone.
  • Diagnosis: the bottleneck is the click stage plus an irregular upload rhythm, not the editing.
  • Next upload: a specific title, a cleaner thumbnail, and a schedule they can keep. Then they compare CTR and returning viewers a few weeks later.

This is an illustration, not a real channel, and no tool can promise that these changes will grow it. The value is in knowing which stage to work on first.

What no tool can tell you

  • Whether people want the topic. If few people are interested in a subject, better packaging only helps so much. YouTube's Trends tab in Analytics can help you look for ideas viewers are searching for (YouTube Help).
  • Why, with certainty. Numbers show where people stop. You still have to watch the video to see the reason.
  • Results. Be wary of any tool or service that promises views or subscribers.

Key takeaways

  • Growth stalls at one of four stages: reach, click, watch or return.
  • YouTube Studio's Reach, Engagement and Audience tabs show which stage is weak, for free.
  • The YouTube Channel Roast gives a free outside view of titles, thumbnails and upload consistency, with 3 fixes.
  • Retention problems need your retention graph; no outside tool can see it.
  • Fix the weakest stage first, change one or two things per upload, and measure again.

FAQ

Is the YouTube Channel Roast an AI tool?

Yes. It is a free AI tool that scores a channel's titles, thumbnails and upload consistency from public data and suggests three fixes.

Can the Roast see my analytics?

No. It only uses public information, so it never needs a login. For impressions, CTR and retention, use YouTube Studio.

How many videos do I need before diagnosing growth?

The Roast needs at least 3 public uploads. In Studio, compare several recent videos rather than judging from one.

What if every stage looks fine?

Then look at topic choice and your schedule: are you making videos your audience asks for, and often enough for them to come back?
