Is there a tool that tells me why my YouTube channel is not growing?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 23 October 2026

Partly. No tool can say for certain why a channel is not growing, because the cause sits in one of four places: YouTube is not showing your videos, people see them but do not click, people click but leave early, or viewers never come back. YouTube Studio shows which stage is failing; an outside tool can check your titles and thumbnails.

Key takeaways

  • Growth stalls at one of four stages: reach, click, watch or return.
  • YouTube Studio's Reach, Engagement and Audience tabs show which stage is weak, for free.
  • Outside tools only see public data, so they help with packaging and upload rhythm, not retention.
  • Retention problems need your own retention graph and a rewatch of the drop with sound on.
  • Fix the weakest stage first, change one or two things per upload, and measure again.

The four places growth usually stalls

Think of growth as a funnel. Every view passes through the same four stages:

StageQuestionWhere to lookTypical fix
ReachIs YouTube showing your thumbnails?Reach tab: impressionsTopics viewers want; consistent uploads
ClickDo people click when they see them?Reach tab: impressions click-through rateClearer titles and thumbnails
WatchDo they stay once they click?Engagement tab and audience retention reportFaster openings, tighter structure
ReturnDo they come back?Audience tab: new, casual and regular viewersSeries, a steady schedule, a clear channel promise

On the click stage, YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%. On the return stage, YouTube splits your audience into new, casual and regular viewers, where regular viewers have returned to watch for at least six months in the past year.

The fix for each stage is different, which is why "why am I not growing?" has no single answer. The first job is to find the stage.

Step 1: find the weak stage in YouTube Studio (free)

YouTube Studio is the only place with your private numbers. Open Analytics from the left menu for the whole channel, or go to Content, point to a video and choose Analytics (YouTube Help). Then check:

  • Reach tab: impressions and impressions CTR. Few impressions points to a reach problem; plenty of impressions with a low CTR points to a packaging problem.
  • Engagement tab: watch time and average view duration. A steep fall in the first 30 seconds points to a hook problem.
  • Audience tab: the mix of new, casual and regular viewers. Many new viewers but few returning ones points to a "one-off video" problem.

Write down one number per stage for your last five videos. The stage that is weak across most of them is your bottleneck.

Step 2: check what a stranger sees

Studio tells you that CTR is low. It does not tell you what is wrong with your titles and thumbnails. An outside view helps here. One option is Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast on asendify.co: you paste a public channel link, and with no sign-up it scores your titles, thumbnails (from your 8 most recent uploads) and upload consistency, and suggests three fixes. Like any outside tool, it uses public information only. It cannot hear your spoken hook, judge your editing or read your retention graph, so it covers the click stage and part of the return stage, not the others.

You can also do this by hand: line up your last eight thumbnails at phone size and ask whether each one is readable in a second, and whether each title says what the viewer gets.

Step 3: check the watch stage with your retention data

If Step 1 pointed to retention, open the audience retention report for your weakest recent video. YouTube shows the share of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds, plus spikes and dips through the video (YouTube Help). Watch each big drop with the sound on and ask: what made someone leave here?

Worked example

Example (hypothetical): a cooking channel with 40 videos has had a flat subscriber count for three months. The creator writes down one number per stage for the last five videos.

StageWhat they foundVerdict
ReachImpressions steadyFine
ClickCTR around 2–3%, the low end of YouTube's typical rangeWeak
Watch30-second retention in line with older videosFine
ReturnFew regular viewers; a three-week upload gapWeak

An outside review flags a vague title ("Sunday cooking vlog") and thumbnail text too small for a phone. The diagnosis: the bottleneck is the click stage plus an irregular schedule, not the editing. For the next four uploads, they name the dish and the benefit in each title, use one readable phrase per thumbnail, and keep to a schedule they can sustain. Then they compare CTR and regular viewers. No tool can promise these changes will grow the channel; the value is in knowing which stage to work on first.

Fixes by stage, in order of effort

Once you know the weak stage, start with the cheapest change.

Reach

  • Keep uploading on a steady schedule, so YouTube has recent videos to test.
  • Choose topics your existing viewers already watch, rather than jumping between niches.
  • Look at which past videos brought in new viewers, and make more on that theme.

Click

  • Rewrite the title of your weakest recent video to name the outcome the viewer gets.
  • Simplify the thumbnail to one face or object and a few large words.
  • Make sure the title and thumbnail add to each other instead of repeating the same words.

Watch

  • Remove intros, greetings and sponsor reads from the first 30 seconds.
  • Confirm the title's promise within the first few sentences.
  • Cut the section at the biggest dip, or add a reason to keep watching just before it.

Return

  • Group videos into series so one leads naturally to the next.
  • End each video by pointing to a specific related video, not a general "subscribe".
  • Keep a schedule viewers can rely on.

Change one or two things per upload. If you change everything at once, you will not know what worked.

What no tool can tell you

  • Whether people want the topic. If few people care about a subject, better packaging only helps so much. YouTube's Trends tab in Analytics can help you look for ideas viewers are searching for.
  • Why, with certainty. Numbers show where people stop. You still have to watch the video to see the reason.
  • Results. Be wary of any tool or service that promises views or subscribers.

FAQ

Is there an AI tool that diagnoses a YouTube channel?

Some AI tools score public packaging, such as titles, thumbnails and upload rhythm. None can see your private analytics without permission, so pair them with YouTube Studio.

Can an outside tool see my analytics?

Not unless you give it access through Google. Tools that only use public data need no login. For impressions, CTR and retention, use YouTube Studio.

How many videos do I need before diagnosing growth?

Compare several recent videos rather than judging from one. Five is a sensible minimum for spotting a pattern.

What if every stage looks fine?

Then look at topic choice and your schedule: are you making videos your audience asks for, and often enough for them to come back?

What is a good click-through rate?

It depends on the channel and traffic source. YouTube says half of all channels and videos sit between 2% and 10%, so compare against your own past videos first.
