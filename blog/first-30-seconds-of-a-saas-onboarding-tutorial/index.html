What should the first 30 seconds of a SaaS onboarding tutorial show?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 26 November 2026 · Last updated 26 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The first 30 seconds of a SaaS onboarding tutorial should show the screen the new user actually sees, say the one thing they will be able to do, and start the first step. Skip the logo sting, the company story and the feature tour. Get to the point immediately.

Key takeaways

  • A Loom (Atlassian) guide advises "Get to the point immediately", opening on the screen a new user will actually see, and covering one main concept per video, aiming for two minutes at most.
  • YouTube Help says the Intro figure shows the share of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds, so those seconds are measured separately.
  • Wistia's analysis suggests 1 to 5 minutes for how-to and tutorial videos.
  • A tutorial viewer is usually stuck on a task. Show them the task, not the company.

Why the first 30 seconds matter for onboarding

People watch an onboarding tutorial because they have just signed up and are trying to do something. They are not browsing. If the video opens with a greeting and a brand animation, they are watching someone else's agenda while their own task waits. The Loom guide is blunt about this: viewers are impatient, so deliver what the video promised straight away.

YouTube Help also gives you a measure: the Intro key moment shows what share of the audience still watched after the first 30 seconds. If the tutorial is on YouTube or a help centre that reports similar numbers, that is the figure to improve first.

Steps to open a tutorial well

  1. Write one outcome. For example "Invite a teammate and give them view-only access".
  2. Start where the user is. The Loom guide opens on the screen shown after first login and then explains it.
  3. Say the outcome in the first sentence. Keep it under 10 seconds.
  4. Do the first step before 30 seconds. Do not wait for the setup talk.
  5. Remove the intro animation. Put the brand in the end card or a small lower-third.
  6. Write a two-column script. The Loom guide suggests narration on one side and what is on screen on the other.
  7. Read it aloud and time it. Change anything that sounds written rather than spoken.
  8. Record a scratch track first, final audio last. That lets you change the script after you see the clips.
  9. Add chapters if there are more than three steps, so viewers can jump.
  10. Watch the first 30 seconds on a phone and with sound off. Check captions and readable text.

Example: a tutorial on connecting a calendar

Example: a scheduling app has a 3-minute tutorial. The first version opens with a 6-second logo animation, a 10-second welcome and a 12-second preview of every feature, so the first step starts at 28 seconds. The second version starts on the empty calendar screen a new user sees. The narrator says "Here is how to connect your calendar in under a minute" and clicks Settings at 6 seconds. The first step finishes at 22 seconds. The company name moves to the end card. This scenario uses invented numbers for illustration and is not a measured result.

What if the viewer needs context?

Some steps do not make sense without a reason. Give the reason in one short sentence, tied to the task: "You need this so reminders go to the right inbox". If the background is long, move it to a separate video and link it. The Loom guide's rule is one main concept per video.

What about a welcome video?

A separate welcome video is fine, and it can say more about the company and what to expect. Do not mix it with task tutorials. A person who needs to invite a teammate does not need to sit through it.

Check the result

Read the retention graph. YouTube Help says dips mean viewers are abandoning or skipping at that moment. If a dip appears at the first step, check whether the screen matches what the viewer sees in the product. If the product interface has changed since you recorded, re-record that part.

Should an onboarding tutorial start with a logo?

No. Put the brand on the end card or a small label so the first seconds show the task.

How long should an onboarding tutorial be?

The Loom guide aims for two minutes at most per video. Wistia suggests 1 to 5 minutes for how-to content.

Should I explain the whole product first?

No. Cover one main concept per video and link to others.

What if my tutorial covers many steps?

Add chapters and keep one action per step.

How do I know the opening works?

Check the Intro figure in YouTube Studio, or the equivalent in your video host.
