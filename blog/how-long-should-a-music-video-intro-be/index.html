How long should a music video intro be before the song starts?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 3 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Short, usually a few seconds before the music or a clear musical moment begins. People click a music video to hear the song, so a long silent skit, logo sequence or slow fade is where many of them leave or skip ahead. If you want a story intro, make it gripping from the first frame and short, or let the song start underneath it, and consider uploading an "audio-first" version for people who just want the track.

Key takeaways

  • The viewer's intent is the song; anything that delays it must earn its place.
  • YouTube's retention report shows an Intro figure: the percentage of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds. A long non-musical intro often shows up there.
  • Dips mean viewers are abandoning or skipping at that point; a dip right after a skit tells you people jumped to the music.
  • Story intros can work when they create a question the song answers, but keep them tight.
  • Start the music under the opening scene if you need visual setup.

How to keep a story intro without losing viewers

  1. Open on the most intriguing frame, not the establishing shot.
  2. Create a question in the first seconds ("Why is he running?") that the song will answer.
  3. Bring in sound early: an ambient bed, the first notes or a vocal ad-lib.
  4. Keep dialogue minimal and captioned.
  5. Cut it ruthlessly. If a shot doesn't build the question, remove it.
  6. Consider two uploads: the full cinematic version and an audio-first version, or put the song in your own channel's music release separately.

Where credits belong

Label logos, production company cards and long title sequences slow the start. Put them at the end or in the description. A small, brief artist-and-title card over the first shot is enough.

Example: a hip-hop single

Example: an artist's music video opens with a 45-second dialogue skit before the beat drops. The retention graph shows a steep early drop, then a spike where many viewers skip to the first verse. For the next release, the skit is cut to 12 seconds with the beat fading in under the final line, and the full skit is posted as a separate Short that links to the video. Details are illustrative.

Ending matters too

A long outro after the song ends also loses people. Use the last 5 to 20 seconds for an end screen pointing to your next video or playlist; end screens can appear in the last 5 to 20 seconds of videos at least 25 seconds long.

FAQ

Is there a book on hooks for the first seconds of a video?

The Retention Editing Playbook (£1,720 one-time) teaches hooks and open loops for YouTube; it isn't music-specific.

Do skit intros hurt music video views?

They can increase early drop-off or skipping. Keep them short, or start the music underneath.

Should the artist's name appear at the start?

A brief title card over the first shot is fine; avoid long logo sequences.

What about the outro?

End soon after the song, with an end screen to your next video.

Should I upload an audio-only version?

Many artists do, so fans who only want the track can find it easily.
