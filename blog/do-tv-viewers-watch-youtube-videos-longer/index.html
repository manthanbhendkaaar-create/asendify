Do people watching YouTube on a TV watch videos for longer?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

YouTube doesn't publish a rule that TV viewers watch longer, so check your own channel. What YouTube has said is that the TV is now the primary device for YouTube viewing in the US, with over a billion hours watched on TVs a day. Open the Device type report in YouTube Studio to see what share of your watch time comes from TVs, then edit with that viewer in mind.

Key takeaways

  • IBC reported in February 2025 that YouTube CEO Neal Mohan said the TV is now the primary device for YouTube viewing in the US, with more than one billion hours watched on TVs each day on average.
  • Nielsen measured YouTube at 11.6% of all US TV viewing time in February 2025.
  • YouTube Help says the Device type report shows what percent of your watch time comes from mobile, computer, TV and tablet.
  • These are platform-wide facts. Your channel may be mostly phone viewers. Check before you change anything.
  • If TV is a big share, the biggest changes are readability, audio and a structure that works without anyone touching the screen.

The honest summary: lots of YouTube watch time now happens on TVs, but YouTube hasn't published a per-device retention benchmark. Your own analytics are the only reliable answer for your channel.

How to check your own device split

  1. Open YouTube Studio and go to Analytics, then the Audience tab.
  2. Find the Device type report. YouTube Help describes it as showing "what percent of your watch time comes from each device (ordered from highest to lowest)".
  3. Do this at channel level, then for your three longest videos and your three shortest.
  4. Use Advanced mode to compare videos or groups and add metrics to your report, so you can see whether long videos lean more towards TV.

If TV is a small share for you, the rest of this page matters less. If it's a large share, read on.

How TV viewing changes what holds attention

Nobody is holding the screen

On a phone, a viewer can scroll comments, check another app or swipe away in a second. On a TV the video is the main thing on the screen. That doesn't mean they stay: they can still pick up their phone. Your job is to make the video worth looking back at.

Text has to be readable from a sofa

Small on-screen text that works on a monitor can be hard to read across a room. Use fewer words on screen, larger type and hold each text card long enough to read. Our page on captions and retention covers when on-screen text helps.

Audio carries more weight

A TV viewer may look away. If your video relies on visuals with no narration, they lose the thread. Make sure the voice track tells the story on its own.

Structure matters more than taps

Chapters, cards and links all need interaction. A TV viewer is less likely to click. Build the pull into the edit itself: open a question early, answer it late, and re-hook in the middle. Our guide to key moments in YouTube Studio shows how to find the dips where viewers drift.

Should I make longer videos if TV viewing is high?

Only if your retention supports it. Our page on video length and retention explains how to decide.

Does YouTube count TV viewing differently?

YouTube's Unique reach metric accounts for shared viewing on TVs, according to YouTube Help. Watch time is shown by device in the Device type report.

Do Shorts get watched on TVs?

Neal Mohan said the "new" television includes things like Shorts. Check your own Device type report for your Shorts to see how much.

Is this only true in the US?

The figures quoted here are for the US. Check your Geography and Device type reports together to see what applies to your audience.
