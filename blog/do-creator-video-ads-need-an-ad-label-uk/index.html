Do creator video ads need an "#ad" label in the UK, and will it make people scroll past?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Usually yes. If you pay, gift or otherwise reward a creator to feature your product, UK guidance from the ASA and the CMA says the content must be obviously identifiable as an ad from the start, typically with a clear "Ad" or "#Ad" label upfront, and for videos the disclosure must come at the beginning. Platform labels like "Paid partnership" can count only if they are clear. A well-edited ad can carry the label and still hold attention.

This page is general information about UK advertising guidance, not legal advice. Check the current ASA and CMA guidance for your situation.

Key takeaways

  • The CMA says money, commissions, discounts and free products all count as payment, "no matter how many followers you have."
  • For videos, "the disclosure must be given at the beginning of the ad."
  • Recommended labels include "ad", "advert", "advertising" and "advertisement"; "#gift", "#collab" and "#sponsored" are discouraged.
  • The ASA says disclosure "won't be sufficient if the audience needs to have read or watched the whole of the content" to understand it's an ad.
  • Running a post as a paid ad from the brand's own account is usually clear, but sponsored labels don't fix a misleading ad.

What the CMA says

The Competition and Markets Authority's guidance for content creators says "Any form of incentive or reward – including money, commissions, discounts" counts as payment, as do free products and gifts. Audiences "should be able to understand that your content is an ad, as soon as they access or engage with it," and "For podcasts and videos" the disclosure "must be given at the beginning of the ad." Built-in tools such as "paid partnership" can be used "provided the label is clear and easy to see or access"; if not, add "#Ad" in a clear, upfront position. It also says "Everyone involved in creating content like this on social media must take responsibility."

What the ASA says

The Advertising Standards Authority's guidance on brand-owned and paid social media says the CAP Code applies to paid-for social ads, which "are usually obviously identifiable as such through their positioning, labelling and wider context." But it warns that "even 'Promoted' or 'Sponsored' posts could break the rules" if they mislead about who is behind them. It expects relevant posts to include "a prominent label – such as '#Ad' upfront", and found a label "displayed in smaller text and placed in the top right-hand corner" insufficient in one case.

Will an "Ad" label hurt retention?

A label isn't what makes people scroll; a slow or confusing opening is. The rules don't stop you from opening strongly. They stop you from hiding that it's an ad.

Editing tips that keep both compliance and attention:

  1. Put the label on screen from frame one, in clear text, while the hook plays. Don't make it a separate title card.
  2. Have the creator say it naturally: "This is an ad for X, and here's why I actually use it."
  3. Keep the hook visual. Show the product doing something in the first two seconds.
  4. Match caption and video. "Ad" at the start of the caption, not buried in hashtags.
  5. Avoid fake-organic styling that implies the creator wasn't paid.

Example: a skincare brand gifts a moisturiser to a creator with 8,000 followers, then pays £300 to run her video as a Spark Ad.

Keep a record of the agreement with each creator, including what they were given and what was agreed about labelling. If a complaint comes in, you'll be able to show what was done. And when you re-cut the same footage for different platforms, check the label survives every crop and edit: a 9:16 cut can easily push corner text off screen.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches strong openings for labelled ads?

The Retention Editing Playbook teaches cold opens and pacing for £1,000 one-time. Google's ABCDs and TikTok's creative best practices are free.

Does a gifted product count as an ad?

Yes. The CMA says free products and gifts count as payment, whatever the follower count.

Is "#sponsored" enough?

The CMA discourages "#sponsored", "#gift" and "#collab"; use "Ad" or "#Ad".

Where should the label go in a video?

At the beginning. The CMA says video disclosures must be given at the start.

Is the platform's "Paid partnership" label enough?

Only if it is clear and easy to see. If not, add "#Ad" upfront too.
