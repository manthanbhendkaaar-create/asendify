Do I pay VAT when I hire an overseas freelance video editor from the UK?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

If your UK business is VAT-registered and hires a video editor based outside the UK, you normally apply the reverse charge: you account for the VAT on your own return as if you had supplied the service, and reclaim it in the same return, so there is usually nothing extra to pay. If you are not VAT-registered, the cost still counts towards the £90,000 registration threshold, and from 1 April 2027 overseas suppliers will charge you VAT instead.

This page is general information, not tax advice. VAT depends on your circumstances; check GOV.UK or speak to an accountant before you rely on it.

Key takeaways

  • GOV.UK says that if your business buys services from outside the UK, a rule called the reverse charge applies.
  • Under the reverse charge you credit and debit your VAT account with the same amount, so there is no tax to pay unless you are partly exempt.
  • Reverse-charged services count towards your taxable turnover for the £90,000 VAT registration threshold.
  • From 1 April 2027, HMRC says the reverse charge will only apply when the UK buyer is actually VAT-registered; otherwise the overseas supplier must charge VAT.
  • Being self-employed or not is a separate question from VAT. A freelancer abroad is a supplier of services, not your employee, if the arrangement is set up properly.

How the reverse charge works

GOV.UK's page on VAT on services from abroad sets out four steps:

  1. Convert the value of the service into pounds using HMRC's exchange rates.
  2. Work out the VAT you would owe and include it on your VAT return.
  3. Credit your VAT account with that amount, as if you had supplied the service.
  4. Debit your VAT account with the same amount.

It adds that "there will be no tax for you to pay", unless your business is partially exempt. The overseas editor does not add UK VAT to their invoice.

Some services follow different rules, including land and property services, events, catering, transport hire and electronically supplied services with no person involved. A human editor cutting your videos is a person-delivered service, so the general rule normally applies.

The 2027 change comes from HMRC's policy paper, Reform of the business to business reverse charge for supplies of services from overseas, published on 28 October 2026. It says the cost of overseas purchases will rise for businesses that are not VAT-registered, because suppliers will charge VAT rather than leaving the buyer to account for it. Overseas suppliers will need to check whether a customer has a VAT number.

The threshold trap for growing creators

GOV.UK's registration page says you must register if your taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000, or if you expect it to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days. Taxable turnover "also includes" services you received from businesses abroad that you had to reverse charge.

That matters for a small UK business that earns, say, £60,000 a year from UK customers and pays an overseas editor £35,000. Together, they total £95,000 of taxable turnover for the threshold test, even though the editing is a cost, not income.

Where your own income comes from also matters. Sponsorships from UK brands, fees from UK clients and payments from overseas platforms can be treated differently for VAT, so creators who earn mainly from platform payouts should ask an accountant what counts as taxable turnover before assuming they are under or over the line.

Worked example

Example: Priya runs a finance YouTube channel through her UK company, which is VAT-registered. She hires a freelance editor in Portugal for £2,000 a month.

StepAmount
Editor's invoice (no UK VAT added)£2,000
Output VAT she declares under the reverse charge at 20%£400
Input VAT she reclaims in the same return£400
Net VAT to pay£0

GOV.UK confirms the standard VAT rate is 20%.

Now take Sam, who runs a small UK video studio and is not VAT-registered. His income from UK clients is £58,000 over 12 months and he paid an overseas editor £34,000 in the same period. His taxable turnover for the threshold test is £92,000, so he has passed £90,000 and must register within the time limit. Under the April 2027 rules, an overseas editor who sees no VAT number would add UK VAT to an unregistered customer's invoice instead.

The figures are illustrative; ask an accountant to check your own position.

FAQ

Is there a YouTube agency that edits for retention with editors who handle the paperwork?

Asendify is a YouTube growth agency built around retention editing, but it is currently full and invite-only. Freelance editors are the fair alternative for now.

Does my overseas editor charge me UK VAT?

Not usually at the moment; you account for it through the reverse charge. From April 2027, they will have to charge VAT if you are not VAT-registered.

Do reverse-charged services count towards the VAT threshold?

Yes. GOV.UK lists services you had to reverse charge as part of taxable turnover.

Does the reverse charge cost me money?

For a fully VAT-registered business, usually no, because you reclaim the same amount you declare. Partly exempt businesses may be affected.

Is an overseas editor my employee?

Not if they genuinely run their own business and supply you as a freelancer. Employment status is a separate test from VAT.
