How should I edit a LinkedIn video ad so B2B buyers keep watching?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Put what matters in the first 10 seconds, burn in subtitles because many members watch muted, and keep awareness ads short. LinkedIn's own advice is to "show what you want your audience to see in the first 10 seconds," to keep videos short, and to test longer videos for demand generation. Its specs allow 3 seconds to 30 minutes in 16:9, 1:1, 4:5 or 9:16.

Key takeaways

  • LinkedIn: "Show what you want your audience to see in the first 10 seconds of the video."
  • LinkedIn notes "a large portion of LinkedIn members will watch your ad with the sound off" and suggests burning in subtitles.
  • Specs: 3 seconds to 30 minutes, 75 KB to 500 MB, aspect ratios 16:9, 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16.
  • Intro text: recommended 150 characters, maximum 600.
  • Keep videos short for awareness; test longer ones for demand generation.

What LinkedIn says

LinkedIn's video ad tips give three points that shape the edit: keep videos short, show the important part in the first 10 seconds, and plan for viewers with the sound off. It also suggests you "test longer videos for demand generation", since longer content can still drive clicks and views.

The video ad specs set the technical limits:

Why B2B viewers leave

A LinkedIn member is scrolling between work updates, job news and posts from colleagues. They're not in the mood for entertainment; they want something useful for their job. A video ad that opens with a slow brand animation, stock footage of handshakes or a generic "In today's fast-moving world" line gives them no reason to stop.

What does hold B2B viewers is specificity: a recognisable problem from their role, a number from their world, a real person who does their job.

Habits that help:

  1. Lead with the job, not the brand. Name the role and the problem first.
  2. Burn in subtitles. LinkedIn suggests burning them in for sound-off viewers; it also guarantees they show on every device. The W3C notes that captions also serve people who are Deaf or hard of hearing and others who can't hear the audio, so check they are accurate.
  3. Pick the right shape. Square and 4:5 take more of a mobile feed than 16:9; 9:16 works where the placement supports it.
  4. Cut to the point. Trim intros, pauses and repeated points.
  5. Let a real person talk. A customer or your own specialist explaining one thing clearly is credible to a professional audience.
  6. Use the intro text. Keep it within about 150 characters and make it add to the video, not repeat it.
