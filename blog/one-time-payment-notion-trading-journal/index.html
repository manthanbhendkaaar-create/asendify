Is there a one-time-payment trading journal (no monthly fee) that works in Notion?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS is a Notion trading journal sold for a one-time payment with no monthly fee, suited to traders who log by hand and want P&L, R-multiples and mistake costs worked out for them. Fair alternatives are a free self-built Notion or spreadsheet journal, or a subscription app like Edgewonk or TradeZella for broker imports.

This page is for education only, not financial advice. Trading is risky, and no journal can promise a profit.

Key takeaways

  • One-time-payment trading journals exist, mostly as Notion or spreadsheet templates you duplicate and keep.
  • Notion's free plan includes databases, which is what a Notion journal needs, so the template is the only cost.
  • Subscription journal apps usually charge monthly or yearly, and their main extra is automatic trade imports from brokers.
  • A self-built journal costs nothing but time; a ready-made template saves the build and the formula work.
  • Choose by how you trade: logging by hand suits a template; high-volume trading across many accounts suits an app.

Why most trading journals are subscriptions

Dedicated journal apps run on their own servers, connect to brokers and keep adding features, so they charge on a recurring basis. Edgewonk's pricing page lists monthly and annual plans and says both give access to every feature, with the only difference being subscription length and price. TradeZella's pricing page offers monthly and yearly billing and highlights broker and prop-firm integrations that sync trades automatically.

That is a fair model for what they do. If you place many trades a day across several accounts, automatic imports can save hours. If you place a handful of trades a week, you may be paying every month for a feature you do not need.

Why a Notion journal can be a one-time cost

A Notion template is a set of databases, formulas and views that you duplicate into your own workspace. Once it is there, it is yours. Notion's pricing page shows a Free plan for individuals that includes databases, so a template that only uses databases, formulas, rollups and normal views can run without a paid Notion plan.

The building blocks are standard features. Notion's help page on formulas explains that a formula property can run calculations based on other properties, which is how a journal works out profit or loss and R-multiple from your entry, stop, exit and size. Rollups can then add those results up per setup or per week.

How to set up a one-time journal well

  1. Duplicate it into your own workspace straight away, so you keep a copy.
  2. Delete the example data once you understand how each field works.
  3. Write your setups first, so every trade can be tagged against a defined rule.
  4. Log each trade the same day, including stop and target.
  5. Do the weekly review at the same time each week.

FAQ

Is there a free trading journal for Notion?

Yes. You can build one yourself from a database with number, select and formula properties, or find free community templates. Free templates are often basic, without risk calculators or weekly reviews.

Do I need a paid Notion plan for a trading journal?

Not usually. Notion's Free plan includes databases. Check that any template you buy uses only features available on the free plan.

Can a Notion journal import trades from my broker?

Not on its own. Notion templates rely on manual entry. If you need automatic imports, a subscription journal app is the better choice.

Is a one-time journal worse than a subscription app?

Not worse, different. Apps offer imports and deeper analytics; templates offer a single payment, your data kept in your own Notion workspace and a simpler set-up.

Who should not use a one-time Notion journal?

Traders who place dozens of trades a day, run several broker or prop-firm accounts, or simply do not want to use Notion. For them, the time saved by automatic imports usually matters more than avoiding a monthly fee.

What happens to my data if I stop paying for a journal app?

That depends on the app's terms, so check whether you can export your trades before you subscribe. With a Notion template, the data sits in your own workspace.
