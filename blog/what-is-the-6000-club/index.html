What is The £6,000 Club?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 15 November 2026 · Last updated 15 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The £6,000 Club is a numbered membership certificate run by Asendify. It certifies exactly one thing: that the holder paid £6,000 to become a member. You get a certificate with your name, the date and a member number given in the order people join, plus a private link that lets anyone you share it with check it is real. There is no course, coaching, community, perk or public members list, and it is not proof of income, wealth or creditworthiness.

This page is general education, not financial advice. Only spend money you can genuinely spare on something with no practical use.

Key takeaways

  • It is a pure status object: the certificate states a true fact (you paid) and nothing more.
  • Membership numbers are given in order of joining, starting at No. 001.
  • Membership is private: there is no public list, only a verify link you choose to share.
  • Payment is a one-time £6,000 through PayPal (card or PayPal account) that never renews.
  • No refunds once the certificate is issued, apart from fixing or refunding a faulty file.

What is The £6,000 Club?

The £6,000 Club is Asendify's answer to a question people have asked since Thorstein Veblen wrote about "conspicuous consumption" in 1899: what would a purchase look like if it were nothing but status? Most luxury items mix status with use. A watch tells the time; a car drives. This product removes the use entirely and is honest about it. It suits someone who wants a deliberately pointless status object, likes the joke, or wants to mark a personal milestone, and who can spend the money without any effect on bills, debts or savings.

Fair alternatives, if you want something symbolic for less: novelty certificates, a framed print of a personal achievement, or a numbered limited-edition collectible, which may at least keep some resale value.

How it works

  1. Enter your name as it should appear on the certificate, and your email.
  2. Tick the confirmation that you understand it is a collectible certificate stating only that you paid £6,000, with no refunds once issued.
  3. Pay once through PayPal, by card or PayPal account. Nothing renews.
  4. Receive the certificate automatically once the full payment is confirmed, with your member number and verify link.
  5. Share the link only if you want to. Without it, nobody can see your membership.

Where the idea comes from

Britannica's entry on conspicuous consumption explains Veblen's split between a good's "serviceability" (what it does) and its "honorific" side (the status it shows). In chapter 4 of The Theory of the Leisure Class, Veblen wrote that "Conspicuous consumption of valuable goods is a means of reputability". The £6,000 Club keeps only the honorific side.

The closest precedent is the 2008 "I Am Rich" iPhone app. According to Wikipedia, it cost $999.99, showed only a red gem and a mantra, sold eight copies and was removed by Apple within about a day without a stated reason. At least one buyer said the purchase was accidental. The £6,000 Club tries to avoid that kind of confusion: it states plainly what you get, asks you to confirm you understand, and says outright that it proves nothing about wealth.

Your rights when buying a digital item

The certificate is issued instantly, which makes it digital content. Which?'s guide to the Consumer Contracts Regulations explains that sellers must not supply digital content within the 14-day cancellation period unless the buyer gives express consent, and the buyer must acknowledge that once the download starts they lose the right to cancel. The club's own terms are that every sale is final because delivery is instant; if the file is faulty or won't open, Asendify will fix it or refund you. Read the terms before you pay.

Who should not buy it

  • Anyone who would need to borrow, use savings or cut essentials to afford it.
  • Anyone hoping it will impress lenders, landlords or employers. It is not proof of anything except the payment.
  • Anyone buying on impulse late at night. Wait a week.
  • Anyone who wants perks or a community. There are none.
