How do I turn Twitch VODs into highlights people actually watch?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Treat a highlight as a new video, not a shorter stream: cut the waiting, setup and quiet chat, keep only moments with a clear build-up and payoff, open on the best one, and title it by what happens rather than the date. Make portrait clips of the very best moments for Twitch's mobile Discovery feed, and export longer edits to YouTube, because Twitch caps Highlights and uploads at 100 hours per channel.

Key takeaways

  • Past broadcasts (VODs) are deleted automatically after a period that depends on your account type; Highlights made from them stay until you delete them, within a 100-hour limit.
  • Since April 2025 Twitch limits each channel to 100 hours of Highlights and uploads combined; clips and past broadcasts do not count.
  • Twitch said Highlights had not been effective at driving discovery, which is a reason to make them tighter, not longer.
  • Clips can appear in the mobile Discovery feed, and Twitch now makes a portrait version of every clip automatically.
  • Live viewers tolerate slow stretches because they are hanging out; replay viewers came for moments, so the edit has to deliver them fast.

What Twitch's own rules mean for your edit

Twitch's help article on video on demand explains that past broadcasts are kept for a limited time based on account type (60 days for Partners, Prime and Turbo users, less for other streamers) and that the Highlighter turns segments of a past broadcast into a Highlight. It also covers the storage limit and exporting to YouTube through a connected account.

In February 2025 Twitch announced a 100-hour storage cap on Highlights and uploads, enforced from April 2025, with the least-viewed Highlights deleted first on channels over the limit. Twitch said the cap affected fewer than 0.5% of active channels, that Highlights had not been effective at driving discovery or engagement, and that "the storage of this content is costly".

Why VOD viewers leave

A live stream works because chat, the streamer and the moment are all happening now. Watching the same four hours later, none of that is true. The viewer wants the funny moment, the clutch play or the reveal, and every minute of queue time or "be right back" stands between them and it. Twitch's own decision to cap Highlights because they were not driving discovery suggests long, lightly edited recaps are not what people watch.

How to cut a highlight that holds

  1. Mark moments live. Use stream markers or ask mods to clip; it saves hours of scrubbing later.
  2. Pick five to ten moments with a payoff. Each one should have a set-up ("I'm going to try the jump") and a result.
  3. Open on the best moment. Show it first, then "here's how we got there", or save it for the end and tease it at the start.
  4. Cut the dead air hard. Loading screens, queue, menu fiddling, long reads of chat.
  5. Add context in a line. A short text card ("Two hours in, last life") replaces the time the viewer missed.
  6. Title by the event. "First win with no armour" beats "Stream 14/11".
  7. Export the long edit to YouTube if you want it to last; keep Twitch Highlights for a small number of your best recaps.

Make clips for the Discovery feed

Twitch rolled out a mobile Discovery feed to all users in April 2024, with a live tab and a clips tab of "top moments from streamers across Twitch". Streamers cannot upload straight to it; eligible content qualifies automatically, and creating and featuring clips improves your chances. In August 2024 Twitch said it would automatically generate a portrait version of every clip and that clips now need a title when published. Trim each clip to start right before the action and end on the reaction.

Common mistakes

  • Turning the whole stream into a Highlight, which uses storage and holds no one.
  • Titles with dates and stream numbers only.
  • No context cards, so replay viewers do not know why a moment matters.
  • Leaving it too late: past broadcasts expire, so cut highlights within days.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to edit long streams into short videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook (Asendify) covers cold opens and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Twitch Creator Camp is a free alternative.

Do clips count toward Twitch's 100-hour limit?

No. Twitch says the limit covers Highlights and uploads; clips and past broadcasts are not included.

How long should a Twitch highlight be?

As long as the moments that matter. Many full-stream recaps work at 10 to 20 minutes, but that is a starting point, not a Twitch rule.

Should I put highlights on YouTube instead?

Many streamers do both: a few best recaps on Twitch, and edited versions exported to YouTube, where they do not count toward Twitch's storage cap.

Can I post Twitch clips in portrait?

Yes. Twitch said in 2024 that it automatically creates a portrait version of every clip.
