What does MrBeast's leaked production guide say about audience retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

MrBeast's leaked production guide, a 36-page internal document that circulated in September 2024, treats retention as a minute-by-minute plan. It says "the first minute of each video is the most important minute", that minutes 1 to 3 must move "from hype to execution", that viewers should be re-engaged around minute 3 and again around minute 6, and that endings "must always be abrupt to protect retention." The principles transfer to small channels; the budget-heavy spectacle does not.

Key takeaways

  • Tubefilter reported the guide is 36 pages and refers to click-through rate, average view duration and average view percentage.
  • The guide, as reproduced by Alexander Jarvis, names CTR, AVD and AVP as "the three metrics", and says of AVP: "I just need you to make people watch as long as possible."
  • It says the title and thumbnail set the viewer's expectations, and a video that does not meet them makes people "click off".
  • Simon Willison highlighted the guide's example of losing 21 million viewers in the first minute after 60 million initial clicks being "considered a reasonably good result."
  • Smaller channels can copy the structure: deliver the promise fast, re-engage on a schedule, avoid signalling the end.

The guide's retention timeline

SectionWhat the guide says
First minute"The most important minute of each video"; the team goes "above and beyond" to make it the best it can be
Minutes 1-3Transition "from hype to execution"; around minute 3, re-engage viewers "because they could get bored of the story and click off"
Minutes 3-6"The next most important part"; plan "the most exciting and interesting content that is also very simple"
Minute 6 onwardUsually "another re-engagement" around 6 minutes; viewers are now invested, so less intense content can sit in the back half
Ending"Don't ever signal the end of the video" unless building hype for a final payoff; endings "must always be abrupt"

The guide also describes a "wow factor", "anything that no other youtuber can do", and says the channel must never lose it. That is the part most creators cannot copy, and do not need to.

Expectations: the thread through all of it

The guide's argument starts with the thumbnail. It says the title and thumbnail "set the expectations for the viewer", and gives an example: if the thumbnail shows a yellow bouncy castle and the video shows a red one, viewers feel misled. "You'd feel like you were lied to and click off."

This matches YouTube's own retention advice. YouTube's key moments help page says a high share of viewers still watching after 30 seconds suggests the opening matched the title and thumbnail. The lesson for every channel is the same: show what you promised, early.

What small channels can take from it

Treat the first minute as its own project

Script and edit the first minute separately, after the rest of the video is done. Open on the promise, cut greetings and channel news, and show progress fast. The guide's example of a man surviving in the woods: rather than covering only day one in the opening, cover several days in the first three minutes so the viewer is "super invested".

Schedule re-engagements

You do not need a crane or a giant prize. A re-engagement can be a reveal, a twist, a new challenge, a number that surprises, or a new open question. Put one around the 3-minute mark and another around 6 minutes in a 10-minute video, then check whether your retention graph flattens there.

Keep the middle simple

The guide asks for content that is exciting "and also very simple". Complex set-ups in the middle are where viewers drift.

Do not announce the end

"In conclusion" and "that's it for today" tell viewers they can leave. End on the payoff and go straight to an end screen.

What not to copy

  • The budget. Most of MrBeast's re-engagements are expensive spectacles. Yours can be information, story or humour.
  • The pace for every niche. A calm explainer or a long interview does not need constant intensity.
  • Abrupt endings that feel rude. End fast, but do not cut mid-sentence.
  • Treating 21 million first-minute leavers as normal. That figure is relative to an audience of tens of millions.

Should every video end abruptly?
