Key takeaways

  • Shorter videos keep learners. Guo, Kim and Rubin's study of 6.9 million edX viewing sessions found "shorter videos are much more engaging."
  • Get to the point. Nielsen Norman Group reports complaints that videos "include lengthy introductions and don't get to the point quickly enough."
  • One task per video. NN/g recommends "multiple videos of low granularity (one for each step)" because "videos move at the creator's pace and not necessarily at the viewer's pace."
  • Captions are not optional: NN/g found "26% of video views are with subtitles" on its own channel.
  • Put the key information early. Wistia's video length guide advises keeping explain videos focused, with the most important information in the first half.

A retention checklist for training videos

  1. Name the task in the first sentence. "This video shows you how to submit an expense claim in under two minutes."
  2. Skip the agenda slide and the company logo animation.
  3. One task, one video. Link the next video at the end.
  4. Show the real screen or real situation, zoomed and readable on a laptop.
  5. Talk naturally. The edX study found "informal talking-head videos are more engaging" than polished studio recordings.
  6. Change the picture when the topic changes: speaker, screen, example, summary.
  7. Captions and a transcript for accessibility and for people watching at their desk without sound.
  8. End with the action: "Now submit your first claim; the policy link is below."

Worked example

Example: a 60-person company replaces its 45-minute recorded onboarding call with a short video series.

BeforeAfter
One 45-minute recording of a live call, including chat and waitingSeven videos of 2 to 6 minutes each
Opens with 8 minutes of introductionsVideo 1 opens: "Here's what your first week looks like, day by day."
Slides read aloudReal screens: email, calendar, HR system, each zoomed
No captionsCaptions checked for names and system terms
Policies covered in passingSeparate 4-minute video on the policies new hires must read, with the links

HR then checks completion in the learning platform. If most new hires stop in one video, that is the one to cut or split. The structure is illustrative; your platform and team will shape the details. Common mistakes

  • Recording a live session and calling it training.
  • Long branded intros on every video.
  • Covering ten systems in one video.
  • Required compliance videos with no scenarios or checks.
  • Judging success on views instead of whether people can do the task.

FAQ

Is there a guide on retention for training videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks, pacing and structure for £1,720 one-time. NN/g's instructional video guidelines are free.

How long should an onboarding video be?

As short as the task allows. Several short videos usually work better than one long one.

Do training videos need hooks?

A light one: say what the viewer will be able to do by the end, in the first sentence.

Should we record live onboarding sessions?

Recordings are a start, but edit them: cut waiting and chat, split by topic and add captions.

How do we know if a training video works?

Check completion and drop-off, then whether people can do the task afterwards.
