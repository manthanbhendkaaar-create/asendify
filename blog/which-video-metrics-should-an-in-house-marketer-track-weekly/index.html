Which video metrics should an in-house marketer track weekly?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Track a short list every week: how many people stop for your video (hook rate or intro retention), how far they get (quartile completion, ThruPlays or average view duration), whether they act (click-through rate), and what each result costs (cost per result). Add one platform-specific number for organic video, such as average view duration on YouTube. Review weekly to spot problems early, but make big creative decisions monthly, once there's enough data to trust.

Key takeaways

  • Google Ads defines view rate as "views / impressions" and reports "Video played to" 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% (Google Ads Help). Those quartiles are a ready-made retention curve for YouTube ads.
  • On YouTube, "Intro tells you what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds", and average view duration is "Average minutes watched among those who stayed to watch" (YouTube Help).
  • Meta counts a video view at 3 seconds, while ThruPlay counts plays to 97% completion or 15 seconds (MarTech). Don't compare "views" across platforms.
  • Wistia's 2026 data found videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate, a useful reference when you judge short website videos.
  • Fewer metrics, reviewed consistently, beat a 40-column dashboard nobody reads.

Metrics that mislead

  • Raw views. Platforms define them differently, and a 3-second view is a low bar.
  • Total watch time on its own. Longer videos rack it up even if most people leave early.
  • Likes. Nice to see, weakly linked to sales.
  • Averages without the curve. An average can hide a big early drop. Look at the retention graph or quartiles.

Quick checklist for your weekly review

  • The same five metrics, in the same spreadsheet, every week.
  • Each video has a one-line note on why its numbers moved.
  • Clear losers are paused; possible fatigue is flagged, not acted on straight away.
  • Broken links, rejected ads and overspend are checked first.
  • Platform "views" are never compared with each other.
  • Retention graphs or quartiles are checked for any video whose average changed sharply.
  • Monthly decisions are based on four weeks of data, not one.
  • The report fits on one screen so people actually read it.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to improve the numbers you track?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify includes a tracker and spreadsheet for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's help pages on metrics are free.

What's the single most useful video metric?

Cost per result for ads, and average view duration or retention for organic video. Neither tells the whole story alone.

Should I track video metrics daily?

Checking daily for broken ads or overspend is fine. Judge performance weekly and decide monthly.

How do I compare TikTok, Meta and YouTube?

Compare cost per result and click-through rate, which mean roughly the same everywhere. Don't compare "views".

Do I need a dashboard tool?

Not at first. A shared spreadsheet updated weekly is enough for most in-house teams.

What should I do when a metric suddenly drops?

First check for something broken: a paused campaign, a rejected ad, a changed audience, a tracking problem or a landing page that's down. If nothing is broken, look at frequency and creative age, because an ad that has been shown many times to the same people often loses its hook rate first. Only then change the creative, and change one thing at a time so you can see what fixed it.
