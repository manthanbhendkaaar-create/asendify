What are the most expensive memberships in the world?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 7 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The most expensive memberships in the world are private golf and residential clubs, where joining fees of $500,000 to a reported $1 million are paid before annual dues. Montana's Yellowstone Club goes further: you must first buy property there, starting around $10 million. Invitation-only cards such as the American Express Centurion cost far less but are still among the priciest memberships.

This page is general education about how these clubs charge, not financial advice. Fees change and many clubs never publish them, so treat every figure as reported, not confirmed.

Key takeaways

  • The top tier is golf and residential clubs, where the joining fee alone can pass $500,000.
  • Yellowstone Club requires buying property (cheapest plot about $10 million), plus a $500,000 refundable deposit and $78,000 a year in dues, according to Forbes.
  • Many famous clubs, such as Augusta National, do not publish fees at all, so lists online are partly guesswork.
  • Annual dues matter as much as the joining fee: over ten years they can match or exceed it.
  • A high fee usually buys access to something real: a course, a ski mountain, a house or travel perks.

Yellowstone Club: a membership you buy with a house

Forbes describes the Yellowstone Club in Big Sky, Montana, as one of the most exclusive clubs in the world. Membership is capped at 914. Admission requires buying land, a home or a condo inside the club. Forbes reports that the least expensive undeveloped plot costs $10 million, condos start just under $7 million and average $15.5 million, and homes cost $20 million or more.

On top of the property, members pay a $500,000 refundable deposit and annual dues of $78,000. The dues cover unlimited skiing and golf for the member's immediate family. Applicants also need strong references and a background check.

This is why Yellowstone is so costly to join. The fee itself is not the largest on paper, but the property you must buy to qualify is.

Golf clubs with six- and seven-figure joining fees

Golf is where the biggest standalone joining fees appear. Golf Monthly's round-up of the most expensive golf club memberships gives these figures:

  • Madison Club, California. One of the few top clubs that publishes its fees. Membership is capped at 225, the joining fee is $500,000 and annual membership is $70,000.
  • Shanqin Bay, China. The joining fee is said to be $1 million, with around 20 members.
  • Queenwood, England. A 2024 Sunday Times report said members paid £175,000 for a share in the club plus a one-off £48,000 playing privilege fee, with an annual subscription of £13,000 that was rising by £2,160.
  • Beaverbrook, England. The same report put the joining fee at £305,000, believed to make it the UK's most expensive course.
  • Augusta National, USA. Home of the Masters. Nobody outside the club knows who all the members are or what they pay.

Golf Monthly also notes that joining fees of around $500,000 are said to be charged at other US clubs, including Liberty National and Pine Valley.

Invitation-only cards: expensive, but a different league

Charge cards are often called memberships, and the American Express Centurion card is the best-known example. One Mile at a Time reports that the Centurion card has a $10,000 initiation fee and a $5,000 annual fee, charged for each user on the account. That makes the first year $15,000 for one cardholder.

That is a lot for a card, but it is a fraction of a top golf club. What you pay for is mainly travel status, lounge access and concierge service, not a physical place.

Why people pay these fees

Most of these memberships buy something you can use: a private course with no queues, a ski mountain with empty runs, or a tee time whenever you want one. Scarcity is part of the price. Yellowstone caps members at 914 and Madison at 225, so a place has value partly because so few exist.

There is also a status element. Thorstein Veblen described "conspicuous consumption" in The Theory of the Leisure Class in 1899: spending that shows others what you can afford. Later research backs the idea that visible spending is partly about position. A study published by the National Bureau of Economic Research found that spending on visible goods such as cars, clothing and jewellery falls as the average income of a household's reference group rises, which the authors explain with a model of status seeking.

In plain terms, a famous club badge tells other people something about you. That signal is part of what members are buying.

A status purchase with no club attached

Not every expensive membership comes with a course or a house. Some exist only as a statement that you paid. It suits someone who wants a deliberately pointless status object and understands it is not proof of wealth. If you want something to use, a real club is the better choice.

How to read "most expensive" lists online

Lists of expensive memberships often repeat each other and mix confirmed figures with rumour. A few checks help:

  1. Look for the original source. A number credited to a named newspaper or the club itself is stronger than one with no source.
  2. Check the date. Fees rise. A figure from five years ago may be well out of date.
  3. Separate joining fees from dues. A club with a lower joining fee and high dues can cost more over time.
  4. Ask what is required. Some clubs require property, a share purchase or a sponsor, which the headline fee hides.
  5. Accept "unknown" as an answer. Augusta National is the best example: the honest answer is that its fees are not public.

FAQ

What is the most expensive club membership in the world?

On published or reported figures, the Yellowstone Club is usually top because members must buy property costing $10 million or more, plus a $500,000 deposit and $78,000 a year in dues. Shanqin Bay in China is reported to charge a $1 million joining fee.

How much does it cost to join Augusta National?

Nobody outside the club knows. Golf Monthly notes figures from $40,000 to $300,000 have been suggested, but none are confirmed.

What is the most expensive golf club in the UK?

A 2024 Sunday Times report, cited by Golf Monthly, put Beaverbrook's joining fee at £305,000, which it said is believed to make it the UK's most expensive course.

How much is the Amex Centurion card?

One Mile at a Time reports a $10,000 initiation fee and a $5,000 annual fee for each user, so $15,000 in the first year for one cardholder. It is invitation-only.

Are expensive memberships worth it?

It depends on how much you use what the club offers. Count the joining fee and at least ten years of dues, then compare that with paying per visit or per round elsewhere.
