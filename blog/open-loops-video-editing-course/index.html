What does learning open loops actually involve?

An open loop is a question you raise in the viewer's mind and answer later, so they keep watching to get the answer. Our guide to open loops in video editing explains the idea in full. Using them well comes down to four skills:

  • Setting up a clear question that the viewer actually cares about.
  • Placing it where viewers usually leave, such as the intro and the middle.
  • Delaying the answer without making the video feel like it is stalling.
  • Paying it off properly, so the viewer feels rewarded rather than tricked.

Some of this is scripting and some is editing: teaser cuts, callbacks, chapter cards and where the payoff sits on the timeline.

Are there courses that teach open loops?

We have not found a well-known course dedicated only to open loops in video editing. The skill tends to appear as one part of a broader course:

  • Storytelling and scriptwriting courses often cover setting up questions and paying them off, even if they do not use the term "open loop".
  • General YouTube courses sometimes touch on it in lessons about hooks or retention. Check the curriculum before you buy.
  • Retention-focused courses are where it is most likely to get proper time. How can you learn open loops for free?

  1. Read the theory once. Start with our open loops guide. You do not need much more than that to begin.
  2. Map other people's loops. Pick three videos you could not stop watching. Write down every question each video raises and the timestamp where it is answered.
  3. Map your own. Do the same for one of your videos. Many people find long stretches with no open question at all.
  4. Re-cut one video. Add a question to the intro that is answered later, and a new one before each slow section.
  5. Check the data. In YouTube Studio, the audience retention report shows dips, where viewers are "abandoning or skipping", and spikes, where more viewers are "watching, rewatching, or sharing". Compare the graph with your loop map.

Repeat this on every video for a month. Practice on your own work teaches more than watching lessons about it.

What should a good open loops lesson cover?

  • How to raise a question that matches the title and thumbnail. Our guide to writing a hook that matches your title and thumbnail covers this.
  • Where to place loops, especially in the first 30 seconds and before predictable drop-offs.
  • How to stack loops, so a new question opens before the old one closes.
  • How to pay a loop off, and why an unpaid loop costs you trust.
  • The difference between an open loop and clickbait.
  • Practice on your own videos, with someone reviewing your choices.

If a lesson only gives you phrases like "stay to the end to find out", it is teaching a trick, not the skill.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free course on open loops?

Not a dedicated one that we know of. You can learn the basics free by reading a guide such as Asendify's open loops article, mapping the questions in videos you enjoy, and checking where viewers drop off in your own retention graph.

Does the Retention Editing Accelerator teach open loops?

Yes. Week 3 of the six-week Accelerator covers structure and open loops, and Week 2 covers openings built on payoff first, clear stakes and open loops. You apply each week to one of your own videos and get feedback.

Are open loops a scripting skill or an editing skill?

Both. The question is usually set up in the script, but the editor decides where it lands, how long the answer is delayed, and whether teasers, callbacks or chapter cards make the loop stronger.

How long does it take to learn open loops?

You can understand the idea in an afternoon. Using it well takes practice across many videos, because you learn most by comparing where you placed your loops with your retention graph.

Are open loops the same as clickbait?

No. An open loop raises a question and then answers it. Clickbait promises something the video never delivers. If viewers feel tricked, they stop trusting your channel.
