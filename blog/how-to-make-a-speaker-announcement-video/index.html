How do you make a speaker announcement video that sells tickets for your event?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open on the speaker saying their most interesting line, from a past talk or a short recorded message, then state in one sentence what attendees will get from their session, and finish with the date and how to book. Keep it under about 30 seconds for social, and give the speaker their own cut to share, because their audience is often the biggest source of new ticket buyers.

Key takeaways

  • A headshot with "Speaker announcement!" text gives a viewer no reason to stop scrolling; a moving clip of the speaker saying something sharp does.
  • Google's ABCDs say "Hook and sustain attention" and "Say what you'd like viewers to do": hook with the speaker, end with the booking action.
  • TikTok recommends the hook in the first 6 seconds and vertical 9:16; LinkedIn feed video supports 1:1, 9:16 and 16:9.
  • Cvent suggests recording speaker soundbites at this year's event for next year's promos, and providing ready-made assets for speakers.
  • Each speaker sharing their own announcement reaches people the event's account can't.

Where the footage comes from

Three sources, best first:

  1. Footage from a past talk, with permission from the organiser who filmed it. A live audience reaction is the strongest proof.
  2. A 20-second self-recorded message. Send the speaker a short brief: landscape or vertical, eye level, quiet room, and the one line to say ("At [event] I'll show you the three pricing mistakes...").
  3. Last year's soundbites. Cvent's event video guide mentions "recording speaker soundbites for next year's promos" and providing "ready-made assets for speakers and partners". Build this into this year's shoot.

The 25-second structure

  • 0-3 s: the speaker's sharpest line, captioned.
  • 3-10 s: name, role and one line on why they matter to this audience.
  • 10-18 s: the session promise: what attendees will be able to do.
  • 18-25 s: date, city, "Book at..." and the event brand.

Google's ABCDs of effective video ads advise getting "to the heart of the story faster", branding early, and adding a clear call to action: "Say what you'd like viewers to do."

Formats per channel

TikTok's creative best practices recommend vertical 9:16, at least 720p and the hook in the first 6 seconds, with captions or text overlays. Wistia's LinkedIn video guide lists 16:9, 1:1 and 9:16 as supported feed formats, recommends captions for every format and says native uploads are preferred. A practical set is one vertical cut and one square cut per speaker, both captioned.

Example: announcing eight speakers for a marketing conference

Example: a conference releases eight speakers over four weeks.

  • Five speakers have past talk footage; organisers grant permission. Three record a 20-second message at home from a one-page brief.
  • Each video: 22-28 seconds, square and vertical, captioned, opening on the speaker's line. One opens with: "Most brand guidelines are written for designers. Nobody else reads them."
  • Posted two per week on the event's LinkedIn and Instagram.
  • Each speaker gets their files plus suggested post text and a tracked booking link.
  • The organiser compares bookings from each speaker's link to decide whom to feature in paid ads.

Keep it honest

Don't imply a speaker endorses a product or sponsor they haven't agreed to, and get approval of the final cut from each speaker. If you mention limited seats, make sure it's true; see the related answer on scarcity claims below.

A brief to send speakers who record themselves

  • Film in a quiet room, phone at eye level, window in front of you, not behind.
  • Record both vertical and landscape if you can, or follow the format we ask for.
  • Start with your sharpest line, not "Hi, I'm..." (we'll add your name on screen).
  • Say one sentence on what attendees will be able to do after your session.
  • Keep it under 30 seconds and send two or three takes.
  • Use a wired or clip-on mic if you have one; otherwise stay close to the phone.

Measuring which announcements work

Give each speaker's post a tracked booking link and compare clicks and bookings after a week. Also compare the share of viewers who watched past the first few seconds: if one speaker's clip holds people much better, study its opening line and reuse the pattern for the next announcements. Over a few events you'll learn whether past-talk footage or self-recorded messages convert better for your audience.

Common mistakes

  • Static headshot cards only.
  • Opening with the event logo animation.
  • No session promise, just a job title.
  • Not giving speakers their own cut and link.
  • Using talk footage without permission from whoever filmed it.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks and cold opens for short promo videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches cold opens and pacing for YouTube, which carries over to speaker announcements. Google's ABCDs guide is a free alternative.

How long should a speaker announcement video be?

Around 15-30 seconds for social. Longer interviews can go on the event website or YouTube.

What if the speaker has no video footage?

Ask for a short self-recorded message with a clear brief. A phone at eye level in a quiet room is enough.

Should speakers share their own announcement?

Yes. Give them ready-made files, post text and a tracked link; their audience often converts well.

Square or vertical?

Make both. Vertical for Stories, Reels and TikTok; square works well in LinkedIn and Instagram feeds.

Do I need the speaker's approval?

Yes. Ask each speaker to approve the final cut and check the session description is accurate.
