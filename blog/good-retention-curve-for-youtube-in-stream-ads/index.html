What does a good retention curve look like for YouTube in-stream ads?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

For a skippable YouTube in-stream ad, a big drop at about 5 seconds is normal, because that's when the skip button appears. A good curve is one that then flattens: the people who chose to stay keep watching, the quartile figures (25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) fall gently rather than in cliffs, and your brand and main message land before most viewers leave. There is no universal "good" percentage. Compare your ads with each other, cut by cut.

Key takeaways

  • On skippable in-stream ads, "Viewers can skip the ad after 5 seconds", and with cost-per-view bidding "you pay when a viewer watches 30 seconds of your video" (Google Ads Help: video ad formats).
  • Google Ads reports "Video played to" 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%, plus view rate, defined as views divided by impressions (Google Ads Help: view metrics). These are your retention curve.
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "Brand early, often, and richly" and to "Get to the heart of the story faster".
  • Eye-tracking research by Teixeira, Wedel and Pieters found brand presence alone increases ad avoidance, but showing the brand briefly and repeatedly ("pulsing") cut zapping by 8 to 10% on average (HBS Working Knowledge).
  • Judge the curve against your goal. An awareness ad needs the brand before the skip; a direct-response ad needs viewers to reach the call to action.

Reading the shape

Sharp fall at 5 s, then another cliffSomething in the ad loses people at that pointFind the scene and cut or rework it

Flat to 30 s, then a big dropPeople stay until they're "counted", then leavePut the call to action before 30 s
Very low 25%The opening doesn't earn the skip decisionTest new first 5 seconds

What "good" looks like in numbers

Google doesn't publish a single benchmark, and averages vary a lot by industry, audience and ad length. Use these internal checks instead:

  1. Compare cuts, not industries. Your best ad is your benchmark.
  2. Look at the gaps between quartiles. A big gap between 25% and 50% points to the first half; a big gap between 75% and 100% suggests a long, slow ending.
  3. Check the 30-second line. For ads longer than 30 seconds on CPV bidding, how many people reach 30 seconds affects what you pay. Our page on why YouTube CPV can be high covers this.
  4. Check where the brand lands. If the brand first appears at 20 seconds but most people skip at 5, most viewers never saw it.

Structuring an in-stream ad for its curve

TimeJobWhy
0 to 5 sHook plus brand (logo, product or spoken name)Everyone sees this, including those who skip
5 to 15 sShow the product working or the main benefitRewards the people who chose to stay
15 to 25 sProof, a second benefit or a quick story beatKeeps interest with a new picture
25 to 30 sCall to action, said and shownLands before many viewers reach 30 s
After 30 sOptional extra detailOnly for people who want more

Brand early doesn't mean a long logo sting. A product shot, the name in speech or a small logo is enough. The pulsing finding suggests short, repeated brand moments rather than one long one. If you can't skip, the rules change: see how to structure a 15-second non-skippable YouTube ad.

Quick checklist for an in-stream ad

  • Brand, product or spoken name appears in the first 5 seconds.
  • The opening gives people a reason to choose not to skip: a problem, a surprise or the result.
  • The main benefit lands by about 15 seconds.
  • The call to action is shown and said before 30 seconds.
  • Brand moments are short and repeated rather than one long logo shot.
  • You have added the "Video played to" columns in Google Ads and noted the biggest gap between quartiles.
  • You are comparing new cuts against your own best ad, not a generic benchmark.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to hold viewers past the skip button?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCDs guidance is free.

Why do so many people leave at 5 seconds?

That's when the skip button appears on skippable in-stream ads. Some drop is unavoidable; a strong first 5 seconds reduces it.

Where do I find the retention data for my ad?

In Google Ads, add the "Video played to" 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% columns. If the ad is a YouTube video, YouTube Studio also shows its retention.

Is a 30-second ad better than a 60-second one?

Often for retention, but not always for results. Test both with the same opening.

Should I put my logo in the first 5 seconds?

Yes, briefly. Google advises branding early, and brief repeated brand moments tend to cause less skipping than one long logo shot.
