What are hook rate and hold rate in video ads, and how do I work them out?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Hook rate is the share of impressions where someone watched your video ad for at least about 3 seconds; hold rate is the share of those people who kept watching to a later point, such as 15 seconds or the end. Neither is an official platform metric. You calculate them yourself from the watch counts each ad platform reports, and together they tell you whether your problem is the opening (low hook rate) or the middle of the ad (low hold rate).

Key takeaways

  • Hook rate is usually worked out as 3-second video plays ÷ impressions on Meta. Madgicx's glossary states plainly that "hook rate is not a metric Meta reports".
  • Hold rate has no single definition. Pick one (for example ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays) and use it consistently.
  • TikTok reports 2-second and 6-second views and plays at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%; Google Ads reports "video played to" 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%.
  • A low hook rate means change the first frames. A good hook rate with a poor hold rate means the middle of the ad is losing people.
  • Compare ads of similar length aimed at the same audience; ratios from very different ads are not comparable.

Why split attention into two numbers

A single "view rate" mixes two separate failures. Some people never stop scrolling. Others stop, watch a few seconds and leave because the ad doesn't keep its promise. Each needs a different fix, so it helps to measure them separately:

RatioQuestion it answersTypical fix
Hook rateDid the opening stop the scroll?New first frame, on-screen text, opening line
Hold rateDid the people who stopped keep watching?Tighter middle, faster proof, a reason to stay
Click-through rateDid watchers act?Clearer offer and call to action

How to calculate them on each platform

Meta (Facebook and Instagram). The common formula, as Madgicx's hook rate glossary sets it out, is "3-second video plays ÷ impressions". For hold rate, many advertisers divide ThruPlays by 3-second plays. Check which ThruPlay length you are using: Meta's ThruPlay has long counted plays of 15 seconds, or near-complete plays of shorter videos, and PPC Land reported on 16 November 2026 that Ads Manager is adding a choice of 6 or 15 seconds, with 15 still the default.

TikTok. TikTok's own video play metrics include "2-second video views", "6-second video views" and views at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of length. A practical hook rate is 2-second views ÷ impressions; a hold rate is 6-second views ÷ 2-second views, or plays at 50% ÷ 2-second views for longer ads.

YouTube (Google Ads). Google's guide to YouTube ad view metrics explains that "video played to" metrics count the share of people who reached 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% of the video. Skippable in-stream ads also have a TrueView view rate (views ÷ impressions), where a view is 30 seconds or the end of the ad, whichever comes first. Use played to 25% as your early-hold signal and played to 75% or 100% as your late-hold signal.

PlatformHook rate (suggested)Hold rate (suggested)
Meta3-second plays ÷ impressionsThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays
TikTok2-second views ÷ impressions6-second views ÷ 2-second views
YouTubePlayed to 25% (early signal)Played to 75% ÷ played to 25%

These are working definitions, not platform standards. Write yours down so everyone on the team compares like with like.

What to fix when each number is low

Low hook rate. Start on the most interesting moment, add on-screen text that works without sound and cut logo stings. The playbook's cold-open rule and the checklist in how to make the first 3 seconds of a video ad stop people scrolling cover this in detail. Google's ABCDs say the same thing in one line: "Get to the heart of the story faster."

Low hold rate. The opening made a promise the next seconds don't keep. Show proof sooner, remove repeated lines, add a visual change every few seconds and open a question the ad answers later. If people leave just before a skip button appears, see how to stop viewers skipping your YouTube ad after 5 seconds.

Both fine, few clicks. Retention isn't the problem. Look at the offer, the call to action and the landing page.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing a 6-second ad's hold rate with a 60-second ad's.
  • Judging after a few hundred impressions, when small changes in counts swing the ratio.
  • Changing the hook and the body in the same test, so you can't tell which helped.
  • Chasing hook rate with a misleading opening. The UK's CAP Code says ads "must not materially mislead", and viewers who feel tricked leave anyway.

Keep a weekly view

Record hook rate and hold rate for each live ad every week. A falling hook rate with rising frequency usually means the opening is tiring; see how to tell if a video ad has creative fatigue.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to raise hook rate and hold rate?

The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing for video, at £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCDs and TikTok's creative best practices are free alternatives.

Is hook rate an official Meta metric?

No. You calculate it from Meta's 3-second video plays and impressions.

What is a good hook rate?

There's no official benchmark. Compare against your own past ads of similar length, audience and placement, and aim to beat your average.

Which matters more, hook rate or hold rate?

Neither on its own. A high hook rate with a poor hold rate wastes the attention you won; fix whichever is weaker first.
