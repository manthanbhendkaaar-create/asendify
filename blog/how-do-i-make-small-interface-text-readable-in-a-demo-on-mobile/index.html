How do I make small interface text readable in a demo on mobile?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Zoom into the part of the screen that matters, record at a high resolution so zooming does not blur, highlight the one element you are talking about, and test the exported video on a real phone at normal reading distance. A full desktop screen shrunk onto a phone makes interface text unreadable; the fix is to show less of the screen at a time, not to show the same screen for longer. Add captions for what you say and short callouts for what the viewer should look at.

Key takeaways

  • Google's video guidance says to "keep visuals bright and high-contrast" for all devices, to keep messaging simple and "avoid overloading" (Google Ads Help).
  • A LinkedIn marketer writing in IAB UK advises overlays "stick to a maximum of 14 words per scene", over at most three lines, with drop shadows or semi-transparent panels for legibility, and reports "79% of videos are watched on mute" on LinkedIn (IAB UK). Figures are LinkedIn's own.
  • Mayer's signaling principle says "people learn better when there are cues that emphasize information that is more important" (Dartmouth). Highlights and zooms are cues.
  • YouTube's retention report says "dips mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part" (YouTube Help). A screen no one can read is a common cause of a dip in demos.

Why interface text fails on mobile

Software interfaces are designed for a desktop monitor viewed at arm's length. A 1920-pixel-wide screen shrunk to fit a phone that is about the width of a hand makes a button label tiny. Viewers on a phone often cannot read the text, even if they can see the layout. They cannot lean closer, and they will not pause and zoom. They leave.

There is a second problem: many demos show the whole screen the whole time. The viewer does not know where to look, and the speaker says "click here" without any visual guide. The solution is to guide the eye and enlarge what matters.

Step-by-step: make it readable

  1. Decide the viewing device. If most of your audience watches on phones, edit for phones first.
  2. Record at high resolution. Record the screen at the highest resolution your computer supports so zooming in later keeps text crisp.
  3. Prepare the interface. Close extra tabs, hide personal data, set browser zoom to 125 to 150 per cent or increase the interface size if the layout allows, and turn off notifications.
  4. Script the sequence. Write the steps so each shows one control or area. Avoid showing 12 things at once.
  5. Zoom in on every step. In the editor, keyframe a zoom to the active area; ease the movement so it does not jump.
  6. Highlight the target. A soft highlight, box or dim of the surroundings tells viewers where to look.
  7. Use short callouts. Label the control in a few words. IAB advice caps overlays at 14 words per scene.
  8. Keep text on screen long enough to read. As a rule of thumb from editing practice, not research, allow time to read it aloud twice.
  9. Add captions for speech. Position them so they do not cover the interface you are explaining. Check the product names.
  10. Export and test on a phone. Play at normal reading distance, in a bright room, with the screen not at full brightness. If you squint, zoom more.

Layout rules for mobile demos

  • Safe areas. Platform interfaces overlay the video (progress bars, buttons, captions). Keep key text away from the edges and the bottom area.
  • Contrast. Use a semi-transparent panel behind callouts, as IAB suggests, or a light drop shadow.
  • Hierarchy. One main element per scene, not three competing highlights.
  • Consistent callout style. The same font, colour and position across the video lets the viewer's eye learn where to look.
  • Avoid thin fonts. Light weights disappear on small screens.

What to cut

  • Whole-screen tours. Show the one area that matters.
  • Long menus read aloud. Show the one item you use.
  • Small print. If it cannot be read on a phone, do not rely on it; say it in the voice.
  • Fast scrolling. If you must scroll, slow down and stop on the target.
  • Unreadable dashboards. Show the one number and explain it.

Example: a billing dashboard demo

Example: a SaaS team records a 60-second demo of a billing dashboard. The first version shows the full screen for the whole video; the sidebar labels and the invoice table are too small on a phone. The re-edit zooms into the invoice table, highlights the "Status" column, shows a three-word callout ("Filter unpaid"), and then zooms to the export button. Captions sit above the bottom controls. The team checks the exported video on a phone and compares drop-off at the table step. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result.

Checking for accessibility

Do not rely on colour alone to show a highlight; use shape or position as well. Make sure captions follow the speech accurately; the W3C notes captions should include dialogue, speaker identification and meaningful sound effects (W3C). Avoid flashing effects that can affect some viewers.

How do I stop text from blurring when I zoom?

Record at high resolution and zoom within it.

How many words per callout?

Keep it short; one practitioner advises a maximum of 14 words per scene.

Should I make a vertical version?

If your audience is on phones, yes; reframe around the active area.
