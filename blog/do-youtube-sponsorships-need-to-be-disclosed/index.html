Do I have to disclose sponsorships and gifted products on YouTube?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Yes. If a video includes a sponsorship, endorsement, paid product placement or other commercial relationship, YouTube requires you to tick the paid promotion option in the video details, which adds a disclosure label at the start of the video. In the UK, the advertising regulator also expects ads to be clearly labelled upfront, and government guidance says the rules apply even if you only received a free or discounted product, however many followers you have.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help says you have to select the paid promotion button for branded content, sponsorships and endorsements; this adds a disclosure label at the beginning of the video.
  • Branded content must also follow YouTube's Community Guidelines and Google Ads policies, and creators and partners are responsible for legal requirements.
  • The UK Advertising Standards Authority is likely to expect a prominent "ad" label upfront, usually meaning at the beginning.
  • GOV.UK guidance for content creators applies if you received a product free or discounted, or other incentives such as payment or commission, no matter how many followers you have.
  • YouTube's branded content settings also let you add age or country restrictions to help meet local advertising rules.

How to disclose properly on YouTube

  1. In YouTube Studio, open the video details, find Paid promotion and tick that the video includes paid promotion or branded content.
  2. Say it in the video as well, at the start of the sponsored part, in plain words: "This video is sponsored by…" or "Ad: …".
  3. Label it in the description near the top, with "Ad" or "Sponsored", before the fold.
  4. For Shorts, put a clear label in the video and description; there is little time for viewers to notice otherwise.
  5. Add restrictions if the product has age or country rules, using YouTube's custom age and country settings for branded content.

The ASA warns against relying only on a channel bio or earlier posts, and says labels must be clear to the average person.

Does disclosure hurt views or retention?

There is little reason to think an honest label upfront damages a good video. What tends to lose viewers is a long, unrelated sponsor read in the middle of a story, not the label itself. Placing sponsor segments where they interrupt least and keeping them short and relevant usually matters more than the disclosure.

Worked example

Example: Maya, a tech reviewer with 40,000 subscribers, is sent a free pair of headphones and is paid £800 for a 60-second segment on a different brand's VPN.

ItemWhat she does
Headphones (gifted)Says "These were sent to me free by the brand" at the start of the review; adds "Gifted" at the top of the description
VPN (paid)Ticks paid promotion in YouTube Studio; opens the segment with "This part is an ad for…"
Affiliate linksLabels them "affiliate links: I earn commission" next to the links
Age rulesNo restriction needed for these products

She keeps her audience's trust and meets YouTube's and the UK's expectations. The figures are an illustration, not a real case or legal advice.

Working with brands and agencies

Put disclosure in the contract. Agree who writes the label, where it appears, and that the paid promotion box will be ticked. Brands are also responsible under UK rules, so a reputable brand will want this done properly. If you work with an editor or agency, add disclosure to your pre-upload checklist alongside titles and thumbnails, so it is never forgotten on a busy week.

A simple disclosure template

Creators often freeze on wording. Plain, short labels work best.

PlacementExample wording
Spoken, start of segment"This part of the video is an ad for [brand]."
Spoken, gifted item"[Brand] sent me this for free; they haven't seen this video."
On-screen text"AD" or "Paid partnership with [brand]" while the segment plays
Description, first line"Ad: this video is sponsored by [brand]."
Affiliate links"Affiliate links: I earn a commission if you buy."

Keep the wording honest about what the brand did and did not control. If the brand approved the script, do not imply the review is fully independent.

Keeping records

Keep a simple record of each brand deal: what you received, the date, the agreed disclosure and a link to the video. If a viewer, brand or regulator ever asks, you can show what was agreed and that you followed it.

Do small channels have to disclose?

Is "#ad" enough?

A clear "ad" label upfront is what the ASA is likely to expect. On YouTube, also tick the paid promotion box and say it in the video.

Do I need to disclose if I bought the product myself?

There is no ad to disclose if there is no commercial relationship, but saying you bought it builds trust.

You risk breaking YouTube's policies and UK advertising and consumer rules, and losing viewers' trust.
