Why do new and returning viewers have different retention on YouTube?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Returning viewers already know your style and trust you, so they usually watch longer and forgive a slow start, while new viewers decide within seconds whether the video matches what they clicked. YouTube's retention report lets you split the curve by new and returning viewers, subscribers and non-subscribers, so you can see which group a drop-off comes from and fix the right thing.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's retention report can be segmented into new and returning viewers, subscribers and non-subscribers, and organic and paid traffic.
  • YouTube says subscribers have been shown to watch twice as much video as non-subscribers.
  • New viewers are people watching your channel for the first time in the selected period; regular viewers have returned for at least six months in the past year.
  • A weak intro usually hurts new viewers most; a mid-video sag that hits returning viewers too is a content or pacing problem.
  • Read segments about two days after upload, once retention data has processed.

What YouTube lets you compare

YouTube's guide to key moments for audience retention says segment views let you compare new and returning viewers, subscribers and non-subscribers, and organic and paid traffic. It also explains the labels on the curve: the intro (share still watching after 30 seconds), top moments, spikes and dips, and notes that retention data typically takes one to two days to process.

YouTube's new, casual and regular viewers tips define the groups: new viewers watched your channel for the first time in the period, casual viewers return occasionally, and regular viewers have returned for at least six months in the past year. It adds that "subscribers have been shown to watch twice as much video as non-subscribers".

Why the difference matters for growth

YouTube's unique viewers help page notes that viewers are, on average, subscribed to dozens of channels, and that subscriber counts are not the best measure of audience size. Growth depends on new viewers liking what they find. If your returning viewers love a video but new viewers leave in 20 seconds, the video serves your community but will struggle to reach beyond it.

Worked example: one video, two audiences

Example: a 12-minute woodworking video, two days after upload.

SegmentStill watching at 30 sBiggest dipAverage percentage viewed
Returning viewers78%7:20 (long sanding sequence)61%
New viewers46%0:10 to 0:25 (channel news before the project)34%

Diagnosis: the opening channel news costs new viewers early, while the sanding sequence costs everyone later. Fixes for the next video: move channel news to the end and speed up the sanding with a time-lapse. Returning viewers lose nothing; new viewers reach the project in seconds. The figures are illustrative.

Subscribers vs non-subscribers, and organic vs paid

The same report offers two more splits, and each answers a different question:

  • Subscribers vs non-subscribers shows whether people who chose to follow you behave differently from people who found the video another way. A large gap at the start suggests the opening relies on familiarity.
  • Organic vs paid matters if you run ads for a video. Viewers who arrive from an ad did not pick the video from a feed or search, so they may behave differently; judge the edit on organic viewers and the ad on its own numbers.

Use the split that matches the decision you are making. For editing choices, new vs returning and organic traffic usually tell you the most.

A routine for every upload

  1. Wait about 48 hours.
  2. Open the retention report and switch to the new vs returning view.
  3. Note the 30-second figure for each group.
  4. Find the biggest dip for each group and watch the 15 seconds before it.
  5. Write one change for the next video: one for new viewers, one for everyone.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Judging only the combined curve. It hides who is leaving.
  • Optimising only for regulars. Comfortable for the community, limiting for growth.
  • Over-explaining for newcomers. Context in seconds, not minutes; regulars will skip a long recap.
  • Reading tiny segments. On small videos, a segment may only have a few dozen viewers, so wait for more data.
  • Changing everything at once. Make one change for new viewers and one for everyone, so you can tell which one moved the curve on the next upload.

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor reads one channel with read-only access and shows drop-off points with a fix for each. YouTube Studio's retention report is the free alternative.

Where do I compare new and returning viewers?

In the video's audience retention report in YouTube Studio, using the segment options for new and returning viewers.

Do subscribers watch longer?

YouTube says subscribers have been shown to watch twice as much video as non-subscribers.

What counts as a regular viewer?

YouTube defines regular viewers as people who have returned for at least six months in the past year.

Should I make videos for new viewers or regulars?

Both. Keep openings clear for newcomers and give regulars depth later in the video or in other uploads.
