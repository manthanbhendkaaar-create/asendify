Do auto-zoom screen recorders make SaaS demo videos easier to watch?

Key takeaways

  • Screen Studio's guide says its zooms are generated from mouse clicks, and that "if there are no clicks in this part of the recording, the zoom follows the cursor's movement". Each zoom can be edited, cut or replaced.
  • TechSmith says Camtasia SmartFocus "follows mouse movements, scrolling activity, and clicks" and its points can be adjusted after preview.
  • Wistia's State of Video analysis suggests 1 to 5 minutes for educate-or-explain videos such as demos.
  • None of the vendors publish evidence that auto-zoom raises retention. Judge it on your own audience graph.

What auto-zoom does and does not do

A SaaS demo has a specific problem: the interface is small. On a phone or in a YouTube player the text in a menu can be hard to read. A zoom brings the relevant part of the screen up so the viewer does not have to hunt for it.

Auto-zoom tools build those zooms for you. Screen Studio generates zooms from your clicks, focusing on the cursor just before a click. Camtasia SmartFocus follows mouse movement, scrolling and clicks on recordings made with its own recorder. Both let you adjust what they produce.

The tools see your cursor, not your intention. If you wave the mouse around while talking, you may get zooms that follow nothing useful. If the important change happens on a part of the screen you did not click, the tool will not zoom to it.

Steps to use auto-zoom well

  1. Plan the path. Write the five or six screens the demo will visit and the one thing viewers should notice on each.
  2. Move slowly and deliberately. Rest the cursor where you want attention. Quick, wandering moves create jumpy zooms.
  3. Click on the thing you are talking about. Click-driven zooms only land where you click.
  4. Record, then watch the raw auto-zoom once. Mark zooms that follow the wrong thing or happen too often.
  5. Cut the unhelpful zooms. A zoom on empty space is worse than none. Use the tool's edit controls.
  6. Add manual zooms for typing and results. Screen Studio's guide describes a fixed manual zoom for things like typing into a text field.
  7. Keep zooms off when the whole screen matters. If the point is the layout, show it whole.
  8. Check on a phone. Play the export at phone size and read every label.
  9. Keep the length honest. Wistia's data points to 1 to 5 minutes for how-tos and demos.
  10. Publish and read the graph. Look for dips right after fast zooms.

Example: a 3-minute invoice-reminder demo

Example: a SaaS team records a demo of an invoice reminder feature. The first pass has 14 auto-zooms. On review, four follow the cursor while the narrator introduces the feature, and two zoom on a sidebar that is not part of the story. They remove those six, leaving eight. They add one manual zoom on the reminder text box while the narrator types, and cut the first ten seconds of setup. They test the export on a phone and find the date field is now readable. When auto-zoom is the wrong tool

Skip it for demos where layout, dashboards or comparisons are the point. Skip it where fast zooming causes motion discomfort, and consider a slower setting if one exists. For web-based recorders, check the vendor's page for what the tool can and cannot capture, since support differs between tools.

Do not skip the opening

Auto-zoom polishes the middle of the video. It does not fix a slow start. Show the result the viewer wants in the first seconds and then demonstrate how to get there. Can I edit auto-zooms afterwards?

Yes in both tools checked. Screen Studio lets you drag, cut or replace zooms; Camtasia lets you move, resize or retime zoom and pan points.

Does auto-zoom work on Windows?

Screen Studio's guide covers Mac only. Camtasia works with recordings made in its own recorder. Check each vendor's current system requirements.

Does auto-zoom increase watch time?

No vendor page checked offers evidence either way. Test on your own audience.

How long should a SaaS demo be?

Wistia's analysis suggests 1 to 5 minutes for how-to and demo content.

Should I zoom on every click?

No. Zoom where the viewer needs to read or notice something, and cut the rest.
