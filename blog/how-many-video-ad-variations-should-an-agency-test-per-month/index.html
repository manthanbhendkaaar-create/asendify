How many video ad variations should an agency test per month?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Test as many variations as the budget can judge, not as many as you can make. For most clients that means roughly 4 to 10 new variations a month, run in rounds of two to five, each round testing one clear idea (a new hook, a new angle or a new format). TikTok suggests putting 20 to 30% of monthly budget into testing new variations, and Meta's testing tool compares two to five ads at a time. A small budget split across 30 variations just produces noise.

Key takeaways

  • TikTok's video advertising guide advises you to "Dedicate 20–30% of your budget each month to testing new variations" and to refresh creatives every 2 to 4 weeks (the guide gives no study for these figures).
  • Meta's creative testing tool lets you test "between two and five" ads, and Meta recommends no more than 20% of budget for the test; Jon Loomer suggests aiming for about 50 optimised events a week for meaningful results (Jon Loomer).
  • Facebook-commissioned research found "70% of the potential ROI from video ads comes from the creative itself" (Search Engine Journal), which is why testing creative is worth the budget.
  • Facebook's own creator guidance says "A/B test your videos to learn what resonates" (Meta for Creators).
  • Make variations meaningfully different. Tiny changes need huge budgets to show a real difference.

How budget limits the number of tests

The number of variations you can judge depends on how many results each one gets. A rough way to plan:

Monthly ad spendCost per resultResults a monthSensible new variations a month
£1,000£20502 to 4
£5,000£202504 to 8
£20,000£201,0008 to 15
£50,000+£202,500+15 or more

These are planning guides, not rules. If a variation can't collect enough results to compare within a couple of weeks, you have too many.

What to vary, in order of impact

LevelExampleWhy test it
Angle or messagePrice saving versus time savingBiggest differences in results
Hook (first 3 seconds)Face versus product versus bold textDecides whether people stop
FormatTalking head versus demo versus UGC-styleDifferent audiences respond to different formats
Length15 s versus 30 sChanges completion and cost
Call to action"Get a quote" versus "See prices"Smaller, but easy
Colour, music, caption styleMinorUsually small effects; test last

Start at the top. Only test small details once you have a winner at the bigger levels. Hooks deserve their own process, covered in how to A/B test video ad hooks properly.

A monthly testing rhythm for an agency

  1. Week 1: launch round one: 3 hooks on the current best body.
  2. Week 2: read early signals (hook rate, hold) and cut obvious losers.
  3. Week 3: launch round two: a new angle or format built on the winning hook.
  4. Week 4: confirm the winner on cost per result, move it into the main campaign, and report.
  5. Ongoing: watch for fatigue in the main ads. Our page on how to tell if a video ad has creative fatigue covers the signs.

Worked example

Example: an agency runs Meta ads for a home-cleaning service spending £6,000 a month, at about £25 per booking.

ItemPlan
Testing budget (25%)£1,500
Expected bookings from testsAbout 60
Round 1 (weeks 1 to 2)3 hooks on the same 20 s body, about £250 each
Round 2 (weeks 3 to 4)3 variations: winning hook with a new angle (before/after, price, booking speed)
New variations this month6
Main campaignCurrent best ad plus last month's winner

With about 20 bookings per variation, the agency can spot large differences but not small ones, so it tests big ideas rather than button colours. The figures are illustrative.

Mistakes that waste the testing budget

  • Too many variations at once, each with too little spend to judge.
  • Changing several things in one variation, so you can't tell what worked.
  • Calling winners on 3-second views alone. Check cost per result too.
  • Stopping tests after a day. Delivery settles over several days.
  • Never retesting old winners. Audiences change; a past loser can win later.

Quick checklist for a month of creative tests

  • The testing budget is agreed with the client as a share of monthly spend.
  • Each round tests one idea: an angle, a hook, a format or a length.
  • Each variation can realistically collect enough results to compare.
  • Every variation changes only the element being tested.
  • Results are judged on cost per result as well as hook rate and hold.
  • Winners move into the main campaign; losers are logged with what you learned.
  • Main ads are checked weekly for signs of fatigue.
  • The monthly report shows what was tested, what won and what's next.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks worth testing?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook by Asendify covers hooks and includes a tracker for £1,720 one-time. TikTok's creative guide is free.

How long should each test run?

Long enough for each variation to collect a meaningful number of results, often one to two weeks.

Should I use Meta's dynamic creative instead of manual tests?

It's useful for finding combinations quickly, but manual tests make it clearer which idea won. Many agencies use both.

How much should I spend on testing?

TikTok suggests 20 to 30% of monthly budget. Meta recommends keeping a creative test under 20%. Pick a level the client agrees to.

How do I explain testing costs to a client?

Frame tests as finding the next winner before the current ad tires, and report what each round learned.
