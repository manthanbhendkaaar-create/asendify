How do I make a real estate market update video that people watch to the end?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open with the one change that matters to your viewer ("prices in our area were flat this quarter, but homes are selling faster"), then spend the rest of the video on what it means for buyers, sellers and landlords. Keep the main update short (around one to three minutes for social, longer only for a YouTube deep dive with chapters), show each number on screen with its source, and finish with one clear next step. Most market updates lose viewers because they read out a page of statistics before saying why anyone should care.

Key takeaways

  • Google's ABCDs for video advise you to "get to the heart of the story faster" and to "keep messaging and language focused and simple".
  • Wistia's 2026 length data reports that engagement "naturally declines after the five-minute mark" and that most viewers leave around the halfway point.
  • Nielsen Norman Group's video guidelines say a common complaint is videos that "don't get to the point quickly enough", and recommend chapters for longer videos.
  • One headline number plus what it means beats ten numbers read aloud.
  • Always name the data source and period on screen; viewers trust (and stay with) numbers they can check.

Tell viewers early what's coming ("in two minutes: what this means if you're selling, and if you're buying"). That small promise is an open loop: it gives people a reason to stay for their section.

Get the numbers right

Use official or clearly named sources and say which period they cover. In the UK that typically means the UK House Price Index from HM Land Registry and the ONS; in other countries use the equivalent official or industry body, or your own portal and sales data, labelled as such. Rules of thumb:

  • Don't mix periods. "Up 3% on last year" and "down 1% on last month" both need labels.
  • Small samples swing. Ten sales in one postcode can move a lot; say so.
  • Don't predict as fact. "Some lenders expect…" is fine with a source; "prices will rise" is not.
  • Keep it accurate. A market update is marketing. In the UK, property marketing must not mislead under consumer protection law; National Trading Standards publishes guidance for agents on what information buyers need.

Editing for retention

  1. Cut the welcome. Your name and brand can sit in a lower third while you deliver the hook.
  2. Change the picture every few seconds while numbers are being discussed: a simple chart, a street shot, a listing photo, a caption of the figure.
  3. Caption everything. Many people watch property content muted on their phones.
  4. One idea per caption. "Average time to sell: 34 days (Q3)" not a full sentence.
  5. Signpost sections with a title card ("Selling?") so viewers know their part has arrived.
  6. Add chapters on YouTube for longer versions so people can jump to buyers or sellers.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to hook viewers in a market update video?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCDs guidance is a free alternative.

How long should a real estate market update video be?

Around one to three minutes for social. A longer YouTube version works if it has chapters and a clear structure.

How often should I post a market update?

Monthly or quarterly, matched to how often reliable data is published for your area.

Should I show charts?

Yes, simple ones: one figure per chart, labelled with source and period.

Can I say prices will rise?

Not as a fact. Attribute forecasts to a named source and present them as forecasts.
