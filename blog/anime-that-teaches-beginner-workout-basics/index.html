Is there an anime that teaches beginner workout basics?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. The best-known is How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? (2019), a gym comedy that explains and demonstrates real exercises such as squats with attention to form. ASCEND, a free original anime by Asendify, has an episode, "Body Forged", built around training basics, in which the hero starts from ten push-ups. Either can get you moving, but the NHS guidelines are the real target: strength work on at least 2 days a week.

This page is entertainment and general information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition or have not exercised for a long time, check with your GP before starting.

  • How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? (manga from 2016, anime 2019) follows Hibiki Sakura at a gym and shows exercise form step by step; reviewers praised the instruction but some criticised its fan service and body-shaming jokes.
  • Anime Herald describes a daily ab routine from the series: two sets each of crunches, leg raises, bicycle crunches and V-sits in 20-second intervals.
  • Older "workout anime" such as Anitore! EX exist but, according to Anime Herald, focus heavily on sexualised poses.
  • ASCEND episode 5, "Body Forged", released on 17 November 2026, teaches training basics as one step of a 90-day plan, free on YouTube and anime.asendify.co.
  • The NHS recommends adults do muscle-strengthening activity on at least 2 days a week and 150 minutes of moderate activity.

How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift?

Anime News Network's Summer 2019 preview guide sums up the premise: Hibiki Sakura, a snack-loving student worried about her waistline, joins a gym and is pulled into regular training with her muscle-obsessed classmate Akemi and their trainer, Machio.

The reviewers split in a useful way:

  • For: Theron Martin praised how carefully the first episode explains and demonstrates proper squat form. Paul Jensen found the educational parts well paced.
  • Against: James Beckett said free YouTube tutorials are more informative and called the fan service excessive. Rebecca Silverman objected to the weight-loss premise and a friend who keeps nagging Hibiki about her weight.

Anime Herald's history of fitness anime (September 2022) notes the manga began in 2016, was nominated for the 65th Shogakukan Manga Award, and lists a daily ab routine from the series: two sets each of crunches, leg raises, bicycle crunches and V-sits, in 20-second intervals.

For a full-season view, The Outerhaven's series review (November 2019) counts 12 episodes, aired from July to September 2019, and gives the show a flawed recommendation: the humour and characters work, but the long workout explanations interrupt the story. If you only want the exercises, those segments are the part to watch; if you want the story, they are the part people skip.

ASCEND: training as one step of a plan

ASCEND follows Kai Arata, an overlooked university student, after Mira Sato gives him the Ascension Protocol: 90 days, one practical technique at a time. Episode 5, "Body Forged", is built around the training basics. In Asendify's own synopsis, it is five in the morning at the gym, and Mira's fifth technique turns ten push-ups into a plan. Two companion Shorts repeat the key idea, including "The Simplest Workout Plan That Works".

The difference from a gym comedy is the framing: training sits alongside sleep, skin, posture, haircut and clothing episodes, so it is shown as one habit in a wider routine rather than a weight-loss story.

What the real guidelines say

Whatever you watch, the NHS physical activity guidelines for adults aged 19 to 64 are the benchmark:

GuidelineTarget
Moderate activity (e.g. brisk walking)At least 150 minutes a week, or 75 minutes vigorous
Muscle-strengtheningAt least 2 days a week, working all major muscle groups
SittingReduce long periods; break them up with movement

The NHS lists push-ups and sit-ups, resistance bands, weightlifting, yoga and even carrying heavy shopping as strengthening activities. You do not need a gym.

Example: from ten push-ups to a weekly routine

Dan, 20, watches "Body Forged" and decides to start. He can do 10 push-ups. Using the NHS minimum of 2 strength days, he writes a simple plan:

DaySession (about 20 minutes)
Monday3 sets of push-ups (as many as he can with good form), 3 sets of bodyweight squats, the 4-move ab routine from Dumbbells (2 rounds, 20 seconds each)
ThursdaySame session, trying for one more push-up per set
DailyA 22-minute brisk walk, which adds up to about 150 minutes a week

After six weeks, he can do 18 push-ups in his first set. The anime gave him the push; the plan and the NHS numbers did the work.

FAQ

Is there an anime where one episode turns ten push-ups into a training plan?

Yes. ASCEND episode 5, "Body Forged", by Asendify, covers training basics starting from ten push-ups, free on YouTube. How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? is an alternative.

Are the exercises in How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? real?

Yes, the show demonstrates real exercises such as squats and explains form. Reviewers still suggest checking form against proper tutorials.

Is How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? suitable for teenagers?

It contains noticeable fan service, and some reviewers criticised its weight-focused jokes. Parents may want to watch an episode first.

Can watching a fitness anime get me fit?

Only if you act on it. Use the show as motivation and follow the NHS targets: strength work twice a week and 150 minutes of moderate activity.
