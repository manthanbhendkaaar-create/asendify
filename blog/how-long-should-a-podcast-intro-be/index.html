How long should a podcast intro be?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Keep a podcast intro short enough that listeners hear real content within roughly the first 30 to 60 seconds. A good intro is a hook from the episode, one line on who this is and why it matters, and then straight in. Long music beds, sponsor thank-yous and housekeeping before the content are the usual reasons people leave early.

Key takeaways

  • There's no official length, but platforms agree the opening is where most listeners decide to stay or go.
  • Get to something worth hearing within the first 30 to 60 seconds.
  • A tight intro is: hook, who and why, start. Music can sit under it, not in front of it.
  • Move housekeeping (reviews, socials, Patreon) to after the first segment or the end.
  • Check the first minute in your Spotify retention graph or YouTube's Intro figure, and cut until it holds.

What the platforms say about the opening

No platform sets a required intro length, but they all point at the same risk. Spotify's guide to episode performance says "listeners have a tendency to drop off early, so it's crucial to capture people's attention as quickly as possible", and that "getting into the heart of your episode as quickly as you can is a great way to keep up retention" (Spotify for Creators).

If you publish a video podcast on YouTube, the platform measures the opening directly. YouTube's key moments report includes an Intro figure that "tells you what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds" (YouTube Help). That makes 30 seconds a useful working target: by then, the viewer should know why they're staying.

How to build a short intro

  1. Pick the hook. Find the strongest 10 to 30 seconds in the episode: a surprising line, a turn in a story, a clear promise. That becomes your cold open.
  2. Write one line of who and why. "This is the Small Shop Show. Today, how a two-person bakery survived losing its biggest customer."
  3. Introduce the guest in a sentence. Credentials that matter to this episode only. Their full bio can live in the show notes.
  4. Duck the music. If you use theme music, keep it short or run it under the voice. Spotify's production guide suggests sidechain compression or dynamic EQ to "duck music out of the way" of speech (Spotify for Creators).
  5. Start with a specific question. Not "tell us about yourself", but "you lost the contract on a Tuesday. What happened on Wednesday?"
  6. Move admin later. Reviews, merch and social links work better once people have had value, often just before or after the first break.

Example: trimming a 2-minute intro to 35 seconds

Example (a hypothetical episode, to show the method): an interview show's original intro.

  • Before (2 minutes 5 seconds): 20 seconds of theme music, 25 seconds of "welcome back, hope you had a great week", 30 seconds asking for reviews, 20 seconds of sponsor thanks, 30 seconds of guest bio, then the first question.
  • After (35 seconds): 15-second clip of the guest saying "we had one customer left and they wanted us to halve the price"; 5 seconds of theme music under the host's voice; 10 seconds: show name and "today, how she kept that customer and doubled the business"; 5 seconds: guest name and role. First question at 0:35.

The review request moves to just after the first segment, the sponsor read moves to the mid-roll, and the bio goes in the show notes. Nothing was lost; it was just moved behind the content. After publishing, the host compares the first minute of the retention graph with the last few episodes to see whether fewer people leave early.

Video podcasts: the intro is also a thumbnail promise

On YouTube, the first seconds need to match what the title and thumbnail promised. If the thumbnail shows the guest's big claim, open with that claim. A slow logo sting or a wide shot of two people adjusting microphones gives viewers an easy reason to go back.

How to check your intro

  • Spotify: look at the episode's retention graph in Spotify for Creators. A steep fall in the first minute means the opening isn't holding. Listen back and find the moment people leave.
  • YouTube: check the Intro figure in the key moments report and compare it with your recent videos of similar length.
  • Apple: if you use chapters, name the first chapter after the hook, not "Intro", so the chapter list itself gives a reason to stay (Apple Podcasts for Creators).

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches podcast hooks?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and re-hooks for podcasts, clips and YouTube.

Do I need intro music at all?

No. Many shows use a few seconds as a signature, but music isn't required. If you use it, keep it short or under the voice.

Should the sponsor read go before the content?

Only if your deal requires a pre-roll. Otherwise a mid-roll after the first segment usually costs fewer early listeners.

Is a cold open the same as an intro?

A cold open is a clip from later in the episode placed at the start. It's usually the first part of a good intro, followed by the show name and topic.

How do I know if my intro is too long?

If your retention graph falls sharply in the first minute, or YouTube's Intro figure is below your recent average, cut it down.

Should I recap last week's episode?

Only for serial shows, and keep it to about 20 to 30 seconds after the hook.
