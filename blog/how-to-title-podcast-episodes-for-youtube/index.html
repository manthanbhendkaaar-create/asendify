How should you title podcast episodes for YouTube?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Title podcast episodes on YouTube around the single most interesting idea or story in the episode, not "Episode 47" or the guest's name alone. Keep titles under about 70 characters with the key words first, make the promise specific, and make sure the first 30 seconds of the video deliver what the title says. Put episode numbers and guest credentials in the description or your podcast app metadata instead.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's podcasting guide: "Try keeping titles under 70 characters and focusing on the most important words/ideas at the front."
  • It also says to "be accurate" so the title and thumbnail are "a true representation of the video".
  • YouTube notes a strong intro score can mean the first 30 seconds "matched the viewer's expectation of the video's thumbnail and title".
  • Episode numbers help in podcast apps (Apple asks for numbers as metadata) but rarely persuade a new viewer on YouTube.
  • Guest names work in titles only when the guest is well known to your audience.

What YouTube recommends

YouTube's podcasting best-practices guide gives clear title advice: "Be concise. Try keeping titles under 70 characters and focusing on the most important words/ideas at the front." It adds that optimising titles for search may "be the most beneficial to growing your audience", and that title and thumbnail should be accurate: "Make sure your title & thumbnail combination is a true representation of the video" (YouTube podcasting best practices, PDF).

Why the title affects retention

A title is a promise, and the first 30 seconds either keep it or break it. YouTube's help page on key moments says a high intro percentage can mean "the content in the first 30 seconds matched the viewer's expectation of the video's thumbnail and title" (YouTube Help). If your title promises the story of a guest losing £1 million and the video opens with five minutes of small talk, viewers leave. Write the title, then make sure the cold open delivers it.

Title formats that work for podcast episodes

FormatExampleWorks when
The story"We had 11 days of cash and a payroll on Friday"The episode has a strong narrative
The question"Why do most small agencies stall at five clients?"It answers a real search question
The claim"Charging by the hour keeps freelancers broke"You can back it up in the episode
The guest + the idea"[Well-known guest] on the deal she turned down"The guest is known to your audience
The how-to"How to price a service without sales calls"Practical, searchable episodes

Formats to avoid on YouTube

  • "Episode 47" or "Ep. 47 | Show Name | Guest Name": the number tells a new viewer nothing.
  • Guest name only when most viewers won't know them.
  • Vague topics: "Talking business and life" promises nothing specific.
  • Clickbait: a title the episode doesn't deliver hurts the intro and future trust.
  • Very long titles that bury the key words past where they get cut off.

Podcast apps vs YouTube

Podcast apps and YouTube reward different things. Apple's best practices ask you to set season and episode numbers as metadata ("Seasons and episodes should start with a non-zero integer") rather than writing them as "01" or "Season One" (Apple Podcasts for Creators). That keeps numbering in the data, where apps use it, and frees your title to describe the idea. Many shows use a slightly different title on YouTube from the one in their RSS feed for this reason.

Thumbnails go with titles

YouTube's guide advises featuring "your host's/talent's face in the thumbnail", using 16:9 at 1280 × 720 pixels under 2MB. Let the thumbnail and title do different jobs: the title states the idea; the thumbnail shows the emotion or the key image. Repeating the title word for word on the thumbnail wastes space.

Test and learn

  • Compare click-through rate and the intro percentage between episodes with different title styles.
  • If click-through is high but the intro is low, the title is promising something the opening doesn't deliver.
  • If both are low, the idea or the packaging isn't landing; try the story or question format.

FAQ

Is there a book that helps match video openings to titles?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers the first 30 seconds and hooks, and applies them to podcasts.

Should I include the episode number in YouTube titles?

Usually not at the start. Put it in the description or at the end if your audience follows the series in order.

How long should a podcast title be on YouTube?

YouTube's guide suggests under about 70 characters, with the key words first.

Should I use the guest's name in the title?

Only if your audience knows them. Otherwise lead with the story or idea.

Can my YouTube title differ from my podcast app title?

Yes. Many shows describe the idea on YouTube and keep numbering in their feed metadata.

How do I know if a title is working?

Check click-through rate and the intro percentage together in YouTube Studio.
