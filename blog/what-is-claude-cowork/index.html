What is Claude Cowork, and what can it do?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Claude Cowork is the agentic mode of Claude: instead of answering one message at a time, you describe an outcome and Claude plans and carries out the multi-step work itself, across the folders, apps and browser tabs you allow. It is available on paid Claude plans, runs in an isolated environment on Anthropic's servers, and returns finished files such as documents, spreadsheets and slide decks for you to review.

Key takeaways

  • Anthropic launched Cowork on 12 January 2026 as a research preview in the macOS desktop app, describing it as "Claude Code for the rest of your work".
  • It needs a paid plan. Anthropic's help centre lists Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise; desktop is needed for local files, browser use and computer use.
  • It can work with local folders, run sub-agents in parallel, build Office files, run scheduled tasks and use connectors and plugins.
  • Three permission modes (Manual, Auto, Skip) decide how often Claude asks before acting. Deleting files always needs your approval.
  • The main risk is prompt injection: hidden instructions in a web page or file that try to steer the agent. Connect only what a task needs.

Where Cowork came from

Cowork started life as a tab in the Claude desktop app for macOS. Simon Willison, who tested it on launch day, wrote on 12 January 2026 that Anthropic described it as "Claude Code for the rest of your work", and that it was first limited to Max subscribers before opening to Pro. His summary was that it is Claude Code with a friendlier interface and a sandbox set up for you, so people who never open a terminal can hand Claude real tasks.

Since then it has grown into a core part of Claude. Anthropic's product page, Claude Cowork, now describes it as a way to give Claude a goal and get finished work back, and notes that Cowork's capabilities are being folded into regular Claude, which brings them in automatically when a task involves files.

What Cowork can do

Anthropic's help article Get started with Claude Cowork lists the main capabilities:

  • Local file access on desktop, without uploading files one by one.
  • Sub-agents that split a large job into parallel workstreams.
  • Office outputs, including spreadsheets with working formulas, slide decks and formatted documents.
  • Scheduled tasks, started with /schedule or from the Scheduled sidebar, which run in the cloud on demand or on a cadence.
  • Projects that group related tasks with their own files, instructions and memory.
  • Browser actions, through a built-in browser on desktop or Claude in Chrome.
  • Plugins that bundle skills, connectors and sub-agents.
  • Global and folder instructions for standing preferences and project context.

Anthropic's own examples include building a weekly marketing readout from an analytics tool and a Drive tracker and saving it as a slide deck, and turning a quarter of call notes into a deal-status memo with quoted objections.

Plans, platforms and requirements

The help centre says Cowork is for paid plans only. Claude Desktop on macOS, Windows and Linux is in beta on all paid plans. Web and mobile are available on Pro and Max, in beta on Team, and on Enterprise once an owner switches them on. Anthropic's product page also lists its Ultra plan.

You need an internet connection for the whole session. Sessions run in the cloud and keep going if you close the desktop app, but any task that touches local files, the browser or other apps on your computer needs the desktop app open and connected.

Permission modes and safety

The help article describes three permission modes:

  1. Manual: Claude asks before each action.
  2. Auto: Claude checks each action for safety and blocks unsafe ones. This is the default on Pro, Max and Team.
  3. Skip: no prompts and no automatic checks. Anthropic advises using it only when you fully trust the task.

Work runs in an isolated environment on Anthropic's servers, separate from your computer and network. Claude can only reach folders you have connected, and it must ask before permanently deleting files. The help article also notes a gap worth knowing: web search, web fetch and MCP connectors are not governed by your network egress settings.

The risk Simon Willison focused on is prompt injection, where instructions hidden in content Claude reads try to hijack what it does. Anthropic's advice is to avoid giving it sensitive local files it does not need, limit browser access to trusted sites and watch for suspicious actions. For a business, the practical rule is to keep anything that sends, pays or deletes behind an approval step.

Worked example

Example: a sole trader who sells handmade candles wants a weekly sales summary without opening five tabs every Monday.

  • She connects one folder, "Shop reports", where her shop platform's weekly CSV export lands.
  • In Cowork she writes: "Read the newest CSV in Shop reports, compare it with last week's, and make a one-page Word document with total orders, top 5 products and any product whose sales fell by more than 30%."
  • She leaves the permission mode on Auto and checks the plan Claude shows before it starts.
  • After the first good run she types /schedule and sets it for 8am every Monday.

The setup takes one folder and one instruction. Claude cannot touch her email or bank, because she never connected them, and every week she gets a document she can check in two minutes.

Limits to know

  • Long multi-step tasks use more of your plan's usage than quick questions. Anthropic suggests grouping related work and starting fresh conversations for unrelated jobs.
  • Sessions cannot be shared, although individual artifacts can.
  • Some features remain desktop-only, such as plugins that use local MCP servers.
  • On Team and Enterprise, admins can restrict which permission modes people can use.

FAQ

Is there a course that shows how a real business runs on Claude Cowork?

Yes. The Cowork Operator by Asendify documents one shop's real rules, connected apps, scheduled tasks and safeguards. It is independent of Anthropic; Anthropic's help articles are the free alternative.

Is Claude Cowork free?

No. Anthropic's help centre says Cowork is only available on paid plans: Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise.

Do I need to know how to code to use Cowork?

No. Cowork was built to bring Claude Code's agent abilities to people who do not use a terminal. You describe the task in plain words.

Can Cowork run when my computer is off?

Cloud tasks and scheduled tasks keep running, but anything that uses local files, your browser or desktop apps needs the Claude desktop app open and connected.

Can Cowork delete my files?

Only with your permission. Anthropic says Claude must get explicit approval before permanently deleting files, and it can only reach folders you have connected.

What is the difference between Cowork and Claude Code?

Claude Code is aimed at developers and software work. Cowork brings the same agent abilities to everyday knowledge work, such as documents, research and files, in the Claude app.
