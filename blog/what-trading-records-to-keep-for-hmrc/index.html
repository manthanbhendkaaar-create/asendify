What trading records do I need to keep for HMRC?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 18 November 2026 · Last updated 18 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Keep a record of every buy and sell: the date, the quantity, the price, and the costs such as broker fees and stamp duty, plus the contract notes from your broker. GOV.UK says individuals should keep Capital Gains Tax records for at least one year after the Self Assessment deadline, and businesses for 5 years. If HMRC treats you as trading, keep them for at least 5 years after the 31 January deadline.

This page is education only, not tax or financial advice. Check your own position with HMRC or an accountant.

Key takeaways

  • Records must show what you paid, what you received, the dates, and costs such as fees and stamp duty.
  • Individuals: keep Capital Gains Tax records for at least one year after the Self Assessment deadline; longer if you filed late or HMRC opens a check.
  • Anyone carrying on a trade: keep records until at least the fifth anniversary of the 31 January after the tax year.
  • UK share disposals are matched by special rules (same day, then the next 30 days, then a pooled average), so you need every buy, not just your closing positions.
  • If records are lost, you must try to recreate them and show which figures are estimates.

What GOV.UK says to keep

GOV.UK's Capital Gains Tax record-keeping page lists the records to keep:

  • receipts, bills and invoices showing the date and amount you paid;
  • receipts for additional costs, such as professional fees and Stamp Duty;
  • records of what you received, including any later instalments;
  • contracts for buying and selling, such as those from your stockbroker;
  • copies of any valuations.

It adds that cryptoassets need specific records, with separate guidance.

How long to keep them

Business (incl. someone carrying on a trade)At least 5 years after the 31 January deadlineGOV.UK / HMRC manual CH14530

HMRC's Compliance Handbook entry CH14530 gives the rule for a person carrying on a trade, profession or business: keep records until the latest of the fifth anniversary of the 31 January after the tax year, the end of any enquiry, or the date the enquiry window closes. Its example: records for the year ending 5 April 2009 had to be kept until at least 31 January 2015. HMRC can charge a penalty for failing to keep records.

Why you need every trade, not a summary

UK shares are not matched first-in, first-out. HMRC's Capital Gains Manual page CG51560 sets the order:

  1. Same-day rule: a sale is matched first with shares of the same company bought on the same day.
  2. 30-day rule: next, with shares of the same company bought in the 30 days after the sale (often called "bed and breakfasting").
  3. Section 104 pool: anything left is matched with your pooled holding at its average cost.

Its example: someone holding 2,500 shares sells 1,700 on 27 March and buys 500 on 30 March. The 500 bought are matched with 500 of the shares sold; the other 1,200 come from the Section 104 holding. You cannot work this out from a year-end statement alone.

Note the account type. GOV.UK describes ISAs as a way to save tax-free (up to £20,000 in 2026/27), so mixing ISA and taxable trades in one log without labelling them causes errors later.

Worked example

Example: Ravi trades UK shares in a general investment account. In the 2026/27 tax year he makes 40 trades.

  • He saves each contract note as a PDF in a folder named by tax year.
  • He logs every trade in his journal with the fields above, including fees.
  • In March he sells 300 shares of one company and buys 100 back eight days later. Because of the 30-day rule, those 100 are matched with 100 of the shares he sold; the other 200 come from his pool. His journal has the dates, so his accountant can match them correctly.
  • He files on time by 31 January 2028. As an individual investor, he keeps the records until at least 31 January 2029, though he chooses to keep them longer in case HMRC asks about later years that use the same pool.

If you have lost records

GOV.UK says you must try to recreate them if they cannot be replaced, and on your tax return show which figures are estimated (you want HMRC to accept them as final) and which are provisional (you will update them later). Brokers can usually reissue past contract notes and statements, so ask them first.

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS is a one-time-payment Notion template that calculates P&L, R-multiple, win rate and position size. A self-built spreadsheet is a free alternative.

How long should I keep share trading records in the UK?

GOV.UK says individuals should keep them for at least one year after the Self Assessment deadline, and businesses for 5 years. Keep them longer if HMRC is checking a return.

Do I need records for trades inside an ISA?

GOV.UK describes ISAs as tax-free savings, so they are kept apart from your taxable trades. Label them clearly so they never mix with taxable ones.

What is the 30-day rule?

If you sell shares and buy the same company's shares within 30 days, the sale is matched with the new purchase first, under HMRC's identification rules.

What if my broker has closed?

Recreate the records as best you can and mark estimated or provisional figures on your return, as GOV.UK advises.
