What are the best free YouTube channel audit tools?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

The best free YouTube channel audit tools are YouTube Studio for your own private data, Social Blade for any channel's public statistics over time, and the free tiers of vidIQ and TubeBuddy for keyword, optimisation and channel health checks. For a fast, blunt outside view, the free YouTube Channel Roast by Asendify scores a channel out of 100 on titles, thumbnails and upload consistency and gives three fixes with no sign-up.

Disclosure: Asendify makes the YouTube Channel Roast. It is ours.

Key takeaways

  • No single free tool does a full audit. Combine one tool that sees your private data with one that gives an outside view.
  • YouTube Studio is the only free source of your retention, click-through rate and traffic sources.
  • Public-data tools are useful for trends and competitors but cannot see private metrics.
  • Quick "roast" or "grade" tools are good for spotting packaging problems fast.
  • An audit only helps if it ends in two or three specific changes you can test.

What a YouTube channel audit should cover

A useful audit answers four questions:

  1. Packaging: do people click? (titles, thumbnails, impressions click-through rate)
  2. Retention: do they keep watching? (the first 30 seconds, average view duration, drops)
  3. Discovery: where do viewers come from? (Search, Browse, Suggested, external)
  4. Consistency and focus: is there a clear promise and a steady rhythm of uploads?

Different free tools cover different parts of that list.

YouTube Studio

YouTube's guide to getting started with Insights explains that the old Analytics page is now called Insights. It has tabs for Overview, Content, Reach, Engagement, Audience, Revenue (for Partner Programme members) and Trends, and an Advanced Mode for comparisons and data export. This is where your real numbers live, and it is free.

What it does not do is interpret them for you. You still have to decide what the numbers mean.

Social Blade

Social Blade tracks public statistics on YouTube and other platforms and gives all users access to its public database. It is good for seeing whether a channel's growth is speeding up or slowing down, and for looking at competitors. It cannot see retention, click-through rate or traffic sources, because those are private to the channel owner.

vidIQ

vidIQ's homepage lists a free tier and offers a free channel grade, along with keyword research showing search volume and competition. It is most useful for topic and keyword ideas. Paid tiers add more.

TubeBuddy

TubeBuddy offers a free "Forever" plan with no credit card required and lists a Channel Health Report among its tools, plus a keyword explorer. Its A/B testing tool judges on click-through rate.

YouTube Channel Roast

The YouTube Channel Roast looks at a channel's public titles, thumbnails and upload pattern, scores it out of 100, and gives blunt feedback and three fixes. It needs no sign-up, which makes it quick for a first look. It does not see private analytics, so pair it with Studio.

Free versus paid audits

Free tools cover most of what a small channel needs. A paid audit or coaching session can add a human view of your videos themselves, but only pay if the person can show their own track record and gives specific, testable fixes. Try the free route first so you know what to ask.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Treating a grade as a verdict. A score is a prompt to look closer, not proof.
  • Trusting public estimates as exact. Third-party tools see public data only.
  • Auditing without rewatching. A drop in retention means nothing until you see what was on screen.
  • Changing ten things at once. Pick two and test them.

FAQ

What is a YouTube channel audit?

A review of how a channel performs on packaging, retention, discovery and consistency, ending in a short list of changes to test.

Can I audit someone else's YouTube channel?

Only using public data such as titles, thumbnails, views and upload dates. Retention, click-through rate and traffic sources are private to the owner.

Is YouTube Studio enough for an audit?

It has all your private data, so it is the most important tool. Many creators add an outside view, because it is hard to judge your own titles and thumbnails.

How often should I audit my channel?

Every two or three months, or whenever growth stalls. Check analytics after every upload in between.

Are free channel graders accurate?

They are useful for spotting obvious problems, such as vague titles or irregular uploads. Treat the score as a starting point and confirm problems in your own analytics.
