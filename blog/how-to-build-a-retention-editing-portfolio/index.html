How do I build a retention editing portfolio?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Build it around before-and-after proof, not a showreel. Pick three to five edits, and for each show the raw or previous version, your cut, the retention graph (with the client's permission) and a short note on what you changed and why. If you have no clients yet, re-edit public-domain or your own footage as spec work and label it honestly. Clients hiring a retention editor want to see your decisions, not just nice transitions.

Key takeaways

  • A retention portfolio is a set of case studies: problem, edit decisions, result.
  • The most persuasive evidence is the retention graph from YouTube Studio, especially the "Intro" figure (share still watching after 30 seconds) and the dips you removed.
  • Ask clients for Viewer (limited) channel access, which shows analytics but "can't access revenue data".
  • Check you're allowed to show the work. Under UK law, commissioned work belongs by default to its creator unless agreed otherwise in writing, but your contract may say otherwise, and the client's footage is theirs. Always get written permission.
  • Never invent or round up results. Show real numbers or say "illustrative".

What clients look for

People hiring a retention editor usually want answers to three questions: can you make my opening hold, can you remove the slow parts without losing meaning, and do you understand the analytics? A showreel of fast cuts answers none of them. A good portfolio piece does.

Step by step

  1. Choose three to five pieces. Cover the formats you want more of: long-form YouTube, Shorts, podcasts or ads. Three strong case studies beat twenty clips.
  2. Get permission in writing. Ask the client if you may show the video, the graph and any numbers. Some will say yes to the video but not the numbers; respect that.
  3. Capture the evidence. With Viewer (limited) access or screenshots from the client, record the intro figure, average percentage viewed and the retention curve, plus a comparison with the channel's previous videos of similar length. YouTube's report includes "typical retention" against your 10 latest videos of similar length.
  4. Write the story briefly. Problem ("viewers left during a 40-second intro"), what you did ("opened on the result, moved the context later"), result ("intro figure up from 58% to 66% vs the channel's previous five videos").
  5. Add spec work if needed. Re-edit your own footage, a friend's channel (with permission) or public-domain material. Label it "spec edit" and don't claim results it didn't have.
  6. Host it simply. A single page or document with embedded clips and screenshots works. Make it quick to scan.

If you don't have retention data yet

Many new editors don't get analytics access. You can still show retention thinking:

  • A side-by-side of an original and your re-cut opening, with notes on why yours should hold.
  • A marked-up transcript showing repeats and tangents removed.
  • A "dip audit" of a public video: timestamps where you would expect drop-off and how you'd fix them. Be clear this is your analysis, not the channel's data.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Cherry-picking a viral video you didn't cause. If traffic jumped for another reason, say so.
  • Showing revenue or private data without permission.
  • Using a client's footage after they asked you not to.
  • Making claims you can't back up, such as "I double retention". Show the real range.

How to present each case study

Keep each piece to one screen. Lead with a single sentence on the problem, then a short clip or side-by-side of the opening, then a screenshot of the retention graph, then three or four bullets on what you changed. Put the format and turnaround at the bottom. A potential client should understand what you did in under a minute. If you share numbers, include the date range and what you compared against, so the claim can be checked. Update the portfolio every few months, replacing the weakest piece with a newer one.
