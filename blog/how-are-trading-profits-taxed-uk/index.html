Tax on Trading Stocks in the UK: CGT, ISAs and Spread Bets

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 14 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

For most individuals in the UK, profits from buying and selling shares and similar investments are taxed as capital gains, not income: you pay Capital Gains Tax at 18% or 24% on net gains above the £3,000 annual exempt amount in 2026 to 2027. HMRC treats individual share dealing as investment rather than a trade unless something takes it "out of the norm". Gains from financial spread betting are not chargeable to Capital Gains Tax, and gains inside an ISA are tax-free.

Key takeaways

  • HMRC's starting point: an individual buying and selling shares is normally investing or speculating, not trading.
  • Capital Gains Tax applies to net gains (gains minus losses) above £3,000 in 2026 to 2027.
  • Rates from 6 April 2026: 24% for higher and additional rate taxpayers; basic rate taxpayers pay 18% on gains within their remaining basic rate band.
  • Spread betting: HMRC says no chargeable gains or allowable losses arise, so gains are not taxed and losses cannot be offset.
  • Trade inside a stocks and shares ISA (up to £20,000 a year in 2026 to 2027) to keep gains tax-free.

This page is education only, not tax or financial advice. Your situation may differ; ask a chartered tax adviser if trading is a large part of your income.

Is trading a "trade" for tax purposes?

HMRC's Business Income Manual, BIM56860, looks at court cases involving individuals who bought and sold shares. Its position is that this is normally investment or speculation, not trading, unless there are factors that take the case "out of the norm", weighing "the relevant circumstances as a whole".

CaseWhat happenedOutcome
Salt v ChamberlainIndividual dealing in sharesNot trading
Wannell v RothwellHighly organised dealingTreated as trading, but losses could not be set against general income because it was not commercial
Manzur v HMRCFrequent dealing"The management of a portfolio of investments rather than trading"; "no definitive checklist"

In practice, this means most people who day trade shares from their own account pay Capital Gains Tax, not Income Tax, on their profits. The rare exceptions involve highly organised, commercial activity, and the position is decided case by case.

The Capital Gains Tax numbers

From GOV.UK's Capital Gains Tax rates for 2026 to 2027:

ItemAmount
Annual exempt amount£3,000
Higher and additional rate taxpayers24%
Basic rate taxpayers18% on gains within the remaining basic rate band, 24% above it

Losses can be set against gains in the same tax year, and unused losses can usually be carried forward if you report them to HMRC.

Worked example: a year of share trading

Example: Tom is a higher rate taxpayer who trades UK shares in a normal brokerage account. In 2026 to 2027 he closes 140 trades.

StepAmount
Total gains on winning trades£15,000
Total losses on losing trades£4,000
Net gain£11,000
Less annual exempt amount£3,000
Taxable gain£8,000
CGT at 24%£1,920

If Tom had made the same trades inside a stocks and shares ISA, he would pay nothing. If he had placed similar positions as spread bets, the gains would not be chargeable to CGT, but his £4,000 of losses could not be used either.

Tom's biggest practical job is the record-keeping: every buy and sell, with dates, quantities, prices and costs. With 140 trades, a clean journal makes the tax return a matter of totting up rather than a weekend of digging through broker statements. Record-keeping checklist

  1. Date and time of each buy and sell.
  2. Instrument, quantity and price.
  3. Fees, commissions and stamp duty.
  4. Account type (ISA, general account, spread betting).
  5. Running total of gains and losses for the tax year.
  6. Broker annual statements, kept with your own records.

FAQ

Do I pay tax on day trading profits in the UK?

Usually yes, as Capital Gains Tax on net gains above £3,000, unless you trade inside an ISA or through spread betting. HMRC rarely treats individual share dealing as a trade.

Is spread betting tax-free in the UK?

HMRC's manual says no chargeable gains or allowable losses arise from spread betting, so gains are not subject to Capital Gains Tax. Losses cannot be offset either.

Can I offset trading losses against my salary?

Generally not for share dealing taxed under Capital Gains Tax. Capital losses offset capital gains. Even in Wannell v Rothwell, where the activity was treated as trading, losses could not be set against general income.

Do I need to report trades if I made a loss?

Reporting losses lets you carry them forward against future gains, so it is often worth doing. Check HMRC's rules on time limits.

What is the ISA allowance for 2026 to 2027?

£20,000 across all your ISAs, according to GOV.UK.
