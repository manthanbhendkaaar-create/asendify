Why is my YouTube video getting impressions but no views?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 18 November 2026 · Last updated 18 November 2026

Because people are seeing your thumbnail and title but not choosing to click. An impression only means your thumbnail was on screen for over a second; a view needs a decision. YouTube says half of channels and videos have an impressions click-through rate between 2% and 10%, so a few hundred impressions can honestly mean only a handful of views. Fix the packaging first.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube counts an impression when a thumbnail is shown for more than 1 second with at least 50% visible.
  • Half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%, according to YouTube. New videos and those under 100 views can vary more.
  • CTR naturally falls when YouTube shows a video on busy surfaces like the homepage, so a dropping CTR is not always bad news.
  • High CTR with short watch time looks like clickbait, and YouTube is less likely to recommend it.
  • The usual fixes: a clearer promise in the title, a thumbnail readable at small size, and a topic people already search for.

What an impression actually is

YouTube's help page on impressions and click-through rate defines a thumbnail impression as a thumbnail shown for more than one second with at least half of it visible. Impressions are counted on YouTube search, the homepage, feeds, Up Next recommendations and playlists, across computers, TVs, consoles and the mobile apps.

Some places do not count, including external websites and embeds, the mobile website, YouTube Kids, cards and end screens, emails and notifications. So your impressions number is how often YouTube put your thumbnail in front of someone on its own surfaces, nothing more.

What a normal click-through rate looks like

YouTube's impressions and CTR FAQ says: "Half of all channels and videos on YouTube have an impressions CTR that can range between 2% and 10%." It adds that new channels, videos under a week old and videos with fewer than 100 views can show a wider range.

If your video has 300 impressions and 4 views, that is about 1.3%: low, but not a sign that something is broken. It means the thumbnail and title are not yet winning against everything else on the screen.

Why it happens

  1. Weak packaging. The thumbnail is cluttered, hard to read on a phone, or looks like every other video on the topic. The title describes the video instead of promising something the viewer wants.
  2. The wrong audience. YouTube is testing your video with people who are not interested. This often happens on new channels with no clear topic.
  3. Busy surfaces. The FAQ explains that videos shown on high-traffic surfaces like the homepage naturally get a lower CTR, while impressions from your channel page get a higher one.
  4. Low demand for the topic. Few people want that video right now, however well it is made.
  5. Mismatch with the video. A clickbait title might lift CTR but, as the FAQ warns, a high CTR with low view duration signals clickbait and YouTube is less likely to recommend it.

Low impressions vs low CTR

These are two different problems, and they need different fixes:

SymptomWhat it usually meansWhere to look
Plenty of impressions, very few viewsPackaging is not winning the clickThumbnail, title, topic demand
Very few impressions at allYouTube is showing the video to few peopleTopic, channel focus, content suitability
Good CTR, few impressionsSmall, loyal audience clickingNormal for small channels; keep publishing

YouTube's FAQ notes that videos with fewer impressions often have a higher CTR, because they reach a narrower, more loyal audience. It also says videos that are not suitable for a broad audience under the Community Guidelines may get fewer impressions. So check that a video is not being limited before you redesign its thumbnail.

What to fix first

  • Thumbnail at phone size. YouTube's own creator advice, in its 2011 post Thumbnails: your video's billboard, says thumbnails should look good at small and large sizes, should accurately reflect the video, and that misleading thumbnails hurt performance over time. It recommended 1280x720 images under 2MB.
  • One clear promise in the title. Put the outcome or the question first. Cut words a viewer does not need to decide.
  • Pick a topic with demand. Check YouTube search suggestions before you film.
  • Be patient with the data. The FAQ advises against checking CTR right after upload; wait until the video has a substantial number of impressions.
  • Change one thing at a time. The FAQ also warns that traffic sources, not your change, may explain a CTR difference, so judge changes over a longer period.

Worked example

Example: a cooking channel with 900 subscribers uploads "My Sunday Batch Cook". After a week: 2,400 impressions, 31 views. CTR is about 1.3%.

  • The thumbnail shows six small containers on a busy kitchen counter. At phone size it is unreadable.
  • The title tells viewers nothing they want.

On the next upload, the creator uses one close-up of a finished dish, a three-word text overlay ("5 lunches, £12") and the title "5 Work Lunches for £12 (Batch Cook in 1 Hour)". After a week: 3,100 impressions and 140 views, about 4.5%, inside YouTube's typical range. These figures are an illustration of how to read the numbers, not a measured case.

FAQ

Is there a free tool that roasts my titles and thumbnails?

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast scores your titles and thumbnails and gives three fixes, with no sign-up. A friend's honest opinion is a free alternative.

What counts as an impression on YouTube?

A thumbnail shown for more than 1 second with at least 50% visible, on YouTube's own surfaces such as search, home and Up Next.

What is a good CTR on YouTube?

YouTube says half of channels and videos fall between 2% and 10%. Compare a video with your own channel's past videos with similar traffic sources.

Why do I have more views than impressions?

Views from outside YouTube, such as links and embeds, do not come from counted impressions, so views can exceed impressions.

Should I change my thumbnail if CTR is low?

Wait until the video has a substantial number of impressions, then change one thing and judge it over time, as YouTube's FAQ advises.
