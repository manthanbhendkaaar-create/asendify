What happens if your supercar is written off?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

If your insurer writes off your supercar, it usually pays what it thinks the car was worth on the open market just before the damage, minus your excess and any outstanding finance, and your policy normally ends. You can challenge a low valuation with evidence of comparable cars, and for Category S or N write-offs you may be able to buy the car back. On an expensive car on finance, GAP insurance can decide whether you walk away owing money.

This page is education only, not financial or legal advice. Check your own policy wording and speak to your insurer or a broker.

Key takeaways

  • A car is written off when it cannot be safely repaired or repairs would cost more than its market value, MoneySuperMarket explains.
  • The payout is the insurer's view of market value now, not what you paid, minus the excess, so a high-depreciation supercar can leave a large gap.
  • If the payout does not cover your finance, you may still owe the lender; GAP insurance covers that shortfall.
  • Cat S and Cat N cars can usually be bought back, with the salvage value deducted from your settlement.
  • If you cannot agree a valuation, use the insurer's complaints process and then the Financial Ombudsman Service.

How the payout is worked out

MoneySuperMarket's guide to write-offs (updated February 2026) says the payout is usually the car's market value just before the damage, minus your excess and any outstanding finance, and that "it will be your insurer's assessment of what your car is worth today". Confused.com gives a simple example: a £5,000 car with a £250 excess pays £4,750.

Your level of cover matters. MoneySuperMarket notes that with third-party only or third-party, fire and theft cover you may get nothing if you were at fault, while fully comprehensive cover should pay out either way. Read your policy wording now to see exactly how it values the car, before you ever need to claim.

Challenging a low valuation

Supercars are hard to value: there are few on sale, specifications vary widely and prices move quickly. That makes evidence important. MoneySuperMarket suggests:

  1. Find several comparable cars, same model, age, mileage and condition, from reputable dealers.
  2. Send them to the insurer and ask it to review the offer.
  3. If it will not move, use its formal complaints process.
  4. If that fails, take the complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

For a rare or highly specified car, include receipts for options, recent servicing and any specialist valuation you have.

Finance and GAP insurance

Confused.com warns that if the payout is less than your loan, you may have to keep paying even though you no longer have the car, or the lender may ask for the full balance. GAP insurance, a separate policy, covers the difference between the market value paid out and what you paid or still owe. On a supercar that has lost a large share of its value in its first years, that gap can be tens of thousands of pounds.

Buying the car back

Both guides say Category S and N cars can be bought back; Confused.com adds that the salvage value is deducted from your settlement and that you should tell your insurer quickly, before the car goes to auction. Get an independent repair estimate first. MoneySuperMarket warns that insurance may cost more afterwards, some insurers will not cover a repaired write-off, and you must always declare it.

For a supercar, buying back only makes sense if a specialist confirms the repair is safe and affordable, and you accept the permanent write-off record and its effect on resale.

Telling the DVLA

GOV.UK says you must tell the DVLA when your insurer has written off and taken your vehicle, which it treats like selling it to the insurer, and you can be fined £1,000 if you do not. You may get a refund of vehicle tax for any full months left. Remove any personalised number plate first.

Example: a written-off car on finance

Leon bought a used supercar for £110,000 two years ago with a £30,000 deposit and a loan for the rest. After an accident, his insurer writes it off and values it at £84,000. His excess is £1,000. He still owes the lender £68,000.

  • Payout: £84,000 − £1,000 = £83,000
  • Insurer settles the finance: £68,000 goes to the lender
  • Leon receives: £15,000

He finds three comparable cars at dealers priced between £89,000 and £93,000 and asks for a review. The insurer raises its valuation to £88,000, so Leon receives £19,000. Had his car been worth less than his loan, only GAP insurance would have stopped him owing the difference. These figures are illustrative.

FAQ

Is there a planner that ranks supercars by what they cost per year at my mileage?

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner ranks cars that fit your budget by yearly cost at your mileage, with editable running costs. Your own spreadsheet is the free alternative.

Will I get what I paid for my supercar?

Usually not. Insurers normally pay their assessment of current market value, minus your excess. Check your policy wording for how your car is valued.

Can I keep my written-off supercar?

Often, if it is Category S or N: you can buy it back, with the salvage value deducted from your payout. Category A cars must be scrapped.

Does a write-off affect my no-claims bonus?

It is likely to, MoneySuperMarket says, and it may raise future premiums unless you have no-claims protection.

Do I get a refund on my insurance premium?

MoneySuperMarket says a premium refund is unlikely, even if you paid annually, and the policy is usually cancelled.
