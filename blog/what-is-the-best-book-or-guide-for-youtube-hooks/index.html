Key takeaways

  • A YouTube hook must do one job: make the viewer want the next 30 seconds. YouTube reports the result as intro retention, "what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds".
  • For Shorts, the matching number is "stayed to watch", "the percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short".
  • Free guides explain the principle; paid resources add templates and practice.
  • The best guide is the one you apply to your next five videos and measure.

What to look for in a hooks guide

  1. Many hook types, with examples. Result first, problem first, question, contrast, bold statement, visual.
  2. Rules for the first frame. What viewers see before any words.
  3. Matching hook to title and thumbnail. A hook that doesn't match the packaging feels like a bait-and-switch.
  4. Payoff planning. A hook is a promise; the guide should show how to keep it.
  5. Measurement. How to tell if the hook worked.

Why curiosity hooks work

Curiosity isn't a trick. In a 2009 study published in Psychological Science, Kang and colleagues found that "higher curiosity in an initial session was correlated with better recall of surprising answers 1 to 2 weeks later". A hook that raises a real question helps people care about, and remember, the answer. A hook that raises a fake question harms trust.

Example: a personal finance creator tests two hooks for a 12-minute video, "I tracked every penny for a year".

The creator publishes with hook B and compares intro retention with their typical retention for similar-length videos. They don't assume a fixed lift; they keep the hook style if it beats their average over several videos. The figure in the hook must be true for their own spending.

Common hook mistakes

  • Greeting first. "Hey guys" spends the most valuable seconds on nothing.
  • Explaining before promising. Context comes after the viewer has a reason to stay.
  • A hook the video doesn't pay off. Viewers leave and may not trust the next one.
  • Copying viral hooks word for word. The structure transfers; the wording usually doesn't.

How to practise hooks without burning uploads

You don't need to publish a video to practise hooks. Three low-risk methods build the skill faster.

MethodHowWhat you learn
Rewrite past hooksTake your last 10 videos and write three new hooks for eachPatterns in what you tend to miss
Hook swipe fileSave hooks from videos in your niche that held you; note the typeWhich hook types suit your topics
Shorts as testsUse the same idea with different hooks in ShortsFast feedback via "stayed to watch"

Shorts work well as a test bed because YouTube shows "the percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short". A hook style that consistently holds viewers in Shorts is worth trying at the start of a long video, though long-form viewers came from different places and may behave differently.

When rewriting hooks, use a simple checklist: Is it specific? Is it true? Does it match the title and thumbnail? Does it promise something the video delivers? Can it be understood muted, with text on screen? A hook that passes all five is usually good enough to test.

Over time, keep notes on which hook types work for which video types on your channel. A result-first hook may suit tutorials, while a question works better for stories. That personal record ends up more useful than any general list, including the templates in a paid guide, because it reflects your own audience.

FAQ

Is there a guide with YouTube hook templates?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook includes hook templates and a 30-day practice plan for £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCD guide is a free starting point on principles.

What is the best free resource for YouTube hooks?

YouTube's own Help pages on audience retention, because they show you how to measure whether your hooks work.

How long should a YouTube hook be?

Usually the first 5-15 seconds, though the promise should be clear in the first sentence.

Should the hook repeat the title?

It should match the title's promise without reading it out word for word.

How do I know if my hook is good?

Compare intro retention with your typical retention for videos of similar length.
