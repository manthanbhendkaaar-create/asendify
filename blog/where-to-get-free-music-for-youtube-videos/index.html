Where can you get free music for YouTube videos without copyright problems?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The safest free source is YouTube's own Audio Library, inside YouTube Studio. It offers "royalty-free production music and sound effects to use in your videos", tracks you download "won't be claimed by a rights holder through the Content ID system", and Partner Programme creators can monetise videos that use them. Some tracks need a credit in your description; others don't. Music from elsewhere needs a licence that clearly covers YouTube use.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's Audio Library is "found exclusively in YouTube Studio" and adds new music twice a month.
  • Creative Commons tracks require a credit: "you must credit the artist in your video's description"; standard-licence tracks don't, and you can filter for "Attribution not required".
  • Content ID scans every upload against copyright owners' files; a match can lead to the video being blocked, monetised by the owner or tracked.
  • A Content ID claim "typically doesn't result in a copyright strike", but three copyright strikes in 90 days can get a channel terminated.
  • "Royalty-free" on another website isn't the same as permission for YouTube; read the licence.

YouTube's Audio Library

YouTube's help page on the Audio Library sets out the key points:

QuestionAnswer from YouTube
What's in it?Royalty-free production music and sound effects
Where is it?YouTube Studio, left-hand menu, Audio Library (also youtube.com/audiolibrary)
Will Content ID claim it?Downloaded tracks "won't be claimed by a rights holder through the Content ID system"
Can I monetise?Partner Programme creators "can monetise videos with music and sound effects from the Audio Library"
Do I need to credit?Creative Commons tracks yes, in the description; standard-licence tracks no
How often is it updated?New music added twice a month

The page doesn't say whether the tracks can be used outside YouTube, so if you also post the same video elsewhere, check the licence terms for that.

How Content ID affects music choices

YouTube's How Content ID works page says that "When a video is uploaded to YouTube, it's automatically scanned by Content ID" against audio and visual files submitted by copyright owners. If your video matches, it gets a claim, and depending on the owner's settings the video can be:

  • blocked from viewing,
  • monetised, with ads that pay the owner (sometimes shared with the uploader), or
  • tracked, so the owner gets viewing statistics.

These actions can differ by country: a video can be monetised in one place and blocked in another.

YouTube's copyright strike basics page warns that disputing a claim without a valid reason can lead the owner to submit a removal request, which can cause a strike. Strikes "expire in 90 days if you complete Copyright School", a four-question course. Our page on what happens if you get a copyright strike on YouTube covers strikes in depth.

Music and retention

Music is also an editing decision. A track that fights your voice makes a video harder to follow, and a sudden change of mood can feel like a natural place to leave. Practical editing habits:

  1. Duck the music under speech so words stay clear.
  2. Match energy to the section: calmer under explanations, more energy in transitions.
  3. Change tracks at chapter breaks, not mid-sentence. Our page on how to add chapters explains chapter structure.
  4. Use sound effects sparingly; the Audio Library includes them too.

Worked example: replacing a claimed track

Example: Tom uploads a travel vlog with a popular song in the background. A Content ID claim appears and the owner chooses to monetise his video.

StepActionResult
1Checks the claim in YouTube StudioLearns it is a claim, not a strike
2Decides not to dispute, since he has no licenceAvoids risking a removal request and strike
3Opens the Audio Library, filters "Attribution not required"Finds a similar-tempo track
4Replaces the music in his editor and uploads the fixed versionNo claim on the new upload
5Saves three favourite tracks for future vlogsFaster editing next time

Tom's original upload earned the song's owner money, not him. Using Audio Library tracks from the start avoids the claim and keeps his options open if he joins the Partner Programme.

FAQ

Is there a self-paced course that teaches retention editing to creators who edit their own videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Accelerator is a self-paced course with assignments on your own footage. YouTube's free help pages are a fair alternative.

Is YouTube Audio Library music really free?

Yes. YouTube describes it as royalty-free music and sound effects, and says downloaded tracks won't be claimed through Content ID.

Do I need to credit Audio Library music?

Only for Creative Commons tracks, which require a credit in your description. Standard-licence tracks don't.

Can I use a popular song if I credit the artist?

A credit alone doesn't give permission. Content ID may claim the video, and the owner can block, monetise or track it.

Will a Content ID claim give me a copyright strike?

Typically not, according to YouTube. But disputing without a valid reason can lead to a removal request and a strike.

Can I monetise videos with Audio Library music?

Yes, if you are in the YouTube Partner Programme.
