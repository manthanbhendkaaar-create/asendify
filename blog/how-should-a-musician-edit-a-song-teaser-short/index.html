How should a musician edit a song teaser Short so viewers go on to the full track?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 3 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Start on the most memorable part of the song (usually the hook or chorus), show the artist or a striking visual in the first frame, and end the clip just before the musical phrase resolves so the viewer wants the rest. Then tell them exactly where the full song is, with YouTube's related video link on Shorts or a clear "full song on my channel" line. A teaser that plays the song's intro is the one people swipe past.

Key takeaways

  • The hook goes first: short-form viewers decide in a second or two.
  • Cut on an unresolved moment (the line before the drop, the last word of a chorus held back) to create an open loop.
  • A face or performance shot usually beats a static cover image.
  • Make several teasers with different moments and see which one people watch and replay.
  • Point to the full song clearly; on YouTube, the related video link on Shorts sends viewers straight to it (see how to add a related video link to Shorts).

Editing steps

  1. Pick the moment. The hook, the drop, or the most quotable line. If it doesn't work out of context, pick another.
  2. Start on a downbeat or the first word of the line, not a fade-in.
  3. Frame vertically with the face or performance in the upper-middle.
  4. Caption the lyric on screen; many people start with sound off.
  5. End on tension. Cut before the resolution, or loop it cleanly so the end flows into the start (see how to make a Short loop seamlessly).
  6. Add the call to action in text near the end: "Full song on my channel" or the release date.
  7. Link the full track with the related video feature.

Example: a pop single

Example: an artist's first teaser shows 20 seconds of the song's quiet intro over the cover art, and viewers swipe away. The next three teasers each start on a different moment: the chorus sung to camera, the bridge line with a text hook "wrote this at 3am", and a shot from the video. Each ends mid-phrase with "full song on my channel" and links to the music video. The artist checks which teaser holds viewers and replays best and makes more like it. Details are illustrative.

Rights reminders

Only use your own masters or tracks you have rights to. If the song features another artist or samples, make sure everyone agrees to the teaser plan before posting.

FAQ

Is there a book on hooks for YouTube Shorts?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and open loops for Shorts for £1,720 one-time; it isn't music-specific.

Which part of the song should a teaser use?

Usually the hook or chorus. Test a few different moments.

How long should a song teaser be?

Often 10 to 30 seconds: long enough for the hook, short enough to leave people wanting more.

Should I post the same teaser on TikTok, Reels and Shorts?

You can, but adapt captions and calls to action to each platform's linking features.

Does a teaser spoil the song?

Not if you cut before the payoff. It should make the full track feel unfinished without it.
