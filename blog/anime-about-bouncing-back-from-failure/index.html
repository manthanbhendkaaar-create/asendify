Which anime are about bouncing back from failure?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Three anime stand out for stories about bouncing back from failure: Haikyuu!!, where a short volleyball player starts again after a crushing tournament defeat; Barakamon, where a proud calligrapher is sent to a remote island after lashing out at a critic; and March Comes In Like a Lion, where a lonely teenage shogi professional slowly rebuilds his life. ASCEND, a free original anime by Asendify, adds one practical technique per episode.

This page is about anime and general life lessons, not mental health advice. If you are struggling with low mood, talk to a GP or call Samaritans free on 116 123.

Key takeaways

  • MyAnimeList lists Haikyuu!! as a 25-episode series from Production I.G, aired April to September 2014, about Shouyou Hinata, who joins Karasuno High after a disappointing middle school tournament.
  • Barakamon is a 12-episode Kinema Citrus series from 2014 in which calligrapher Seishuu Handa is sent to the Goto Islands after reacting badly to criticism.
  • March Comes In Like a Lion (3-gatsu no Lion) is a 22-episode Shaft series from 2016 to 2017 about Rei Kiriyama, a teenage professional shogi player living alone in Tokyo.
  • Each shows a different kind of comeback: from defeat, from pride and from isolation.
  • The useful lesson is not "never fail" but what the characters do in the weeks after it.

Haikyuu!!: starting again after a heavy defeat

Hinata is inspired by a famous player known as the "Little Giant" and wants to play volleyball at the top level, even though he is short. After a disappointing middle school tournament, in which Tobio Kageyama's team beats his badly, he enrols at Karasuno High, the Little Giant's old school, which has fallen from its former strength. Then he finds Kageyama is now his teammate.

What it shows about failure: The defeat is the starting point, not the end. Hinata has to drop his grudge, learn from the person who beat him and accept that being part of a team means changing how he plays. For anyone who has lost to a rival, it is a story about turning that rival into a teacher.

Barakamon: when pride is the failure

Seishuu Handa is young, talented and, in MyAnimeList's words, "unfortunately, a narcissist to boot". When a veteran calls his award-winning work "unoriginal", he lashes out, and his father sends him to the remote Goto Islands to cool off. He hopes to find a new style in peace and quiet, but local children, led by Naru Kotoishi, and the villagers keep interrupting him.

What it shows about failure: Sometimes the setback is your own reaction to criticism. Handa's comeback comes from being pulled out of his own head by ordinary people, and from slowly accepting that the criticism had a point. It suits anyone who takes feedback personally or feels their work has gone stale.

March Comes In Like a Lion: recovering from isolation

Rei Kiriyama reached professional status in shogi while still in middle school, so on paper he is a success. But he is under heavy pressure from the shogi world and his adoptive family, lives alone in Tokyo and struggles to look after himself or connect with people his age. Things begin to change when he meets the Kawamoto sisters, who run a traditional sweet shop with their grandfather.

What it shows about failure: Not every failure is a lost match. Rei's struggle is with loneliness and his own feelings, and his recovery is slow and built on small kindnesses and routine. It is the most thoughtful of the three, and a good reminder that success on paper can hide a hard time.

None of the comebacks happens in one episode. Each character changes their surroundings, lets other people in and makes small improvements over time. Those are ideas you can borrow.

Turning the lesson into action

Watching a comeback story can make you feel motivated for an evening. Turning it into a change takes a plan. A simple way to do it:

  1. Name your setback in one sentence: "I failed my driving test", "My shop had a bad first year".
  2. Pick the matching story. Defeat: Haikyuu!!. Criticism: Barakamon. Feeling cut off: March Comes In Like a Lion.
  3. Choose one habit the character builds and shrink it: Hinata asks for help from a better player; Handa listens to feedback; Rei accepts invitations.
  4. Schedule it for this week, with a day and a time.
  5. Review after two weeks and keep, change or drop it.

Worked example

Example: Sam, 22, failed his second-year university exams and has to resit in August. He feels embarrassed and has stopped seeing friends. He picks one show and one habit for each problem.
