Why do viewers skip my YouTube pre-roll ad at exactly five seconds?

Key takeaways

  • Google's ad help says viewers can skip a skippable in-stream ad five seconds in (Google Ads Help). The spike you see at five seconds is the button, not a coincidence.
  • Under cost-per-view bidding you pay when someone watches 30 seconds, or the whole ad if it is shorter, or interacts with it, so a skip at five seconds usually means you paid nothing for that view (same source). It still costs you the chance to be heard.
  • Google's study of thousands of TrueView ads found an "inverse relationship between recall and engagement": showing the brand in the first five seconds raised recall but led to more skipping (Think with Google).
  • The same study linked smiling faces and humour in the opening to more viewing, and calm or action-led music to more skipping.

What actually happens at second five

A viewer who sees a skippable ad is not deciding at second zero. They are waiting for the button. The button's arrival gives them a clear moment to choose, and most make the choice quickly. Your job is not to stop them clicking; it is to make staying more interesting than leaving at the exact moment the option appears.

Google's research gives a useful tension. Putting the brand on screen early raises recall, because people see who you are, but it also raises skipping, because a brand opening looks like an advert. The same study says logos that float without a product attached did worse on viewing and recall. So if the brand must appear early, tie it to something real, such as the product in use.

Step-by-step: edit the first five seconds

  1. Mark second five on the timeline. Treat it as a deadline, not a mid-point.
  2. Open on the point. Replace any logo card or fade with the strongest image or line you have.
  3. Plant an open loop before the button. Give the viewer a reason to wait, such as "Here is the mistake costing most shops a week" or a result shown but not yet explained.
  4. Show a face early if you have one. Google's analysis tied smiling and recognisable faces in the first five seconds to better viewing.
  5. Tie the brand to the product. If your name appears early, put it on the thing being shown, not on a floating card.
  6. Test the music. Google advises testing with and without music; calm tracks were more likely to be skipped on average.
  7. Add words for silent viewers. A short on-screen line carries the hook for anyone with the sound off.
  8. Cut one beat from every shot. Tighter shots make five seconds feel fuller.
  9. Deliver on the promise by second ten. Do not make the viewer wait until the end for what you hinted at.
  10. Compare versions. Run two openings and compare how many viewers pass the skip point.

Do not chase the skip rate alone

A skip at five seconds on cost-per-view bidding is not charged, so a high skip rate is not always a money problem. It is an attention problem. The fair question is who stayed, and whether they were the right people. If viewers who stay go on to watch past 30 seconds or interact, the opening may be filtering well. If almost nobody passes the button, change the opening.

Google's own guidance says averages are not guarantees and no formula works for every ad, so use its findings as things to test, not rules.

Where the five second skip fits in the wider video

The same logic applies to an organic video. The first seconds are where the viewer judges what the video promises, and the promise has to be kept. A pre-roll ad is simply the most extreme version, because the exit is right there on screen.

When can viewers skip a YouTube pre-roll ad?

After five seconds on skippable in-stream ads, according to Google Ads Help.

Do I pay when someone skips at five seconds?

Under cost-per-view bidding you pay when a viewer watches 30 seconds, or the whole ad if shorter, or interacts. A skip at five seconds is normally not billed.

Should I put my logo in the first five seconds?

It can raise recall, but Google's study found it also raised skipping. Tie the brand to the product shown, not a floating logo.

Does music help the opening?

It depends. Google's study linked calm or action-led music to more skipping, so test with and without.
