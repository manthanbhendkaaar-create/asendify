What retention report should an agency send clients each month?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Send a one-page report that a busy client can read in two minutes: three or four retention numbers compared with the client's own baseline, the two biggest drop-off points and what caused them, what you tested and learned, and the changes you will make next month. Use the platform's own metrics (YouTube's 30-second intro figure and average percentage viewed, or ad "video played to" percentages), and never present a figure you can't show the source for.

Key takeaways

  • Report against the client's own baseline. YouTube's audience retention report compares each video with the channel's 10 latest videos of similar length ("typical retention").
  • For organic video, lead with the "Intro" figure (share still watching after 30 seconds) and average percentage viewed.
  • For ads, use Google Ads' "video played to" metrics, which "count the percentage of people who watched 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% of the video", plus view rate (views divided by impressions).
  • Get access the right way: YouTube's Viewer (limited) role shows analytics without revenue data.
  • The most valuable part of the report is "what we'll change next month", not the numbers.

The one-page structure

SectionContentLength
1. HeadlineOne sentence on the month: what improved, what didn't1-2 lines
2. Scorecard3-4 metrics: this month, baseline, changeSmall table
3. Biggest drop-off pointsTwo timestamps or ad seconds where viewers left, with the cause2-4 bullets
4. Tests and learningsWhat was tested, result, decision2-3 rows
5. Next monthThe specific changes you will make2-3 bullets
6. NotesAnything outside the edit that affected results (topic, budget, seasonality)1-2 lines

Pick three or four, not twelve. Keep the same ones every month so trends are visible.

How to present comparisons fairly

  1. Use a baseline from before you started, or the previous quarter's median, and say which.
  2. Compare like with like. Shorts against Shorts, long videos against long videos of similar length, ads against ads with similar budgets.
  3. Use medians or ranges, not one standout video.
  4. Flag outside factors. A viral topic, a seasonal dip or a change in ad spend affects retention as much as editing does.
  5. Link to the source. A screenshot of the Studio graph or Ads table lets the client check the figure.

Worked example

Example: an agency edits four YouTube videos a month and runs YouTube ads for a home-fitness brand.

Headline: Openings held more viewers this month; the middle of longer videos still sags.

Biggest drop-off points: 4:30 in two videos, where the equipment list starts (cause: long spoken list); second 7 of the main ad (cause: logo card).

Tests: result-first opening on two videos vs problem-first on two (result-first held more; adopt). Logo card moved to the end of one ad variant (running now).

Next month: show the equipment list as an on-screen graphic; remove the logo card from second 7 if the test confirms; test a shorter 15-second ad cut.

All figures are illustrative; your report uses the client's real data.

What not to put in the report

  • Vanity numbers with no comparison ("12,000 views").
  • Benchmarks from other industries presented as targets.
  • Claims you can't support, such as crediting all growth to the edit.
  • Twenty charts. If the client has to search for the point, the report has failed.

Making the report quick to produce

Build the report from a template so it takes under an hour per client. Keep a tab in your tracker for each client with the agreed baseline, the monthly figures and the test log, and copy the summary into the one-page report. Take screenshots of the retention graphs as you review each video during the month, rather than hunting for them on the last day. Write the "next month" section first: if you can't say what you will change, the review hasn't been done properly. Send the report on the same day each month so clients know when to expect it.

FAQ

Is there a playbook with a retention tracker agencies can use for client reports?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify includes a tracker and spreadsheet for £1,720 one-time. YouTube Studio and Google Ads reports are free.

How many metrics should a monthly report include?

Three or four, kept the same each month so trends are clear.

Should I include revenue?

Only if the client wants it and you have access; retention reports work without it, and Viewer (limited) access excludes revenue.

What if retention went down?

Say so in the headline, explain the likely cause and state what you will change.

Monthly or weekly?

Monthly for clients; weekly internal reviews help the team act faster.
