Who should choose the Playbook

  • You learn well from reading and can set your own schedule.
  • You want the techniques (open loops, re-hooks, pattern interrupts) in one reference you can return to.
  • You want templates and a tracker to apply them to your next videos.
  • You would rather spend less and see whether the method suits your channel first.

Who should choose the Accelerator

  • You have bought books before and not finished them.
  • You want the material broken into short lessons with a task after each.
  • You are training yourself or a junior editor and want a set sequence to follow.
  • The higher price is affordable and the extra structure is worth it to you.

Why structure matters more than content volume

The difference between the two is less about information and more about whether you will apply it. The research on online learning is sobering: the MIT Teaching Systems Lab's summary of Reich and Ruipérez-Valiente's 2019 Science paper calls low completion "the bane of MOOCs". A course does not guarantee you finish; it just gives you more prompts to.

Format also affects engagement. In Guo, Kim and Rubin's study of 6.9 million edX sessions, students watched only about 2 to 3 minutes of each tutorial video and rewatched tutorials more than lectures (paper PDF). People dip in for what they need. A book with a cheat sheet can suit that habit well.

Example: choosing between them

Sam edits their own gaming videos and averages 9 minutes per upload. Their retention graph shows a steep drop in the first 30 seconds and a slow fade after minute 4. Sam has a full-time job and edits at weekends.

  • Sam is self-directed and wants a reference to keep open while editing, so the Playbook fits. The 30-day plan gives a weekly rhythm without set lesson times.
  • If Sam had tried several books and stalled, the Accelerator's 24 lessons with assignments might be worth the higher price for the extra prompts.

Either way, Sam should first note their current 30-second intro figure and typical retention, so they can tell whether changes worked. Our page on what the Most replayed graph means helps with the other half: what viewers rewatch.
