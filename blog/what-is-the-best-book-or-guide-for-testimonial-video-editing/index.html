Key takeaways

  • Testimonials lose viewers fast. Wistia found testimonial videos under a minute average a 46% engagement rate, a little below the 52% average for all short videos.
  • Google's ABCDs advise "Humanize the story": "Feature people to help you tell a better product story and relate to your consumer."
  • Open on the result or the problem, not the customer's job title.
  • Captions are essential; many viewers watch muted, and W3C notes captions serve people "who cannot hear the audio".

Why testimonials are hard to edit

Customers aren't presenters. They warm up slowly, repeat themselves, and often say the best line ten minutes into the interview. The editor's job is to find that line, put it first, and keep the story honest. That means no cutting words together to create claims the customer didn't make.

What a testimonial editing guide should cover

  1. Finding the hook line. The single most specific result or feeling.
  2. Story order. Problem, attempt, change, result.
  3. Trimming without distorting. Cut repetition, not meaning.
  4. B-roll. Show the product, the place, the work.
  5. Text and captions. Name, role and the key result on screen.
  6. Versions. A 15-30 second ad cut and a 1-2 minute full story.

Worked example

Example: an accountancy firm films a 25-minute interview with a café owner who switched to them.

Raw interview momentUse in the edit
Minute 14: "I used to do the books at midnight. Now I don't touch them."Hook, 0-4 s
Minute 3: two minutes on how she started the caféCut to one sentence of context
Minute 7: describing missed VAT deadlinesProblem section
Minute 10: the onboarding callChange section with office B-roll
Minute 19: "I got my Sunday evenings back."Result

The 60-second cut opens on the midnight line, with captions. The firm checks the customer is happy with the edit before publishing, and compares view-through with its previous testimonial, which opened on the café exterior. Results vary; it's a test, not a promised lift. In the UK, testimonial claims in ads still need to be genuine and not misleading.

Common mistakes

  • Opening on name and job title. Viewers don't know why they should care yet.
  • Too many customers in one video. One clear story beats five fragments.
  • Over-cutting. Jump cuts every two words feel unnatural; use B-roll to cover edits.
  • Changing meaning. Never splice words into a claim the customer didn't make.

Getting better raw material for the edit

Most testimonial editing problems start in the interview. A few habits on filming day make the edit far easier.

HabitWhy it helps the edit
Ask for answers in full sentences"Before we switched, I did the books at midnight" works alone; "Yeah, that" doesn't
Ask for specifics"How many hours?" gives you a usable number the customer actually stated
Ask the same question twice, laterThe second answer is often shorter and clearer
Film b-roll of the customer at workCovers edits and shows real use
Record room toneSmooths audio between cuts

Prepare five to eight open questions rather than a script. Good ones include: "What was going wrong before?", "What made you try us?", "What changed?" and "What would you tell someone in your position?" Let the customer talk, then follow up on the most specific thing they say.

During the edit, keep a list of every claim in the final cut and check each against the raw footage. This protects both the customer and the brand, and it matters more when the video runs as a paid ad. If a claim needs context to be accurate, include the context or drop the claim.

Lastly, plan the cut-downs at the start. A 60-second story, a 15-second ad and a few silent social clips can all come from one interview if you film enough b-roll and capture a strong one-line result.

FAQ

Is there a book on editing testimonial videos?

Not a well-known one. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers the retention methods that apply, for £1,720 one-time; you adapt it to testimonials.

How long should a testimonial video be?

Make a 15-30 second ad cut and a 1-2 minute full story. Both should open on the result.

Should the customer approve the edit?

It's good practice, and it protects you if the testimonial is used in ads.

Can I script a testimonial?

You can guide with questions, but the words should be the customer's own and true.

What B-roll works best?

The customer using the product or service, in their own place of work.
