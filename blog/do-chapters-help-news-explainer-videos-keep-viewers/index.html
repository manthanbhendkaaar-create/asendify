Do chapters help news explainer videos keep viewers?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 3 min read · Published 26 November 2026 · Last updated 26 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Chapters can help viewers find the part they want, but YouTube does not say they raise retention, so test them on your own videos. YouTube says chapters add context to each part of a video and make it easy to rewatch sections. They need a first timestamp at 00:00, at least three timestamps and a minimum of 10 seconds each.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help on video chapters says the first timestamp must start at 00:00, you need at least three timestamps in ascending order and each chapter must last at least 10 seconds.
  • It says chapters "add info and context to each portion of the video and let you easily rewatch different parts of the video", and that automatic chapters may be generated for some videos and can be turned off.
  • YouTube's retention help says spikes show parts viewers rewatch and dips show where they leave, which is what you will see change if viewers skip around.
  • Neither page says chapters increase average view duration; chapters can let viewers skip, which may lower it while raising satisfaction.
  • Measure with your own retention graph before deciding.

How to write chapter titles for news

  1. Use plain labels such as "What happened", "Why it matters" and "What happens next".
  2. Avoid spoilers in the title if the answer is the hook payoff.
  3. Keep to 3 to 6 chapters for a 10-minute video.
  4. Check the rules: start at 00:00, at least three chapters, at least 10 seconds each.
  5. Add them to the description as a list.

How to test whether they help

Publish two similar explainers, one with chapters and one without, and compare average view duration and the retention graph shape. Look for dips right after chapter starts; they show viewers jumping. Note that two videos are only a hint, not proof, because topic and traffic differ.
