Why do most CFD traders lose money?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Most retail CFD traders lose money because leverage magnifies small price moves into large account swings, trading costs are charged on every position, and common habits such as cutting winners early and holding losers make the maths worse. European regulators found that 74-89% of retail CFD accounts typically lost money before rules were tightened in 2018, and UK providers must still publish their own loss rate, often around 70%.

This page is education only, not financial advice. CFDs are complex, leveraged products and you can lose money rapidly.

Key takeaways

  • ESMA (March 2018): national regulators found "74-89% of retail accounts typically lose money", with average losses of €1,600 to €29,000 per client.
  • UK rules since 1 August 2019 cap retail leverage at 30:1 to 2:1, close positions at 50% of required margin and stop clients losing more than their account balance.
  • Each UK provider must show the share of its retail accounts that lose money; IG's UK site shows 70%.
  • The FCA estimated its rules would save retail consumers £267m to £451m a year.
  • What a trader can control: position size, risk per trade, costs, and following a written plan.

What the regulators found

The European Securities and Markets Authority announced its CFD restrictions on 27 March 2018. National regulators had found that 74-89% of retail accounts typically lost money on CFDs, with average client losses from €1,600 to €29,000. ESMA chair Steven Maijoor said the measures "will for the first time ensure that investors cannot lose more money than they put in."

The UK's Financial Conduct Authority made similar rules permanent, announcing permanent restrictions on 1 July 2019 after "evidence of firms aggressively marketing CFDs to the general public". In its policy statement PS19/18, the FCA said ESMA's temporary leverage limits "reduced client losses by £77m between August 2018 and October 2018", and that one firm reported the share of loss-making accounts stayed the same, but clients lost less money overall.

Worked example: how leverage turns 1% into 30%

Example (illustrative): Sam has £2,000 in a CFD account and opens a position on a major currency pair worth £30,000, using the maximum 30:1 leverage, so £1,000 of margin.

Price move against SamLossLoss as % of margin usedLoss as % of account

About 3.3%About £1,000About 100%About 50%

A 1% move in a currency pair is not unusual. Now add costs: if the spread costs 0.01% each time, and Sam trades 10 times a week, that is about 0.1% of £30,000, or £30 a week, before any move in price. Over a year that is a large share of a £2,000 account.

Compare with Alex, who risks 1% of her £2,000 account per trade (£20) by setting a stop-loss and sizing the position to match. A string of five losing trades costs her about £100, roughly 5% of her account, and she can keep learning.

What you can control

  • Risk per trade. Many educators suggest risking a small, fixed share of the account per trade. Size positions from your stop-loss, not from the leverage available.
  • Leverage used, not leverage offered. 30:1 is a maximum, not a target.
  • Costs. Fewer, better-planned trades pay less spread and financing.
  • A written plan. Entry, exit, size and when not to trade. See how to build a trading plan.
  • Records. A journal shows whether you have an edge after costs. See how many trades you need to know if a strategy works.
  • Tax. In the UK, CFDs and spread bets can be taxed differently; see how trading profits are taxed in the UK.

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS is a one-time-payment Notion template that calculates P&L, R-multiple, win rate, position size and the cost of each mistake. A spreadsheet with a "rule followed" column is the free alternative.

What percentage of CFD traders lose money?

ESMA reported that national regulators found 74-89% of retail accounts typically lost money. UK providers publish their own figures; IG's UK site shows 70%.

Can I lose more than I deposit with CFDs in the UK?

Not as a retail client. Negative balance protection means you cannot lose more than the funds in your CFD account.

What is the maximum leverage for retail CFD traders?

30:1 on major currency pairs, falling to 2:1 on cryptocurrencies, under rules the FCA made permanent from August 2019.

Do the leverage limits stop people losing?

They reduce the size of losses. The FCA said one firm reported the share of losing accounts stayed similar, but clients lost less money overall.

Is trading CFDs gambling?

CFDs are regulated financial products, but without an edge after costs, results behave much like a bet. Treat them as high risk and only use money you can afford to lose.
