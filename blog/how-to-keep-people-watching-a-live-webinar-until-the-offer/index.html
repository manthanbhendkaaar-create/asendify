How do I keep people watching my live webinar until the offer?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Earn the first 90 seconds with a specific promise of what attendees will leave with, then deliver that value in short segments with an interaction point roughly every 8 to 10 minutes. Tell people early that there will be a live Q&A and a bonus at the end, so they have a reason to stay, and make the offer the obvious next step from what you've just taught rather than a sudden change of tone. Most webinars lose people because the opening is housekeeping and the middle is a monologue.

Key takeaways

  • Zoom's during-event guide says the "first 60–90 seconds" often determine whether attendees stay, and recommends keeping housekeeping under 30 seconds.
  • The same guide suggests "5–7 structured interaction points" in a 60-minute session, launching a poll "within the first five minutes", and one specific call to action in the final 3 to 5 minutes.
  • Zoom warns that saving all questions for the end "can reduce participation and create a pressure drop at the end of the session".
  • If attention drops, Zoom suggests a poll, a direct question or a new voice to reset it.
  • In the UK, "falsely stating that a product will only be available for a limited time" is a banned practice under the DMCC Act 2024, Schedule 20. Fake countdowns lose trust and can break the law.

Mention the bonus or Q&A at the start ("stay to the end and I'll answer as many questions as I can"). That's an open loop, and it only works if you keep the promise.

Techniques that keep people in the room

  1. Name what's next before each segment ends. "Next, the mistake that costs most people their first sale."
  2. Use attendees' answers. Read out poll results and chat replies by first name if they've chosen to share.
  3. Change the visual. Switch between slides, your face, a screen share and a live example every few minutes.
  4. Have a moderator. Zoom recommends a dedicated person to filter questions and manage chat so the presenter stays on content.
  5. Share resources during the session, not only in the follow-up email, as Zoom suggests.
  6. Turn on captions. Zoom offers automatic captions; they help attendees who join muted or in a second language.

Worked example

Example: a course creator teaching bookkeeping to freelancers runs a 60-minute webinar.

  • 0:00 "In the next hour you'll set up a system that takes 15 minutes a week. Stay to the end for live Q&A."
  • 2:00 Poll: "How do you track expenses now? Spreadsheet, app, shoebox?"
  • 5:00 to 35:00 Three lessons, each ending with a chat prompt; a live fix of one attendee's spreadsheet at 20:00.
  • 38:00 "If you want templates and monthly check-ins, that's what the course adds." Price stated once, clearly.
  • 48:00 Q&A, then a recap and link.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to keep a webinar audience engaged?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing for £1,720 one-time. Zoom's during-event guide is free.

When should I make the offer in a webinar?

After you've delivered the promised value, typically in the last third, followed by live Q&A.

How often should I interact with attendees?

Roughly every 8 to 10 minutes, according to Zoom's guide.

Should I use a countdown timer for my offer?

Only for a real deadline. False limited-time claims are banned in the UK under the DMCC Act 2024.

How long should a sales webinar be?

Long enough to deliver real value plus Q&A; many run about an hour.
