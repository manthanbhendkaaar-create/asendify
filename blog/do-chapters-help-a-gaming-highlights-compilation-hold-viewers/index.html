Do chapters help a gaming highlights compilation hold viewers?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Chapters help a long gaming compilation with clear sections, such as "Top 10 clutches" or "Funny fails", but they do little for a fast 3-minute montage. YouTube does not publish proof that chapters raise watch time, so use them where viewers might want to jump, and rely on clip order and pacing to hold attention elsewhere.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube chapters "break up a video into sections, each with an individual preview" (YouTube Help).
  • They need a 00:00 start, at least three timestamps and a minimum of 10 seconds per chapter.
  • Chapters suit compilations that people dip into, such as a 20-minute "best of the month", more than a short montage watched start to finish.
  • YouTube says "the shape of the audience retention graph can tell you which parts of your video are most and least interesting to viewers" (YouTube Help). Use it to judge whether sections hold viewers.
  • Mayer's segmenting principle, "People learn better when the information is presented in user-paced segments" (Dartmouth summary), is about learning, so treat it as a loose guide for entertainment.

The risk: chapters can give away your best clip

A chapter titled "Insane 1v4 clutch at 14:20" tells viewers exactly where the payoff is. Some will skip straight there and leave. For a compilation built on suspense, that can hurt the rest of the video.

Two ways to handle it:

  1. Name chapters by theme, not by result. "Clutch plays" instead of the specific clip.
  2. Skip chapters for tight montages. If the whole video is a build-up, leave it unchunked.

How to chapter a longer compilation

  1. Start with an honest 00:00 chapter, such as "Intro and top clip".
  2. Group by game, by event or by type of moment.
  3. Keep each chapter at least 10 seconds, and ideally one to five minutes.
  4. Write labels viewers search for, like the game name.
  5. Put your biggest moment early in the video as the hook rather than in a chapter name.

Reading the graph

With chapters you will see a spike where viewers jump to a popular section, and dips where they leave. YouTube says spikes appear when viewers are "watching, rewatching, or sharing" a part (YouTube Help). If one game section keeps spiking, make more of that game. If one section always dips, shorten it or move it later.

What to fix first if retention is poor

Chapters will not repair the opening. Many gaming viewers leave in the first seconds if there is a long intro, a logo sting or a slow start. Open on the best clip or a clear promise, then roll the intro. Check clip order so the strongest moment is not wasted in second three and followed by weaker ones. Keep sound consistent between clips so viewers do not reach for the volume.

If you reuse game footage, make sure you have the right to use it under the game publisher's content policy. Rules differ by publisher, so check theirs.

Quick checklist

  • Is the video long enough to need chapters (about 8 minutes or more)?
  • Do labels describe a theme rather than the payoff?
  • First timestamp is 00:00, at least three chapters.
  • The hook is in the first 10 seconds, not in a chapter title.
  • You will compare retention across two or three videos, not one.

FAQ

Is there a playbook for editing gaming highlights for retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and structure with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's free help covers chapters and the retention report.

Should a short gaming montage have chapters?

Usually not. A 2-3 minute montage is watched straight through, and chapters can reveal the best clip.

Can chapters hurt retention?

They can if they let viewers jump to the payoff. Name chapters by theme and test.

How long must a chapter be?

At least 10 seconds, with a minimum of three timestamps and a first one at 00:00.

Should I use automatic chapters?

You can, but they are not guaranteed. Writing your own gives you control over spoilers.
