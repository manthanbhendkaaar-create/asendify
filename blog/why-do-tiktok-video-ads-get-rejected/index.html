Why do TikTok video ads get rejected, and how can editors avoid it?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

TikTok video ads are most often rejected for exaggerated or misleading claims, sloppy on-screen text, low-quality or silent video, and a mismatch between the ad and its landing page. Editors can prevent most of these with a short pre-export check: proofread every caption, export at proper resolution with clear audio, keep claims provable and make sure the product and price on screen match the website.

Key takeaways

  • TikTok's help centre lists rejection reasons including claims that are "exaggerated, overstated, or misleading", spelling errors, "Excessive and distracting capitalization", "Low-resolution and blurry video", and "The video does not have any audio or sound" (TikTok Ads Help).
  • TikTok also rejects ads that feature the TikTok logo or claim a product is a "TikTok Bestseller".
  • TikTok's creative policy guide asks advertisers to "Ensure that the same message and products promoted in the ad are reflected on the landing page" (TikTok for Business).
  • "Typically most ads are reviewed within 24 hours", and editing the creative "will automatically trigger an ad review" (TikTok Ad Review FAQs).
  • Many hook tricks that work organically (all-caps text, "S@LE", shocking openings) are exactly what fails ad review.

Reasons that are not the editor's job (but the editor can flag)

TikTok's list also includes landing page problems: sites that 404, are not mobile-friendly, lack contact information, a privacy policy, or, for e-commerce, a refund or return policy. And it rejects ads where "the products and prices in the ad are not consistent" with the website, such as an ad claiming "Up to 50% off" when the site shows no such offer.

The editor can't fix the website, but should check it. Before handing over an ad, open the landing page and confirm the product, price and offer shown in the video match it.

A pre-export checklist for TikTok ads

  1. Read every word on screen out loud, including auto-captions.
  2. Check hook text is in sentence case with no symbol spellings.
  3. Watch the ad on a phone and confirm no text is cut off by the interface.
  4. Confirm resolution and that no clip looks soft or pixelated.
  5. Play with sound: is there audio, and is the voice clear?
  6. Remove any TikTok logos or platform claims.
  7. Check every claim with the client's evidence.
  8. Open the landing page and match product, price and offer.

If an ad is rejected anyway

Read the rejection reason in Ads Manager, fix the specific issue and save the edit, which sends it back for review. If you believe it was rejected wrongly, TikTok's FAQ says to submit an Advertiser Support ticket and request a re-review. Keep a log of rejections and fixes per client; patterns usually show up quickly.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on writing compliant TikTok ad hooks?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and pattern interrupts with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. TikTok's ad policies are the free rulebook.

How long does TikTok ad review take?

TikTok says most ads are reviewed within 24 hours, though some take longer.

Does editing a live ad trigger a new review?

Yes. Saving changes to the creative triggers a review that may take up to 24 hours.

Can a TikTok ad be silent?

TikTok lists "The video does not have any audio or sound" as a reason ads fail review, so add audio.

Can I say "as seen on TikTok"?

Avoid the TikTok logo and claims such as "TikTok Bestseller", which TikTok lists as rejection reasons.
