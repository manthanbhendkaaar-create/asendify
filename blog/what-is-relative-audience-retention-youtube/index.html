What does relative audience retention mean on YouTube, and how do you use it?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 7 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Relative audience retention tells you how well your video keeps viewers compared with other YouTube videos of a similar length, rather than in raw percentages. In YouTube's own data it runs from 0 to 1, where 0.5 is the median: above 0.5 means you hold viewers better than most similar-length videos at that moment, below it means worse.

Key takeaways

  • Absolute retention shows the share of viewers still watching at each moment; relative retention shows how that compares with similar-length videos.
  • YouTube's Analytics API defines relativeRetentionPerformance as a value between 0 and 1, with 0.5 as the median.
  • In YouTube Studio, the "See more" view compares your video with all YouTube videos of similar length, and "typical retention" compares it with your own 10 latest similar-length videos.
  • Relative retention is best for spotting where a video falls behind, not for judging the whole video by one number.
  • Retention data typically takes one to two days to process, so read it after that.

Absolute vs relative retention

There are two ways YouTube describes the same curve. The YouTube Analytics API metrics reference defines audienceWatchRatio as "the absolute ratio of viewers watching the video at the given point in the video", calculated by comparing how many times a portion was watched with the total views. That is the line most creators look at: it starts near 100% and falls as people leave.

The same reference defines relativeRetentionPerformance as "a measurement that shows how well a video retains viewers during playbacks in comparison to all YouTube videos of similar length". It has a value between 0 and 1. A 0 means the video retains viewers worse than any other video of similar length, a 1 means better than any other, and "a median value of 0.5 indicates that half of the videos of similar length retain viewers better".

Where to find it in YouTube Studio

YouTube's guide to key moments for audience retention says you can click "See more" to see how your video compares with all YouTube videos of similar length, and use typical retention to compare your 10 latest videos of similar length. It also explains the shapes on the chart: a flat line means viewers are watching that part through, gradual declines mean viewers are losing interest over time, spikes are moments rewatched or shared, and dips mean viewers are abandoning or skipping at that part.

The typical line arrived in October 2021. Social Media Today's report on the update describes "a new 'typical' retention comparison line in the chart" plus comparison charts that sort videos by intro, top moments, spikes and dips.

Key moments only appear on videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views, and YouTube notes that retention data typically takes one to two days to process.

How to read a relative retention line

Think of relative retention as a ranking at each point of the video, not a percentage of people.

  1. Start with the first 30 seconds. YouTube's intro figure tells you what share of viewers was still watching after 30 seconds. If your relative line sits low here, your opening is weaker than most videos of the same length.
  2. Look for the point where the line drops below 0.5. That is where you go from better than median to worse than median. It usually lines up with a slow section, a long setup or a repeated point.
  3. Check whether it recovers. A line that dips below 0.5 and climbs back means one weak section, not a weak video. A line that slides steadily down means interest fades across the whole video.
  4. Compare with your typical line. If you are below the YouTube median but above your own typical line, the video is an improvement for your channel even if it is not yet strong against everyone.

Common mistakes with relative retention

  • Treating 0.5 as a pass mark for the whole video. The value changes moment by moment. Average it in your head and you lose the information about where the problem is.
  • Comparing a 3-minute and a 20-minute video directly. Absolute retention on long videos is naturally lower, which is exactly why YouTube compares like with like.
  • Reading it on day one. Data typically needs one to two days to process.
  • Reacting to tiny samples. A video with 150 views can swing a lot from a few viewers. Look for the same pattern on two or three videos before you change your editing style.
  • Ignoring dips because the relative line is high. A strong video can still have a fixable dip. Use the absolute curve to find the exact second.

How relative retention fits with other metrics

Relative retention tells you where you sit against similar videos. It does not tell you why. For the why, open the absolute curve at the same timestamp, watch the 15 seconds before the drop and ask what changed on screen. For the bigger picture, pair it with average view duration and click-through rate: a video with a good relative line but few impressions may have a packaging problem rather than an editing problem.

FAQ

Is there a tool that reads my retention data and flags where I fall behind?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor reads one channel with read-only access and flags each drop-off with a fix. YouTube Studio's comparison views are the free alternative.

What is a good relative retention score?

Above 0.5 at a given moment means you are holding viewers better than the median similar-length video. Aim to stay above 0.5 through the first 30 seconds.

Why is my absolute retention low but relative retention fine?

Long videos naturally lose more viewers. Relative retention adjusts for that by comparing with videos of similar length.

What is typical retention in YouTube Studio?

A comparison line showing how your video performs against your own 10 latest videos of similar length.

Can relative retention go above 1?

No. YouTube's relativeRetentionPerformance always sits between 0 and 1. Only the absolute watch ratio can exceed 1, when viewers rewatch a section.

Why can't I see key moments on my video?

YouTube only shows them on videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views, and data can take one to two days to appear.
