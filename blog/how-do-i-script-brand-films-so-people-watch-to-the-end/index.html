How do I script a brand film so people watch to the end?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Script a brand film around one identifiable character facing a believable problem, open on tension rather than your logo, raise a question in the first seconds and pay it off near the end. Keep the brand as the helper, not the hero, and cut lines that talk about the company. Research on storytelling in marketing shows identifiable characters, an imaginable plot and believability pull people into a story.

Key takeaways

  • A meta-analysis of 132 effect sizes from 76 articles by van Laer et al. (2014) found narrative transportation, being drawn into a story, is driven by identifiable characters, an imaginable plot and verisimilitude ("the likelihood that story events may actually happen").
  • The same study found transportation had positive effects on attitudes and intentions and reduced critical thoughts. That is about persuasion, not watch time, but stories that pull people in are also stories people keep watching.
  • Wistia's retention guide advises: "Abbreviate the introduction", "Experiment with removing intro graphics and logo sequences" and "Avoid talking about yourself too much".
  • Wistia's video length analysis found engagement "naturally declines after the five-minute mark", so a long brand film needs a reason to stay at every stage.
  • Measure the result with the drop-off points in YouTube's key moments report or your video host's engagement graph.

Script rules that protect retention

  1. One character, one problem. Viewers follow people, not companies.
  2. Open on tension, not a logo, a drone shot or a mission statement.
  3. Plant a question early ("Will she make it?") and hold the answer until near the end.
  4. Make it believable. Real places, real details and honest outcomes support verisimilitude.
  5. Let the brand help rather than lead. Show what it makes possible; do not list features.
  6. Write for pictures. Each line should be filmable; cut abstract lines like "we believe in excellence".
  7. Cut every line that talks about the company unless the story needs it.
  8. Keep it as short as the story allows. If a scene does not move the story, cut it at script stage.

Worked example

Example: a regional bike shop commissions a 3-minute brand film. The first draft opens with 20 seconds of aerial footage and a voiceover about the shop's history, then shows staff, then products, then customer quotes.

Aerial shot and shop historyCold open: a commuter's bike breaks down on the way to a job interview

Product montageTwo products shown only where they matter in the story
Customer quotesThe commuter arrives in time; payoff
Logo for 6 seconds at the startLogo and address on a 4-second end card

The shop and its agency compare the engagement graphs of the two cuts on its website and social channels. This is an illustrative scenario: results depend on audience and placement, and the story must be true or clearly presented as a dramatisation.

FAQ

Is there a guide on story structure and open loops for brand films?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan. Screenwriting books and storytelling research are good companions.

How long should a brand film be?

As long as the story needs and no longer. Wistia's data shows engagement declines after about five minutes, so longer films need strong tension throughout.

Should the brand appear at the start?

Usually not prominently. Open on the story and save the logo for the end card.

Can a brand film use actors?

Yes, but present dramatisations honestly. Believable details matter more than whether the people are real customers.

What is narrative transportation?

Being drawn into a story's world. Research links it to identifiable characters, an imaginable plot and believability.
