How do I write the first five seconds of a skippable YouTube in-stream ad?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 25 November 2026 · Last updated 25 November 2026

Treat the first five seconds as a complete mini-ad: show what it is about, who it is from and why the viewer should care, before the skip button appears. Then give people a reason to continue rather than hoping they will.

Key takeaways

  • Google Ads Help says of skippable in-stream ads: "After 5 seconds, the viewer has the option to skip the ad and continue watching the video" (Google Ads Help).
  • The same page says that for CPV bidding you pay "when a viewer watches 30 seconds of your video," or interacts, whichever comes first, so the first five seconds is where you earn or lose the rest (Google Ads Help).
  • It lists recommended lengths of ":15-:20" for awareness and action goals, so the opening must carry a short ad, not a long one (Google Ads Help).
  • For organic videos, YouTube says the intro metric shows "what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds" (YouTube Help). It is a useful second check on any opening.

What the first five seconds must do

Viewers did not choose your ad. They came for another video, and the skip option is coming. In five seconds you have three jobs. First, make clear what the ad is about, in a picture and a line, so the viewer can decide quickly. Second, make the brand known without a long logo sequence, because a viewer who skips may still remember who you are. Third, give a reason to stay, such as a question, a problem or a result they care about.

Two common failures are opposites. One is the slow start: a logo, a swoosh, a calm intro that wastes the five seconds. The other is the empty shock: a loud opening that has nothing to do with the product, which gets attention and then loses it.

Step-by-step: write the five seconds

  1. Write the one sentence the ad has to land. If you cannot say it in a line, five seconds will not carry it.
  2. Put the picture of that sentence in the first frame. Do not build up to it.
  3. Say or show the brand within the five seconds. A name on a product, a line of speech or a small corner mark can do it.
  4. Open on the problem or the result. These answer "why should I care" faster than a description.
  5. Write the first spoken line to be heard with the sound low. Add captions or on-screen text so it works without audio.
  6. Cut dead air. No fade-in, no pause before the first word.
  7. Plant a reason to continue. A question left open, a promised demonstration or a number about to be explained gives the viewer a reason not to press skip.
  8. Check the cut at five seconds. The viewer should be mid-thought, not finished, when the skip button appears.
  9. Make the next ten seconds pay off the opening. An opening that promises and then changes subject loses people.
  10. Test two openings. Keep the rest of the ad the same and compare how many view past the early seconds.

Example: a booking app ad

Example: a booking app for local gyms plans a 20-second ad. The first version opens with a two-second logo animation, then a calm voice says the app's name and slogan. The second version opens on a person staring at a full class schedule with a line over it: "Class full again?" At second three the app's name appears on the screen they tap, and the voice adds "Book the next open one in two taps." The close-up of the tap is the reason to stay for the demonstration. Honest limits

A strong opening does not make a poor offer work, and some viewers will skip regardless. Do not mislead to hold attention: an opening that promises something the ad does not deliver may keep viewers for five seconds and cost trust. Also, results vary by audience and placement, so test with your own data.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with a logo or intro that wastes the five seconds.
  • Making the first line depend on audio.
  • Opening with a hook that has nothing to do with the offer.
  • Delivering the whole message in five seconds and giving no reason to continue.

FAQ

When can viewers skip a YouTube in-stream ad?

After 5 seconds, according to Google Ads Help.

Should I put my logo first?

Rarely. Show the brand within five seconds, but not as a standalone opening.

How long should the whole ad be?

Google Ads Help lists ":15-:20" as recommended for awareness and action goals.
