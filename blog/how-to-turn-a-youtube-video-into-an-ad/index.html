How do I turn one of my YouTube videos into an ad?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

To turn a YouTube video into an ad, use your own retention data to find the strongest moment (where almost nobody leaves or where people rewatch), cut that moment into a short ad of 15 to 30 seconds, add a new opening that states the promise in the first seconds, burn in captions, end with a clear call to action, and export vertical and horizontal versions. Don't just run the full video as an ad; the opening that suits subscribers rarely suits a cold audience.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's key moments mark "top moments" (where almost no viewers dropped off) and "spikes" (parts that "were rewatched or shared").
  • Those moments are the best raw material for an ad.
  • Google recommends Shorts ads under 60 seconds and vertical 9:16; skippable in-stream ads have no maximum, but under 3 minutes is recommended.
  • A cold audience needs the promise stated within the first seconds; TikTok says within 3.
  • Make the ad's claim match what the full video actually shows.

Step 1: find the best moment

YouTube's guide to key moments for audience retention defines four markers on each video's retention graph. Two matter here:

MarkerDefinitionUse for an ad
Top momentsSections where almost no viewers dropped offYour most gripping content
Spikes"Moments in your video that were rewatched or shared"Your most valuable or surprising moment (check it isn't just confusing)

Key moments show on videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views. For how to read them, see what key moments for audience retention are.

Step 2: write a new hook

Your video's original opening was written for people who chose to click. An ad interrupts people who didn't. Add a 2 to 3-second opening that states the result or the question the clip answers. TikTok's creative best practices say to "introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds".

Step 3: cut to length and format

PlacementLengthShapeSource
YouTube Shorts adsUnder 60 s; 10 to 30 s for action-focused9:16Google Shorts ads guide
YouTube skippable in-streamNo max; under 3 min recommended; skip after 5 s16:9Google ad formats
YouTube bumper6 s16:9Google ad formats
ReelsUnder 90 s9:16, bottom 35% clearMeta for Developers

Reframe for vertical rather than just cropping: keep your face and any key visual in the centre.

Step 4: add captions and a call to action

Burn in captions for sound-off viewing (see how to make a video ad work with the sound off) and end with one clear instruction: watch the full video, visit the site, or buy. Google's ABCDs say: "Say what you'd like viewers to do."

Step 5: test against a fresh ad

Run the repurposed clip against an ad made specifically for advertising, to the same audience. Clips from your best-performing video sometimes win because the content is proven; sometimes a purpose-made ad wins because it's tighter. The data decides.

Worked example

An illustrative cooking channel has a 14-minute video on making sourdough. YouTube's key moments show a spike at 9:40, where the creator reveals the crumb after the first cut, and a top moment from 6:00 to 7:30 on shaping.

The 25-second ad:

SecondsContent
0 to 3The crumb reveal from 9:40, text: "My first sourdough that actually rose"
3 to 15Shaping highlights from 6:00 to 7:30, sped up, captions
15 to 22Back to the loaf, close-up
22 to 25"Full method on the channel" or the course offer, with button

The creator exports 9:16 for Shorts and Reels and 16:9 for in-stream, then compares hook rate and clicks with a purpose-shot ad. To understand early drop-off, see hook rate and hold rate in video ads.

Mistakes to avoid when repurposing

Repurposed ads fail for predictable reasons. Check for these before you launch.

MistakeWhy it hurtsFix
Running the original introIt was written for subscribers who chose to watchWrite a new 2 to 3-second hook
Inside jokes or channel referencesCold viewers don't get themCut anything that needs prior context
Ending with "subscribe" when you want salesMixed messageOne call to action that fits the campaign goal
Cropping horizontal to vertical without reframingFaces and products get cut offReposition each shot for 9:16
Using a spike that was actually confusionYou're amplifying a confusing momentCheck why viewers rewound before using it
Claims in the clip that need contextMay mislead out of contextAdd context or cut the claim
Music you don't have ad rights forLicences for organic videos may not cover adsCheck the licence or replace the track

Check the music. Music that's fine in your organic video may not be licensed for paid advertising. Meta's Reels ads documentation says any third-party music must be free of copyright claims. Replace tracks if you're unsure.

Keep claims accurate out of context. A line that's fair inside a 14-minute video ("this changed everything for me") can read as a bigger promise in a 20-second ad. Under the UK CAP Code, ads must not materially mislead or exaggerate performance, so review every line as a stand-alone claim.

Build a clip library. Each time you publish a long video, note its top moments and spikes in a simple spreadsheet: video, timestamp, what happens, possible ad hook. Over time you'll have a bank of proven moments to cut into ads quickly when you need fresh creative.

Combine clips. Some of the best repurposed ads stitch two or three strong moments from different videos around one theme, such as three quick before-and-afters. Keep one promise and one call to action.

Compare with organic performance. A moment that held viewers who chose your video may not hold strangers in a feed. Treat repurposed clips as tests and let the ad data decide. For measurement, see where viewers drop off in a video ad and how to A/B test video ad hooks.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches cutting your best moments into short video?

The Retention Editing Playbook covers reading retention and cutting at £1,000 one-time. YouTube's key moments and Google's ABCDs are free.

Can I run my whole YouTube video as an ad?

You can on skippable in-stream, but a short cut with a new hook usually holds a cold audience better.

Which part of my video should become the ad?

Look at top moments and spikes in YouTube Studio's retention report.

Do I need a vertical version?

For Shorts, Reels and TikTok, yes; Google says vertical suits the Shorts format best.
