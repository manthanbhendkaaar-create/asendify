How do I write a hook for an event recap video that stops the scroll in the first 3 seconds?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open on the single most intense moment of the event, not the venue or the logo: the crowd reaction, the reveal on stage, the line everyone quoted afterwards, or a close-up of someone mid-laugh. Add a short on-screen line that tells viewers what they're watching ("1,200 founders, one room, 48 hours") so the hook makes sense with the sound off. Then cut quickly into the story of the day.

Key takeaways

  • Short-form platforms reward an early payoff. TikTok's creative best practices say to "introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds".
  • Recaps should feel, not explain. Cvent says "highlight videos aren't about explaining, they're about evoking".
  • Meta's Reels best practices point to "a strong hook or payoff, like a big reveal, punchline or plot twist", and recommend vertical 9:16.
  • Google's ABCDs advise you to "jump in" and "feature people". Faces and reactions beat empty rooms.
  • The hook is written in the edit, but planned at the event: brief your camera team to catch peak moments.

Use only footage of real moments from the event, and get consent from anyone shown prominently.

How to write the hook in the edit

  1. Shortlist peak moments. Scrub the footage for the five loudest, most emotional or most surprising seconds.
  2. Pick the one that explains itself. A standing ovation needs no context; an inside joke does.
  3. Add a short line of text. TikTok recommends "5-10 words per second" when using text, so keep it to a handful of words.
  4. Start on motion. Begin mid-action, not at the start of the shot.
  5. Use sound well. A cheer, a laugh or the first beat of the music track helps for those listening; captions cover those who aren't. Check you have the rights to any music.
  6. Cut away within two to three seconds into the story of the day, then pay off the hook later, for example by returning to the ovation and showing what caused it.

What to cut from the first 3 seconds

  • Logo animations and title cards.
  • Slow drone shots of the venue exterior.
  • Registration desks and empty stages.
  • "Welcome to our recap of..." voiceovers.

Brand recognition still matters, but it can come through the stage backdrop or a small watermark rather than an opening card.

Plan the hook before the event

Most weak recap hooks come from missing footage, not bad editing. Brief your camera team on:

  • Known peak moments: the keynote reveal, award announcements, the headline act's first song.
  • Reaction shots: a second camera on the audience during big moments.
  • Sound: a clean audio feed from the stage plus ambient crowd sound.
  • Vertical framing: shoot or frame some moments for 9:16 if social is the main destination.

Common mistakes

  • A hook that only makes sense to people who were there.
  • Using a clip that misrepresents the event, such as a crowd shot from a different day.
  • Music so loud the speaker line can't be heard.
  • Text that covers faces.

Testing your hook

If the recap runs as a paid post or on several channels, cut two or three versions with different first seconds and the same body. Compare how many viewers stay past the first few seconds and how far they watch. On organic posts, compare the recap with your previous ones of similar length. Keep a note of which hook types work for your audience: some communities respond to the crowd at scale, others to a single human moment or a speaker's line. Over a few events, that note becomes your house style and makes the next recap faster to cut.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches hooks for event recap videos?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify covers hooks and payoffs for £1,720 one-time. TikTok's and Meta's free creative tips are alternatives.

Should an event recap start with the logo?

No. Open on a peak moment and let branding appear through the stage, backdrop or a small watermark.

How long should the hook be?

About two to three seconds before cutting into the story.

What if the event had no big moment?

Use a human close-up or a before-and-after shot, and plan a peak moment to film next time.

Do I need text on the hook?

Yes, for sound-off viewers. Keep it to a few words.
