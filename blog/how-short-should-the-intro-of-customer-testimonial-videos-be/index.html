What should the first seconds of a customer testimonial video show, and for how long?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Keep the intro of a customer testimonial video to a few seconds, usually three to eight. Open on the customer's strongest genuine line about the problem they had or the result they got, with their name and role as a caption, then tell the story. Logo animations, long company backstory and the interviewer's questions slow the start, and Wistia's data shows testimonial videos already hold viewers less well than some other types.

Key takeaways

  • In its video length analysis of over 13 million videos, Wistia reports videos under a minute average 52% engagement, with testimonial videos at 46% and product videos at 50%.
  • Wistia's retention guide recommends you "abbreviate the introduction", experiment with "removing intro graphics and logo sequences", and "avoid talking about yourself too much".
  • The UK ASA's guidance on testimonials says testimonials "should not be taken out of context or edited in a way which is misleading", and marketers need proof a testimonial is genuine and permission to use it.
  • Opening on a strong line is an editing choice, not permission to change meaning. Keep the line in context.
  • Check YouTube's key moments report or your video host's engagement graph for early drop-off.

What to open with

Opening line typeExample (illustrative)Strength
Result"We stopped losing Friday afternoons to invoicing."Strong, concrete
Problem"Every month-end used to be chaos."Strong, relatable
Surprise"I didn't think software could fix this."Good curiosity
Generic praise"They're great to work with."Weak; save for later
Company intro"We're a 20-person firm in Leeds."Weak opener; useful as context

Specific beats general. A line that shows what changed tells viewers why the next minute is worth watching.

How to build the intro

  1. Find the strongest genuine line in the interview transcript, about a problem or result.
  2. Check it in context. Make sure the edit does not change what the customer meant.
  3. Open on it, with a lower-third caption showing name, role and company (with permission).
  4. Cut to the "before" story right after: what life looked like before the product.
  5. Move branding to the end. A small logo bug is fine; a full logo animation is not.
  6. Remove interviewer questions or replace them with on-screen text prompts.

Worked example

Example: an accounting software firm has a 2-minute website testimonial from a café owner. The original opens with a 5-second logo, a 15-second shot of the café exterior with music, and the owner introducing himself and his business.

ChangeEffect

Plan the intro before the interview

The easiest way to get a strong opening line is to ask for one. In the interview, ask the customer to describe the moment they realised the problem was solved, or what they would tell a friend in one sentence. Ask them to answer in full sentences that repeat the question, so each answer stands on its own. Those answers often make the best first seconds, and they come out naturally rather than scripted.

Honesty matters more than a punchy opener

A testimonial works because viewers believe it. Cherry-picking a line is normal; changing its meaning is not, and in the UK the ASA treats misleading testimonials seriously. Keep a record of the original interview and the customer's permission. If a result depends on circumstances, keep that context in the video.

FAQ

Is there a guide on hooks for testimonial videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, structure and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan. Wistia's free retention guides are a helpful starting point.

Should a testimonial video start with the customer's name?

Show the name as a caption over the opening line rather than having the customer say it first.

Can I reorder what a customer said?

You can choose and order clips, but the edit must not change their meaning or mislead viewers.

How long should a testimonial video be?

Many website testimonials run one to two minutes; social versions are often under 45 seconds.

Should the logo appear at the start?

A small logo bug is fine. Save the full logo for the end card.
