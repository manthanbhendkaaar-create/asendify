How many tags should a YouTube video have?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A handful of accurate tags is enough, and many videos need none. YouTube's own help page says tags "play a minimal role in your video's discovery" and that the title, thumbnail and description matter more. Tags are most useful for words people often misspell. The hard limit is 500 characters in total, and stuffing extra tags into the description breaks YouTube's spam policy.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube says tags play a minimal role in discovery compared with the title, thumbnail and description.
  • Tags help most when your subject is commonly misspelled.
  • The tag field allows 500 characters in total; commas count, and tags with spaces count their quotation marks too.
  • Hashtags are different: up to three can show above the title, and a video with more than 60 hashtags has all of them ignored.
  • Adding excessive tags to the description is against YouTube's spam, deceptive practices and scams policies.

What YouTube says about tags

YouTube's help page Add tags to videos describes tags as "descriptive keywords you can add to your video to help viewers find your content", then adds that your title, thumbnail and description matter more, and "tags play a minimal role in your video's discovery". The one clear use it gives: "Tags can be useful if the content of your video is commonly misspelled." It also warns that "adding excessive tags to your video description is against our policies on spam, deceptive practices, and scams."

The YouTube Data API videos reference sets the limit: the tag list has "a maximum length of 500 characters", commas between tags count, and a tag containing a space is treated as if wrapped in quotation marks, which also count. So "Foo-Baz" uses 7 characters but "Foo Baz" uses 9.

YouTube's hashtags help page says up to three hashtags "that are considered most engaging" appear by the title, that more than 60 hashtags means YouTube will "ignore each hashtag on that content", and that hashtags must be directly related to the video.

How many tags, in practice

There is no official number. A sensible approach:

  1. Your main topic as people would search it: "sourdough starter".
  2. Common misspellings or alternative spellings: "sour dough starter", "sourdough starter UK".
  3. One or two close variants: "how to make sourdough starter".
  4. Your channel or series name if people search for it.

That is usually 3 to 8 tags. Beyond that, extra tags rarely add anything YouTube does not already get from your title, description and the video itself.

Worked example: cleaning up a tag list

Example: a baking channel's video "How to Make a Sourdough Starter in 7 Days" has 42 tags using almost all 500 characters, including "bread", "food", "cooking", "recipe", "viral", "trending", rival channel names and "2026".

Removed: rival channel names (misleading), "viral" and "trending" (not descriptive), broad words like "food" (too vague to help). Five tags, about 110 characters. The title and description already carry the main keywords, which matter more.

Where your keyword effort pays off instead

If tags play a minimal role, where should the time go? YouTube's help page on how YouTube search works says relevance looks at how well the title, tags, description and video content match the query, alongside engagement and quality signals. In practice:

  • Title: put the words people search near the start, in plain language.
  • Description: write two or three sentences that explain what the video covers, using the phrases a viewer would use. Add chapters if the video is long.
  • Spoken words and on-screen text: say the topic out loud early; it makes the video clearly about what the title says.
  • Engagement: a video people keep watching sends stronger signals than any keyword list.

Tags take two minutes. The title, the opening and the description deserve the next twenty.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Other creators' names as tags. It does not describe your video and risks breaching YouTube's misleading metadata rules.
  • Pasting tags into the description. YouTube says excessive tags in the description are against its spam policy.
  • Dozens of hashtags. More than 60 and YouTube ignores all of them.
  • Spending more time on tags than on the title. YouTube is clear about which matters more.
  • Copying a competitor's tags wholesale. Their tags describe their video, not yours.
  • Never revisiting old videos. If a topic is often misspelled and an old video gets search traffic, adding the misspelling as a tag takes seconds.
