How quickly should an explainer video get to the point?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

An explainer video intro should usually take five to fifteen seconds: state the question, say why it matters to the viewer, and promise what they will understand by the end. Then start explaining. Wistia's data shows early drop-off rises with video length, and learning research supports a brief signpost of what is coming. Long scene-setting, logo animations and greetings cost viewers before the explanation starts.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia reports an average drop in the first 2% of a video of 4.9% for 1-2 minute videos and 17.3% for 5-10 minute videos, and advises: "Abbreviate the introduction to the material. Get to the point, already!"
  • Mayer's signaling principle, "people learn more deeply from a multimedia message when cues are added that highlight the organization", had positive results in 26 of 28 tests. A short "here is what we'll cover" can help, if it is brief.
  • Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014) found shorter videos "are much more engaging" and that videos planned for online viewing beat recorded lectures.
  • YouTube's key moments report shows the share of viewers still watching after 30 seconds; use it to judge your intro.
  • Signpost briefly, then deliver. A roadmap helps; a long preamble hurts.

These are working ranges, not platform rules. Check them against your own retention graph.

What a good explainer intro contains

  1. The question. "Why do flights get cheaper on Tuesdays, or do they?"
  2. Why the viewer should care. "It could change when you book."
  3. The promise. "By the end you'll know what really moves the price."
  4. Optional roadmap, one sentence, for longer videos: "We'll look at demand, algorithms and the myths."

That can all fit in under 15 seconds. Everything else, including the presenter's name, channel plugs and background history, can come later or be cut.

Common mistakes

MistakeFix
Starting with definitionsStart with the question or a surprising fact
Roadmap longer than the hookOne sentence, or chapters instead
Answering the question in the introPromise the answer; deliver it through the video
Same branded intro on every videoShort, topic-specific opening each time
Visual intro with no voice or textState the question clearly in words

Testing two intros

If you are unsure, make two versions of the opening for the same video: one that opens on the question and one that opens on a surprising fact or a visual. On YouTube you can compare similar videos over time; in ads you can run both openings against the same audience. Keep everything after the first 15 seconds identical so the intro is the only difference, and judge on how many viewers are still watching at 30 seconds.

Should the intro give away the answer?

For many explainers, no. Give a reason to stay, not the full answer. But do not tease forever: if the payoff comes too late, viewers feel tricked and leave. A useful pattern is to give part of the answer early, then build the full explanation.

FAQ

Is there a guide on writing explainer video intros that hold attention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan. Wistia's free retention library is a useful companion.

Should an explainer start with a roadmap?

A one-sentence roadmap helps on longer explainers. Keep it after the question and stakes, not before.

How long should an animated explainer be?

Many product explainers run one to two minutes. Make the intro a few seconds and get to the problem fast.

Is it OK to start with a story?

Yes, if the story raises the question within a few seconds. Long stories before the point lose viewers.

Where should the sponsor go in an explainer?

After the intro, once viewers know what they will get. Many creators place it a few minutes in.
