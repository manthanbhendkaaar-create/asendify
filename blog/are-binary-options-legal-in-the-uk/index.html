Are binary options legal in the UK?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

No, not for ordinary investors. The FCA permanently banned the sale, marketing and distribution of binary options to retail consumers in the UK from 2 April 2019, calling them "gambling products dressed up as financial instruments". If a website, app or social media account offers you binary options in the UK, treat it as a serious warning sign: it is either targeting professionals only or, far more likely, operating without authorisation.

Key takeaways

  • A binary option pays a fixed amount if a yes/no condition is met (for example, "will this price be higher in 5 minutes?") and nothing if not.
  • ESMA banned binary options for retail clients across the EU on a temporary basis from 2 July 2018.
  • The FCA made the ban permanent in the UK from 2 April 2019, and extended it to "securitised" binary options.
  • The FCA estimated the ban could save retail consumers up to £17 million a year and reduce fraud.
  • Binary options scams remain common; check any firm on the FCA's Firm Checker before sending money.

What the FCA decided

The FCA's statement confirming the permanent ban says its rules are in substance the same as ESMA's EU-wide temporary restrictions, but also apply to securitised binary options, which ESMA had excluded. The rules came into force on 2 April 2019. It estimated savings of up to £17 million a year for retail consumers.

Christopher Woolard, then the FCA's Executive Director of Strategy and Competition, said: "Binary options are gambling products dressed up as financial instruments."

The policy statement, PS19/11, notes that ESMA had already prohibited the sale of binary options on a temporary basis since 2 July 2018, and that the FCA's rules would replace ESMA's measures after the UK left the EU.

Why regulators banned them

FeatureWhy it hurts retail traders
Very short expiries (minutes or seconds)Outcomes are close to random; encourages rapid, repeated betting
Fixed payout below 100% on wins, 100% loss on lossesThe structure builds in a negative expected return
Firm often takes the other sideConflict of interest: the firm profits when you lose
Marketed as "investing"Hides that the product behaves like a bet

The FCA's protect yourself from scams page lists binary options scams among common investment scams, alongside forex, crypto and online trading scams.

Worked example: the maths of a fixed payout

Example (illustrative): a platform offers an 80% payout on winning 5-minute "higher or lower" bets, and you lose your full stake on losing bets.

Your win rateResult per 100 bets of £10
50%50 × £8 − 50 × £10 = −£100
55%55 × £8 − 45 × £10 = −£10
56%56 × £8 − 44 × £10 = +£8
60%60 × £8 − 40 × £10 = +£80

You need to be right about 56% of the time on very short price moves just to come out ahead, before any manipulation or withdrawal problems. Most people cannot predict 5-minute moves better than a coin flip. Our page on what trading expectancy is explains how to run this calculation for any strategy.

Red flags if someone offers you binary options

  1. Contact through social media, WhatsApp or a dating app.
  2. Promises of fixed high returns or "guaranteed" profits.
  3. Pressure to deposit more to "unlock" withdrawals.
  4. A firm not on the FCA's Firm Checker, or one using a real firm's name with different contact details (a clone).
  5. Requests to install remote-access software on your device.

If you have already sent money, see what to do if you have been scammed by a trading platform. For the related topic of social media trading tips, see are trading signal groups and finfluencers legal in the UK.

What is still allowed

The ban covers binary options for retail consumers. Regulated, leveraged products such as CFDs and spread bets are still available to retail clients, with leverage caps and negative balance protection; see the FCA's CFD leverage limits. They are still high risk, and most retail accounts lose money.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking an offshore licence makes it safe. Firms outside the FCA's reach may not follow UK rules or let you complain to the Financial Ombudsman Service.
  • Calling it "investing". Short binary bets behave like gambling.
  • Paying a "recovery" firm that promises to get lost money back for a fee; these are often scams too.

A quick self-check if you are being offered "binary trading"

QuestionSafe answer
Did they contact me first?No, I found a regulated firm myself
Is the firm on the FCA Firm Checker with matching details?Yes
Are returns promised?No, risks are explained
Am I being asked to pay to withdraw?No
Is the product a binary option for retail clients?No, because these are banned in the UK

If any answer is not the safe one, stop and check with the FCA before sending money.

Why the ban matters for spotting scams

Because no FCA-authorised firm can sell binary options to UK retail clients, any binary options offer aimed at you is either outside UK rules or fake. That makes the product name itself a useful warning sign: you do not need to understand the details to know something is wrong.

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS calculates win rate, P&L and R-multiple from your logged trades. A spreadsheet is the free alternative.

When did the UK ban binary options?

The FCA's permanent ban for retail consumers came into force on 2 April 2019.

Can professionals trade binary options?

The ban covers retail consumers. Professional clients are treated differently, but very few individuals qualify.

Are binary options gambling?

The FCA described them as "gambling products dressed up as financial instruments".

Is a binary options app on social media legal?

If it offers binary options to UK retail customers, it is breaking FCA rules or operating without authorisation. Check the Firm Checker.

What should I do if I lost money to a binary options site?

Contact your bank straight away, report it to Report Fraud, and tell the FCA.
