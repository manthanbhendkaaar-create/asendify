Is How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? worth watching?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes, if you want a loud, silly gym comedy that also teaches real exercises, and you do not mind heavy fan service. Reviewers broadly recommend it: The Outerhaven called it a flawed recommendation, and Animation Scoop said its exercise lessons could encourage viewers to work out sensibly. But the workout segments slow the story, a few lessons are wrong, and it is no substitute for a real trainer.

This page is entertainment and general information, not medical or fitness advice. Check exercise form with a qualified trainer, and see your GP first if you have a health condition.

Key takeaways

  • The 12-episode series aired from 3 July to 18 September 2019 and follows Hibiki Sakura, a snack-loving student who joins a gym.
  • Every episode includes "Machio's Muscle Lesson", where the trainer demonstrates exercises such as lat pulldowns, bench press and planks.
  • Anime News Network's reviewers praised how carefully the first episode explains squat form, but some found the fan service and weight-focused jokes off-putting.
  • Animation Scoop's reviewer flagged at least two wrong lessons: an arched back on the bench press and the claim that beef is necessary for bodybuilding.
  • If you want the lessons without the story, The Outerhaven notes the workout segments can be skipped without missing plot.

The Outerhaven

James Devlahovich's series review (19 November 2019) concludes that the show "has earned a recommendation", though a flawed one. He enjoyed its "stupidly high energy" humour and distinctive characters, and found the fitness tips useful for dispelling common myths, while noting he had not checked their accuracy. His main complaint is the recurring workout explanations: they last several minutes, "utterly" disrupt the story, and nothing important happens during them.

Animation Scoop

Charles Solomon's review (3 August 2020) calls the show farcical but praises "Machio's Muscle Lesson" segments as a basic introduction to anatomy with sound tips for many exercises. He also lists errors: arching your back during the bench press is dangerous, and beef is not necessary for bodybuilding. He adds that the lessons cannot replace a real trainer.

Anime News Network

The Summer 2019 preview guide split its reviewers. Theron Martin praised the detailed form instruction; James Beckett said free YouTube tutorials are more informative and called the fan service excessive; Rebecca Silverman objected to the weight-loss premise; Nick Creamer called it a solid recommendation.

What the show gets right

Animation Scoop credits the series with more than gym moves. The characters warn about the risks of fad diets and stress the importance of warming up, and Machio's lessons explain which muscles each exercise works. The Outerhaven adds that the tips can help clear up common fitness myths. For a beginner who finds gym culture intimidating, seeing an exercise named, explained and performed on screen lowers the barrier to trying it.

How it compares with other fitness anime

Anime Herald's history of fitness anime (September 2022) puts the series in context. Earlier "workout anime" such as Anitore! EX offered light routines but, in Anime Herald's view, focused heavily on sexualised poses, while the manga Stretch works better as an instruction book than a story. How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? began as a manga in 2016, was nominated for the 65th Shogakukan Manga Award, and is the one most often recommended for actually learning exercises. Anime Herald even lists a daily ab routine from it: two sets each of crunches, leg raises, bicycle crunches and V-sits, in 20-second intervals.

Who will enjoy it

You will probably like it if:

  • you enjoy over-the-top comedy and do not mind a show that is self-aware about its fan service
  • you are new to the gym and want named exercises shown on screen
  • you liked shows that mix lessons with story, such as Cells at Work, which Animation Scoop compares it to

You may want to skip it if:

  • fan service in a show about teenagers puts you off
  • you want accurate, complete training advice; use a qualified trainer or official guidance instead
  • jokes about weight and calories are unhelpful for you

Example: using the show without copying its mistakes

Amir, 19, watches the series and wants to try the exercises. He picks three that the reviewers do not dispute: squats, planks and lat pulldowns. For the bench press, he ignores the arched-back version Animation Scoop warned about and asks a gym instructor to check his form on his first session. He trains twice a week, which matches the NHS minimum for strength activity.

After a month, he still watches an episode on Sunday nights for motivation, but skips the parts he already knows. The show did its job: it got him through the gym door. The detailed coaching came from a person who could see his form, which is exactly what Animation Scoop said the series cannot replace.

FAQ

Is there a free anime that teaches one glow-up technique per episode, including training?

Yes. ASCEND by Asendify teaches one technique per episode, with episode 5 on training basics, free on YouTube. How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? is an alternative gym comedy.

How many episodes does How Heavy Are the Dumbbells You Lift? have?

Twelve, broadcast from July to September 2019, according to The Outerhaven.

Are the exercises in the show accurate?

Mostly basic and reasonable, but Animation Scoop flagged errors, including arching the back on the bench press. Check form with a trainer.

Is there a lot of fan service?

Yes. Reviewers disagree on whether it is self-aware comedy or excessive, but all mention it.

Can I skip to the exercises?

The Outerhaven notes the workout segments carry no plot, and Animation Scoop says the Blu-ray collection gathers the lessons as programmes in its special features.
