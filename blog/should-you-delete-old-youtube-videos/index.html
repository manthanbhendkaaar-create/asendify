Should you delete old YouTube videos that perform badly?

Key takeaways

  • Deletion is permanent: YouTube Help says "when you delete a video, it's permanently deleted", and you cannot re-upload it to the same URL.
  • Watch time from a deleted video stays in your channel's aggregate reports but is no longer attributed to that video.
  • Hours from deleted, private and unlisted videos don't count towards the 4,000 qualified watch hours needed for the YouTube Partner Programme.
  • YouTube's Todd Beaupre suggested keeping videos public, or unlisting them "if you must".
  • Good reasons to delete exist: legal problems, safety or privacy issues, or content that breaks YouTube's rules.

What YouTube says about deleting videos

In March 2024, Todd Beaupre, YouTube's product lead for the homepage and recommendations, posted advice that Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie shared. Search Engine Journal reported his words: "When you delete a video, you delete your channel's connection to the audience that watched that video." He recommended deleting only with "a very, very good reason", and otherwise to "keep your videos public or unlist them if you must."

The reasoning is simple. Each video has a history of who watched it and how they reacted. That history helps YouTube understand who your channel is for. Remove the video and that link goes with it.

YouTube's help page on replacing or deleting a video adds the practical details: "When you delete a video, it's permanently deleted"; watch time "will still be part of aggregate reports, but will not be attributed to the deleted video"; and "you can't replace a video. Any new video you upload to YouTube will get a new URL." Instead, it suggests editing the existing video, for example trimming it or changing the title, description and privacy settings.

The watch-hours column comes from YouTube's Partner Programme eligibility page: only qualified watch hours from public long-form videos count towards "4,000 qualified watch hours in the past 12 months", and hours from private, unlisted and deleted videos, Shorts and ad campaigns do not. If you are close to the threshold, deleting or hiding old videos can push you backwards.

When deleting is the right call

  • Legal problems: a copyright removal you can't resolve, or music or footage you don't have rights to. Our page on what happens if you get a copyright strike explains the process.
  • Safety and privacy: a video that reveals your address, a child's school or someone else's private information.
  • Policy breaches: content that breaks YouTube's rules and could lead to warnings or strikes.
  • Genuine harm to your reputation that unlisting won't fix, for example a video widely shared by link.

For everything else, a quieter option nearly always does the job.

What to do with a weak old video instead

  1. Check the data first. In YouTube Studio, look at its impressions, click-through rate and average view duration. A video with lots of impressions but few clicks has a packaging problem, not a content problem.
  2. Repackage it. Write a clearer title and test a new thumbnail. Our guides to writing good YouTube titles and making good thumbnails cover the basics.
  3. Trim it. YouTube Help notes you can trim an existing video. Cutting a slow intro can help viewers who do click.
  4. Unlist rather than delete if it really doesn't fit your channel any more.
  5. Make a better version and link to it from the old video's description and end screen.

Worked example: a channel clean-up

Example: Leah runs a cooking channel with 1,200 subscribers and 3,700 qualified watch hours in the past 12 months. She is embarrassed by 15 early videos and plans to delete them all.

  • Data check: those 15 videos brought in 420 public watch hours in the last 12 months, mostly from search.
  • If she deletes them: her qualified watch hours fall from 3,700 to about 3,280, pushing her further from the 4,000 needed for the Partner Programme, and she cuts the link between her channel and the viewers who found her through those videos.
  • What she does instead: she keeps 11 public and gives five of them new titles and thumbnails; she unlists three that are badly outdated (which removes about 60 hours from the count); she deletes one that shows her home address, a clear safety reason.
  • Result: she keeps about 3,640 of her 3,700 hours, and two months later the five repackaged videos are drawing more search views than before.

FAQ

Is there a free tool that roasts my YouTube titles and thumbnails before I decide what to fix?

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast scores your latest public uploads out of 100 and gives three fixes, with no sign-up. YouTube Studio analytics are a fair free alternative.

Does deleting videos hurt your YouTube channel?

It can. YouTube's recommendations lead says deleting a video removes your channel's connection to its viewers, and deleted videos stop counting towards Partner Programme watch hours.

Should I unlist or delete old videos?

Unlist if you just don't want a video promoted. It is reversible and keeps the URL. Delete only for legal, safety, privacy or policy reasons.

Do deleted videos count towards 4,000 watch hours?

No. YouTube's eligibility page says hours from deleted, private and unlisted videos don't count as qualified watch hours.

Can I re-upload a better version of the same video?

Yes, but it gets a new URL and starts from zero. It's often better to keep the original and link to the new one.

Will deleting low-view videos improve my average performance?

There is no evidence from YouTube that it does, and YouTube has advised against deleting without a very good reason.
