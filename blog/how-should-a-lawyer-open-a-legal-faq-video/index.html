How should a lawyer open a legal FAQ video so viewers don't click away?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Say the viewer's exact question in your first sentence, then give the short general answer within about 15 seconds, before any introduction. "Can my landlord keep my deposit for cleaning? Usually only if... here's how it works." People searching legal questions want to know quickly whether they're in the right place; a name, firm and "in today's video" opening sends them back to the search results. Then explain, flag the exceptions, and make clear it's general information.

This page is about video structure, not legal advice. Legal content in your videos should be accurate for the jurisdiction you name and reviewed before publishing.

Key takeaways

  • The first sentence should match the search: the same words a client would type.
  • A short general answer up front builds trust; holding it back as a "tease" feels evasive on legal topics.
  • Then use an open loop that's honest: "There are two exceptions, and the second one catches most people out."
  • Name the jurisdiction early ("in England and Wales"); the law differs between countries and states.
  • YouTube's retention report shows an Intro figure, the share of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds, which tells you whether the opening worked.

Openings compared

Weak openingStrong opening
"Hi, I'm Sarah from Smith & Co Solicitors, and today...""Can your employer change your contract without asking? In England and Wales, usually not without your agreement. Here's when they can."
"Wills are very important...""What happens to your house if you die without a will? It depends on whether you're married. Let me explain."
"Let's talk about divorce""How long does a divorce take in England? At least 26 weeks from application, and here's why."

Put your name and firm in a lower-third during the answer instead of saying them first.

A structure for a three-minute FAQ video

  1. 0:00 Question + short answer + jurisdiction.
  2. 0:15 Honest loop: "There are three situations where that changes."
  3. 0:20 The explanation, one point at a time, each with on-screen text.
  4. 2:00 Common mistakes people make in this situation.
  5. 2:30 What to do next: documents to gather, where official guidance sits, when to speak to a lawyer.
  6. 2:50 Disclaimer and next step. "This is general information, not advice on your situation."

Add chapters for anything over a couple of minutes; YouTube chapters need 00:00 first, at least three timestamps and at least 10 seconds each.

Keeping it general information, not advice

  • Speak in general terms ("usually", "in most cases") and name the exceptions.
  • Don't answer viewers' specific situations in comments; invite them to contact the firm properly.
  • Date the video in the description and update or unlist it if the law changes.
  • In England and Wales, publicity must be "accurate and not misleading" under the SRA Code; other regulators have similar rules.

Editing for retention

  • Cut breaths and repeats, but keep a natural pace; legal topics need clarity, not speed.
  • On-screen text for key terms and dates so people can follow without rewinding.
  • Simple graphics for timelines (for example, the stages of a process).
  • Change framing between points (close, medium) to reset attention without distracting.
  • End screen pointing to the next related question.

Example: a deposit-dispute video

Example: a property lawyer's video opens with a 30-second introduction of the firm. The re-edit opens with "Can your landlord keep your deposit for cleaning in England? Only for cleaning beyond normal wear and tear, and here's how the deposit schemes handle disputes." The firm name appears as a lower-third, and the video ends with where to find the official scheme guidance. Details are illustrative.

The Retention Editing Playbook (£1,720 one-time) teaches hooks and open loops for YouTube; it isn't law-specific.

Should I give the answer away at the start?

Yes, the short general answer. The detail and exceptions are what keep people watching.

Where should the disclaimer go?

A brief line on screen near the start or end, and in the description, without a long spoken preamble.

How long should a legal FAQ video be?

Long enough to answer one question properly, often two to five minutes.

Should I reply to legal questions in comments?

Keep replies general and invite people to contact the firm; don't advise on individual cases publicly.
