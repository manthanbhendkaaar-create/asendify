How much is road tax on a supercar in the UK?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 14 November 2026 · Last updated 14 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

For a new petrol supercar registered in the UK from April 2017 onwards, road tax (Vehicle Excise Duty) is usually £5,690 for the first year if it emits over 255 g/km of CO2, then £640 a year for the next five years because of the expensive car supplement for cars with a list price over £40,000, and then £200 a year after that. Over the first six years, that adds up to £8,890.

Key takeaways

  • First-year rate depends on CO2: £5,690 for petrol cars over 255 g/km, £4,850 for 226 to 255 g/km.
  • From the second year, cars with a list price over £40,000 pay £640 a year (£200 standard rate plus a £440 supplement) for five years.
  • After those five years, the rate drops to the standard £200 a year.
  • The supplement is based on the original list price, not what you paid, so a used supercar still pays it until it is six years past first tax.
  • Electric cars only pay the supplement above a £50,000 list price, from 1 April 2026.

This page is general information, not tax advice. Rates change, usually each April, so check the current GOV.UK tables before you buy.

The rates for cars registered from 1 April 2017

From the GOV.UK rates for cars registered on or after 1 April 2017 and the main vehicle tax rate tables:

Paying monthly costs a little more: GOV.UK lists £672 over 12 Direct Debit payments for the supplement rate, against £640 in one go.

How "list price" works

GOV.UK defines the list price as the published price of the vehicle before its first registration, before any discounts. It is not what you pay on the used market. A ten-year-old car that cost £180,000 new and now sells for £70,000 was still a "list price over £40,000" car, but by then its five supplement years are long over.

GOV.UK advises checking the list price with your dealer if you are unsure.

Electric supercars

The government policy paper on the supplement threshold raised the threshold for zero-emission cars from £40,000 to £50,000 from 1 April 2026, while keeping £40,000 for all other cars. Zero-emission cars registered before 1 April 2025 do not pay the supplement. An electric hypercar with a six-figure list price still pays it.

The first-year rate is paid by whoever first registers the car, which is one reason buying used can be cheaper to run. For a supercar, though, road tax is usually a small part of total running costs next to insurance, servicing, tyres and depreciation.

Older cars

Cars first registered before 1 April 2017 are taxed under a different table based on CO2 emissions (or engine size for older models), and they do not pay the expensive car supplement. GOV.UK has separate rate tables for these, so check the right table for the car's first registration date.

FAQ

Do supercars pay more road tax every year?

Only for the first six years of their life. After year one, they pay £640 a year for five years if their list price was over £40,000, then the standard £200.

Does a used supercar pay the expensive car supplement?

Yes, if it is still within five years of its second tax payment. The supplement depends on the original list price, not the used price.

How much is road tax on a Lamborghini or Ferrari?

For a new model registered from April 2017 with CO2 over 255 g/km, £5,690 in the first year, £640 a year for years two to six, then £200. Older models follow the pre-2017 tables.

Do electric supercars pay road tax?

Yes. Since April 2025 electric cars pay vehicle tax, and those with a list price over £50,000 pay the expensive car supplement.

Can I avoid the expensive car supplement?

Not legally, if the car's list price was over the threshold. Buying a car more than six years past its first tax avoids it in practice, because the five supplement years will have passed.
