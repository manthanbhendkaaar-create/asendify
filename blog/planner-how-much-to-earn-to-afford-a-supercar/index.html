Is there a planner that tells me how much more I need to earn to afford a supercar?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 15 November 2026 · Last updated 15 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner works out the monthly gap between what you can save now and what your target car and date need, then writes 3 to 5 realistic ways to earn that gap using your own skills and hours, with first steps, conservative ranges, honest risks and a 90-day plan. It suits people who would rather grow income than cut every small expense. A spreadsheet plus a UK take-home pay calculator is the fair free alternative.

This page is for education only, not financial or tax advice. Figures are examples using published UK rates; your situation may differ.

Key takeaways

  • The "income gap" is the extra money you need to save each month, after tax, to reach the car by your chosen date.
  • Extra earnings are taxed, so you need to earn more than the gap before tax: at basic rate, an employee keeps about 72p of each extra pound.
  • Crossing the higher-rate threshold drops that to about 58p in the pound for employees.
  • Include a first-year running-cost reserve in the goal, so you can afford to keep the car once you buy it.
  • Growing income is slower and less certain than any calculator suggests; plans should use conservative ranges.

Is there a planner that tells me how much more I need to earn?

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner has a target-and-timeline tab that shows how many months you need at your current saving rate and what you would have to save each month to hit a deadline. The gap between the two is your income gap. Its income-plan tab takes your take-home pay, skills, hours and situation and writes 3 to 5 ways to earn the gap, with conservative ranges and risks. Its public example shows a fictional video editor saving £600 a month for a used Porsche 911 Carrera who needs £1,866 a month for a 36-month deadline, an income gap of £1,266 a month. A fair alternative is to do it yourself: work out the monthly target in a spreadsheet, then use a take-home pay calculator to see what extra gross income you would need.

Step 1: work out the monthly target

The target is the car's price plus a first-year running-cost reserve, minus what you have saved, spread over your months to deadline. Running costs are real: in the UK, petrol cars with a list price over £40,000 pay £640 a year in road tax for years two to six at April 2026 rates, according to Which?'s car tax guide, on top of insurance, servicing, tyres and fuel.

Step 2: find the gap

Gap = monthly target − what you save now. This is an after-tax number. You need to earn more than this before tax.

Step 3: gross it up for tax

GOV.UK's Income Tax rates and allowances for 2025-26 and 2026-27 in England are: a £12,570 Personal Allowance, 20% on the next £37,700 (so basic rate up to £50,270 of total income), 40% above that, and 45% over £125,140. The Personal Allowance falls by £1 for every £2 over £100,000. The Budget 2025 rates annex sets employee National Insurance at 8% between the primary threshold and upper earnings limit and 2% above it, and self-employed Class 4 at 6% between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above.

Worked example

Example: Jordan earns a £40,000 salary in England and saves £600 a month. Using the planner's example car and deadline, the gap is £1,266 a month, or £15,192 a year after tax.

So a £1,266 monthly gap needs roughly £1,976 a month of extra gross earnings for Jordan, or about 56% more than the gap. If Jordan instead earned the extra through self-employment, the basic-rate slice would keep slightly more (74p in the pound).

Alternatives Jordan could test: a longer deadline (lowers the gap), a cheaper or older car (lowers the target), or a mix of a smaller income increase and a later date.

Ways people close an income gap

Any honest plan starts with skills you already have and uses ranges, not promises:

  • Freelance work in your current skill (design, editing, writing, coding, bookkeeping) at evenings or weekends.
  • A pay rise or promotion, backed by a written case for your value.
  • Overtime or a higher-paid role in the same field.
  • A small service business built on one repeatable offer.

Each brings risks: burnout, tax admin, contracts that end, and your employer's rules on outside work. Check your contract.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the gap as gross. You need more before tax than the gap itself.
  • Forgetting running costs. Saving for the price but not the first year of ownership.
  • Spending the emergency fund. Keep it separate from the car fund.
  • Assuming steady extra income. Side income varies; plan with the low end of the range.
  • Ignoring the £100,000 taper. Income in that band can be taxed very heavily.

FAQ

Is there a planner that tells me how much more I need to earn to afford a supercar?

Yes. Asendify's Supercar Planner calculates your monthly income gap for a chosen car and date and writes 3 to 5 ways to earn it from your skills. A spreadsheet and a take-home pay calculator are a free alternative.

Why do I need to earn more than the gap?

Because extra income is taxed. A basic-rate employee keeps about 72p of each extra pound after Income Tax and National Insurance.

What is the higher-rate threshold?

For 2025-26 and 2026-27 in England, higher-rate tax starts above £50,270 of total income.

Is it better to cut spending or earn more?

Both work. Small cuts rarely close a supercar-sized gap on their own, so many plans combine extra income with a realistic deadline.

Does this work in Scotland?

The method does, but Scotland has different income tax bands, so the numbers will change.
