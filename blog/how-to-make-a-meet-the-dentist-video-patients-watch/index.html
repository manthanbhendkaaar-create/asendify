How do I make a "meet the dentist" video that nervous patients actually watch?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Start with what the viewer is feeling, not with qualifications. A nervous patient clicks a "meet the dentist" video to answer one question: "Will this person be kind to me?" So open with the dentist on camera naming that worry in their own words, show a real, calm moment from the surgery, keep credentials to one line, and finish in about 60 to 90 seconds with how to book.

Key takeaways

  • The hook is empathy: "If you haven't been to a dentist in years, I'd like you to know what happens at your first visit."
  • Viewers stay for a person, so most of the video should be the dentist talking naturally, not stock shots of instruments.
  • In the UK, the GDC's guidance on advertising says publicity "should be legal, decent, honest and truthful" and that your GDC registration number should be included where information names you.
  • The same guidance says dentists not on a specialist list "must not use titles which may imply specialist status", so watch on-screen titles.
  • Avoid drills, needles and close-ups of treatment unless the video is explicitly about a procedure.

A 75-second structure

SecondsOn screenPurpose
0 to 5Dentist to camera: names the viewer's worryHook through empathy
5 to 20Name, role, one line on experience, and why they became a dentistHuman connection
20 to 45What a first appointment involves, with footage of reception and the surgeryReduce uncertainty
45 to 60How they help nervous patients (stop signals, explaining each step)Answer the real objection
60 to 75How to book, with name, title and registration number on screenNext step and compliance

Filming tips that keep it watchable

  1. Interview, don't script. Ask questions off camera ("What do you say to someone who's scared?") and cut the best answers together. Read scripts sound stiff.
  2. Two angles. A front camera and a side camera let you cut out pauses without obvious jump cuts.
  3. Good audio first. A small clip-on mic matters more than a better camera; surgeries echo.
  4. Calm B-roll. Reception, a friendly greeting, the dentist explaining a model to a patient (with consent). Avoid instruments and bright lights in faces.
  5. Captions. Many people watch practice videos on their phone in a waiting area or at work with sound off.
  6. Lower-third with name, title and GDC number in plain text, not a long animation.

What not to put in the video

AvoidWhy
"Pain-free dentistry guaranteed"You can't guarantee another person's experience; it risks being misleading
Implied specialist titlesGDC guidance restricts them to people on a specialist list
Patient footage without written consentPatients' images are personal data and need their agreement
Price claims without the detailsKeep prices on your website where conditions can sit beside them
Drill noise in the backgroundIt's the one sound nervous viewers react to

Example: a mixed NHS and private practice

Example: a practice's original video opens with a logo, then the dentist reads her qualifications for 40 seconds. The re-edit opens on her saying, "Lots of my patients tell me they've been putting off coming in for years. That's fine, we'll go at your pace." It then shows reception, the surgery with a chair reclined and a patient chatting, and ends with how to book and her name, title and registration number. Details are illustrative.

Where to use it

Put it on the practice website's "about" or "nervous patients" page, the Google Business Profile, and YouTube. Cut a 15 to 20 second version of the opening for social.

FAQ

Is there a book on making practice videos people watch?

The Retention Editing Playbook (£1,720 one-time) teaches hooks and pacing for YouTube; it isn't dental-specific but the structure applies.

How long should a meet-the-dentist video be?

About 60 to 90 seconds for a website, with a 15 to 20 second cut for social.

Do I need to show my GDC number in the video?

GDC guidance says your registration number should be included where information names you, so add it to the lower-third or description.

Can I call myself a cosmetic dentist?

Be careful with titles. GDC guidance says non-specialists must not use titles that may imply specialist status; check the guidance for your case.

Should I show treatment footage?

Not in an introduction video. Calm, human footage works better for nervous patients.
