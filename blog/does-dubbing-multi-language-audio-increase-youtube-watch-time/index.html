Does adding dubbed audio tracks increase watch time on YouTube?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Often, yes, if your audience includes people who don't speak the video's language. YouTube says creators who uploaded multi-language audio tracks saw over 25% of their watch time come from views in a non-primary language. Dubbing doesn't fix a weak edit, though: a dubbed video loses viewers at the same weak moments, so check retention for each audio track separately.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's help page on multi-language features says creators who uploaded multi-language audio saw "over 25% of their watch time" from views in the video's non-primary language. YouTube's September 2025 post dates that figure to July 2025.
  • There are two routes: upload your own recorded dubs (needs access to advanced features), or use automatic dubbing, which YouTube's auto dubbing explainer says covers 27 languages.
  • Viewers get the track that matches their preferred language by default and can switch in the player.
  • YouTube Analytics can split views and watch time by audio language, so you can see whether a dub is worth keeping.
  • More watch time from new languages is not the same as better retention. A dull middle stays dull in Spanish.

To add your own dub, YouTube's help page describes the route: YouTube Studio on desktop, Languages, pick the video, Add language, then add an audio-only file of roughly the video's length next to "Dub". If an automatic dub exists for that language, delete it first.

Why dubbing can lift watch time

It opens the video to people who would have left in seconds

A viewer who can't follow the language usually leaves in the first moments. With a dub in their language, the same viewer can stay for the whole video. That's the main source of the extra watch time YouTube reports.

Translated titles help people find it

YouTube Help says that once titles and descriptions are translated, videos can appear in search when viewers use the translated wording. More relevant viewers arrive in the first place.

The default track does the work

Viewers don't have to hunt for the dub. YouTube Help says audio defaults to the viewer's preferred language based on their watch history, and they can switch tracks in the player.

Why dubbing can disappoint

  • Weak pacing travels. If your original audio track has a slow intro, the dub will too. Fix the edit first.
  • Jokes and wordplay don't always translate. Automatic dubs translate meaning, not timing. A pun that lands at 0:05 in English may fall flat.
  • On-screen text stays in one language. If key information is only in burned-in English captions, dubbed viewers miss it. Our page on captions and retention covers when on-screen text helps.
  • Small or local audiences. A channel about one city's football club may not have meaningful demand in other languages. The analytics will show it.

How to check whether a dub is working

  1. Open the video in YouTube Studio analytics.
  2. Use the audio track filter (YouTube's explainer says creators can see performance by language this way).
  3. Compare views, watch time and the retention curve for each track.
  4. Look at where each curve dips. If both tracks dip at the same moment, the problem is the edit, not the language. Our guide to key moments in YouTube Studio explains what intro, dips and spikes mean.
  5. Give it time: a new dub needs several weeks of data before you can judge it.

Example: testing Spanish on a cooking channel

A UK cooking channel with mostly English-speaking viewers uploads a 10-minute recipe video and turns on automatic dubbing for Spanish.

TrackWhat the creator watches forDecision rule
English (original)Usual intro hold and middle dip at 4:30Baseline

After four weeks, the creator sees that both tracks lose viewers at 4:30, where the recipe pauses for a long ingredient story. The fix is an edit: cut the story to 20 seconds and add a re-hook ("this is the step most people get wrong"). The dub brought new viewers; the edit is what keeps them.

FAQ

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops, re-hooks and pattern interrupts, with a Bonus Pack including a 30-day plan. YouTube's help pages on dubbing are a free companion.

Does automatic dubbing hurt my video's discovery?

YouTube's auto dubbing explainer says dubbing has no negative impact on viewer discovery, and creators can turn it off or replace it with their own dubs.

How many people watch auto-dubbed videos?

YouTube says that as of December 2025, over 6 million viewers a day watched 10 minutes or more of auto-dubbed content.

Can I see watch time for each language?

Yes. YouTube Help says Analytics can break down views and watch time by audio language.

Should I dub Shorts or long videos first?

Start with long videos that already hold viewers well in the original language. They have the most watch time to gain, and you'll have a clear baseline to compare against.

Do I still need translated titles?

They help. YouTube Help says translated titles and descriptions let videos appear when viewers search in that language.
