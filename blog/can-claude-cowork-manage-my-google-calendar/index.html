Can Claude Cowork manage my Google Calendar?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Yes. With the Google Calendar connector turned on, Claude Cowork can view your events and shared calendars, find times when attendees are free, create, update and delete events, set up recurring meetings and accept, decline or tentatively accept invitations. Key takeaways

  • Anthropic's help centre says the Calendar connector can "view events and calendars, including shared calendars you have access to" and "create, update, and delete events with full customization".
  • It can "find mutual availability across attendees" and respond to invitations.
  • Each action needs your approval by default, outside Auto mode. On Team and Enterprise plans, an owner must enable the Google Workspace connectors first.
  • Claude acts with your Google account's access, so it can only see and change calendars you can.
  • Start read-only (summaries and suggestions) before letting Claude book or move meetings.

Approvals and Auto mode

Anthropic's help article says: "By default, outside Auto mode, each action Claude takes on your behalf requires your explicit approval." In Auto mode, actions can run without a prompt unless an automated check flags them or your organisation requires approval, and Anthropic warns that this check can miss risky actions. For calendars, a wrongly deleted meeting or an accepted invite you didn't want is annoying but recoverable; a meeting sent to a client at the wrong time is more embarrassing. Keep approvals on for anything that sends invites to other people.

The first two are good starting points as scheduled tasks. The last two touch other people's calendars, so approve each one.

Sharing and permissions

Claude works through your Google account, so what it can do on someone else's calendar depends on what they've shared with you. Google's help on sharing a calendar lists levels from "See only free/busy (hide details)" up to "Make changes and manage sharing". If an assistant's calendar is shared with you at "See only free/busy", Claude can see when they're busy but not the event details, and can't edit their events.

Worked example: a morning calendar task

An illustrative set-up for a solo founder:

  1. Turn on the Google Calendar connector (on Team plans, ask the owner to enable it).
  2. Create a scheduled task for 7:30 each weekday: "Summarise today's calendar: meetings, attendees, gaps of 60 minutes or more, and any clashes. Suggest changes but don't make them."
  3. Read the summary over breakfast; if a change is needed, ask Claude to make it and approve the action.

After two weeks, if the suggestions are reliable, allow Claude to add focus blocks to your own calendar while keeping approvals for anything involving other people.

Safe set-up checklist

Before letting Claude act on your calendar, a few minutes of set-up avoids most problems.

StepWhy
Start with read-only prompts (summaries, free slots)Lets you check Claude reads your calendar correctly
Keep approvals on for anything that invites or notifies othersMistakes are visible to clients and colleagues
Be specific about time zones in promptsAvoids meetings booked at the wrong hour
Name calendars explicitly if you have severalPrevents events landing on the wrong calendar
Review scheduled-task output for a week or twoBuilds confidence before giving more freedom
Check your organisation's settings on Team plansOwners decide whether members can skip approval

Write clear prompts. "Book a meeting with Sam next week" leaves a lot to guess. "Find a 30-minute slot between 10am and 4pm UK time on Tuesday to Thursday next week when Sam and I are both free, then draft an invite titled 'Q3 plan' and show it to me before sending" is much safer.

Watch recurring events. Changing or deleting a recurring meeting can affect every instance. Ask Claude to confirm whether a change applies to one occurrence or the whole series before approving.

Private events. If you mark some events as private, think about whether you want them included in summaries. You can ask Claude to skip events marked private, and check the output.

Combine with email carefully. A powerful routine is "summarise today's meetings and draft prep notes from related emails". It's also where mistakes multiply. Keep the output as drafts for you to review, rather than sending anything automatically. See can Claude Cowork manage my Gmail inbox.

Know how to stop it. Learn where to pause or delete a scheduled task and how to disconnect the connector, so you can stop quickly if something isn't working as expected. For more on safe use, see is Claude Cowork safe for business.
