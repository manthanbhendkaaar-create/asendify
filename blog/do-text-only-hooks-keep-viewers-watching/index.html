Do text-only hooks keep viewers watching, or do you need a spoken hook?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A text-only hook can hold viewers, especially in feeds where many people watch with the sound off, but it works best when it is short, readable in a second or two and backed up by the picture. For most videos the strongest option is text and voice saying the same thing at the same time. Platform guidance from Google, TikTok and Meta all points that way: make the opening understandable without sound, and reinforce it with audio for those who have it on.

Key takeaways

  • Sound-off viewing is common on phones. A 2019 US survey by Verizon Media and Publicis Media, reported by Next TV, found 92% of respondents viewed videos with the sound off on mobile, and 80% said they were more likely to finish a video when captions were available.
  • Google's ABCD guidance for video ads says to "reinforce your message with audio and text" and to "avoid competing elements". For Shorts it adds: "Announce yourself with audio."
  • TikTok's creative best practices say to prioritise the hook in the first 6 seconds and recommend "5-10 words per second when using text".
  • A Facebook-commissioned report covered by Search Engine Journal said video ads "should be able to be viewed, understood, and enjoyed without sound".
  • None of these sources says text-only beats voice. The practical answer is: design for sound off, then add voice for sound on.

When a text-only hook makes sense

  1. The platform is mostly muted. Ads in Facebook and Instagram feeds, and silent autoplay on websites, are watched without sound by many people.
  2. The video is set to music. If there is no voice-over at all, the hook has to be on screen.
  3. The message fits in one glance. "3 mistakes that kill your ad in 2 seconds" works. A 25-word sentence does not.
  4. The picture supports the words. Text over a static, empty shot gives the viewer one reason to stay instead of two.

When you need a spoken hook as well

  • YouTube long-form. Most viewers have chosen to watch and have sound on. A voice carries energy and personality that text cannot.
  • Podcast clips. The appeal is the speaker. Use captions, but let the first spoken line be the hook.
  • Anything with emotion or humour. Tone of voice does a lot of the work.
  • Shorts. Google's own Shorts advice is to announce yourself with audio, so do not rely on text alone there.

TikTok's upper limit of 5-10 words per second is a ceiling, not a target. Read every card aloud at normal speed; if it vanishes before you finish, hold it longer.

Worked example

Example: a skincare brand runs a 20-second Reels ad. Version A opens with the founder talking to camera and no on-screen text. Version B opens with the same shot plus the text "Stop doing this after you wash your face" in large type, and the founder saying the same words.

Common mistakes

  1. Text and voice that say different things. The viewer has to choose one, and often chooses to scroll.
  2. A paragraph on the first frame. Nobody reads it in time.
  3. Text in the caption-bar zone. Platform buttons and captions cover it.
  4. Relying on auto-captions as the hook. They appear word by word and are often too small to carry the opening.
  5. No visual change. Text alone on a still shot rarely holds attention.

Is there a playbook that covers text hooks and spoken hooks?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and open loops with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. Google's ABCD guidance is a free starting point.

How many words should a text hook have?

Aim for 3-8 words on the first card, so it can be read in a second or two on a phone.

Do captions count as a hook?

Not on their own. Captions help muted viewers follow along, but the hook should be a deliberate, large line of text or a strong spoken line.

Should YouTube long-form videos use text hooks?

They can, but most long-form viewers have sound on, so the spoken line and the picture usually matter more. Text helps reinforce the main promise.

What text size works on mobile?

Large enough to read at arm's length on a small phone. Test on the smallest screen you can find before publishing.
