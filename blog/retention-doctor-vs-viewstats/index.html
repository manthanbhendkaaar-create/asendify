Retention Doctor vs ViewStats: which is better for improving YouTube retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

They solve different problems. ViewStats is a research suite for finding what works on YouTube in general: outlier videos, competitors, thumbnails and ideas. Retention Doctor reads your own channel's retention after each upload and tells you the second viewers left and what to change. If you need better ideas and packaging, ViewStats fits; if viewers click but leave, a retention tool fits.

Key takeaways

  • ViewStats' Pro tools, per its help centre, are Competitors, Outliers, Alerts, Thumbnail Search, A/B Test, Collections/Ideation and Outliers Advanced Search.
  • ViewStats' pricing page lists Business "starting at $249/mo"; the Pro price was not shown when we checked in November 2026.
  • Retention Doctor does one job: it reads your channel read-only and reports drop-offs, with a fix for each, a Retention Score and a Monday three-fix plan.
  • Your own retention curve lives in YouTube Studio; research tools mostly look outward at other channels.
  • Many creators need research first and retention second; very few need both from day one.

What ViewStats is built for

ViewStats' pricing page describes Pro as "our Youtube analytics suite. Perfect for creators of all sizes looking to grow their channel" and Business as "our most powerful plan now with unlimited credits. Great for teams and agencies", starting at $249 a month. Its general sections talk about finding what makes videos go viral, tracking competitors and identifying successful formats in a niche.

The ViewStats Pro help centre names the tools: Competitors, Outliers, Alerts, Thumbnail Search, A/B Test, Collections/Ideation and Outliers Advanced Search. That is an outward-looking toolkit: it helps you study videos that over-performed, on your channel and others, and plan ideas and thumbnails around them. It is strong at the "what should I make and how should I package it" question.

What Retention Doctor is built for

Retention Doctor by Asendify looks inward at one thing: your own audience retention. It connects read-only to one channel and, about 48 hours after each upload, shows the second viewers left with a suggested fix for each drop, gives the video a Retention Score from 0 to 100 and sends a three-fix plan every Monday. It does not do keyword research, competitor tracking or thumbnail search.

The data it reads is the same retention data YouTube shows in Studio. YouTube's key moments page describes it: the intro figure (still watching at 30 seconds), top moments, spikes (rewatched or shared parts) and dips (parts viewers skipped or left), on videos of at least 60 seconds with at least 100 views. Outside tools get it through the YouTube Analytics API, which exposes metrics such as audienceWatchRatio and stoppedWatching for small slices of each video. Neither tool can see data YouTube doesn't share.

Which problem do you have?

YouTube's Content tab analytics tips describe a three-step funnel: appeal (does the thumbnail earn the click, measured by click-through rate), engagement (do people watch) and satisfaction (do they keep watching, measured by average view duration). Use it to pick a tool:

  • Low impressions or low click-through rate: your problem is appeal. Research tools such as ViewStats, or the free YouTube Studio Reach tab, help most.
  • Good click-through, poor retention: your problem is satisfaction. A retention tool, or careful reading of Studio's retention graph, helps most.
  • Both fine, slow growth: you may need more videos, a clearer niche or better topics, which again is research.

Worked example

Example: two channels with the same 10,000 subscribers.

Channel A, a finance explainer, gets 4% click-through on its thumbnails and 55% of viewers still watching at 30 seconds. Retention is healthy, but impressions are flat. The creator needs topics that the audience wants: an outlier-research tool is the better spend.

Channel B, a travel vlog, gets 9% click-through, but only 38% still watching at 30 seconds, with a sharp dip at 0:12 where a long drone shot plays before the creator speaks. Packaging works; the opening does not. A tool that flags that 0:12 dip on every upload, with a fix, is the better spend. These figures are illustrative.

Questions to ask before paying for either

  1. What is my weakest number: impressions, click-through rate or retention? Studio's Reach and Engagement tabs answer this for free.
  2. How often do I upload? A per-upload retention report is worth more at weekly uploads than monthly ones.
  3. Who will act on it? Research suits whoever plans topics; retention notes suit whoever edits.
  4. What access does each tool need, and can I revoke it easily from my Google account?
  5. Can I cancel monthly? Check each site's current terms before you subscribe.

FAQ

Is there a tool that only reads my retention and tells me the second viewers left?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor reads one channel read-only and flags each drop-off with a fix after every upload. ViewStats is built for research instead.

Does ViewStats show audience retention?

Its listed Pro tools focus on competitors, outliers, alerts, thumbnail search, A/B tests and ideation. Your own retention curve is in YouTube Studio.

How much does ViewStats cost?

Its pricing page lists Business from $249 a month. Check the site for the current Pro price, as it was not displayed when we checked.

Can I use both?

Yes. Some creators research ideas with one tool and check retention with another. Pay for both only if both problems are real.

Which should a new channel choose?

Usually neither at first. YouTube Studio is free; research ideas and packaging before paying for retention analysis.
