Should you let viewers clip your YouTube videos and live streams?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

For most channels, leave Clips on. A YouTube Clip is a 5 to 60 second slice of your video or live stream that a viewer shares; it plays from your original video, and Studio shows you how many clips each video has, who made them and how many views each got. That makes clips a free signal of which moments people found worth sharing. Turn them off only if you have a specific reason, such as licensed footage you can't have excerpted or moments you don't want lifted out of context.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube says clipping "is turned on by default".
  • Clips must be at least 5 seconds and no more than 60 seconds.
  • Live streams can only be clipped with DVR on, and only within the DVR window; other clips play after the stream is posted as a video.
  • Clips disappear if the original is deleted, made private or removed for breaking the Community Guidelines; clips of unlisted videos stay available.
  • In YouTube Studio you can see each video's clips, who made them and the views on each clip.

All facts on this page about how Clips work come from YouTube's help article Create & manage Clips unless another source is linked.

What a Clip is (and isn't)

A Clip is not a new upload. It points to a section of your existing video, so the original stays yours and the clip is only viewable when the viewer could also watch the original. YouTube says clips can appear on "select search, discovery, and analytics surfaces".

YouTube Clips are also different from the clips you cut yourself and upload as Shorts. Those are new videos with their own analytics; viewer Clips are bookmarks to a section of yours.

Why clips help a retention-minded channel

  1. They show your best moments. If five people clipped the same 40 seconds, that's the part of the video that landed. Use it as your next Short, your next thumbnail idea or your next cold open.
  2. They pair with "Most replayed". The most replayed graph tells you where people rewatch; clips tell you what they wanted to share.
  3. They carry your best part to new viewers. A clip that lands in a group chat starts on your strongest moment, which a full link can't do.
  4. They're useful for long formats. Podcasts, live streams and event talks are long; clips let fans pull out the minute that matters.

When turning clips off makes sense

SituationWhy you might switch them off
You use licensed footage or music with excerpt restrictionsClips republish short sections out of context
Interviews or debates where a line could be misleading aloneA 20-second clip can strip away the answer that follows
Courses or paid previews where the payoff is the productYou may not want the key step shared on its own
Brand or client content with approval rulesThe client may want every cut approved

To turn them off, YouTube's help article links to the steps; creators commonly use YouTube Studio, Settings, Channel, Advanced settings, then untick the Clips option. Check the article first, as menus change.

How to use clips as editing feedback

  • Monthly check: in Studio, filter your content to videos with clips and note the timestamps people clipped.
  • Compare with retention: if a clipped moment sits right after a big drop in the retention graph, viewers who stayed loved it, but many never reached it. Move it earlier or tease it in the hook.
  • Turn clipped moments into Shorts with your own edit: tighter framing, captions and a clean loop.
  • Live streams: make sure DVR is on if you want viewers to clip during the stream.

Worked example

Example: a video podcast has a 70-minute episode. Studio shows six clips, four of them within the same two-minute story at 48:00. The retention graph shows most viewers have left by then. For the next episode the editor teases that story in the first 30 seconds as a cold open and posts the story as a Short with a related-video link back to the episode. Details are illustrative.
