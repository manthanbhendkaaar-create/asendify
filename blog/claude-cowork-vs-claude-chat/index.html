What is the difference between Claude Cowork and normal Claude chat?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 7 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Normal Claude chat answers you one message at a time, while Claude Cowork takes on a whole multi-step task and hands back finished work such as documents, spreadsheets or organised files. Cowork keeps running in the cloud after you step away and can use your connected apps and local files. Anthropic is now merging the two into one conversation.

Key takeaways

  • Chat is best for questions, drafting and thinking things through together. Cowork is built for tasks with several steps and a finished output.
  • Cowork uses the same agentic architecture as Claude Code, but with no terminal, and it needs a paid Claude plan.
  • Cowork sessions run in the cloud, so work continues if you close your laptop. Local files and your browser need the Claude Desktop app open.
  • Because Cowork can take real actions, permission settings matter more than in a normal chat.
  • Anthropic is rolling out a version of Claude where chat and Cowork share one conversation, so the choice is becoming automatic.

What Claude Cowork actually is

Anthropic's own get started with Claude Cowork guide describes Cowork as bringing "Claude Code's agentic capabilities to knowledge work beyond coding". Instead of responding to prompts one at a time, Claude takes on complex, multi-step tasks and carries them out for you. The guide puts it simply: you can describe an outcome, step away and come back to finished work.

When you start a Cowork task, Claude analyses the request and makes a plan, breaks big jobs into subtasks, runs code in an isolated environment on Anthropic's servers, can coordinate several workstreams in parallel, and delivers the output to your session for you to preview and download. You can watch progress and step in to redirect it at any point.

Cowork is available on paid plans: Pro, Max, Ultra, Team and Enterprise. On the Claude Desktop app it is available on all paid plans. On web and mobile it is available on Pro, Max, Ultra and Team (in beta on Team), and on Enterprise when an owner turns it on.

The practical differences that matter

1. Tasks, not turns

In chat, you lead each step. You ask, Claude answers, you ask the next thing. In Cowork, you hand over the whole job. A good test is the output: if you want an answer, use chat; if you want a file or a finished piece of work, Cowork is the better fit.

2. Work that continues without you

Cowork runs your sessions in the cloud, so your sessions and files live with your Claude account and follow you across desktop, web and mobile. Anthropic says work continues if you close your laptop. The exception is anything on your own computer: tasks that use local files, your browser or computer use need the desktop app open and connected.

3. Files and apps

On desktop, Claude can read from and write to folders you connect, with no manual uploads. Apps you have connected, such as Google Drive or Slack, can be used while Claude carries out a task. By default, Claude asks for your permission before permanently deleting any files.

4. Scheduling

Cowork can run saved tasks on demand or on a schedule. Anthropic's page on scheduled tasks in Cowork lists uses such as daily briefings, weekly reports and recurring research. Scheduled tasks run remotely, so they run even when your computer is asleep. Each run is its own Cowork session that you can review.

5. More care with permissions

A chat mostly produces text. Cowork can act. Anthropic's guide to using Cowork safely explains that the risk depends on what Claude can read and what it is allowed to do, and warns about prompt injection: hidden instructions in content Claude reads, such as an email or web page. Its advice includes avoiding access to local files with sensitive information, like financial documents, and only extending internet access to sites you trust.

Cowork offers three permission modes. Manual asks you to approve actions. Auto lets Claude keep working while each action is checked for safety. Skip removes the checks and should only be used when you fully trust everything involved. For anything with real consequences, such as money or messages sent in your name, Anthropic recommends staying close and reviewing what Claude does.

6. Usage

Multi-step tasks use more of your plan's usage than a quick question, because every step uses tokens. The Cowork guide suggests grouping related work into one task, starting a new conversation for unrelated work, and telling Claude when you only need a quick answer rather than a file.

Chat and Cowork are becoming one Claude

Anthropic is rolling out what it calls the new Claude experience. Its article Claude Cowork and chat are one Claude says chat and Cowork are merged into a single conversation, "so you don't need to decide which option better suits your task". Claude answers a quick question quickly and takes on larger work, like research, reports, spreadsheets and presentations, in the same thread.

The rollout is gradual on Pro, Max and Ultra plans. On Team and Enterprise plans it appears where the "Chat and Cowork unified" setting is on. If your message box still shows separate "Chat" and "Cowork" options, you do not have it yet. Your chats, tasks, projects and settings move with you, and once your account has the new experience you cannot switch back.

So the difference is shifting from "which product do I open" to "what kind of request am I making". The habits in this page still apply: the way you describe a task, the permissions you give and how you review the output all decide whether the result is useful.

Example: the same job done both ways

Example: you run a small online shop and want a summary of last month's 40 customer emails, with the five most common complaints and a draft reply for each.

In chat, you would spend most of the time moving text around. In Cowork, you spend it checking the result. The second approach still needs you: Claude can misread tone or miss context, and replies to customers should be reviewed by a person before they go out.

Which should you use?

Use chat when you want to think something through, get a quick explanation, or draft a short piece you will edit straight away. Use Cowork when the job has several steps, touches files or apps, or should end in a deliverable. If you have the merged experience, simply describe what you want and Claude chooses.

FAQ

Is Claude Cowork free?

No. Anthropic lists Cowork as available on paid plans only: Pro, Max, Ultra, Team and Enterprise. Availability on web and mobile varies by plan.

Does Cowork replace Claude chat?

In the new Claude experience, chat and Cowork are merged into one conversation, so you no longer pick a mode. Until that reaches your account, you choose "Chat" or "Cowork" in the message box.

Does Cowork keep working if I close my laptop?

Cloud sessions keep running when you close your laptop or step away. Tasks that need your local files, browser or computer need the Claude Desktop app open.

Is Cowork the same as Claude Code?

Cowork uses the same agentic architecture as Claude Code, but it is designed for knowledge work and needs no terminal. Claude Code is aimed at software development.

Is Cowork riskier than chat?

It can be, because it can take actions in your files, apps and websites. Use the permission modes, connect only what a task needs, and review anything that sends messages, spends money or changes important files.
