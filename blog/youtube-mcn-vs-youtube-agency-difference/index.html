What is the difference between a YouTube MCN and a YouTube agency?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A multi-channel network (MCN) is a third-party company that affiliates many YouTube channels and usually takes a percentage of the creator's YouTube revenue in return for services such as rights management or audience development. A YouTube agency is a service business you pay directly, usually a fee, for work like editing, strategy or thumbnails, without your channel joining a network.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube says MCNs are "third-party service providers that affiliate with multiple YouTube channels" and are not endorsed by YouTube or Google.
  • Joining an MCN does not change your revenue share with YouTube, but MCNs typically take a percentage of the creator's portion before paying them.
  • MCN contracts are legally binding, and leaving an MCN can change your monetisation status.
  • Agencies are not a YouTube category at all; they are ordinary service providers, so the contract is the only protection.
  • You do not need either to succeed on YouTube, as YouTube itself says.

What YouTube says about MCNs

YouTube's MCN overview for creators describes MCNs as third-party service providers that may offer "audience development, content programming, creator collaborations, digital rights management, monetization, and/or sales". It stresses three things:

  1. No endorsement. "MCNs and other third-party service providers are not endorsed by YouTube or Google."
  2. Money. "Joining an MCN has no impact on your revenue share with YouTube", but "MCNs typically take a percentage of revenue from the creator's portion before paying them."
  3. Contracts. "MCN contracts are legally binding", so creators should know what services or results they get and what protection they have if the MCN pays late.

It also says an MCN cannot prevent strikes or takedowns, and YouTube's own support is available whether you are in an MCN or not.

YouTube's page on the Partner Program for MCN affiliates adds that affiliate channels must still join the YouTube Partner Program and contract with YouTube on its terms, and that when an MCN releases a channel, monetisation status can change.

Which one fits which creator

  • Large catalogues with rights issues (music, clips, compilations) sometimes benefit from an MCN's rights management, because Content ID work is specialised.
  • Creators who want better videos usually need an editor or agency, because what changes growth is the video itself: hooks, pacing, packaging and retention.
  • Small channels should be careful with any revenue-share deal. YouTube's own Partner Program overview sets entry at 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, so early revenue is often small and a long contract can outlast its value.

The MCN looks cheaper today and dearer if the channel grows; the agency costs more now but does not scale with success. These figures are illustrative, not quotes from any company. The real question is which service would actually move your views, and which contract you can leave.

Questions to ask before signing either

  1. What exactly will you deliver each month, in writing?
  2. How is payment calculated, and on which revenue lines?
  3. How long is the contract, and how do I leave?
  4. Who gets access to my channel, and with what role?
  5. Who owns the edited files and project files? (See who owns copyright when you hire a video editor.)
  6. What happens if you pay me late, or I pay you late?

Red flags in either kind of deal

  • Guaranteed subscriber or view numbers. Nobody controls YouTube's recommendations, so a promise of specific results should make you more careful, not less.
  • Asking for your Google password. Agencies and networks can work through channel permissions; there is no reason to share login details.
  • Revenue share on lines they do not touch. If an MCN only handles rights, ask why it takes a share of brand deals or memberships.
  • Auto-renewing terms with long notice periods. A two-year deal that renews silently can outlast the reason you signed.
  • Vague deliverables. "Growth support" is not a deliverable; "four edited videos and one written retention review a month" is.
  • No named contact. You should know who works on your channel and how to reach them.

If any of these appear, ask for changes in writing before you sign, or walk away.

Do I need an MCN to monetise on YouTube?

No. YouTube says you don't need to join an MCN to be successful, and every channel applies to the Partner Program on YouTube's terms.

Does joining an MCN lower my YouTube revenue share?

YouTube says it does not change your revenue share with YouTube, but MCNs typically take a percentage of your portion before paying you.

Can an MCN protect me from copyright strikes?

No. YouTube says an MCN can't prevent strikes or takedowns.
