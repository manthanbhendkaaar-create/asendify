Key takeaways

  • A curiosity gap is the space between what the viewer knows and what they want to know. Golman and Loewenstein describe curiosity as arising when a person "becomes aware of a gap in his or her knowledge" (Carnegie Mellon paper).
  • An open loop is the editing tool that creates that gap on purpose and closes it later.
  • Curiosity affects behaviour and memory: in a 2009 Psychological Science study, more curious people spent scarce tokens or waiting time to learn answers and recalled surprising answers better 1 to 2 weeks later.
  • You can check your loops in YouTube's key moments for audience retention report, which shows intros, dips and top moments.
  • The best learning set-up is one structured method plus practice on your own footage, not a pile of tips.

What editors actually need to learn

Most writing on curiosity gaps is aimed at copywriters and headline writers. Editors have a different job. You rarely write the script, but you decide what the viewer sees first, what gets held back and when the payoff lands. That means an editor's resource has to cover:

  • Spotting the payoff in raw footage or a transcript.
  • Moving or teasing it without changing what the speaker meant.
  • Holding the gap open with pacing, b-roll and sound rather than filler.
  • Re-opening new gaps in long videos before the viewer drifts.
  • Reading the retention graph to see whether a loop worked.

A resource that only explains the psychology will leave you guessing in the edit. A resource that only shows software tricks will not explain why a cut works.

The core method, in editing terms

  1. Find the answer first. Scrub the transcript for the strongest reveal: a result, a number, a twist or a strong opinion.
  2. Write the question it answers. One line, in the viewer's words.
  3. Put the question on screen early. Either a teased frame of the answer or the question as text and voice in the first seconds. TikTok advises advertisers to "introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds".
  4. Hold the gap with progress. Each section should move the viewer closer to the answer. Cut tangents that don't.
  5. Drop a clue in the middle. A partial reveal reassures the viewer the answer is coming.
  6. Open the next gap before closing this one. In long videos, this is the re-hook that stops the mid-video dip.
  7. Close every loop honestly. A gap that never closes feels like clickbait.

How to tell if a resource is any good

  • It uses real examples with timestamps, not only definitions.
  • It shows how to measure the result in YouTube Studio or ad dashboards.
  • It includes reusable templates or checklists you can apply to the next edit.
  • It avoids invented numbers. Be wary of claims like "loops double watch time" with no source.
  • It is honest about the research. The popular Zeigarnik claim that people remember unfinished tasks better is shaky; a 2025 meta-analysis found no memory advantage for unfinished tasks.

Common mistakes editors make with curiosity gaps

  • Vague teases. "You won't believe what happens" gives no specific question.
  • Spoiling the payoff. Showing the whole answer in the opening tease removes the reason to stay.
  • Misleading cuts. Reordering lines so a speaker seems to say something they didn't damages trust.
  • One loop for a long video. A single question rarely carries 15 minutes.
  • Never checking the graph. Without data you can't tell a working loop from a lucky upload.

What is the difference between a curiosity gap and an open loop?

A curiosity gap is the feeling of missing information. An open loop is the technique that creates it: a question raised early and answered later.

Can an editor add open loops if the script doesn't have them?

Often, yes. You can move a strong line or a glimpse of the result to the front, as long as you don't change its meaning.

How many open loops should a long YouTube video have?

There's no fixed number. Open a new question before you close the current one, so the viewer always has a reason to keep watching.

How do I know if my curiosity gap worked?

Compare the intro figure and the dips in YouTube's key moments report with a similar video that didn't use the technique.
