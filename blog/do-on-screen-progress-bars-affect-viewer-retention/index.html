Do on-screen progress bars affect viewer retention?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

An on-screen progress bar may help or hurt retention, and there is no solid published video study either way. The closest evidence comes from web surveys, where a meta-analysis found a steady progress bar did not significantly reduce drop-off, a bar that felt slow increased it, and one that moved fast early reduced it. So test it on your own videos rather than adding it by habit.

Key takeaways

  • An on-screen progress bar is a line or shape added in the edit that fills as the video plays, separate from the player's own scrub bar. It is common in Shorts, Reels, TikTok, video sales letters and list videos.
  • A meta-analysis of web survey experiments by Villar, Callegaro and Yang (2013) found "using a constant progress indicator does not significantly help reduce drop-offs", and that "fast-to-slow indicators reduced drop-offs, whereas slow-to-fast indicators increased drop-offs".
  • Harrison and colleagues' progress bar research at Carnegie Mellon found how a bar moves (acceleration, pauses) changes how long a wait feels.
  • YouTube's own way to show viewers where they are is chapters, which need at least three timestamps starting at 00:00, each at least 10 seconds long.
  • Use YouTube's key moments report to compare dips with and without a bar.

What the evidence does and doesn't say

Survey and user-interface research gives useful ideas, but it can't prove what will happen on your video. Treat the patterns below as hypotheses to test.

Why a progress bar might help

It answers "how long is this?" In a feed, viewers can't always see the length of a vertical video. A bar shows that the end is near, which may keep someone watching the last few seconds of a short list or tutorial.

It can make progress feel fast. The survey finding that fast-to-slow indicators reduced drop-off suggests early visible progress matters. In a video, splitting the bar into sections that fill quickly at the start (short first section) may feel similar.

It sets up a promise. A segmented bar for "5 tips" tells viewers how many items are left, which works like a small open loop.

Why a progress bar might hurt

It can feel slow. In the survey studies, indicators that showed slow progress increased drop-off. On a 10-minute video, a thin bar that barely moves may remind people how much is left.

It encourages skipping. If viewers can see the payoff is in the last section, some will scrub ahead. YouTube's key moments report counts skipping as a dip.

It adds clutter. On mobile, a bar can collide with captions, platform buttons or the player's own interface.

How to test a progress bar

  1. Pick a format you publish often, such as a weekly Short or list video.
  2. Alternate versions. Publish similar videos with and without a bar, keeping hooks and length as close as you can.
  3. Make the early part feel fast. If segmented, make the first segment short.
  4. Keep it out of the way. Place it where it won't clash with captions or platform buttons; test on a phone.
  5. Compare like with like. Look at the 30-second intro figure, the overall shape and the dips. Compare across several videos, not one.
  6. Keep or drop. If results are mixed after several videos, choose based on how it looks and what your audience says.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on structuring videos so people watch to the end?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops, pacing and structure, with templates and a 30-day plan. YouTube's chapter and retention help pages are free starting points.

Is a progress bar the same as YouTube chapters?

No. Chapters are a YouTube feature that splits the player's bar into labelled sections. An on-screen bar is a graphic you add in the edit.

Do progress bars increase watch time on TikTok?

There's no public study showing that. Test it on your own videos and compare several posts.

Where should I put a progress bar?

Usually at the top or bottom edge, clear of captions and platform buttons. Check it on a phone before publishing.

Should a video ad have a progress bar?

Rarely. Most ads are too short for a bar to help, and the space is better used for the product and message.
