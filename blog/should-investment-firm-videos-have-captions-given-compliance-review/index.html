Should investment firm videos have captions given compliance review?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

Yes, add captions, and treat them as part of the content that compliance reviews. Captions make a video usable for people who are deaf or hard of hearing and for anyone watching without sound, but they also put your words on screen in writing. Review the caption file as you review the script, keep required risk warnings on screen rather than only in a caption field, and get your own compliance team to sign off.

Key takeaways

  • The FCA's social media guidance expects financial promotions to be "fair, clear and not misleading" (FCA FG24/1).
  • For short-form video it describes compliant practice as "the risk warning is clearly and prominently displayed across the screen throughout the financial promotion", and treats a risk warning found "within the caption of the video" as non-compliant (same document, Table 1).
  • For long-form video, "the risk warning is displayed clearly and prominently on the screen for the section of the video" it relates to (same document).
  • The FCA also says firms should "take account of the latest version of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines" and consider its principles or an equivalent standard (same document).
  • WCAG says captions "convey not only the content of spoken dialogue" but also "meaningful sound effects", so people who are deaf or hard of hearing can follow (W3C).

This page is general information, not legal or compliance advice. Whether a particular video is a financial promotion, and what it must carry, is for your compliance function and, where needed, your lawyers.

Two different things called a caption

The word causes confusion. In this context there are two:

  1. Captions on the video: on-screen text that transcribes speech and meaningful sound, usually from a caption file or burned into the picture.
  2. The caption of a post: the text that sits under a video on social platforms.

The FCA guidance treats a risk warning that appears only in the second kind, the post caption below the video, as poor practice, at least for short-form video. On-screen captions that carry the warning as part of the picture are a different matter. Be clear which one you mean when you brief editors and compliance.

Why captions help an investment firm

Investment firms often publish explainers, market commentary and adviser videos. Many viewers watch on phones, at work or in public with sound off. Without captions, the message and the risk language are inaccessible to them. Captions also help non-native speakers and anyone in a noisy place. The FCA points firms to WCAG principles, which include captions for prerecorded video with sound.

Step 1: Treat the caption file as controlled text

If your firm reviews scripts, it should review captions too. Auto-generated captions can contain errors, especially with product names, tickers, percentages and numbers. A wrong number in a caption is a wrong number in the promotion.

  1. Generate captions, then proofread against the approved script.
  2. Fix names, figures and dates carefully.
  3. Send the final caption file to compliance with the video.
  4. Version the file so the approved captions are the ones published.
  5. Re-check after any edit. Retiming or trimming can break captions.

Step 2: Keep risk warnings on screen

The FCA guidance describes where warnings should appear for video: displayed clearly and prominently for the relevant section of a long-form video, and across the screen throughout a short-form video. Do not rely on a risk warning only in the post text or description. Design the on-screen layout so that the caption text and the risk warning do not overlap or compete.

Step 3: Make captions readable

  • Size and contrast: large type, strong contrast, a plain font.
  • Line length: short lines, a couple at a time.
  • Timing: on screen long enough to read and in sync with speech.
  • Speaker identification: WCAG says captions identify who is speaking when it is not clear.
  • Meaningful sounds: include them when they carry information.
  • Safe area: keep text clear of platform buttons and the edge of the frame.

Step 4: Be careful with numbers and claims in captions

Captions can make numbers prominent. If the video mentions returns, performance or tax treatment, the caption displays those words. The FCA expects promotions to be fair, clear and not misleading, so make sure that any figure in the caption has the context the script gives it: period, source, and a note that past performance is not a guide to the future where relevant. Check with compliance on what wording your firm uses.

Step 5: Keep retention in mind without compromising compliance

Captions can help retention because viewers can follow without sound. But do not trim caption text to make it punchier if that changes the meaning or drops a required qualification. Pacing edits, such as cutting silence, must be reviewed again for the effect on the message and warnings.

You can still use ordinary retention techniques: a clear opening question, signposting, visual variety and chapters on long videos. YouTube's retention report is a useful check; it shows dips as moments where "viewers are abandoning or skipping" (YouTube Help).

Step 6: Record the process

Keep a record of the approved script, the approved caption file, the sign-off and the published version. If a regulator or client asks how a video was approved, you can show it.

Example: a firm's two-minute market explainer

Example: an investment firm records a two-minute explainer on diversification. The editor generates captions, then proofreads them against the approved script and corrects a misheard fund name. The compliance team reviews the video and the caption file together. The on-screen layout has captions in the lower third and the required warning displayed in line with the firm's compliance guidance, with the post text also repeating it. The firm saves the approved files and sign-off. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result and not advice about what any firm must do.

Common mistakes

  • Unchecked auto-captions with wrong figures or names.
  • Risk warning only in the post caption.
  • Captions overlapping the warning.
  • Editing after sign-off without re-review.
  • Trimming qualifications to fit the line.
  • No record of the approved version.

FAQ

Do investment videos need captions?

There is no single answer for every video, but the FCA points firms to WCAG principles on accessibility, and captions help viewers without sound. Check with your compliance team.

Can the risk warning go in the post caption?

The FCA guidance describes a warning found only in the caption of a short-form video as non-compliant. Keep warnings on screen as the guidance describes.

Should compliance review the caption file?

It is sensible to review it with the video, because captions are part of what viewers read.

Can I use auto-generated captions?

Only after proofreading, especially for names, figures and dates.
