Key takeaways

  • The first 3 seconds are measured directly. Instagram, as reported by Social Media Today, defines skip rate as "the percentage of views from people who decided to skip your reel during those first 3 seconds."
  • The retention chart shows the rest. Instagram says "the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is."
  • Meta's Reels best practices favour Reels with "a strong hook or payoff, like a big reveal, punchline or plot twist."
  • Shoot vertical and post clean files: Meta says "vertical content (9:16) helps ensure your reels are immersive" and to "avoid 3rd-party fonts and watermarks".
  • A guide gives you the method; your own insights tell you which Reels need it.

How retention editing applies to Reels

  1. Open on the payoff or the problem. The first frame should be the most interesting thing you have: the finished result, the surprising line, the moment of tension.
  2. Say the promise in on-screen text in the first second or two, for people watching muted.
  3. Cut every pause. Reels viewers swipe the moment nothing is happening.
  4. Change something every few seconds: angle, framing, text, a cutaway.
  5. Use an open loop. "The third one surprised me" gives a reason to stay to the end.
  6. Pay it off. If the hook promised a reveal, deliver it. Bait-and-switch hooks raise skips next time.
  7. Make the loop seamless. A Reel that ends where it began can be rewatched without a jarring jump.

Free guides versus a paid playbook

Free guidance from Meta, Instagram and TikTok is accurate and worth reading first, but it is short: it tells you what to do (hook early, vertical, no watermarks) more than how to do it shot by shot. A paid guide earns its price only if it gives you a repeatable method, templates and a practice plan you will actually use. If you post a few Reels a month as a hobby, the free guidance is probably enough. If Reels drive your business or your clients' sales, a structured method can save trial and error. Neither guarantees views.

Common mistakes

  • Greeting the viewer before showing anything.
  • Reposting TikToks with the watermark, which Meta advises against.
  • Text that covers faces or sits under the Reels interface.
  • Hooks that promise something the Reel never delivers.
  • Judging a Reel by views alone instead of skip rate and the retention chart.

FAQ

Is there a downloadable guide on Reels retention editing?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook from Asendify is an instant download with templates, for £1,720 one-time. Meta's Reels tips are a free alternative.

What is skip rate on Instagram?

The share of views where people skipped your Reel in the first 3 seconds, according to Instagram.

How do I read the Reels retention chart?

Every chart slopes down; Instagram says a flatter line means a more engaged audience. Look for steep drops and check what happens on screen there.

Does the playbook only cover YouTube?

It was written for YouTube and Shorts. Hooks, open loops and pacing apply to Reels too, but Instagram-specific insights are not its focus.

Should Reels have captions?

Yes. Many people watch muted, so on-screen text or captions help them follow.
