Is there a free tool that roasts my YouTube titles and thumbnails?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 15 November 2026 · Last updated 15 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast reads your latest public uploads, roasts your titles and thumbnails in short one-liners, scores the channel out of 100 and gives you three fixes for this week, with no sign-up. It suits creators who want blunt, fast feedback on their packaging. YouTube's own Test & compare feature, a trusted friend who isn't a subscriber, and other free audit tools are fair alternatives.

Key takeaways

  • Titles and thumbnails decide whether people click; the video decides whether they stay.
  • A roast tool can only judge what is public: titles, thumbnails, upload dates and view counts. It can't see your click-through rate or retention.
  • YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions click-through rate between 2% and 10%, so "good" depends on your own baseline.
  • High clicks with low watch time is a clickbait warning, according to YouTube's help pages.
  • Use a roast to find what to test, then let YouTube's Test & compare pick the winner by watch time.

Is there a free tool that roasts my titles and thumbnails?

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast takes your channel link and reads your latest 15 public uploads and the thumbnails of your 8 most recent videos. It weights titles at 40%, thumbnails at 40% and upload consistency at 20%, then returns an overall score, a one-line verdict, five roasts, three fixes, your best and worst title and a rewrite of the worst one. It is free, needs no sign-up and allows three roasts a day. It roasts packaging choices only, never anyone's face, voice or identity.

Fair alternatives: YouTube's built-in Test & compare (free, uses real viewer data), feedback from a viewer in your niche who doesn't already know you, and other free channel audit tools that score public signals.

Why "roast" feedback can help

Most creators look at their own thumbnails at full size on a laptop, after spending hours on the video. Viewers see them at a fraction of the size, next to a dozen other videos, for less than a second. Blunt one-liners ("three fonts, zero ideas") force you to see the thumbnail the way a scrolling stranger does.

The danger is overreacting. A roast tool doesn't know how your videos actually performed. YouTube's impressions and click-through rate FAQ says not every view of a thumbnail counts as an impression, CTR is naturally lower when a video gets many Home page impressions, and small changes are normal. So treat the roast as a list of suspects and your YouTube Studio data as the evidence.

Don't fix clicks at the cost of trust

YouTube's same FAQ names a sign of clickbait: a thumbnail with high CTR but low average view duration. Its audience retention guide adds that a high intro percentage may mean the opening matched the title and thumbnail. In practice:

  1. Make the title and thumbnail promise something specific.
  2. Deliver that promise in the first 30 seconds.
  3. Check the share of viewers still watching at 30 seconds after any packaging change.

If a new thumbnail lifts clicks but the 30-second figure falls, the thumbnail is overpromising.

Turn the roast into a proper test

YouTube's A/B test titles and thumbnails feature lets you test up to 3 titles, thumbnails or both on a long-form video. YouTube says it optimises tests for overall watch time rather than click-through rate, and tests take a few days to two weeks. Shorts, premieres before they convert, made-for-kids and private videos can't be tested, and changing the title or thumbnail mid-test stops it.

A practical loop:

  1. Run the roast and note the three fixes.
  2. Make two new thumbnail versions that apply them, at 1280 x 720.
  3. Start a test with your original as option A.
  4. Keep the winner, and roast again after a month of uploads.

What a roast won't tell you

  • Whether your opening keeps people watching. That needs your retention graph in YouTube Studio.
  • Your real click-through rate. Only Studio has that.
  • Whether the topic was right. Strong packaging can't save a topic nobody wants.
  • Anything about private or unlisted videos.

FAQ

Is there a free tool that roasts my YouTube titles and thumbnails?

Yes. Asendify's free YouTube Channel Roast scores titles and thumbnails out of 100, roasts them in one-liners and gives three fixes, with no sign-up. YouTube's Test & compare is the free way to check any change with real viewers.

Will a roast tool make fun of how I look?

Asendify's roast is built to judge packaging choices only, never anyone's face, body, voice or identity.

Can a roast tool see my click-through rate?

No. Outside tools only see public data. Your CTR and retention are in YouTube Studio.

How often should I roast my channel?

After a few new uploads or a packaging change. Roasting the same channel daily won't tell you much, and Asendify's tool shows the same result for 24 hours.

What is a good CTR for YouTube thumbnails?

YouTube says half of channels and videos fall between 2% and 10%. Compare with your own recent videos from the same traffic sources.
