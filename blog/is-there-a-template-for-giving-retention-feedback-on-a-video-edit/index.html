Is there a template for giving retention feedback on a video edit?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. A good retention feedback template asks the reviewer to judge the edit at fixed points (hook, promise, middle, re-hooks, payoff and ending) and to give a timestamp, the problem from the viewer's side, and one suggested fix for each note. Key takeaways

  • Retention feedback is different from taste feedback. It asks "would a viewer leave here, and why?" rather than "do I like this?"
  • Every note needs a timestamp. Frame.io's help pages put it simply: "This timecode is exactly what the editor needs to be accurate".
  • After release, YouTube's key moments report shows dips, where viewers are "abandoning or skipping", so you can check whether your notes were right.
  • For short ads, TikTok's guidance to "prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds" is a useful benchmark for the hook check.
  • One clear fix per note beats a list of vague reactions.

Why vague feedback slows edits down

"It feels slow" or "make it pop" leaves the editor guessing. They change several things, the reviewer still isn't happy, and the next round repeats. Retention feedback fixes this by tying each comment to a moment and a viewer behaviour: the viewer is confused, bored, misled or satisfied. The editor can then make a precise change.

The template

Copy this into a sheet or your review tool. Fill one row per note.

SectionQuestion for the reviewerTimestampViewer problemSuggested fixPriority
Hook (first 3-10 s)Is there a clear reason to keep watching? Does it match the title or thumbnail?High
Promise (first 30-60 s)Does the viewer know what they'll get?High
Set-upIs any context unnecessary before the first payoff?Medium
MiddleWhere would you reach for your phone?High
Re-hooksIs a new question opened before each section ends?Medium
Visual varietyAny stretch where the picture doesn't change and nothing new is said?Medium
AudioAny point where speech is hard to hear or music masks it?High
PayoffIs the promised answer delivered clearly?High
EndingDoes it end soon after the payoff?Medium

Use three priority levels at most. If everything is high, nothing is.

How to write each note

  1. Watch once as a viewer. No pausing. Note the first moment your attention drifts.
  2. Watch again as a reviewer. Pause and fill the template.
  3. Describe the viewer's experience, not the edit. "I don't know why I'm watching this part" is more useful than "cut this".
  4. Give one fix. Suggest a specific change, such as "Move the result shot from 2:10 to the open".
  5. Use ranges for long problems. Frame.io and similar tools let you mark an in and out point for a section rather than one frame.
  6. Separate brand notes. Logo size, colour and legal lines go in a separate list so they don't drown out retention issues.
  7. Review against data later. When the video is live, compare your high-priority notes with the real dips.

Common mistakes

  • Notes without timestamps. The editor has to hunt, and may fix the wrong moment.
  • Mixing taste with retention. "I don't like this font" is fine, but it belongs in a separate list.
  • Rewriting the video in notes. Big structural changes need a conversation, not 40 comments.
  • Too many reviewers. Collect notes from several people into one list before sending.
  • Never checking the result. Without data, nobody learns whether the feedback helped.

FAQ

Is there a template for retention feedback on a video edit?

Yes. Use the free template above, or Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook, which includes templates and a tracker for £1,720 one-time.

How many notes should one review include?

As few as solve the main problems. Five to ten prioritised notes are easier to act on than forty.

Should feedback come before or after the rough cut?

Retention feedback is most useful on the rough cut, when structure can still change cheaply.

What tool should I use for timestamped feedback?

Any tool with timecoded comments works, such as Frame.io or a shared sheet with timestamps.

How do I know if my feedback was right?

Compare your high-priority notes with dips in the key moments report once the video is live.
