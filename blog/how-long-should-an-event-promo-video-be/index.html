How long should an event promo video be?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

An event promo video should usually run under a minute for social feeds and paid ads, and about one to three minutes for a recap or a promo on your event page. Length matters less than whether every second earns the next: open on your strongest moment, cut anything that explains rather than shows, and make a separate short cut for each channel instead of one long video for all of them.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia's 2025 length guide puts promos, teasers and brand clips in the under-one-minute group, and says videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate.
  • Vimeo's guide to recap videos says they usually run about one to three minutes.
  • Cvent recommends keeping LinkedIn event clips under a minute and email video to roughly 20-30 seconds of value.
  • On YouTube, skippable ads can be skipped after five seconds, so the first five seconds matter more than the total length.
  • Make one master edit, then cut channel versions from it. One length for every channel usually means the wrong length for most of them.

Why there is no single right length

People ask "how long should it be?" because they want a safe number. The honest answer is that length follows the job the video has to do and the place it will be watched.

A teaser in a social feed has to stop someone who never chose to watch it. A recap on your event page is watched by people who already clicked, often past attendees or prospects deciding whether to buy a ticket. Those two viewers have very different patience.

Wistia's guide to marketing video length (July 2025) groups videos by goal. It puts promos, product teasers and brand clips under one minute, and says longer pieces of five to 30 minutes, such as webinars and case studies, "shine on event pages". It also reports that videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate, and that most viewers drop off around the halfway point. In other words, even short videos lose people; a short video just loses fewer seconds.

These ranges come from the guides above plus the platform rules. Google's ad formats page says bumper ads last between five and six seconds and non-skippable in-stream ads are 60 seconds or shorter. Cvent's event video guide (2025) describes a highlight video of roughly 90 seconds and says LinkedIn clips should stay under one minute.

The first five seconds matter more than the total

On YouTube, viewers can skip a skippable in-stream ad after five seconds, according to Google. Cvent puts it more bluntly: "People decide in the first three seconds whether to keep watching."

That is why the Retention Editing Playbook starts every video with a cold open: the video begins on the most interesting thing in it. For an event promo that might be the loudest crowd reaction, the headline speaker mid-sentence, or the reveal on stage. Not the venue exterior. Not the logo animation. Not a slow pan across empty seats before doors open.

Google's ABCDs of effective video ads say the same thing in ad language: "Get to the heart of the story faster, and use engaging pacing and tight framing to draw viewers in."

How to decide the length for your event

Work through these questions in order:

  1. Who is watching, and did they choose to? Strangers in a feed get the shortest cut. Past attendees on your event page will watch longer.
  2. What one action do you want? Buy a ticket, register interest, book a sales call. One action per video.
  3. How much real footage do you have? If you have three strong moments, you have a 30-second video, not a two-minute one. Padding is the fastest way to lose viewers.
  4. What does the platform allow and reward? Check the specs every time. YouTube Shorts can now be up to three minutes, square or vertical, per YouTube Help, but a three-minute Short still has to hold attention for three minutes.
  5. Can you cut it shorter and lose nothing? If yes, do it.

Example: one event, four lengths

Example: a two-day marketing conference with 600 attendees films 14 hours of sessions, plus crowd shots and 20 short attendee interviews.

  • Teaser (25 seconds, vertical): opens on the closing keynote's biggest laugh, three fast cuts of the packed hall, one attendee line ("I came for one session and stayed for all of them"), then the date and ticket link as on-screen text.
  • YouTube ad (20 seconds plus a 6-second bumper): first 5 seconds show the headline speaker's strongest claim; the bumper repeats the date and link for people who skipped.
  • Highlight film (75 seconds): music-led, no voiceover, built around five moments of energy.
  • Recap (2 minutes 30 seconds, landscape): three key takeaways, each introduced by a speaker clip, for the event page and sales follow-up.

Each cut comes from the same footage. Each has a different job, so each has a different length.

How to keep viewers to the end, whatever the length

  • Open a loop early. Tease something you will show later ("and the moment that got a standing ovation"). An open question gives people a reason to stay.
  • Change something every few seconds. Shot size, angle, speaker, on-screen text. Monotony is what makes a 60-second video feel long.
  • Carry the story with text. Many people watch feeds with sound off, so the key message should work as on-screen text.
  • End on the action, not a montage of logos. Sponsor thanks can go in the description or the full recap.
  • Check the data. On YouTube, the key moments for audience retention report shows the percentage still watching after the first 30 seconds, plus spikes and dips. Cut the dips out of the next version.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches cold opens and the 30-second scorecard for video?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers cold opens, open loops, pacing and a 30-second scorecard. It is written for YouTube, so event editors adapt it; Google's ABCDs are a free alternative.

Is 2 minutes too long for an event promo?

For a social ad, usually yes. For a recap on your event page, two minutes is normal if every section shows something new. Vimeo says recaps typically run one to three minutes.

How long should an event teaser be?

Short. Wistia groups teasers and promos under one minute. Most teasers work at 15 to 45 seconds with one strong moment and a clear date.

Should I make one video or several?

Several. Make one master edit, then cut a vertical teaser, a short ad and a longer recap from it, each sized for its channel.

How long should a sizzle reel be?

Usually one to two minutes. It has to show range and energy, so cut it as tight as your best footage allows.

Does a longer video rank better on YouTube?

Not by itself. YouTube's retention report compares your video with others of similar length, so a shorter video that holds viewers can beat a longer one that sags.
