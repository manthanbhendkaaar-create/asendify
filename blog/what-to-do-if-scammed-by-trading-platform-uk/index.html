What should I do if I have been scammed by a trading platform in the UK?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Stop sending money straight away, even if the platform says you must pay a fee or tax to withdraw. Call your bank using the number on the back of your card and tell them you have been scammed. Report it to Report Fraud (0300 123 2040) and to the FCA (0800 111 6768). Keep every message, receipt and screenshot. If you paid by bank transfer in the UK on or after 7 October 2024, you may be entitled to reimbursement of up to £85,000 under the Payment Systems Regulator's rules, and you should report within 13 months.

This page is general information, not legal or financial advice. If you are in immediate danger or being threatened, call 999.

Key takeaways

  • Act fast: banks can sometimes stop or recover payments if told quickly.
  • Fake "withdrawal fees" and "taxes" are a classic sign the platform is a scam.
  • The FCA says to contact Report Fraud on 0300 123 2040 if you have lost money to a scam.
  • Under Payment Systems Regulator rules, most UK bank transfer (APP) fraud since 7 October 2024 is covered up to £85,000.
  • Beware "recovery" firms that contact you offering to get your money back for a fee.

What to do, in order

StepWhoWhy
1Stop all payments and contact with the scammerScammers often ask for more money to "release" funds
2Call your bank or card providerThey can try to stop or recall payments and start a reimbursement claim
3Report to Report Fraud (0300 123 2040)The UK's national fraud reporting service; gives you a crime reference
4Report to the FCA (0800 111 6768)Helps warn others and may add the firm to the Warning List
5Change passwords and secure devicesEspecially if you installed remote-access software
6Keep evidenceScreenshots, chat logs, emails, transaction details
7If your bank refuses, complain, then go to the Financial Ombudsman ServiceIndependent review

The FCA's protect yourself from scams page gives these contact routes: call the FCA on 0800 111 6768 to report a scam, and for anything the FCA does not regulate, or if you have lost money, contact Report Fraud on 0300 123 2040. It also warns that people who have been scammed are often targeted again, including by "recovery room" scams offering to get money back for a fee.

Reimbursement for bank transfers

The Payment Systems Regulator's page on APP fraud reimbursement protections explains the rules for authorised push payment (APP) fraud, where you are tricked into sending a payment yourself:

RuleDetail
Start datePayments made on or after 7 October 2024
Who is coveredIndividuals, microenterprises and charities
Maximum claim£85,000; firms may choose to pay more
ExcessFirms may apply an excess of up to £100, but not for vulnerable consumers
Time limitReport as soon as possible, within 13 months of the payment
If more than £85,000 is lostYou can raise the case with the Financial Ombudsman Service, which has a £430,000 limit

Card payments and crypto purchases follow different rules. Card payments may be disputed through your card provider (chargeback or, for credit cards, Section 75 in some cases). Money sent as crypto to a scammer's wallet is much harder to recover.

Worked example: a "withdrawal fee" scam

Example (illustrative): Mark found a trading app through an Instagram advert. Over six weeks he sent £6,000 by bank transfer. His dashboard showed £19,000. When he asked to withdraw, the platform asked for a £2,500 "tax clearance fee".

What happenedWhat it means
Fast "profits" on the dashboardFake numbers to encourage bigger deposits
Fee demanded before withdrawalClassic scam sign; real firms deduct fees, they do not demand new deposits
Payments sent by bank transfer after October 2024Likely within the PSR reimbursement rules

Mark does not pay the fee. He calls his bank, reports to Report Fraud and the FCA, and sends his bank screenshots and transaction details. Because he reported quickly and paid by UK bank transfer, he has a strong basis for a reimbursement claim, subject to the bank's assessment. Names and amounts are invented for the example.

Avoiding a second scam

  • Ignore anyone who contacts you offering recovery for a fee, including people claiming to be from the FCA, police or a law firm.
  • The FCA will never ask you to transfer money to it or ask for PINs and passwords.
  • Do not install apps or software a caller asks you to.
  • Check any firm on the FCA Firm Checker; see how to check a broker is FCA authorised.

Common mistakes

  • Paying the "last fee" in the hope of getting the rest back.
  • Waiting because of embarrassment. Speed matters, and scams target clever people too.
  • Deleting the chats. They are your evidence.
  • Trusting a recovery company that cold-calls you.

FAQ

Is there a trading journal that keeps your own record of every deposit, trade and withdrawal?

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS logs trades with P&L. It does not detect scams or recover money.

Can I get my money back from a trading scam?

Possibly. UK bank transfers since 7 October 2024 may be reimbursed up to £85,000 under PSR rules. Contact your bank immediately.

Who do I report a trading scam to?

Your bank, Report Fraud on 0300 123 2040, and the FCA on 0800 111 6768.

Should I pay a fee to withdraw my profits?

No. Demands for new payments before withdrawal are a strong sign of a scam.

What is a recovery room scam?

A follow-up scam where someone offers to recover your lost money for an upfront fee.

How long do I have to report APP fraud to my bank?

As soon as possible and within 13 months of the payment, according to the PSR.
