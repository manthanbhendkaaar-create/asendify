Should you use lower thirds in short-form video?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use lower thirds in short-form video only when a name, role or credential adds trust, and do not put them in the traditional bottom-third position. On Reels, TikTok and Shorts, the bottom of the frame is covered by the caption, account name and buttons, so a classic lower third is often hidden. Place a short, simple name tag higher in the frame for two or three seconds instead.

Key takeaways

  • TikTok's ad specifications say to keep text and logos out of areas "where buttons, usernames, and captions may appear", and that "the longer the caption, the smaller the safe zone will be".
  • Meta's Reels ads guide, as summarised by Website Builder Expert, warns that captions and calls to action can be cut off once a Reel goes live, and recommends checking the safe zone before publishing.
  • Mayer's signaling principle found cues that highlight organisation helped in 26 of 28 tests, so a label that tells viewers who is speaking or what section they are in can help, if it is visible.
  • No study measures short-form retention against lower thirds. The practical risk is that a hidden or cluttered lower third does nothing, or covers the hook.
  • Check your effect with the platform's own metric, such as YouTube's "Stayed to watch" for Shorts in YouTube Help.

How to design a short-form name tag

  1. Write the fewest words possible. "Dr A. Patel, GP" beats a full job title and organisation.
  2. Place it above the bottom interface, ideally upper-middle, away from the right-hand buttons.
  3. Keep it on screen briefly, long enough to read once.
  4. Do not cover the face or the hook text. Name tags come second to the opening line.
  5. Use a clean, high-contrast style that matches your captions.
  6. Check with each app's overlay or preview, because interfaces differ.
  7. Show it once. Repeating the tag every few seconds adds clutter.

Worked example

Example: a podcast production company cuts 45-second vertical clips from a guest interview. Its template puts a coloured lower-third bar with the guest's name, job title and company at the bottom of the frame for the first eight seconds, under the hook caption.

Name tag over the hook textShow it after the hook
Repeating the tag on every cutShow it once
Busy animated barsSimple fade or pop

Say it instead of showing it

In many short-form videos, the simplest name tag is spoken. A guest who opens with "As a GP, the question I get most is..." gives the credential and the hook in one line, with nothing on screen to hide. If the credential matters to trust, say it early; if it does not, leave it out. A written tag then becomes optional support for viewers watching with the sound off.

Long-form vs short-form

On landscape YouTube videos, a traditional lower third works fine because the player interface is mostly hidden while the video plays. When you cut that footage into Shorts or Reels, rebuild the graphics for vertical rather than cropping the landscape version, which can cut the lower third in half or push it into the interface.

FAQ

Is there a guide on graphics and on-screen text for short-form retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers on-screen text, hooks and pacing with templates and a 30-day plan. Meta's and TikTok's safe-zone tools are free for checking placement.

Where should a lower third go in a vertical video?

Above the bottom interface, usually upper-middle or just above centre, and away from the right-hand buttons.

How long should a name tag stay on screen?

Long enough to read once, often two to three seconds in short-form.

Do podcast clips need lower thirds?

Usually yes for guests, so viewers know who is speaking. Keep it short and show it once.

Should I reuse my YouTube lower thirds in Shorts?

Rebuild them for vertical. Cropped landscape graphics often end up cut off or hidden by the interface.
