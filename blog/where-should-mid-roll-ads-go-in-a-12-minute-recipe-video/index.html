Where should mid-roll ads go in a 12-minute recipe video?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 3 min read · Published 26 November 2026 · Last updated 26 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Put them at natural breaks between recipe stages, such as after the prep and before the cooking, and not in the middle of a step or while someone is speaking. YouTube says slots at natural breakpoints, like a pause in audio or a visual transition, are more likely to serve ads. A 12-minute video qualifies, since mid-rolls need 8 minutes or more.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help says mid-rolls can be turned on for monetised videos 8 minutes or longer, placed manually, automatically or both.
  • It says slots that interrupt content (mid-sentence or mid-action) are less likely to serve ads, and a slot never guarantees an ad.
  • Use the retention graph to find where viewers already leave and avoid adding a break right at a dip.
  • Fewer, well-placed breaks protect viewers; there is no single correct number.

Natural breaks in a recipe

A recipe has a clear stage structure, which makes it easier than most formats. YouTube's guidance suggests choosing a pause in audio or a visual change. In cooking, that is when a stage finishes: ingredients chopped, sauce simmering, dish in the oven.

Placement options for a 12-minute video

TimestampMomentVerdict
About 0:45After the result preview and introEarly; use only if you must
About 4:00Prep finished, before heat goes onGood natural break
About 8:00Dish in oven or simmering, before the finishing stepsGood natural break
Mid-chop or mid-sentenceAction in progressAvoid
Final 30 secondsBefore the revealAvoid; viewers want the result

Steps

  1. Mark the stage ends in your timeline.
  2. Check the length: over 8 minutes qualifies.
  3. Choose one or two breaks at stage ends.
  4. Look at the red or green feedback in YouTube Studio and move slots that look unlikely to serve.
  5. Avoid a break right before the reveal.
  6. Publish, then read the graph. If a big drop follows a slot, move it next time.
  7. Use an open loop before the break ("after this, the one step that stops the sauce splitting").

What to watch

Revenue and viewer experience trade off. More breaks can lower retention; check the data rather than copying a rule. YouTube also says creators can turn mid-rolls off for videos where they do not suit.
