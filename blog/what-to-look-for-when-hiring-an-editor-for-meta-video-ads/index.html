What should I look for when hiring an editor for Meta video ads?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Look for proof that they edit for attention, not just looks: ads where the first second is the strongest, captions that work with the sound off, several hooks and cut-downs from one shoot, and a habit of revising based on the retention graph. Run a small paid test edit with your own footage, ask how they'd improve an ad that loses viewers early, and agree in writing who owns the project files and footage.

Key takeaways

  • Facebook's own advice, as reported by Search Engine Journal (2021), included "Video ads should be able to be viewed, understood, and enjoyed without sound" and "movement or fast edits at the start of an ad can capture consumers' attention". A good Meta editor works this way by default.
  • Meta's video insights reference describes a retention graph in "40 equal intervals". Ask candidates how they'd use it to revise an edit.
  • UK government guidance on ownership of copyright works says that for commissioned work "the first legal owner of copyright is the person or organisation that created the work" unless agreed otherwise in writing. Sort out ownership before work starts.
  • A paid test edit with your footage tells you more than any showreel.
  • Ask for metrics with context (what changed, what happened next), and treat unverifiable claims with care.

Questions to ask in the interview

  1. "Show me an ad you re-cut after seeing the results. What did you change, and why?"
  2. "How would you open this ad if the first two seconds had to work on mute?"
  3. "How many hook variations would you make from this footage?"
  4. "What do you need from me in a brief?"
  5. "What do you hand over at the end: project files, exports, music licences?"
  6. "How do you handle a client asking for the logo up front?"

FAQ

Is there a playbook that helps me judge and brief video ad editors on retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops, pacing and pattern interrupts, with templates and a 30-day plan you can share with an editor. A paid test edit is the free-to-design way to compare candidates.

Should I pay for a test edit?

Yes. Paying is fair and gets a more serious effort. Keep it small: one short ad and two alternative hooks.

Who owns the edit and project files?

In the UK, the creator of a commissioned work is usually the first owner of copyright unless you agree otherwise in writing. Put ownership and handover in the contract.

What should a Meta ad editor deliver?

Usually the main ad, alternative hooks, versions for each placement ratio, captions, and the files you agreed on.

How do I judge an editor's portfolio?

Watch each ad on a phone, on mute, and stop after one second. If you'd keep watching and understand the point, that's a good sign.
