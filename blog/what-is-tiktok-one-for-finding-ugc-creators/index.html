What is TikTok One, and how do brands find UGC creators for video ads through it?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 7 min read · Published 24 November 2026 · Last updated 24 November 2026

TikTok One is TikTok's own platform for brand and creator collaboration. According to TikTok, it "brings creator discovery, trusted partner access, and content tools into one platform." Brands find creators with filters, curated highlights or open casting calls, or by pasting a brief into Creator AI Search for a shortlist. Finding a creator is only the first step: the edit and the first seconds of the video decide whether people watch. Features and names change, so confirm the current options in TikTok's own help pages before you plan a campaign.

Key takeaways

  • TikTok's blog calls TikTok One "the official platform for brand-creator collaboration" and says you can "use advanced filters, curated highlights, or open casting calls to find creators who align with your goals" (TikTok for Business).
  • The same post says Creator AI Search lets you "paste in your campaign brief and get a curated shortlist," and lets you "start with natural language" rather than keywords alone. Partner Exchange "connects you with TikTok-approved creative partners."
  • TikTok's creative guidance for performance ads says "introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds," "prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds," and recommends "featuring people such as creators, employees, or customers" (TikTok Ads Help).
  • Neither source says that any particular creator will produce a result. Treat creator choice as a test.

What brands use TikTok One for

  1. Discovery. Searching for creators by audience, content style and other filters.
  2. Briefing. Setting out what the campaign is and what you need.
  3. Working with partners. Using approved creative partners for production.
  4. Content tools. Planning and producing creator content.

For video ads, the interest is usually in creator-style content: a person talking to the camera, demonstrating the product or telling a story in a native TikTok style.

How to find creators step by step

  1. Write the brief first. One audience, one problem, one message and one call to action. A vague brief gets a vague shortlist.
  2. Search with filters. Narrow by audience, topic and the other filters the platform offers.
  3. Try plain language. If Creator AI Search is available to you, describe the creator you want in a sentence and compare the shortlist with your brief.
  4. Consider an open casting call. Describe the project and let interested creators respond.
  5. Review each creator's content. Watch several videos, not just the best one. Check whether the voice, look and pace fit your product.
  6. Check the audience. Look at who watches them, not just how many.
  7. Agree the deliverables. Number of videos, length, hook options, usage rights and revision rounds.
  8. Brief the hook. Ask for two or three different opening lines recorded in the same take.
  9. Plan the edit. Decide who edits, what you will cut and how you will add captions.
  10. Test and learn. Run variants and keep a log.

Questions to ask before hiring a creator

  • Do they have experience with ads, not just organic posts?
  • Can they give alternative hooks?
  • Will you get raw footage so your editor can recut?
  • Who owns the content and for how long can you run it?
  • How do they handle claims, disclosure and product safety?

Check platform and advertising rules on paid partnerships and disclosure. This page is not legal advice.

Measuring the result

  1. Define the metric before launch, such as video views or conversions in your ad account.
  2. Compare creators and hooks one variable at a time.
  3. Review weekly and keep what works.
  4. Ask the creator for feedback on what viewers said.

Example: a skincare brand briefing three creators

Example: a brand writes a one-page brief and searches for creators who talk about skincare routines. It shortlists five, watches ten videos from each, picks three and asks each for a 20-second video with two alternative openings. The editor adds captions, trims dead air and builds six ads from three videos with different hooks. This is an illustrative scenario, not a measured result.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing on follower count alone.
  • Not asking for alternative hooks.
  • No usage rights agreed.
  • Over-polishing the edit so it no longer looks native.

What to put in a creator brief

A brief that gets good UGC-style videos is short and specific. Include the audience and the single problem the product solves, the one message you want remembered, the call to action, the length range and the format, such as vertical with a creator speaking to camera. Add a short list of things to avoid, such as claims you cannot support or competitor names. Give two or three examples of videos you like, and explain what you like about them.

Ask for alternative hooks. A creator can record three different opening lines in the same session at almost no extra cost, and the edit can then test them. Ask for raw clips and any B-roll so your own editor can recut for different placements. Agree usage rights, the period you can run the content as an ad, and whether the creator may post it too.

Checking a creator before you commit

Watch at least ten of their videos. Look at how they open a video, how quickly they get to the point, how they talk to the camera and how their audience responds in the comments. Check that their pace suits your product: a calm explainer voice may not suit a fast-moving gadget demo, and the reverse. Look for past sponsored content and see how natural it feels. A creator who sounds like a script reader will probably produce an ad that feels the same.

Finally, plan for failure. Some creators will not fit, and some videos will not perform. Keep a small test budget and a log so you learn from each round rather than betting on one creator.
