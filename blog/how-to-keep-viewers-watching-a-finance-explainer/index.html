How do you keep viewers watching a finance explainer video without hype or clickbait?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open with the viewer's own money question, show one worked number within the first minute, and organise the video so viewers always know where they are. Use simple on-screen numbers and charts rather than walls of text, and keep the risks in the same tone as the benefits. Hype and "get rich" hooks can lift clicks, but they invite drop-off when the video doesn't deliver, and in the UK they can raise financial promotion problems.

Key takeaways

  • The UK Financial Conduct Authority's social media guidance (FG24/1, March 2024) says "Financial promotions on all advertising channels should be fair, clear and not misleading" and should "provide a balanced view of the benefits and risks". It covers influencers and affiliate marketers.
  • Richard Mayer's multimedia principles (University of New Hampshire summary) say people learn more when "extraneous material is excluded" and when "cues are added that highlight the organization of the essential material".
  • The same summary says people "do not learn more deeply when on-screen text is added to narrated graphics". Don't paste your script on screen.
  • YouTube's key moments report shows what share of viewers "still watched your video after the first 30 seconds" and where they dipped.
  • In finance, trust is retention. A viewer who feels misled leaves and often doesn't come back.

Editing techniques that work

  1. Signal the structure. On-screen section labels ("Factor 2 of 4") are the kind of cue Mayer's signalling principle describes.
  2. Animate numbers, don't list them. Build a figure step by step as you explain it.
  3. Cut extraneous material. Stock footage of money falling and stock-ticker B-roll add noise, not meaning.
  4. Keep risk in the main voice. A rushed disclaimer in a different tone feels like small print and breaks trust.
  5. Use pattern interrupts with purpose. A cut to a whiteboard, a quick question to camera or a change of shot every so often keeps attention without gimmicks.
  6. Show your workings. A spreadsheet on screen builds more trust than a claim.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on hooks and structure for finance explainer videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, open loops and pacing, with templates and a 30-day plan; it is about editing, not financial advice. The FCA's FG24/1 guidance is the free reference for UK promotion rules.

Do finance videos need a disclaimer?

It depends on what you say and where you are. In the UK, promotions must be fair, clear and not misleading; if you promote financial products, read the FCA guidance or take advice.

How long should a finance explainer be?

Long enough to answer the question properly with one worked example. Cut definitions and background that don't change the answer.

Should I put my script on screen as text?

No. Mayer's research summary says adding on-screen text to narrated graphics doesn't improve learning. Show key numbers and labels only.

Do charts help or hurt retention?

Simple charts that build step by step help. Dense charts shown all at once make viewers pause or leave.
