Key takeaways

  • Wistia's data shows engagement "naturally declines after the five-minute mark", yet people who stay through long videos are often more invested.
  • YouTube lets you compare long videos fairly: "use typical retention to compare your 10 latest videos of similar length".
  • Chapters help viewers navigate. Some will skip ahead; that's better than leaving.
  • In long videos, the shape of the curve matters more than the final percentage. Look for steep drops, not the gentle slope.

Why long videos are different

A 20-minute video can't hold everyone. Some viewers came for one part. Others will leave at a natural break. The goal is to keep the viewers who want the full video and avoid losing them to slow sections. That means more structure, not just more cuts.

How to find your long-video dips

  1. Open typical retention for your last 10 videos of similar length.
  2. Note every steep drop and its timestamp.
  3. Check what's on screen 10-15 seconds before each drop.
  4. Look for patterns. Sponsor reads, recaps, long intros and section changes are common causes.
  5. Fix one cause per video so you can tell what worked.

YouTube also notes that spikes can mean viewers "had to rewatch a section" because it wasn't clear. In long, dense videos, check spikes too.

Worked example

Example: a history channel posts a 32-minute video on the fall of an empire. Past videos of similar length show steep drops at 0:45, around the sponsor read at 3:00, and at 18:00 where a long background section usually sits.

ProblemChange
Drop at 0:45 after a slow introOpen on the final battle; promise the real reason it fell
Sponsor read at 3:00Move to 9:00, after the first big reveal, with a clear return line
Background section at 18:00Cut by a third; split with a map animation and a re-hook ("The decision that doomed them happens next")

The creator compares the new video's curve with typical retention, focusing on the three old drop points. Results are specific to the channel; it's a test across several videos, not a promised percentage.

Common long-video mistakes

  • Long recaps of what was already said.
  • Sponsor read in the first two minutes, before viewers have a reason to stay.
  • No chapters. Viewers who want one section may leave instead of skipping.
  • The same visual for minutes. Long talking-head stretches need variety.
  • Hiding the payoff after the outro starts.

Sponsor reads and mid-rolls in long videos

Long videos often carry sponsor segments and mid-roll ads, and both affect retention. The placement matters more than the existence of the break. Spotify's advice to podcasters is useful here: dips around ads often recover because many people skip ahead rather than leave. Viewers behave similarly on YouTube when they can see the main content continues.

PlacementRiskBetter approach
Sponsor in first 2 minutesViewers leave before getting valueAfter the first payoff or reveal
Mid-roll in the middle of a story beatBreaks immersionAt a natural section change
Several ads close togetherFeels like an ad blockSpace them across sections
No return line after a sponsorViewers forget the threadA one-line re-hook back into the story

A simple return line, such as "Now, back to the moment the plan fell apart", gives viewers a reason to stay through the break and reminds them of the open question.

Also consider how chapters interact with sponsors. Labelling a sponsor as its own chapter is honest and lets viewers skip it; most will skip to the next chapter rather than close the video. Hiding a sponsor inside a content chapter can feel like a trick and may cost trust over time. For long videos, steady trust tends to matter more than squeezing a few extra seconds from one upload.

FAQ

Is there a guide on retention for long YouTube videos?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers long-form retention with templates and a tracker for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's retention help is a free starting point.

What is good retention for a 20-minute video?

There's no single number. Compare with your own typical retention for videos of similar length.

Do chapters hurt retention?

They can let people skip, but skipping is better than leaving. They also help viewers find value.

How many re-hooks does a long video need?

Enough to cover each known drop point and section change; many long videos use several.

Should long videos be cut shorter?

Only if the content doesn't justify the length. Cut repetition first, not substance.

Should I split one long video into a series?

If the topic has natural breaks and viewers tend to leave at the same point, a two-part series can work. Link the parts with end screens and playlists.
