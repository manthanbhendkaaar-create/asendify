Should podcast clips on YouTube be Shorts or 8 to 15 minute highlights?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Most podcasts on YouTube do best with both: Shorts to reach people who have never heard of the show, and horizontal highlights of roughly 8 to 15 minutes to give viewers a complete story or argument. Choose the format for each moment. A single sharp line or reaction belongs in a Short; a story with a set-up, a turn and a pay-off needs a highlight.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube treats square or vertical uploads up to three minutes as Shorts, and says to "use a wider aspect ratio such as 16:9 for long-form videos". The aspect ratio decides which shelf your clip lands on.
  • In the Shorts feed, "people are swiping through content not knowing what comes next", YouTube's Todd Sherman told TechCrunch. Shorts are built for discovery by strangers.
  • YouTube's podcasting best practices guide advises clip titles that "focus on the specific topics or personalities" in the clip, and keeping clips out of the full-episode playlist.
  • Long highlights are judged like any long video: YouTube's retention report shows what share of viewers are still watching after the first 30 seconds and where they drop away.
  • Neither format is "better". They do different jobs, so most shows need a mix.

When a moment should be a Short

Make a Short when the whole point fits in one breath. Good examples are a strong opinion ("I'd never take venture money again"), a surprising number the guest can back up, a funny exchange or a one-step tip. The Short needs no history. The first line carries it.

Shorts also suit moments that are visual: a big laugh, a guest's reaction, someone holding up the product they're talking about. These work with captions and no sound.

When a moment should be a long highlight

Make a highlight when the value is in the journey. A founder telling how a company nearly collapsed, a doctor explaining a myth step by step, or a heated but useful disagreement all need room. Cut to three minutes, they lose the turn that made them interesting.

Long highlights also suit search. People type questions like "how did X start their company" into YouTube, and a 10-minute highlight titled around that question can keep being found for months. The full episode is often too long and too broad to rank for the specific question.

Example: one guest, two formats

Example: a health podcast records an 80-minute episode with a sleep researcher. The team plans its clips like this.

Quick-fire listener questions6 minutesSplit into 2 ShortsEach answer stands alone

Each Short gets a related-video link to the full episode. Each highlight gets an end screen pointing to the full episode and a title built on the viewer's question. After a month the team compares Shorts' "stayed to watch" with the highlights' intro figure and average view duration, and moves effort toward whichever brings more people to full episodes. This is an illustrative plan, not a reported result. Keep any medical claims in clips accurate and in context; a clip is general information, not medical advice.

How to edit each format

Shorts

  1. Start on the line, not the build-up.
  2. Reframe to 9:16 so the speaker's face sits in the upper-middle of the frame.
  3. Add accurate captions; many people scroll muted.
  4. End on the pay-off. Don't add a long outro.

Long highlights

  1. Open with a 10-20 second taste of the best moment, then go back to the start of the story.
  2. Cut repeated points and long pauses, but keep natural back-and-forth.
  3. Add a simple title card or lower third so new viewers know who is speaking.
  4. Finish with a clear line, then point to the full episode.

FAQ

Is there a playbook on editing podcast Shorts and highlights for retention?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks, pacing and open loops with templates and a 30-day plan, for £1,720 one-time. YouTube's free podcasting guide is a useful companion.

Can a 3-minute vertical clip replace a long highlight?

Sometimes, for a tight story. But many stories need more room, and vertical videos are shown in the Shorts feed, not with long-form videos.

Should highlights be vertical too?

No. Keep long highlights at 16:9 so YouTube treats them as long-form and they appear alongside other long videos.

Do Shorts bring viewers to full episodes?

Some do, especially with a related video link and a moment that opens a question. Check clicks in your analytics rather than assuming.

If I can only do one, which should it be?

If the show is new and needs reach, start with Shorts. If you already have an audience that searches for your topics, highlights may pay off more.
