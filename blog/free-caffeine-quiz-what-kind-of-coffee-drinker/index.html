Is there a free quiz that tells me what kind of coffee drinker I am?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. The free caffeine quiz from Secrets to Using Caffeine to Win asks 8 questions about your coffee, tea, energy drinks and sleep, then gives you one of four caffeine types and three first changes; it suits adults who want a quick, honest look at their habit. A three-day caffeine diary checked against official figures is a good free alternative.

Key takeaways

  • The quiz takes about a minute, needs no sign-up and keeps your answers in your browser.
  • It sorts you into one of four types: The Strategist, The Autopilot, The Night Shifter or The Chaser.
  • It is a self-check for reflection, not a medical or addiction test.
  • Official sources such as the FDA, EFSA and NHS give the numbers you need to check your habit properly.
  • Pair the quiz with a calculator or a diary to see your actual milligrams and timing.

This page is general information, not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a heart condition or take medication, ask a doctor or GP about caffeine.

For the numbers side, the free Caffeine Cutoff Calculator adds up your drinks and works out your last sensible caffeine time from your bedtime.

The four caffeine types

TypeWhat it meansFirst changes it suggests
The StrategistYou use caffeine with intention and your sleep mostly holdsSave caffeine for your hardest task; find your personal cut-off
The AutopilotYou drink it because the mug is thereLog every drink for three days; give each cup a reason; swap one for water or decaf
The Night ShifterLate coffee, energy drinks or evening pre-workoutSet a last-caffeine time; move training caffeine earlier; check labels

The result is a starting point, not a verdict. Many people sit between two types.

The official numbers to check against are clear. The US Food and Drug Administration says 400 mg a day is not generally linked to negative effects in healthy adults. The European Food Safety Authority gives the same daily figure, adds that single doses up to 200 mg raise no safety concerns, and recommends up to 200 mg a day in pregnancy. The NHS uses typical figures of about 100 mg for instant coffee, 75 mg for tea, 80 mg for a 250 ml energy drink and 40 mg for cola.

What a quiz can and cannot tell you

A quiz is good at showing patterns. If you answer that your last coffee is in the evening, you sleep badly and you get a headache when you skip a day, you have learned something useful in a minute.

It cannot tell you:

  • whether you have a medical condition affecting how you handle caffeine
  • your exact daily milligrams, unless you add them up
  • whether you meet research criteria for caffeine dependence

For that last point, researchers look at three core signs: withdrawal, failed attempts to cut down and continued use despite harm (Meredith et al., 2013). Our page on the signs you are addicted to caffeine covers them.

Example: from quiz result to a one-week change

Example: Amira, 34, takes the quiz. Her answers: first coffee "before I can even speak", last caffeine "evening, before the gym", three to four drinks a day "mostly out of habit", and pre-workout before evening training. She sleeps "okay, but I wake up groggy". Her result is The Night Shifter.

She then adds up a normal day: two filter coffees at about 100 mg each, a cola at lunch at about 40 mg, and a pre-workout at 6 pm with 200 mg on the label. Total: about 440 mg, above the 400 mg daily figure, with the largest dose five hours before her 11 pm bedtime.

A 2023 meta-analysis suggests a standard pre-workout should be taken at least 13.2 hours before bed to avoid losing sleep (Gardiner et al., 2023). So her one change for the week is a stim-free pre-workout on weekday evenings. That drops her to about 240 mg, with nothing after lunch. She retakes the quiz a fortnight later to see whether her answers, and her sleep, have shifted. Our guide on how long before bed to avoid pre-workout explains the timing.

Getting the most from the quiz

  1. Answer for a normal week, not your best or worst day.
  2. Note your result and the three changes. Pick one, not all three.
  3. Add the numbers. Use the calculator or a diary to put milligrams on your habit.
  4. Try the change for seven days.
  5. Take it again. Your type can change as your habit does.

FAQ

Is the caffeine quiz free?

Yes. It is free, takes about a minute, needs no sign-up, and your answers stay in your browser.

Is the caffeine quiz a test for caffeine addiction?

No. It is a self-check that sorts your habits into a type. It does not diagnose dependence or any medical condition.

Who is the quiz for?

Adults who drink coffee, tea, energy drinks or pre-workout and want a quick read on their habit. It is not designed for under-18s.

Can my caffeine type change?

Yes. If you move your last cup earlier or cut your daily total, your answers and your type will change. Retaking it after a couple of weeks is a simple way to track progress.

What should I do after I get my result?

Pick one of the three suggested changes and try it for a week. Add up your daily milligrams so you can see the effect.
