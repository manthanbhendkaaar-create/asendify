Where can I learn how to use open loops in client video ads?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

You can learn open loops for client video ads from three places: free platform guidance (Google's ABCDs for YouTube ads and TikTok's creative best practices), a structured paid resource such as Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook, and your own retention data from live ads. The skill itself is simple to describe: raise one clear question early, keep it open while the ad does its work, and answer it right before the call to action.

Key takeaways

  • An open loop is a question the viewer wants answered. Psychologist George Loewenstein described curiosity as a feeling that arises "when attention becomes focused on a gap in one's knowledge", as summarised by PBS NOVA.
  • The old claim that people "remember unfinished things better" is shaky. A 2025 meta-analysis by Ghibellini and Meier found no memory advantage for unfinished tasks, but did find a general urge to resume them. For ads, the useful part is that urge to finish.
  • Platforms reward early attention. Google's ABCD guidance tells advertisers to "hook and sustain attention with an immersive story", and TikTok says to put the hook "in the first 6 seconds".
  • For client work, you learn fastest by pairing a written method with each client's retention curve.

What an open loop is in a client ad

In a client ad, an open loop is a promise the viewer can only collect by watching on. "Here's why this kitchen fitter's quotes are £2,000 lower than everyone else's" opens a loop. The ad then shows the van, the team, the stock room, and pays off the reason at second 25, just before "Book a free survey".

The loop has three parts:

  1. The open. A specific, believable question in the first few seconds.
  2. The hold. Proof, product shots or a short story that move towards the answer without giving it away.
  3. The close. A clear answer that leads straight into the offer.

If the close never comes, the viewer feels tricked. That is clickbait, and it damages the client's brand and, often, the ad account's quality signals.

How to practise open loops on client ads

  1. Write the loop before you cut. One sentence: "The viewer wants to know ___; we answer at ___ seconds."
  2. Open it within three seconds. On-screen text plus voice works on muted feeds. TikTok recommends text at "5-10 words per second" at most.
  3. Keep the loop specific. "The one thing we never do on a roof" beats "You won't believe this".
  4. Show progress. Every few seconds, add a clue that points towards the answer.
  5. Close before the call to action. The payoff should make the offer feel like the obvious next step.
  6. Check the retention data. On YouTube, the audience retention report shows dips where "viewers are abandoning or skipping". A dip right after the open usually means the question wasn't clear or didn't matter to the viewer.

Common mistakes in client ads

  • A loop the product can't close. If the answer is weak, the viewer feels cheated.
  • Too many loops in 15 seconds. One clear question is enough for a short ad.
  • Logo first, loop later. The opening seconds go on branding that the viewer hasn't been given a reason to care about.
  • Closing after the call to action. People who leave at the offer never see the answer.
  • Copying a loop from another niche. "This one trick" may work for a gadget and feel wrong for a law firm.

How to teach open loops to an agency team

Give every editor the same one-line loop template, a shared library of approved openings per client, and a weekly review of the two best and two worst ads by three-second hold. Ask each editor to mark the open, hold and close on the timeline before export. Over a few weeks, the team builds a feel for which questions hold each audience, and new editors pick it up from real examples rather than theory.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops for client video ads?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops, hooks and pacing for £1,720 one-time; it was written for YouTube and Shorts, so you adapt it to ads. Google's ABCD guide is a free starting point.

How long should an open loop last in a 15-second ad?

Usually most of the ad: open it in the first two or three seconds and close it a few seconds before the call to action.

Are open loops just clickbait?

No, as long as you pay them off honestly. Clickbait opens a question the video never answers.

Do open loops work on muted feeds?

Yes, if the question is on screen as text, not only in the voiceover.

How do I know if the loop worked?

Compare the early hold rate and the share of viewers reaching the call to action against a version without the loop. On YouTube, look for dips in the retention report.
