Is spread betting tax-free in the UK?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Usually, for individuals in the UK, but it is not a guarantee. Spread bets are treated as bets rather than investments, and GOV.UK lists "betting, lottery or pools winnings" among the things you do not pay Capital Gains Tax on. The other side is that losses cannot normally be used to cut tax on other gains, and if spread betting is your main income, HMRC may look at it more closely.

This page is education only, not financial or tax advice. Spread bets are leveraged and most retail accounts lose money. Check HMRC guidance or ask a qualified tax adviser about your own situation.

Key takeaways

  • GOV.UK says you do not pay Capital Gains Tax on betting, lottery or pools winnings, which is why most personal spread betting profits are not taxed.
  • Because you never own the underlying shares, there is normally no Stamp Duty on a spread bet either.
  • The trade-off: spread betting losses generally cannot be set against other taxable gains, while CFD losses can.
  • "Tax-free" depends on your circumstances; if spread betting is your main or only income, the position needs closer review.
  • Since 2019 the FCA has treated financial spread bets as CFDs for its retail rules: leverage limits, a 50% margin close-out and negative balance protection.

Why spread betting profits are usually not taxed

GOV.UK's page on what you pay Capital Gains Tax on lists the things you do not pay it on, including ISAs and PEPs, UK government gilts and Premium Bonds, and "betting, lottery or pools winnings".

A spread bet is a bet on how far a price moves, at a stake per point. You never buy the shares, index or currency. CMC Markets, a UK spread betting provider, explains in its guide to spread betting tax in the UK that this is why profits "are usually exempt from CGT and Stamp Duty for many UK individuals, subject to circumstances". It adds that HMRC's Business Income Manual treats betting and gambling as not normally being a trade.

The catches

The same provider is clear that "'tax free' should not be read as a blanket guarantee". The main catches are:

CatchWhat it means
Losses are not usually allowableYou generally cannot use spread betting losses to reduce tax on gains from shares or property
Main or only incomeIf spread betting is your main source of income, HMRC may look at how often you trade, how organised it is and your intent
Other structuresHedging, business-linked bets, trading through a company and living outside the UK can all change the treatment
Tax law can changeToday's treatment is not promised for the future

For many people the tax difference is smaller than it looks. If you lose money, which most retail accounts do, the CFD route at least gives you a loss you may be able to use. Our page on how trading profits are taxed in the UK covers shares and CFDs in more detail.

The FCA's rules for spread bets

The Financial Conduct Authority's press release confirming permanent restrictions on CFDs and CFD-like options, published on 1 July 2019, says its definition of CFDs includes financial spread bets and rolling spot forex. From 1 August 2019 firms selling them to retail customers must:

  • limit leverage to between 30:1 and 2:1, depending on the asset;
  • close out a customer's positions when their funds fall to 50% of the margin needed;
  • guarantee that a client cannot lose more than the total funds in their account;
  • show a standard warning with the percentage of their retail accounts that lose money;
  • stop offering incentives to encourage trading.

The FCA said its intervention followed "evidence of firms aggressively marketing CFDs to the general public". CMC Markets' own warning on the page above says 69% of its retail investor accounts lose money when spread betting and/or trading CFDs. Our page on why most CFD traders lose money looks at the reasons.

Worked example: a profitable year and a losing year

Example (illustrative): Raj, a UK basic-rate taxpayer with a full-time job, spread bets on the FTSE 100 at £5 a point. He also sold some shares this year at a gain.

Year 1Year 2
Spread betting result+£4,000 profit−£4,000 loss
Capital Gains Tax on the spread betting resultNone, treated as betting winningsNo tax to pay, but no relief either
Gain on shares sold outside an ISA£6,000£6,000
Can the spread betting result change the tax on the share gain?NoNo: the £4,000 loss cannot be set against it

If Raj had made the same trades with CFDs, his Year 1 profit would usually be taxable, but his Year 2 loss could usually reduce the tax on his share gain. So "tax-free" helps only if you win. Keep records anyway

Even if you owe no tax on spread bets, keep a record of every bet, its stake, open and close prices and result. It helps if HMRC ever asks whether your activity is a trade, and it is the only honest way to see whether you are winning. Do I pay Capital Gains Tax on spread betting?

Usually not as a UK individual, because GOV.UK lists betting winnings among things you do not pay Capital Gains Tax on.

Can I claim spread betting losses against other gains?

Generally no. That is the trade-off for profits usually being untaxed.

Is spread betting tax-free if it is my job?

Not necessarily. If it is your main or only income, the tax position needs closer review; ask a tax adviser.

Can I lose more than my deposit spread betting?

With an FCA-regulated firm, retail clients have negative balance protection, so you cannot lose more than the funds in your account.

Is spread betting the same as a CFD?

They work similarly and the FCA treats spread bets as CFDs for its rules, but they are taxed differently.
