How can a video editor see a client's YouTube audience retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Ask the client to add you in YouTube Studio under Settings > Permissions with the Viewer (limited) role. It lets you see every video's analytics, including the audience retention graph, without revenue figures and without being able to change anything. Never ask for the client's Google password; channel permissions exist so you never need it.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube channel permissions have seven roles; Viewer (limited) is the safest one that still shows retention.
  • Editor (limited) adds upload and publishing rights, still without revenue data.
  • Invites expire after 30 days, and Brand Account channels must migrate to channel permissions first.
  • Invited people cannot use YouTube APIs for the channel, so third-party tools still need the owner to connect them.
  • Retention key moments appear about one to two days after upload and only on videos of at least 60 seconds with 100+ views.

Which role to ask for

YouTube's help page on channel permissions sets out what each role can do. For an editor, four matter:

RoleSees analytics and retentionSees revenueCan upload or publishCan delete published videosBest for
Viewer (limited)YesNoNoNoEditors who only cut and need feedback
ViewerYesYesNoNoStrategists who also advise on income
Editor (limited)YesNoYesNoEditors who also upload drafts and set titles
ManagerYesYesYesYesTrusted channel managers only

The page also notes that only managers and the owner can see the names and emails of people with access, and that an invitation expires 30 days after it is sent. If the channel is a Brand Account, the owner must first migrate to channel permissions.

How the client adds you

  1. In YouTube Studio, the owner opens Settings > Permissions.
  2. They select Invite, enter your email and choose Viewer (limited).
  3. You accept the invite from your email within 30 days.
  4. You switch to the client's channel from your profile picture in Studio.

When you act on someone else's channel, YouTube attributes public actions to the channel, but private actions like searches and watch history use your own account.

What to look at once you are in

YouTube's guide to key moments for audience retention explains the four labels an editor should check on every video:

  • Intro: the share of viewers still watching after 30 seconds. A weak intro is usually an editing or structure problem.
  • Top moments: sections where almost no one left. Study what you did there.
  • Spikes: moments rewatched or shared. Strong content, or something confusing.
  • Dips: sections where viewers skipped or stopped. These are your to-do list.

You can also compare against "your 10 latest videos of similar length", which is fairer than comparing a 6-minute video with a 30-minute one. Data typically takes one to two days to process.

The editor never touched revenue or settings. Viewer (limited) was enough to turn four dips into four concrete edit rules, and the intro figure rose 14 points over the month. Your numbers will differ; the point is the habit of one change per video.

Why tools are different from roles

The channel permissions page states that invited users cannot access YouTube APIs for the channel. So if you want an automated tool that reads retention, the channel owner has to connect it with their own Google sign-in. Under the YouTube API Services Developer Policies, such tools must explain their data use in a privacy policy and delete data after the owner revokes access.

What to send the client each month

A short monthly note keeps the arrangement useful for both sides:

  1. The intro figure for each video, side by side.
  2. The single biggest dip per video and what you changed because of it.
  3. One thing that worked and will become part of the template.
  4. One question for the client, such as whether a sponsor read can move.

This shows the client that their access is being used to improve the videos, not just to watch numbers, and gives you a record of your own impact.

Mistakes editors make with client analytics

  • Asking for Manager by default. You rarely need it, and it puts the channel at more risk if your account is compromised.
  • Judging on day one. Retention needs one to two days to settle.
  • Only looking at averages. Average view duration hides where people left; the curve shows it.
  • Changing many things at once. Change one element per video so you can see what worked.

FAQ

What YouTube role should a video editor have?

Viewer (limited) if you only need analytics, or Editor (limited) if you also upload. Neither shows revenue.

Can I see retention without the client's password?

Yes. Channel permissions let the owner invite your own Google account, so no password sharing is needed.

Why can't I see retention on a client's Short?

How long does a channel permissions invite last?

YouTube says an invitation expires 30 days after it is sent.
