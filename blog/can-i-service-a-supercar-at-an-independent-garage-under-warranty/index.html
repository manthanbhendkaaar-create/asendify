Can I service my supercar at an independent garage without voiding the warranty?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Usually yes. In the UK, competition rules mean a car maker generally can't make its warranty conditional on routine servicing at its own dealers, as long as the work follows the manufacturer's schedule, uses parts and fluids of the right specification, and you keep itemised proof. Warranty repairs and recalls are still done by the franchised network, and a claim can be refused if a fault was caused by poor work or a faulty part.

Key takeaways

  • The Motor Vehicle Agreements Block Exemption Order 2023 (MVBEO) came into force on 1 June 2023, and the CMA's guidance on it covers warranties.
  • The CMA says a warranty "made conditional on" non-warranty repair and maintenance being done only by network members may breach competition law.
  • Requiring maker-branded parts for routine servicing may also be caught, but makers can refuse a claim where a fault is causally linked to the repairer or a third-party part.
  • The RAC advises keeping a stamped service book and an itemised invoice showing the work, parts, oils and fluids.
  • Extended or third-party warranties can have their own servicing terms, so read them separately.

What the rules say

The UK's Motor Vehicle Block Exemption Regulation case page records that the Competition Act 1998 (Motor Vehicle Agreements Block Exemption) (No. 2) Order 2023 came into force on 1 June 2023, replacing the retained EU regulation. The CMA's guidance on motor vehicle agreements explains how it applies to warranties:

CMA guidanceWhat it means for you
Para 5.36: a warranty conditional on non-warranty repair and maintenance being done only by network members may be caught by the Chapter I prohibitionAn independent can do routine servicing
Para 5.37: requiring maker-branded parts for non-warranty replacements may also be caughtParts of matching quality can be used for servicing
Para 5.37: makers can still refuse a claim where the fault is causally linked to a repairer's failure or a third-party partPoor work or a bad part can cost you the claim
Para 5.61: authorised repairers using maker-supplied parts for warranty repairs, free servicing and recall work is objectively justifiedWarranty and recall work stays with the dealer
Para 5.41: terms that clearly state your right to use an independent are unlikely to raise concernsCheck your warranty booklet for this wording

What the independent must do

The RAC's guide sums up the practical side: the car must be serviced on time, to the manufacturer's schedule, with "the correct parts, oils and fluids", and you should keep "a stamped service book and itemised invoice that details all of the work".

For a supercar, add a few checks of your own:

  1. Specialist experience. Ask how many cars of your model they service each year.
  2. Diagnostic access. Many modern cars need the maker's diagnostic software to reset service indicators and run checks.
  3. Exact fluid specs. Oil approvals matter on high-revving engines; the invoice should list the approval, not just "5W-40".
  4. Digital service records. Some makers keep online service histories; ask whether the independent can update it or give you paperwork the dealer will accept.
  5. Labour rate vs dealer. Get a written quote for the same schedule from both.

Worked example: a three-year-old supercar

Example: Dan owns a three-year-old supercar still inside its manufacturer warranty. His dealer quotes for the annual service; a marque specialist quotes noticeably less for the same schedule, using parts of the same specification and listing every oil and filter by part number. (Figures vary widely by model and garage, so get your own quotes.)

He uses the specialist and keeps the itemised invoice and the stamped book. Six months later a sensor fails. He takes the car to the dealer, which carries out the warranty repair at no cost because the fault has nothing to do with the service. If the specialist had used the wrong oil and the engine had been damaged, the maker could refuse that claim, and Dan would look to the garage instead.

How it fits into your running costs

Servicing is one line in a supercar's yearly cost of ownership, alongside depreciation, insurance, tyres and tax. A cheaper independent service can make a real difference over several years, but only if it doesn't cost you a warranty claim or resale value.

Checklist before you book an independent

  • Read your warranty booklet for the servicing conditions and any wording about independent repairers.
  • Ask the garage to follow the manufacturer's schedule for your car's age and mileage, not a generic service.
  • Ask for parts and fluids that meet the manufacturer's specification, listed by part number and approval on the invoice.
  • Get the service book stamped and keep the itemised invoice with your records.
  • Check whether the car has any open recalls or software campaigns; those go to the dealer.
  • If you have an extended warranty or service plan, read its terms separately.

Keep every invoice together with the car's history file. When you sell, a clear record of on-time servicing with correct parts reassures buyers whether the work was done by a dealer or a specialist.

Will an independent service void my manufacturer warranty?

Generally not in the UK, if the work follows the schedule with correct parts and fluids and you keep proof.

Can the maker refuse a warranty claim after an independent service?

Yes, if the fault is causally linked to the repairer's work or a third-party part.

Who does recalls and warranty repairs?

The franchised dealer network.

Does this apply to extended warranties?

Not always. Extended or third-party warranties can set their own servicing terms, so read them.

Do I need a main dealer for the MOT?

No. The RAC says you don't need a main dealer for the MOT to keep the warranty.
