How do I pace a sales video (VSL) so viewers do not leave at the pricing section?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 25 November 2026 · Last updated 25 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Digistore24's VSL guide puts the offer sixth of nine parts, after proof, and says "withholding it reads as a trap" when no price is stated; it also says "a cliff at 6:00 means whatever you're doing at 6:00 is wrong" (Digistore24).
  • Vidpros says "most people experience a mid-video drop from minute 3-5 due to pacing issues" and that cuts hanging around for 9 seconds instead of 4 show "you have pacing problems, not content problems" (Vidpros).
  • Panda Video says typical VSL length "can vary from 5 to 20 minutes, depending on the offer", and suggests setting when the call-to-action button appears (Panda Video).
  • YouTube says dips mean "viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video" (YouTube Help).

Why viewers leave at the price

A viewer who reaches the pricing section has already given you minutes of attention. If they leave there, one of three things usually happened. They were not convinced enough to justify the price. They felt the build-up was too long and lost interest before the offer arrived. Or the price felt hidden, so the reveal felt like a trick. Digistore24 is blunt about the last one: withholding the price reads as a trap and pushes the decision off the page.

Each cause needs a different fix, and the graph helps you tell them apart. A drop at the exact second the price appears points to the offer itself. A slow slide in the minutes before it points to pacing. A cliff much earlier points to the hook. Digistore24's advice is to fix the earliest drop first, because everything after it loses viewers anyway.

Step-by-step: pace a VSL into the pricing section

  1. Read the graph first. Mark the exact second of the biggest drop, and what is on screen at that moment.
  2. Check the structure. Digistore24's nine parts run hook, problem, failed fixes, mechanism, proof, offer, objections, risk handling and one call to action. See what you are missing.
  3. Cut padding before the offer. If your proof section repeats the same point three times, keep the strongest one.
  4. Make the proof specific. Digistore24 says specific beats impressive: a modest, verifiable result outperforms a spectacular vague one. Only use results you can show.
  5. Shorten long shots. Vidpros suggests that cuts hanging for 9 seconds instead of 4 signal pacing problems.
  6. Add a pattern interrupt before the offer. A change of picture, a short question or a graphic helps reset attention before the key moment.
  7. State the price plainly. Say what it costs, what it includes and how it is paid. Ambiguity reads as evasion.
  8. Do not tease the price forever. Vidpros suggests not giving away the offer until viewers have earned it, at least by the five-minute mark, but avoid hiding it from people who have already decided.
  9. Answer objections out loud. Say them before the viewer does, as Digistore24 advises.
  10. Show the button at the offer. Panda Video suggests setting when the call-to-action button appears. Vidpros suggests it stay on screen longer than six seconds.
  11. Match length to price. Digistore24 says there is a correct relationship between length and price. A low-priced product does not need twenty minutes of argument.
  12. Re-test and compare. Publish the revised cut, wait for enough views, then compare the graph at the same points.

Example: a 12-minute coaching VSL

Example: a coach has a 12-minute video that loses 30% of viewers in the minute before the price at 9:00. Looking at the cut, the proof section has four testimonials that make the same point, and several shots last about 9 seconds. The coach keeps the two strongest testimonials, trims long shots to about 4 seconds, and adds a short recap line before the offer. The price, what is included and the payment terms are stated in the first 20 seconds of the offer section. The video drops to about 10 minutes 30 seconds. These numbers are invented to show the method and are not a measured result. The test is whether the new graph is flatter in the minute before the price and whether the price itself still causes a drop.

What not to do

Do not use fake urgency, invented testimonials or claims you cannot support to keep people watching past the price. They may hold viewers for a moment, and they damage trust and can break advertising rules. If your product is expensive, the right response to a drop at the price is usually clearer proof, not louder pressure.

If you offer a guarantee, state it exactly as it is. Do not imply protection the offer does not give.

Should I hide the price until the end of a VSL?

No. Digistore24 says withholding the price reads as a trap. State it plainly after you have shown proof.

How long should a VSL be?

Panda Video says 5 to 20 minutes depending on the offer, and Digistore24 says length should relate to price. Test your own.

Where should the call-to-action button appear?

Panda Video suggests controlling when it appears, and Vidpros says to keep it on screen longer than six seconds. Time it to the offer.

How do I find where viewers leave?
