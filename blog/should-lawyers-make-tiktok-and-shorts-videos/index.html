Should lawyers make TikTok and YouTube Shorts videos, and how do you keep viewers watching?

Short videos work for lawyers because people search and scroll for answers to everyday legal worries. To keep viewers watching, open with a surprising-but-true question ("Can your boss read your work messages?"), give the general answer fast, add one exception that matters, and stop.

This page is about video content for lawyers, not legal or regulatory advice. Follow your regulator's rules and your firm's social media policy.

Key takeaways

  • One question per video; short-form punishes anything that wanders.
  • The best hooks are myths people believe ("Squatters' rights mean..."), everyday situations, and "what happens if" questions.
  • Always name the jurisdiction on screen; a viral clip will reach people where the law is different.
  • Publicity must be accurate and not misleading; in England and Wales that's paragraph 8.8 of the SRA Code.
  • Don't give advice in comments or duets on someone's personal situation.

Hook formats that work

What happens if"What happens if you die without a will in England?"

Reacting to a common questionOn-screen comment: "Can my neighbour's tree be cut back?"

Make sure the hook is accurate. "Surprising" shouldn't become "misleading".

A 30-second structure

  1. 0 to 2 seconds: Question on screen and spoken, jurisdiction in text ("England & Wales").
  2. 2 to 10: The general answer in one or two sentences.
  3. 10 to 22: The exception or the step people miss.
  4. 22 to 28: What to do next, in general terms (official guidance, keep records, get advice).
  5. 28 to 30: Name and firm in text; "general information, not legal advice".

Editing for retention

  • Start talking in frame one. No intro card, no logo.
  • Captions on every word, big and placed away from the app's buttons.
  • Cut pauses tightly, but keep a calm, credible pace.
  • Use props or on-screen text for the key rule (a tenancy agreement, a date).
  • Consistent look. Same setting and framing across videos helps people recognise you.
  • Series. Number related videos ("Tenant rights, part 3") so viewers look for the next one.

Guardrails

  • Have a second lawyer or supervisor check scripts on less familiar topics.
  • Avoid commenting on live cases or named individuals.
  • Don't promise outcomes or quote success rates.
  • Keep a record of what was posted and when, and correct or remove videos when the law changes.

Is there a book on making Shorts and TikToks people finish?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and pacing for Shorts for £1,720 one-time; it isn't law-specific.

Can lawyers be on TikTok?

Many are. The same professional rules apply as to any publicity: accurate, not misleading, no improper approaches.

How do I handle comments asking for advice?

Reply generally and invite them to contact the firm, or not at all. Don't advise on individual facts publicly.

How long should a lawyer's short video be?

Often 20 to 45 seconds: one question, one answer, one exception.

Should I name the jurisdiction every time?

Yes. Short videos travel, and the law differs between countries and states.
