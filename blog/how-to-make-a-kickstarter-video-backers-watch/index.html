How do I make a Kickstarter video that backers watch to the end?

Key takeaways

  • Kickstarter: "A project video is not required to launch," but compelling videos tend to perform better.
  • Kickstarter's product guide: a video "is typically best when kept short, usually around two to three minutes."
  • Show, don't describe: the video "should demonstrate your product's core features by showing it in action."
  • It's "not a commercial": avoid elaborate fictional stories; be open and transparent.
  • For the app's vertical Discovery Mode video, Kickstarter suggests 60 to 90 seconds, real people and captions.

What Kickstarter itself recommends

Kickstarter publishes its own advice, which is the best starting point because it comes from the platform that hosts the video.

Its help article on project videos says the video should introduce who you are, what you're raising funds for, why the project matters to you and how you'll deliver it. Its lesson on creating an effective campaign video lists five parts: a short introduction, core features and benefits, your backstory, supporting details that build credibility, and a clear call to action. It also says "you don't need a large budget or fancy equipment."

Its product-launch guide, how to tell your product story, suggests opening with a "snapshot overview of your product and vision", then a demo, then the team and process, then a deeper look at how it works.

Why people leave a campaign video early

Backers arrive curious but sceptical. They're asking three questions: what is it, does it really work, and can these people deliver? A video that spends its first 30 seconds on a founder's life story before showing the product makes them wait for the answer to the first question. A video that only shows renders makes them doubt the second.

A structure for a 2 to 3 minute video

TimeSectionWhat to show
0:00 to 0:15SnapshotThe product in use, with one line on what it does
0:15 to 0:50DemoEach core feature working, real footage
0:50 to 1:30Who and whyThe team, the problem that started it, the workshop or prototype stages
1:30 to 2:10Proof and planPrototype tests, manufacturing steps, what's done and what's left
2:10 to 2:30The askWhat backing gets you and what to do now

Editing habits that help it hold:

  1. Open on the product, not the logo. The first 15 seconds should answer "what is it?"
  2. Cut each demo to the moment it works. Trim set-up time; keep the result on screen.
  3. Use honest footage. Kickstarter warns against "elaborate fictional narratives". If a shot shows a prototype or a render, say so on screen.
  4. Keep the founder section specific. One real reason the project exists beats a long biography.
  5. Plan the ask. End with what to do next: back the project or share it.
  6. Add captions. Many people watch on phones with the sound off; Kickstarter recommends captions for the Discovery Mode video.

The vertical Discovery Mode video

Kickstarter's apps have a Discovery Mode that uses a separate vertical video. Its help page suggests 9:16, 60 to 90 seconds, "real people holding or talking about your project" rather than heavy production, clear audio, captions, and keeping faces, products and text away from the edges where the app interface may cover them. Cut this from the same footage, but don't just crop the landscape edit; reframe each shot.

Worked example

Example: a two-person team launching a modular desk organiser.

TimeShot
0:00 to 0:12A messy desk becomes tidy in one time-lapse; text: "A desk organiser that changes shape with your work"
0:12 to 0:45Modules clicking together, a phone stand, a cable channel, each shown working
0:45 to 1:20The founders in their workshop explaining the first 3D-printed version and what was wrong with it
1:20 to 2:00Injection-moulded samples, a drop test, and a simple timeline graphic of the steps left
2:00 to 2:20The reward tiers in one frame and "Back us to get the first batch"

Total: 2 minutes 20 seconds. Every claim in the video matches the campaign page. The timeline is presented as a plan, not a promise of a date that hasn't been confirmed.

Do I need a video to launch on Kickstarter?

No. Kickstarter says a project video isn't required, though it notes compelling videos tend to perform better.

How long should a Kickstarter video be?

Kickstarter's product guide suggests around two to three minutes for the main video, and 60 to 90 seconds for the vertical Discovery Mode video.

Can I use renders in my Kickstarter video?

You can, but show real prototype footage too and label renders clearly; Kickstarter advises being open and transparent.
