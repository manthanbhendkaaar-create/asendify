Is PCP, HP, leasing or paying cash cheaper for a supercar?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Paying cash is usually cheapest overall for a supercar, because you pay no interest, though your savings stop earning. Hire purchase (HP) usually costs less interest than PCP, because you pay the loan down faster. Leasing can have the lowest outlay over a few years, but you never own the car.

The right answer depends on interest rates, how long you keep the car and what it is worth at the end. A deal that looks cheap per month can be the most expensive in total, so always compare the total amount payable alongside what you will own when the agreement ends.

This page is for education only, not financial advice. Speak to a regulated adviser before borrowing.

Key takeaways

  • Compare the total cost over the time you will keep the car, not the monthly payment.
  • PCP has lower monthly payments than HP, but interest is charged on the whole amount you borrow, including the large final balloon.
  • HP ends with you owning the car after the final payment and an option-to-purchase fee, usually about £100.
  • With leasing (personal contract hire), you never own the car and must hand it back, with charges for extra miles and damage.
  • With cash, the hidden cost is lost interest on your savings and the depreciation you carry in full.

PCP

MoneyHelper's guide to personal contract purchase explains that monthly payments cover the car's value minus your deposit and a final balloon payment, plus interest, and that interest is "based on the whole value of the car (minus your deposit)". The balloon is set at the start using the Guaranteed Minimum Future Value. Mileage limits are usually around 10,000 miles a year, and going over can bring penalties. MoneyHelper warns it is "easy to get locked into a cycle of changing your car every few years to avoid the balloon payment."

Hire purchase

MoneyHelper's hire purchase guide says you usually pay a deposit of around 10%, then fixed monthly payments, and that "Once you've made your final payment, including the option to purchase fee, you'll become the car's owner." The fee is usually about £100. HP is a secured loan, and until you have paid a third of the cost, the finance company can repossess the car without a court order.

Leasing (personal contract hire)

MoneyHelper's guide to personal contract hire says "you never own the vehicle during the agreement and must hand it back at the end of the term." Payments are usually higher than PCP, but the total over the contract is often lower. Excess mileage is typically charged at around 10p a mile, and you may be charged for damage beyond fair wear and tear. Ending early can be expensive.

Cash

Cash avoids interest and credit checks entirely. The trade-offs are less obvious: you tie up a large sum that could be earning interest elsewhere, and you carry the full depreciation. MoneyHelper notes that new cars can lose about half their value in three years, though this varies widely by model.

Under these assumptions, cash and HP come out cheapest, PCP is the most expensive way to end up owning the car, and leasing sits between them with no ownership. Change the interest rate, the end value or the lease price, and the order can change. That is why it pays to run your own numbers with real quotes.

Questions to ask before choosing

  1. How long will I keep the car? Short ownership favours leasing or PCP; long ownership favours cash or HP.
  2. What is the total amount payable? Every finance agreement must show it. Compare that, not the monthly figure.
  3. What happens if my income changes? Finance commits you to payments; MoneyHelper notes you could lose the car if you fall behind.
  4. How many miles will I drive? Mileage limits on PCP and leasing can add real costs.
  5. Can I cover running costs too? Insurance, servicing and tyres on a supercar are large on their own.

Ending a deal early

MoneyHelper explains that with HP you have a legal right to return the car and end the agreement once you have made at least half of your payments. With PCP, voluntary termination is also possible once you have paid half the total, but because the total includes the balloon, this usually only becomes available near the end. Leasing is generally the hardest to exit early.

FAQ

Is PCP or HP better for an expensive car?

HP usually costs less interest if you want to own the car, because the balance falls faster. PCP suits people who want lower monthly payments and expect to hand the car back or change it.

Is leasing a supercar a waste of money?

Not necessarily. It can have the lowest total outlay over a short period, and you avoid depreciation risk. But you own nothing at the end and may pay mileage and damage charges.

Why is paying cash not always the best choice?

You lose the interest your savings could earn and carry all the depreciation. If a finance deal has a very low interest rate, the gap can narrow.

What deposit do I need for supercar finance?

MoneyHelper says HP deposits are usually around 10% and PCP deposits at least 10%. Lenders may ask for more on high-value cars.
