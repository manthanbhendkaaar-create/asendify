Do landscape videos still work on Facebook now that every video is a Reel?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Yes. Meta said in June 2025 that all Facebook videos would be shared as reels with no length or format restrictions, and that the change would not alter which videos get recommended, so a landscape video can still be posted and shown. But most people scroll Facebook on a phone held upright, so reframe to 4:5 or full-screen 9:16 when the content allows, and keep landscape for footage that needs the width, such as screen recordings, wide shots and long talks.

Key takeaways

  • Meta's June 2025 announcement: "all videos on Facebook will be shared as reels", and reels "will also not have any length or format restrictions".
  • Meta said the change would not affect what is recommended; you will still see videos of different lengths.
  • Before the change, Facebook reels were capped at 90 seconds, according to TechCrunch.
  • Meta's creator best practices call 4:5 the recommended aspect ratio for feed video, and Smart Crop could reframe 16:9 footage to 1:1 or 4:5.
  • The frame matters less than the first seconds: a landscape video with a strong opening beats a vertical one with a slow start.

What changed in 2025

Meta's newsroom post Making it easier to create videos on Facebook (17 June 2025, updated 14 July 2025) said uploading a video and posting a reel would stop being separate choices: all videos would be shared as reels, rolled out gradually to profiles and Pages. It said reels would have no length or format restrictions, that recommendations would stay tailored to each person's interests, and that Feed and Reels would share one default audience setting. TechCrunch added that this applied "regardless of their length or orientation" and removed the previous 90-second cap on Facebook reels.

So which frame should you use?

The format rule went away; the screen did not. A landscape video on an upright phone fills only a band across the middle, with small faces and text. Meta's video best practices for creators call 4:5 the recommended ratio for feed video, and its tools could crop 16:9 footage to 1:1 or 4:5.

Talking head, tip, reaction9:16 or 4:5Face fills the phone screen

Long talk or full episode16:9 with a vertical teaserPeople who commit will rotate or watch wide

The table reflects Meta's 4:5 recommendation plus common editing practice; it is not an official Meta rule for each content type.

How to reframe without losing the story

  1. Find the subject in every shot. Keep faces and hands in the centre third so a vertical crop holds them.
  2. Re-crop shot by shot. Automatic crops miss action at the edges; check each cut.
  3. Enlarge on-screen text. Lower-thirds designed for TV are unreadable in a crop.
  4. Add captions. Meta recommends burned-in subtitles for people watching with the sound off.
  5. Keep a wide master. Post the landscape version where it suits, such as your website or YouTube.

How to tell which frame works for you

Post the same piece in two frames a week or so apart, or test two cuts with Meta's Post Testing tool, which can compare up to four versions on a metric such as one-minute views. Compare one-minute views and average watch time rather than raw views, because a frame that looks better on a phone should keep people longer, not just catch more taps. Keep notes, because the answer can differ between talking-head videos and wide event footage.
