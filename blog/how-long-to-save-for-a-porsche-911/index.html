How long will it take me to save for a Porsche 911 if I put away a fixed amount every month?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 11 November 2026 · Last updated 11 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

To find how long it takes to save for a Porsche 911, divide your target price (plus a running-costs buffer) by what you save each month. For example, a £90,000 target at £1,500 a month takes 60 months (5 years) with no interest, or about 55 months if savings earn an assumed 4% a year.

These are planning estimates for education only, not financial advice.

Key takeaways

  • The basic formula is target ÷ monthly saving = months. Interest shortens it a little; price rises lengthen it.
  • Pick your exact target first: new or used, which model, which year. Prices vary widely, so check current listings rather than guessing.
  • Add a buffer for the first year of running costs, including insurance, servicing, tyres and vehicle tax.
  • In the UK, cars registered since April 2017 with a list price over £40,000 pay an extra annual vehicle tax charge for five years.
  • Review the plan every few months. Your saving rate and the car's price will both change.

Step 1: Set a real target price

"A 911" is not a price. A new car, a three-year-old car and a fifteen-year-old car can differ by tens of thousands of pounds. Decide:

  • New or used. For a new car, Porsche's UK configurator shows current list prices, which change with options. For a used car, look at several dealer listings for the exact model, year and mileage you want.
  • Your must-haves. Gearbox, body style and a few options. Each one moves the price.
  • A date. When you want to buy changes how much you need to save each month.

For this page we use a £90,000 target as an illustrative round number. Swap in your own figure.

Step 2: Add a running-costs buffer

Buying the car is only the start. Before you hit your target, plan for at least the first year of ownership:

  • Insurance. Get real quotes for your age, postcode and the exact car before you commit.
  • Servicing and tyres. Ask a specialist or dealer for the service schedule and typical costs for your model.
  • Vehicle tax. The UK government's vehicle tax rate tables show the rates. Cars first registered on or after 1 April 2017 with a list price over £40,000 pay an additional "expensive car" rate on top of the standard rate for five years from the second time the vehicle is taxed.
  • A repair fund. Older performance cars can need expensive work. Many owners keep a separate pot for this.

In our example we add a £6,000 buffer, so the full savings target becomes £96,000. Your own buffer should come from real quotes.

Step 3: Do the maths

With no interest, the formula is simple:

Months to save = (target − what you already have) ÷ monthly saving

If your savings earn interest, the time is a little shorter. The table below uses a £90,000 target, starting from zero, and compares no interest with an assumed 4% a year (compounded monthly). The 4% is an assumption for illustration only; real rates change and may be higher or lower.

Two things stand out. Interest helps most when the timeline is long. And the monthly amount matters far more than the rate: going from £1,000 to £1,500 a month cuts the 0% timeline by 30 months.

Working backwards from a deadline

If you have a date in mind, flip the formula:

Monthly saving needed = (target − what you already have) ÷ months until your deadline

For £90,000 in four years (48 months) from zero, that is £1,875 a month before interest.

Step 4: Allow for tax on interest and price changes

  • Tax on savings interest. In the UK, interest above your Personal Savings Allowance can be taxable. The government explains the rules on its tax on savings interest page. If your pot is large, this can reduce the benefit of interest.
  • Prices move. New car prices often rise over time, and used prices go up and down. Re-check your target every six months and adjust the plan.

Common mistakes

  • Using the cheapest listing you can find as your target. Use a typical price for the car you would actually buy.
  • Forgetting running costs. A car you can buy but not run is not affordable.
  • Saving in a way you cannot keep up. A steady £1,200 a month beats £2,000 for three months and then nothing.
  • Mixing your emergency fund with your car fund. Keep them separate, so a surprise bill does not reset your plan.

Independent, free guidance on building savings is available from the government-backed MoneyHelper savings pages.

FAQ

How much do I need to save each month for a Porsche 911?

Divide your target price, plus a running-costs buffer, by the number of months until you want to buy. For a £90,000 target in five years from zero, that is £1,500 a month before interest.

How long does it take to save £100,000 at £2,000 a month?

50 months, or just over four years, with no interest. Interest would shorten that slightly, depending on the rate you actually get.

Should I include insurance in my savings target?

It is sensible to include at least the first year of insurance, servicing, tyres and vehicle tax, so you can run the car from day one without touching other savings.

Does a 911 pay the expensive car supplement?

New 911s have a list price well over £40,000, so in the UK they pay the expensive car rate on top of standard vehicle tax for five years from the second time the car is taxed. Check the GOV.UK rate tables for current figures.

Is this financial advice?

No. This page explains planning maths for education only. For personal advice about saving or borrowing, speak to a regulated financial adviser.
