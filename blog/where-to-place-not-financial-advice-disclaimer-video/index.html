Where should a not-financial-advice disclaimer go in a YouTube investing video?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 25 November 2026 · Last updated 25 November 2026

Put the disclaimer on screen, in the section it relates to, and not only in the description. The FCA's guidance treats a risk warning found only in a video's caption as a poor example. A short line in the opening and a visible one beside any claim about returns is a sensible pattern. This is general information, not legal advice.

Key takeaways

  • The FCA's guidance (FCA FG24/1) says the complete risk warning should be displayed for the duration of the financial promotion, and lists a warning found only in a video caption as a non-compliant example.
  • For long-form video, the same table says the warning should be displayed clearly and prominently on screen for the section of the video it covers.
  • The FCA also says information that must be prominent should be easily identified and not need a click-through (FCA FG24/1).
  • A disclaimer is not a hook, but a calm on-screen line placed well costs little retention. Test it on your graph.

Who this applies to

This page is general information, not legal, financial or compliance advice. Rules differ by country and by what the video does. A video that teaches how markets work is treated differently from one that promotes a product, a trading service or a particular investment. If you are in the UK and your video could count as a financial promotion, the FCA's finalised guidance on social media is the main reference (FCA). The FCA's page also states that unauthorised persons, including influencers, who promote a regulated product or service may be committing a criminal offence. If you are unsure which side you are on, ask a qualified compliance adviser before you publish. Other regulators, such as those in the US or EU, have their own rules.

What the FCA guidance says about video placement

The FCA's document FCA FG24/1 gives examples by format. In Table 1 it describes, for short-form video, a risk warning clearly and prominently displayed across the screen throughout the promotion. For long-form video it describes the warning displayed clearly and prominently on screen for the section of the video that the promotion covers. For livestreams, it points to the warning being displayed on screen for the duration of any part of the stream that includes the promotion. It also lists, as a non-compliant example, a risk warning found within the caption of the video.

Elsewhere the guidance says the complete risk warning should be displayed for the duration of the financial promotion, that information required to be prominent should be presented in a way that is easily identified, and that it should be shown without needing click-through. It also says firms should provide a balanced view of benefits and risks.

Reading these together, the safe assumption is that a line buried in the description does not do the job for a video that promotes something. For a purely educational explainer, many creators still show an on-screen line near the start and wherever they discuss returns. That is a choice, not a ruling from this page.

Steps to place it without losing viewers

  1. Decide what the video is. Education only, or promoting something. If promoting, get professional advice on wording and placement.
  2. Draft the line once. Use plain words. Avoid a wall of small print.
  3. Design it to be read. Contrast, size and time on screen matter. A line that flashes for half a second does not meet the idea of clear and prominent.
  4. Time it with the claim. Bring the line in when the returns chart or the product appears, and keep it up for that whole section.
  5. Do not rely on the voiceover alone if the guidance for your format expects it on screen. Do both where useful.
  6. Keep the hook clean. Put the first line on the picture, not as a long spoken preamble. Wistia says a drop at the start means the video failed to hook someone who was interested enough to press play.
  7. Check the graph. YouTube Help says dips show where viewers leave. If a dip lines up exactly with the disclaimer, shorten the wording or move it onto a visual that is already on screen.
  8. Keep a record. Save the version of the video and the wording that you published.

Example: an 8-minute video on dividend investing

Example: a creator's first draft starts with a 12-second spoken disclaimer, then the hook. The graph shows a fall in the first 15 seconds. In the next video of similar length, the hook comes first with a small on-screen line, "Education only. Not financial advice.", for the first 20 seconds. At minute 4 the video shows a chart of past dividend payments, and a longer on-screen warning that returns are not guaranteed and values can fall appears with it for the whole section. The description repeats both. The creator compares the two graphs and checks the typical retention band. This scenario uses invented numbers and is not a measured result or a compliance sign-off.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not put the whole warning in tiny text that disappears quickly. Do not assume a caption counts. Do not copy wording from another channel without checking it suits your situation. Do not use the disclaimer as a shield for claims you cannot support; the guidance expects a balanced view regardless. And do not expect a disclaimer to fix a video that gives a one-sided pitch. When unsure, seek qualified advice.

Is a disclaimer in the description enough?

For a video that promotes something, the FCA's guidance lists a risk warning found only in the caption as non-compliant. See FCA FG24/1. Take advice for your case.

How long should the on-screen line stay up?

For the section it covers. The FCA's guidance describes long-form warnings displayed on screen for that section.

Does a disclaimer hurt retention?

A short, still line usually costs little. Check your graph, since a dip at the same moment is a cue to adjust.
