What does the YouTube retention graph mean?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The YouTube retention graph shows, moment by moment, what percentage of viewers were still watching your video. The line always slopes down; what matters is where it drops sharply, where it stays flat and where it spikes, because each shape points to a specific moment in your video to keep or fix.

To read your own curve quickly, paste its numbers into the free YouTube Retention Curve Checker, which flags the biggest drops with timestamps.

Key takeaways

  • The horizontal axis is time in the video. The vertical axis is the share of viewers still watching at that moment.
  • A gradual downward slope is normal for every video. Sharp drops are what to investigate.
  • The "Intro" figure tells you how many viewers were still there after 30 seconds.
  • Spikes mean a moment was rewatched or shared. Dips mean viewers skipped or left.
  • Compare a curve with your own similar videos, not with a universal "good" number.

The two axes in plain English

Open a video in YouTube Studio, go to its Insights (formerly Analytics) and choose the Engagement tab. YouTube's guide to key moments for audience retention explains that the report is only available for individual videos and can take one to two days to process.

  • Left to right is the running time of your video, from 0:00 to the end.
  • Bottom to top is the percentage of viewers watching at that point. If the line reads 45% at 3:00, roughly 45% of views were still playing at three minutes.

The curve can go above 100% at the very start or at a spike. That happens when people rewind and watch a section again, so it is counted more than once.

The four key moments YouTube labels

YouTube highlights four kinds of moment on the graph:

MomentYouTube's meaningWhat it usually tells you
IntroPercentage of viewers still watching after the first 30 secondsWhether your opening matched the title and thumbnail
Top momentsPoints where almost no one dropped offContent your viewers value; consider moving similar material earlier
SpikesMoments that were rewatched or sharedEither a great moment or a confusing one people replayed
DipsMoments that were skipped or where viewers stopped watchingA slow section, tangent or broken promise

YouTube suggests that if your Intro figure is low, you revisit your thumbnail and title, or test different opening styles. It also notes that if top moments appear late in a video, you could bring compelling content earlier, because audiences generally shrink over the course of a video.

Common curve shapes and what they mean

The cliff at the start

A steep fall in the first 10 to 30 seconds is the most common problem. Viewers clicked, saw something different from what they expected, and left. Typical causes are a long greeting, a logo sting, or a story that delays the point.

The steady slope

A line that falls gently and evenly is normal. People leave for all sorts of reasons: they got what they needed, the bus arrived, dinner was ready. You improve a steady slope with tighter pacing, not a single fix.

The mid-video cliff

A sudden drop halfway through usually lines up with something on screen: a sponsor read with no lead-in, an off-topic tangent, or a section that repeats what came before. Rewatch the 10 seconds before the drop.

The plateau

A flat section means almost nobody left. That is a top moment. Note what you were doing there and use more of it.

The spike

A bump upwards means people rewound or jumped to that point. If it follows a slow section, viewers may have been skipping ahead. If it sits on a clever demonstration, people may have rewatched because they enjoyed it.

The end drop

A sharp fall in the last 20 to 40 seconds is common when viewers sense the video is wrapping up. A long outro makes it bigger.

How to judge whether your curve is good

There is no single benchmark that fits every video. YouTube offers a fairer one: the report's "typical retention" view compares your video with your 10 latest videos of similar length, and you can also compare against all YouTube videos of similar length.

Why does the curve matter? YouTube's own discovery guide says its systems use both average view duration and average percentage viewed as ranking signals. A video that holds viewers better than your usual is a good sign, whatever the raw percentage.

YouTube's page on understanding content performance defines average view duration as the average minutes watched among those who stayed to watch. The retention graph shows how that average was built up, second by second.

Example: reading a real-looking curve

Example: Mei uploads a 12-minute video, "How I organise a tiny kitchen". Her retention points read:

TimeRetentionWhat she sees when she rewatches
0:00100%Start
0:3061%Intro figure; she spent 20 seconds on a channel welcome
2:0054%Gentle slope, normal
5:1050%Plateau while she shows a drawer hack
5:4051%Small spike; people rewound the hack
7:0038%Cliff: she starts a long story about her landlord
11:2030%End drop begins as she says "that's it for today"

Her three fixes: cut the welcome and show the finished kitchen in the first five seconds, move the drawer hack earlier, and drop the landlord story. These figures are illustrative; the method is what matters.

A quick routine after every upload

  1. Wait two days for the data to settle.
  2. Write down the Intro figure and compare it with your last five videos.
  3. Mark every drop of five points or more within 30 seconds.
  4. Rewatch 10 seconds before each mark and write one sentence on what happened.
  5. Pick one fix for the next video.

FAQ

Why does my retention graph start below 100%?

Some viewers leave in the first second, for example when a video autoplays and they scroll on. The very first point can also vary slightly depending on how YouTube counts the start.

Why does my retention graph go above 100%?

When viewers rewind and rewatch a section, those seconds are counted again. That can push the line above 100% at a spike or at the very start.

What does a dip mean on the retention graph?

YouTube describes dips as moments that were skipped or where viewers stopped watching. Rewatch the seconds before the dip to see what caused it.

Where do I find the retention graph?

In YouTube Studio, open Content, choose the video, open its Insights and go to the Overview or Engagement tab. It is only available for individual videos.

Is the retention graph different for Shorts?

Shorts loop and are very short, so their curves look different. Compare Shorts only with other Shorts.
