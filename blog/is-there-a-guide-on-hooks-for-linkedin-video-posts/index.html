Key takeaways

  • LinkedIn advises: "Show what you want your audience to see in the first 10 seconds of the video", because "viewer attention drops after that point". That guidance is for ads, but the same feed behaviour applies to posts.
  • LinkedIn also notes "a large portion of LinkedIn members will watch your ad with the sound off" and suggests burned-in subtitles.
  • Captions also serve accessibility; W3C describes them as "a text version of the speech and non-speech audio information".
  • Professional doesn't mean slow. Specific beats polished.

What makes a LinkedIn hook different

LinkedIn viewers are scrolling between work tasks. They respond to hooks about their job, industry or career, phrased in plain professional language. Hooks that work on TikTok ("Wait for it...") often feel out of place. Specific numbers, honest opinions and clear problems tend to fit better.

Hook types for LinkedIn video

Hook typeExampleBest for
Specific result"We cut our sales cycle from 90 to 45 days. Here's the one change."Founders, sales leaders
Problem"Most onboarding emails are ignored. Here's why."Marketers, SaaS
Opinion"I don't think cold calling is dead. I think bad lists are."Thought leadership
Mistake"I hired the wrong first salesperson. Three signs I missed."Founders, managers
Quick how-to"How to write a job ad in 10 minutes"Recruiters, HR
Data point (true and sourced)"Our last 100 demos taught us one thing about pricing."Analysts, operators

Make sure numbers in hooks are true for you; a false number destroys trust fast on a professional network.

How to build the first 10 seconds

  1. Text on screen in frame 1. The hook as 5-10 words at the top of the frame.
  2. Say it in the first sentence. Don't introduce yourself first; your name is on the post.
  3. Show something. A whiteboard, screen, product or place beats a plain wall.
  4. Burn in subtitles. For the many muted viewers.
  5. Deliver quickly. The first point should come within 15-20 seconds.

Common mistakes

  • Introducing yourself first. LinkedIn already shows who you are.
  • Logo animations. They spend seconds with nothing for the viewer.
  • No subtitles. Many people won't turn on sound.
  • Vague hooks. "Some thoughts on leadership" gives no reason to stop.
  • Clickbait. Professional audiences punish it quickly.

How to measure a LinkedIn hook

LinkedIn organic reach varies a lot from post to post, so judge hooks over several posts, not one. Keep a simple log with the hook line, the hook type, the video length and the numbers LinkedIn shows you for each post, such as views and watch time where available, plus comments.

PostHook typeLengthWhat to note
1Specific result45 sWatch time and comments vs your average
2Opinion60 sSame
3Mistake50 sSame
4How-to40 sSame

After eight to ten posts, patterns usually appear. Many professionals find one or two hook types suit their voice and audience far better than the rest. Keep those, and retire the ones that feel forced.

Two habits make the log useful. First, change one thing at a time: if you change the hook and the length in the same post, you won't know which mattered. Second, read the comments as data. A hook that gets people disagreeing politely in the comments is often doing its job, even if views are modest.

Finally, match the post text to the video. The first line of the post copy appears beside the video in the feed, so it works as a second hook. Repeating the video's hook word for word wastes it; a short line that adds context ("Rewrote 40 job ads this year. This was the most common mistake.") gives two reasons to press play.

FAQ

Is there a guide on hooks for LinkedIn videos?

Yes. LinkedIn's free video tips cover the first 10 seconds and sound-off viewing; Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook adds hook templates for £1,720 one-time, written for YouTube and adaptable to LinkedIn.

How long should a LinkedIn video be?

LinkedIn advises keeping videos short for most goals and testing longer ones for demand generation.

Should LinkedIn videos be vertical?

Vertical or square usually fills more of the mobile feed; test what suits your audience.

Do LinkedIn videos need subtitles?

Yes. LinkedIn notes many members watch with sound off.

Can I reuse a TikTok hook on LinkedIn?

Reuse the structure, but rewrite the wording for a professional audience.

Should I post the video natively or link to YouTube?

Native video plays in the feed without leaving LinkedIn, which suits most posts. Link to YouTube in the comments if viewers want the full version.
