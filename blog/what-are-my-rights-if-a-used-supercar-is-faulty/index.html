What are my rights if a used supercar from a dealer turns out to be faulty?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

If you bought the car from a dealer, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 lets you reject it for a full refund if a fault shows up within 30 days. After that, the dealer gets one chance to repair or replace it; if that fails, you can ask for a price reduction or reject it for a refund, minus a deduction for use. Faults that appear within six months are presumed to have been there when you bought it.

This page is general information, not legal or financial advice. For your own case, contact Citizens Advice or a solicitor.

Key takeaways

  • The short-term right to reject lasts 30 days from the day after you take ownership and delivery.
  • If you agree to a repair or replacement, the 30-day clock pauses while you wait.
  • After one failed repair or replacement, you can claim a price reduction or use the final right to reject.
  • For cars, the dealer can deduct an amount for your use from a final-rejection refund, even in the first six months.
  • A fault appearing within six months is presumed to have existed at delivery, unless the dealer proves otherwise.

The three stages of your rights

Citizens Advice's page on problems with a used car says you may have a legal right to "a repair, the cost of a repair, or some or all of your money back" if the car is damaged, does not work or does not match the advert or description. Section 19 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 sets out the remedies in order:

StageRightKey rule
1. First 30 daysShort-term right to rejectFull refund; the period pauses during any agreed repair
2. After 30 days, or if you chooseRepair or replacementThe dealer gets one attempt, within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience to you
3. If the repair or replacement failsPrice reduction or final right to rejectRefund can be reduced for your use of the car

Stage 1: the 30-day right to reject

Section 22 says the short-term right to reject ends 30 days after the day on which ownership has passed to you and the car has been delivered. Any agreement that makes you lose the right sooner is not binding.

If you ask for or agree to a repair within those 30 days, the clock stops during the "waiting period", which runs from the day you request or agree to the repair until you get the car back. If the repaired car still has the fault, you then have at least 7 days, or the rest of your original period, whichever is longer, to reject it.

Stage 3: price reduction or final rejection

Section 24 gives you the final right to reject, or a price reduction, if after one repair or replacement the car still has a fault, or if the dealer fails to repair it within a reasonable time without significant inconvenience to you. You cannot have both a price reduction and a final rejection.

For most goods, no deduction for use can be taken from a refund in the first six months. Cars are an exception: the Act allows a deduction for use of a motor vehicle even within six months. On a supercar, how much use you have had, and how that is valued, can make a large difference to the refund.

The six-month presumption

Section 19(14) says that if goods fail to conform within six months of delivery, they are taken not to have conformed on the day of delivery, unless it is shown that they did or this would not fit the nature of the goods or the fault. In practice, during the first six months the dealer usually has to show the car was fine when sold.

Citizens Advice notes you may not have a claim if the dealer told you about the fault, if you inspected the car and should have spotted it, if you caused it, or if it is fair wear and tear for a car of that age and mileage. That last point matters on supercars, where clutches, brakes and tyres wear quickly.

Worked example: a gearbox fault after three weeks

Example (illustrative): Mo buys a used Audi R8 from a dealer for £65,000. On day 20, a gearbox warning appears and the car goes into limp mode.

1Takes delivery30-day clock starts the next day
20Gearbox faultWithin 30 days: can reject for a full refund
21Agrees to let the dealer repair itClock pauses with 10 days left
35Car returned; fault comes back on day 37Can still reject: at least 7 days left, or the remaining 10, whichever is longer
38Rejects in writingEntitled to a refund under the short-term right

If Mo had found the fault in month four instead, he would first have to allow one repair. If it failed, he could ask for a price reduction or reject, with a deduction for the miles he had driven. Because the fault appeared within six months, it would be presumed to have existed at delivery.

How you paid matters

Citizens Advice's guide to buying a used car notes that if the dealer arranged finance, you can take action against the finance company as well as the dealer, and that paying at least part by credit card can give Section 75 protection on goods costing £100 to £30,000. Buying at auction is probably the riskiest route, with likely less legal protection. If you are financing the car, our page on PCP vs HP vs lease vs cash for a supercar compares the options, and how to check a used supercar's history covers checks that help prevent problems.

What to do if your car has a fault

  • Stop driving it if the fault could make it unsafe or worse.
  • Tell the dealer in writing straight away, saying which right you are using.
  • Keep evidence: photos, warning lights, inspection reports and all messages.
  • Contact the finance company if the dealer arranged the finance.
  • Use a trade association or the Motor Ombudsman if the dealer is a member, or get advice from Citizens Advice.

How long do I have to reject a faulty used car?

30 days from the day after ownership and delivery, paused while you wait for any repair you agreed to.

Can a dealer refuse a refund after 30 days?

After 30 days the dealer can first try one repair or replacement. If that fails, you can ask for a price reduction or reject the car.

Will I get all my money back?

Within the first 30 days, yes. After a failed repair, the dealer can deduct an amount for your use of the car.

Do I have the same rights buying privately?

No. The Consumer Rights Act covers buying from a trader. Buying privately or at auction gives you less protection.

Who has to prove the fault was there at purchase?

Within six months, the fault is presumed to have existed at delivery unless the dealer shows otherwise.
