YouTube growth: books, courses, tools and free resources

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 21 October 2026 · Last updated 10 November 2026

Growing a YouTube channel comes down to two things you can work on: getting the right people to click, through titles, thumbnails and topics, and keeping them watching, through hooks, structure and pacing. Start with YouTube's own free help pages and your Studio data. Add paid books, courses or tools only when you know which gap they fill.

Key takeaways

  • Most creators should start with free resources: YouTube Help, the Creators site and their own Studio analytics.
  • Work on packaging (click-through) and retention (watching) separately, because they have different fixes.
  • Compare your numbers with your own recent videos, not with other channels.
  • Paid options mainly buy structure, feedback or time; none can promise growth.
  • Pick one resource for your biggest gap, use it for a month, then reassess.

Start here: the best free resources (not ours)

  • YouTube Help: audience retention. YouTube's own guide to the audience retention report explains the intro figure (the share of viewers still watching after 30 seconds), top moments, spikes, dips, and how your video compares with your 10 latest videos of similar length. If you read one page, read this one.
  • YouTube Help: impressions and click-through rate. The impressions and CTR FAQ says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%, and that CTR varies by content type, audience and where the thumbnail appears. It also warns against clickbait.
  • YouTube Creators. YouTube's official Creators hub has guides on getting started, growing a channel and how things work.
  • YouTube Studio. Your own analytics are free and are the only source of your real retention, traffic and click-through data.
  • Your own best videos. Re-watching your top-retaining videos next to your weakest ones costs nothing and is often the most useful lesson.

Worked example: finding the gap first

Example: a personal finance channel has 40 videos. Over the last ten uploads, Studio shows an impressions CTR of around 3% and an intro figure (viewers still watching at 30 seconds) of around 55%. These are illustrative numbers, not a benchmark.

  1. Check packaging. A 3% CTR sits inside the 2% to 10% range YouTube describes for half of channels, so packaging is not obviously broken. The creator still tests clearer titles on the next three uploads.
  2. Check retention. The intro figure drops sharply on videos with long intros. The typical-retention comparison shows the same pattern across her 10 latest videos of similar length.
  3. Pick one resource. Because the gap is the first 30 seconds, she starts with YouTube's retention help page, then runs her next three scripts through a hook checker before filming.
  4. Reassess after a month. If the intro figure improves on her own comparison, she keeps going. If not, she looks for feedback from someone who can watch the edit.

The point is not the tool. It is naming one gap, using one resource for it, and measuring against your own past videos.

How to judge any paid resource

  • Does it say who it is not for? Honest resources do.
  • Does it use your own data or videos? General advice is useful; specific feedback is more useful.
  • Does it avoid promises? Nobody can promise views or subscribers.
  • Can you check it first? Look for free samples, example pages or a clear outline.

For more detail on reading your own numbers, see how to analyse your YouTube analytics to know what to make next, why people are not watching your YouTube videos and the best AI tools for YouTube creators.

A simple monthly routine

Resources only help if you use them on a schedule. A light routine for most creators:

  1. After each upload (10 minutes, after a few days): note impressions, CTR and the intro figure against your last few videos of similar length.
  2. Once a month (30 minutes): group your recent videos by topic and format, and write one sentence on what worked and one on what did not.
  3. Pick one change for next month. A clearer title style, a shorter intro, or a new topic from your search terms. Only one, so you can see whether it helped.
  4. Once a quarter: decide whether you need outside help, such as a book, feedback or an editor, and for which gap.

Keep the notes in one simple document. After six months, it becomes your own guide to what your audience responds to, which no outside resource can replace.

FAQ

What is the single best free resource for YouTube growth?

YouTube's own help page on the audience retention report, used alongside your YouTube Studio data. It is official, free and specific to your videos.

Do I need a paid course to grow on YouTube?

No. Many creators grow with free resources and steady practice. A paid course mainly buys structure and feedback on your own videos.

What is a good click-through rate on YouTube?

YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%. Compare yours with your own recent videos rather than chasing a single number.
