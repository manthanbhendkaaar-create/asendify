Are trading signal groups and finfluencers legal in the UK?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Sharing opinions about trading is legal, but promoting a regulated financial product or service, such as a CFD broker, without approval from an FCA-authorised firm may be a criminal offence. The FCA's 2024 guidance on social media promotions applies to influencers and affiliate marketers as well as firms. Before following a signal group or influencer, check any firm they push on the FCA Register and treat promises of high returns with suspicion.

This page is education only, not financial or legal advice. Trading is risky and you can lose money.

Key takeaways

  • FCA guidance FG24/1 (March 2024) covers financial promotions on social media by firms and by unauthorised people, including influencers and affiliate marketers.
  • Unauthorised people who promote a regulated product or service without approval from an appropriate FCA-authorised firm "may be committing a criminal offence."
  • Promotions must be fair, clear and not misleading, with a balanced view of benefits and risks.
  • The FCA's scam warning signs include unexpected contact, pressure to act fast, promises of high returns and offers described as exclusive or secret.
  • Pump-and-dump schemes use hype to push a price up so promoters can sell; later buyers are left with losses.

What the FCA rules say

The FCA's finalised guidance FG24/1 on financial promotions on social media, published on 26 March 2024, explains how the existing rules apply to social media. It is aimed at authorised firms, at unauthorised people including influencers and affiliate marketers, and at trade bodies. The guidance does not create new rules, but it makes the position clear: an unauthorised person who promotes a regulated financial product or service without approval from an appropriate authorised firm may be committing a criminal offence.

Promotions on every channel, including short videos and posts, must be fair, clear and not misleading, and give a balanced picture of benefits and risks.

Sharing your own trades and opinionsYes, if honest and not a promotion for a productPresenting cherry-picked wins as typical

Pump and dump: the oldest trick in the feed

The SEC's guide to pump-and-dump schemes describes two stages. Promoters first try to boost a price with false or misleading statements, often online, often claiming "inside" information and urging people to buy quickly. Then they sell their own holdings into the rising price. When they stop promoting, the price usually falls, and people who bought at the inflated price lose money. Signal groups that call small, thinly traded stocks or tokens are an obvious place for this to happen.

How to check before you follow

The FCA's page on protecting yourself from scams lists warning signs and checks:

Warning signWhat to do
Unexpected contact by message, social media or callIgnore or hang up; a genuine firm will not mind you taking time
Pressure to act quickly or "limited time" offersStop and check
High returns, "exclusive" or "secret" opportunitiesTreat with suspicion
Flattery or someone building a friendshipBe cautious, especially before any money changes hands
A broker you have not heard ofCheck the FCA Firm Checker at the official address, matching the firm reference number and contact details, and check the FCA Warning List

Some firms pretend to be authorised, so use only the contact details on the Firm Checker. Never share passwords or download apps from sources you do not trust. Report suspected scams to the FCA on 0800 111 6768, and losses to Report Fraud on 0300 123 2040.

Safer habits

  • Check every firm on the FCA Firm Checker and Warning List before depositing.
  • Ask for the full record, not screenshots: every trade, including losers.
  • Paper-trade first and log the results yourself.
  • Stay a retail client so you keep negative balance protection; see can you lose more than you deposit trading on margin.
  • Write your own rules so you are not reliant on someone else's calls; see how to build a trading plan.

Is it illegal to sell trading signals in the UK?

Not automatically. But promoting regulated products or services without approval from an FCA-authorised firm may be a criminal offence, and claims must not be misleading.

Do finfluencers need FCA approval?

If they promote a regulated financial product or service, the promotion needs approval from an appropriate authorised firm, or they may be committing an offence.

How do I check if a broker is FCA authorised?

Use the FCA Firm Checker at the official FCA address, confirm the firm reference number and contact details, and check the FCA Warning List.

What is a pump and dump?

A scheme where promoters hype a stock or token with false or misleading claims, then sell their own holdings, leaving later buyers with losses.

Where do I report a trading scam?

To the FCA on 0800 111 6768, and to Report Fraud on 0300 123 2040 if you have lost money.
