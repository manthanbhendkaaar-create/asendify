What does typical retention mean in YouTube Studio?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Typical retention in YouTube Studio is your own benchmark: it compares a video's audience retention with your 10 latest videos of similar length, shown as a shaded band behind the retention line. If the line sits above the band, that part of the video held viewers better than usual for you; if it falls below, that part held them worse.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's help says you can "use typical retention to compare your 10 latest videos of similar length".
  • It is a comparison with your own channel, not with all of YouTube. The comparison with all YouTube videos of similar length is a separate view (relative retention).
  • When YouTube launched the feature in 2021 it appeared as a grey band behind the retention line, as reported at the time by Search Engine Journal.
  • Use it to answer one question per video: where did this one hold better or worse than my usual?
  • It needs data: key moments appear only on videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views, and retention data typically takes one to two days to process.

What YouTube says about typical retention

YouTube's help page on key moments for audience retention describes the report and adds: "You can also use typical retention to compare your 10 latest videos of similar length." The same wording appears on the engagement reports page. To find it, open YouTube Studio, go to Content, select Analytics on a video, and look for the audience retention report on the Overview or Engagement tab.

When the feature was introduced, Search Engine Journal reported (October 2021) that it showed as a grey band and was based on videos of similar length from the creator's channel within the past year. YouTube's current help text describes the comparison as your 10 latest videos of similar length, so go by the current wording.

The YouTube Analytics API describes relative retention as relativeRetentionPerformance: a value from 0 to 1 showing how well a video retains viewers "in comparison to all YouTube videos of similar length", where 0.5 is about average. Typical retention is the personal version of that idea.

How to read the band

  • Line above the band at the start: your opening worked better than your recent openings. Note what you did differently: the first line, the first shot, the order of the promise.
  • Line inside the band: normal for you. Not a problem, but not a lesson either.
  • Line drops below the band at one point: something in that section lost more people than usual. Watch the 15 seconds before the drop.
  • Line below the band all the way: the whole video held worse than usual. Check the title and thumbnail against the opening first; YouTube's own advice for a weak intro is to make the thumbnail and title "better reflect your video content" and to modify the first 30 seconds.
  • Line above the band near the end: viewers who stayed were more engaged than usual, often because the ending paid off a promise from the start.

Worked example

Example: a cooking channel's last 10 videos of 12 to 16 minutes usually keep about 60 to 66% of viewers at 0:30 and about 35 to 40% at the halfway point. That range is the band.

The new 14-minute video starts at 71% at 0:30, above the band, after opening on the finished dish. But at 6:40 the line falls from 48% to 33%, below the band, where an ingredient substitution tangent begins. It ends inside the band.

What to do: keep the new opening style, because it beat your usual; cut or shorten tangents like the one at 6:40 in the next edit. Then check whether the next few videos stay above the band at the start. The figures are illustrative.

Limits of typical retention

  1. It moves with you. Because it is built from your latest similar-length videos, improving your videos also raises the band. Beating it every time gets harder, which is the point.
  2. It says nothing about why. The band shows where a video differed from your usual, not the cause. You still have to watch the moment.
  3. New and small channels. With few videos of similar length, the comparison is thinner, and key moments need at least 100 views per video.
  4. It is one video at a time. The retention report is available at the video level only, so spotting patterns across many videos is manual unless you use Advanced mode or a tool.

FAQ

Is there a tool that reads my retention against my usual and tells me what to fix?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor reports each upload's drop-offs with fixes and tracks whether they work on later videos. YouTube Studio's typical retention band is the free way to compare yourself.

What does typical retention compare my video with?

YouTube says it compares your video with your 10 latest videos of similar length on your own channel.

Is typical retention the same as relative retention?

No. Typical retention compares with your own recent videos; relative retention compares with all YouTube videos of similar length.

Why can't I see typical retention or key moments?

Key moments need a video at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views, and data typically takes one to two days to process. A channel with few similar-length videos also has less to compare.

Is being inside the band good?

It means the video performed as usual for your channel. Aim to move parts of the line above the band, starting with the opening 30 seconds.
