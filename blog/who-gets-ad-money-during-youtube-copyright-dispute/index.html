Who gets the ad money while a YouTube copyright claim is disputed?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Nobody, until it is settled. If both you and the claimant want to monetise the video, YouTube keeps the ads running, holds the revenue separately, and pays it to the appropriate party once the dispute is resolved. Dispute within 5 days of the claim and the held money is counted from the day the claim was placed; dispute later and it is only held from the day you file.

Key takeaways

  • Revenue is held during a dispute only when both sides want to monetise the video.
  • Disputing within 5 days of the claim means holding starts from the day the claim was placed.
  • If you do nothing for 5 days, held revenue is released to the claimant.
  • Disputed revenue doesn't show in YouTube Analytics during the dispute; it appears later, usually between the 10th and 20th of a following month.
  • Only dispute with a valid reason: a rejected dispute can lead to a removal request and a copyright strike.

How YouTube handles the money

YouTube's page on monetisation during Content ID disputes explains that, during a dispute, revenue is held separately and later paid to "the appropriate party". The rules depend on timing:

When you disputeWhat happens to revenue
Within 5 days of the claimAds keep running; all revenue is held from the day the claim was placed
After 5 daysRevenue before your dispute has gone to the claimant; holding starts on the day you file
You never disputeAfter 5 days, held revenue is released to the claimant

The same page notes that appeals follow the same 5-day timing, that you can turn off monetisation during a dispute in YouTube Studio, and that disputed revenue appears in Analytics later, usually between the 10th and 20th of a following month. YouTube's fair use page confirms the principle: if both sides monetise a disputed video, earnings accrue and are paid to the right party once it's resolved.

When a dispute is worth it

YouTube's dispute page lists three valid grounds: you have all the necessary rights, an exception such as fair use applies, or the claim was a mistake. Giving credit, owning a copy or not monetising are not valid. After a dispute, the claimant has 30 days to release, reinstate, file a removal request, or let it expire. If they file a valid removal request, the video is removed and your channel gets a strike.

So the 5-day window is a reason to decide quickly, not a reason to dispute everything. YouTube's claims page reminds creators that removing the claimed content, by muting, trimming or replacing audio, clears the claim automatically and avoids the risk entirely.

Worked example: deciding inside 5 days

Example: on Monday a creator's tutorial gets a claim on its intro music. The creator commissioned that music and has a written licence.

  • Tuesday (day 1): the creator checks the licence, confirms it covers YouTube use, and files a dispute stating they hold the rights.
  • Effect: because the dispute is within 5 days, all revenue from Monday onwards is held, not paid to the claimant.
  • Day 12: the claimant releases the claim. The held revenue is paid to the creator and appears in Analytics in a later month.

Had the creator waited two weeks, the revenue from the first 5 days onwards would already have gone to the claimant, with holding starting only on the dispute date. The example is illustrative.

What if the claim blocks or tracks instead?

Holding revenue only applies when both sides want to monetise. YouTube's claims page explains that a rights owner can choose to block, monetise or track a video, and that the choice can differ by country:

Claim policyWhat it means for revenueYour realistic options
MonetiseOwner earns from ads unless you dispute in timeDispute within 5 days if valid, or remove the content
TrackOwner follows the video's viewing statistics rather than taking ad revenue through the claimOften fine to leave; dispute only if it's wrong
BlockVideo unavailable where blocked, so no ad revenue thereRemove the claimed content, or dispute if you hold the rights

For claimed music, the same page notes that Partner Program channels may be able to share revenue with the music publisher, which can be a middle path when you don't want to remove a song.

A quick decision guide

  1. Do you clearly hold the rights, or is it a misidentification? Dispute within 5 days.
  2. Is it a fair-use argument? Think carefully and consider advice; a rejected dispute risks a strike.
  3. No rights and no strong exception? Remove the claimed content instead.
  4. Not sure who claimed it? Check the claim details in YouTube Studio before deciding.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting too long. After 5 days, revenue already earned goes to the claimant.
  • Disputing to "save" revenue without rights. It can turn a harmless claim into a strike.
  • Panicking at missing Analytics revenue. Disputed revenue appears later, usually in a following month.
  • Forgetting the licence file. Keep licences where you can find them on day one.
  • Ignoring claims on old videos. A claim on a back-catalogue video that still earns steadily is worth the same quick check as one on a new upload.

Do I lose the money if I dispute after 5 days?

Why can't I see disputed revenue in Analytics?
