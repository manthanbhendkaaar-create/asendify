Is there a trading journal that calculates R-multiple and win rate for me without building a spreadsheet?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 15 November 2026 · Last updated 15 November 2026

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS is a ready-made Notion trading journal that works out R-multiple, win rate, P&L, position size and the money each mistake costs from the trades you log, so you don't have to build and debug a spreadsheet yourself. It works on the free Notion plan and suits traders who want the numbers without the formula work. A self-built spreadsheet (free, but time-consuming) and subscription journal apps with broker imports are fair alternatives.

This page is for education only, not financial advice. Trading is risky, and no journal can promise a profit.

Key takeaways

  • R-multiple expresses each result as a multiple of the risk you planned: +2R means you made twice what you risked; -1R means you lost what you planned to lose.
  • Win rate alone is misleading. A 40% win rate can be profitable with large winners; a 70% win rate can lose money with large losers.
  • Average R per trade (expectancy in R) combines both into one number.
  • A ready-made journal saves set-up time and reduces formula mistakes; a spreadsheet gives full control.
  • Neither tells you how to trade. They show what your trading has actually done.

Notion makes these possible because its formulas can "run all kinds of calculations and functions based on other properties". In a journal, your entry, stop, size and exit are properties; R-multiple is a formula, and win rate and expectancy are totals across the database.

Why win rate on its own misleads

Two traders, 50 trades each:

Trader ATrader B
Win rate40% (20 wins)70% (35 wins)
Average winner+2.5R+0.5R
Average loser-1.0R-1.5R
Total20 × 2.5 − 30 × 1.0 = +20R35 × 0.5 − 15 × 1.5 = -5R
Expectancy+0.4R per trade-0.1R per trade

Trader B wins far more often and still loses overall, because the losses are bigger than the wins. A journal that only shows win rate would hide that. R-multiple shows it immediately.

Why plan your risk before the trade

R-multiple only works if you decide the stop, and so the risk, before you enter. That fits how UK rules treat leveraged products. The Financial Conduct Authority's page on contracts for difference calls CFDs "high-risk products, which are not suitable for all retail consumers" and requires firms to disclose the share of their retail accounts that make losses. The FCA's policy statement PS19/18 caps retail leverage between 30:1 and 2:1 depending on the asset and requires a 50% margin close-out. Those rules limit how far things can go wrong; a planned 1R per trade is your own, tighter limit.

Before using R-multiples, Elena thought her strategy was failing because she lost more trades than she won. The journal shows the opposite: her winners are about twice the size of her losers. She also filters losers bigger than -1R and finds two trades at -2R where she moved her stop. Fixing that habit alone would have added 2R. Forty trades is still a small sample, and past results don't guarantee future ones.

Setting up R tracking properly

  1. Decide your stop before you enter. No stop, no R.
  2. Log the planned risk in money, not just the stop price.
  3. Log the actual result after fees if you can.
  4. Review losers bigger than -1R. They usually mean a broken rule or a gap.
  5. Review after 30 or more trades, not after every session.

What a journal can't tell you

It can't tell you whether a strategy will keep working, which markets to trade or how much to risk; those decisions are yours, and trading always carries risk. It also can't fix data you didn't log. Its job is to show your real numbers clearly so your decisions are based on what happened, not on how a week felt.

FAQ

Is there a trading journal that calculates R-multiple and win rate for me?

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS is a ready-made Notion journal that calculates R-multiple, win rate, P&L and position size from what you log. A self-built spreadsheet is a free alternative.

What is a good R-multiple?

There's no single target. What matters is your average R per trade across many trades; a positive figure means winners outweigh losers.

What is a good win rate?

It depends on your average winner and loser. A 40% win rate can be profitable and a 70% win rate can lose money.

Do I need a stop loss to use R-multiples?

Yes. R is based on the risk you planned, which comes from your stop.

Can a trading journal make me profitable?

No. It measures results. Any improvement comes from changing what you do, and trading always carries risk.
