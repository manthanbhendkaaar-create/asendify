Where should I put mid-roll ads so viewers don't leave my YouTube video?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Put mid-roll ad slots at natural pauses: the end of a point, a scene change or a chapter break, never in the middle of a sentence or an action. YouTube says slots at natural breaks are more likely to serve an ad because viewer retention tends to be higher there. Then check your retention graph just after each slot to see what the break cost you.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help says mid-rolls are available on monetised videos that are eight minutes or longer, and that slots placed mid-sentence or mid-action are less likely to serve ads.
  • You can use automatic slots, manual slots, or both. YouTube's mid-roll update post (March 2025) reported an average 5% revenue increase in its experiments when creators combined the two.
  • Not every slot gets an ad. YouTube's systems pick which ones to fill, balancing revenue against the viewer's experience.
  • The best place for a slot is just after a payoff and just before a new open question, so the viewer has a reason to wait through the ad.
  • Measure the cost: compare the drop just after each slot with the normal slope of your graph.

How YouTube decides which slots run

YouTube gives creators three set-ups for mid-rolls, as described on its help page:

Set-upWho picks the slotWhat YouTube doesGood for
AutomaticYouTube's systemsFinds natural breakpoints for youCreators who don't want to place slots by hand
ManualYouDecides which of your slots actually serve an adVideos with clear sections and strong opinions on pacing
Automatic + manualBothHas more options to choose fromMost long videos; YouTube reported higher average revenue in tests

The help page is clear that "ad slots are not guaranteed to serve ads". YouTube's own update post goes further: serving an ad in every slot "could make for very disruptive viewer experience". The system skips some slots on purpose.

In May 2025 YouTube changed placement so that, in its words, "we'll serve more ads at natural breakpoints and less ads on more interruptive points". The same post asks creators to avoid placing ads "abruptly in the middle of sentences, action sequences, or critical moments".

Why placement matters for retention

A mid-roll is a forced pause. Some viewers leave during or after it. You cannot stop that entirely, but you can make it cheaper in two ways.

Give the viewer a reason to come back

If the ad lands just after you've opened a question ("the third test is where it all went wrong"), the viewer has something to wait for. That is an open loop, and it works across an ad break the same way it works across a TV commercial break.

Don't cut a thought in half

An ad in the middle of a sentence breaks the thread. When the video resumes, the viewer has to rebuild context. Many won't bother. YouTube's systems also tend not to fill these slots, so you lose the revenue as well.

Keep the first few minutes clear

The steepest part of most retention graphs is the start. Viewers who are still deciding whether the video is worth it are the easiest to lose. Our guide to what the retention graph means explains why the opening carries the most weight.

Where to put slots: a simple checklist

  1. At chapter breaks. If you use chapters, each break between them is already a natural pause.
  2. After a payoff, before a new question. Close one loop, open the next, then break.
  3. At a scene or location change. Visual transitions already signal "new section".
  4. Not inside a sponsor read. Stacking an ad next to a sponsor segment doubles the interruption. Our page on whether sponsor reads hurt retention covers that segment separately.
  5. Not during a demonstration or a punchline. These are the moments viewers came for.
  6. Spread them out. Several slots close together give the system more interruptive options; leave real content between them.

How to measure what a slot costs

YouTube Studio's audience retention report is the tool. YouTube's key moments help page says dips mean viewers are abandoning or skipping at that part of the video, while a gradual decline is normal for most videos.

So for each mid-roll timestamp:

  • Find the slot on the retention graph.
  • Look at the slope in the minute before it.
  • Look at the drop across the slot and the 30 seconds after.
  • If the drop is much steeper than the slope before, that slot is costing you viewers.

Bear in mind that a slot doesn't run an ad for every viewer, and data takes a day or two to process. Look for patterns across several videos rather than judging one slot on one upload.

Example: re-placing slots on a 14-minute video

A tech channel uploads a 14-minute comparison of three laptops. Its first draft had automatic slots plus a manual slot at 2:10, in the middle of the opening test.

TimeWhat happensOriginal slotRevised slot
0:00-1:30Hook and set-upNoneNone
2:10Mid-way through laptop 1 battery testManual slotRemoved
4:45Laptop 1 verdict, then "laptop 2 surprised me"NoneManual slot added
9:20Laptop 2 verdict, then "now the cheapest one"AutomaticKept
12:30Final comparison tableNoneNone (key moment)

In the revised version, each slot sits after a verdict and before a teaser for the next section. The creator then compares the drop at 4:45 and 9:20 with the drop the old 2:10 slot caused on the earlier upload, using the retention report. If the new slots show a smaller step down, the change worked.

How long does a video need to be for mid-roll ads?

YouTube's help page says mid-rolls are available on monetised videos that are eight minutes or longer.

Should I use automatic or manual mid-rolls?

Many creators use both. Manual slots let you protect key moments; automatic slots give YouTube more natural breaks to choose from. YouTube reported higher average revenue in tests when both were combined.

Will every mid-roll slot show an ad?

No. YouTube says ad slots are not guaranteed to serve ads, and its systems deliberately leave some empty to avoid a disruptive experience.

Should I turn mid-rolls off on some videos?

YouTube's help page suggests turning them off where they don't suit the content, giving meditation videos as an example.

Do mid-rolls hurt how YouTube recommends my video?

YouTube doesn't publish a direct rule on this. What you can measure is retention: if a slot causes a steep drop, fewer people reach the rest of the video, so place slots where the drop is smallest.
