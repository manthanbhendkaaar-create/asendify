How do I promote an event with YouTube ads so people don't skip?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

To promote an event with YouTube ads, put your single best moment and the event's name in the first five seconds of a skippable in-stream ad, because viewers can skip after five seconds. Follow with one reason to attend and a clear date and link, then run six-second bumper ads to remind people who already saw it. Test two or three different openings and keep the one that holds viewers and drives ticket clicks.

Key takeaways

  • Google: in skippable in-stream ads, "Viewers can skip the ad after five seconds."
  • With CPV bidding you pay when a viewer watches 30 seconds of your video or interacts with it, whichever comes first.
  • Bumper ads are non-skippable and last between five and six seconds; non-skippable in-stream ads are 60 seconds or shorter.
  • Google's ABCDs: Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction. "Get to the heart of the story faster."
  • Google says applying the ABCDs can deliver up to a 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood, citing a 2021 Google/Kantar study; that is Google's figure, not a promise for your ad.

The first five seconds

Everything depends on what the viewer sees before the skip button appears. The Retention Editing Playbook's cold open rule fits ads exactly: start on the most interesting thing in the video. For an event, that's usually:

  • The loudest crowd moment from last year.
  • The headline speaker or act saying their strongest line.
  • A striking reveal: a stage lighting up, a product unveiled.
  • A direct question your buyer is already asking ("Still looking for a conference that isn't just sales pitches?").

Google's ABCDs of effective video ads say: "Get to the heart of the story faster, and use engaging pacing and tight framing to draw viewers in." They also say to "Introduce your brand or product from the start", so put the event name on screen early, ideally inside the hook shot rather than as a separate logo sting.

A 20-second event ad structure

  1. 0-3 s: the hook shot, event name visible.
  2. 3-5 s: the promise in text: "Two days. 40 speakers. No sales pitches."
  3. 5-14 s: proof: three quick moments, one short attendee line.
  4. 14-18 s: date and city.
  5. 18-20 s: call to action, on screen and spoken. The ABCDs advise: "Reinforce your onscreen CTA with voice-over to ensure that the next step is clear."

Use bumpers to finish the job

Bumpers are too short to sell an event to a stranger, but good for reminding someone who saw the longer ad. A simple sequence: the skippable promo to a broad audience, then bumpers to people who watched it, with one message each ("Early-bird ends Friday", "Line-up announced", "Last 100 tickets", each only when true).

Test openings, not whole ads

Make three versions of the ad that differ only in the first five seconds. Run them to the same audience with the same budget. Compare how many viewers watch past the skip point and how many click. Keep the winner, then test a new challenger against it. The rest of the ad can stay the same.

For your organic recap on YouTube, the key moments report shows the percentage still watching after the first 30 seconds, plus dips. Use those dips to decide which moments to keep out of the ad.

Example: a regional conference campaign

Example: a two-day regional marketing conference with a ticket price of £295 runs a four-week YouTube campaign.

  • Week 1: three 20-second skippable ads with different openings: a crowd laugh, the keynote speaker's boldest line, and a question on screen. The speaker-line version keeps the most viewers past five seconds and gets the most ticket-page clicks.
  • Weeks 2-3: the winner runs to the broad audience; bumpers ("Early-bird ends 31 May") run to people who watched at least part of the ad.
  • Week 4: a new challenger opening is tested against the winner; bumpers switch to "Tickets still available" (true at the time).

The team judges the campaign by tickets sold from YouTube (tracked through the ticket page), not by views.

Common mistakes

  • Opening with the venue exterior or a logo animation.
  • A promise vaguer than the event ("An amazing experience").
  • No date, or a date shown only at the very end.
  • Fake urgency. UK ad rules require availability and time-limit claims to be true; the ASA's availability guidance covers this.
  • Judging by views alone.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches cold opens and a 30-second scorecard for video?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook teaches cold opens, intro killers and a 30-second scorecard. It doesn't cover ad buying; Google Ads Help is a free alternative for that.

Do I pay if someone skips my ad?

With CPV bidding, Google says you pay when a viewer watches 30 seconds or interacts, whichever comes first. Impression-based bidding works differently.

How long should a YouTube event ad be?

Long enough to hook, prove and give the date. 15 to 30 seconds is common; bumpers are 6 seconds.

Should I use non-skippable ads?

They can work for short messages to warm audiences, but they cost per impression and can annoy viewers. Test against skippable.

Can I reuse my recap as an ad?

Yes, after re-cutting it so the best moment and the event name are in the first five seconds.

What should I measure?

Views past five seconds, clicks to the ticket page and tickets sold, compared across openings.
