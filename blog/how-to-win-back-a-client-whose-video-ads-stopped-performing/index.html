How do I win back a client whose video ads stopped performing?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Start by finding out why the ads stopped working before you pitch anything: check tracking, audience frequency and creative fatigue, changes to the offer or landing page, and seasonality. Then go back to the client with a short, honest diagnosis and a plan: two or three new concepts with fresh hooks, a test schedule with clear success metrics, and what you'll report each week. Clients come back to agencies that explain what happened, not ones that promise a quick fix.

Key takeaways

  • Most "the ads stopped working" problems have one of five causes: tracking, fatigue, offer, landing page or market. Only one of them is fixed by new edits.
  • Overexposure is a common culprit. Google's frequency capping limits how often ads appear to the same person, and showing the same ad too often to a small audience wears it out.
  • Use the platforms' testing tools to rebuild trust with evidence: Google's video experiments compare different video ads with the same audience, and TikTok's split tests pick a winner only at a 90% confidence rate.
  • New creative should follow a clear framework such as Google's ABCDs: hook and sustain attention with an immersive story, brand early, connect, and direct to action.
  • Don't promise results. Promise a process, a timeline and transparent reporting.

Diagnose first: the five causes

Tracking brokeConversions drop to near zero overnight; sales didn'tCheck pixel, tags, consent settingsNo

Offer changedPrice rise, discount ended, stock issuesAgree an offer test with the clientPartly
Landing page changedClicks steady, conversions fallCompare page versions, speed, formNo
Market or seasonCompetitors louder, seasonal dipAdjust budget and expectationsPartly

What to bring to the meeting

  1. A one-page diagnosis. What happened, when, and the evidence. Charts of hook rate, frequency and cost per result over time.
  2. Own your part. If the creative was left running too long, say so.
  3. Three new concepts. Each with two or three hook variants and a rough storyboard.
  4. A six-week test plan. Concept test, then hook test, then format, with the success metric for each.
  5. A reporting promise. Weekly short update: what ran, what won, what's next.
  6. A low-risk start. A short trial period or a smaller first batch so the client isn't asked for a big commitment straight away.

Example: an e-commerce skincare client

Example: an agency's skincare client cut spend after cost per purchase doubled over two months. The agency's diagnosis shows the same two ads had run for 14 weeks to a small retargeting audience, with frequency steadily climbing and hook rate halving, and the landing page had also lost its review section in a site redesign. The agency presents this honestly, admits the ads should have been refreshed sooner, and proposes three new concepts (texture close-up, dermatologist explainer, before-and-after morning routine with honest claims), a six-week test plan and a frequency cap. The client agrees to a one-month trial and restores the reviews on the landing page. By week 4, cost per purchase is back close to where it was. Figures and details are illustrative.

What not to do

  • Blame the platform or the algorithm without evidence.
  • Pitch the same ads with new music.
  • Promise a specific return on ad spend.
  • Disappear for weeks after winning the trial.

FAQ

Is there a set of hook templates for refreshing stale video ads?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook includes hook templates and a tracker. Google's free ABCDs are an alternative framework.

How do I know if it's creative fatigue?

Frequency rising while hook rate and click-through fall over several weeks is the usual pattern.

Should I offer a discount to win the client back?

A short trial or smaller first batch lowers risk without cutting your rate. Be clear about what's included.

How fast can performance recover?

It depends on the cause and budget. Tests need time to reach confident results, so plan in weeks, not days.

What if the problem isn't the ads?

Say so. Showing a tracking or landing page issue builds trust, even if it isn't your work to fix.
