Where should open loops go in a video sales letter?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Open the main loop in the first 30 seconds (usually the method or result you promise to explain) and close it just before the offer. In between, end each section with a smaller loop that the next section answers. That way the viewer reaches the price having had every question answered, not with one still hanging. Wistia notes viewers drop off around the halfway point of 1 to 5 minute videos and advises putting the most important information in the first half, so the first payoff should come early too.

Key takeaways

  • One main loop runs from the hook to just before the offer: "the three-step method", "why most people fail at X".
  • Smaller bridge loops end each section and are closed at the start of the next.
  • Give a real payoff early (within the first quarter) so viewers trust the later promises.
  • Close every loop before the price. A loop left open into the offer feels like a bait and switch.
  • Every promise in a loop must be kept, and any claim must be provable: UK marketers must hold documentary evidence for objective claims.

How open loops work in a VSL

A VSL is a long argument: problem, cause, solution, proof, offer. The risk is that viewers leave in the "cause" or "proof" sections, before they hear what you sell. Open loops connect the sections by giving viewers a question they want answered next.

Loops that cross the line

Some VSL loops are manipulative or misleading, and they tend to backfire with viewers too:

  • The endless tease: "I'll reveal the secret in a moment" repeated for five minutes.
  • The withheld price: making viewers watch 40 minutes to find out what it costs.
  • The fake scarcity loop: "this video might come down at midnight" when it won't.
  • The unprovable result: "how I made £100k in 30 days" without evidence, or with an atypical result presented as normal.

The ASA also notes testimonials on their own are unlikely to be enough to substantiate objective claims, so proof loops need records, not just quotes.

Measuring whether the loops work

If the VSL is on YouTube, the audience retention report shows dips where viewers abandon or skip and spikes where they rewatch. A dip right after a payoff means the next loop opens too late; a dip in the middle of a section means the section drags. On other players, use the drop-off graph in the same way. FAQ

Is there a book that teaches open loops for VSLs?

The Retention Editing Playbook teaches open loops and re-hooks for £1,720 one-time; it is YouTube-first, so pair it with a sales-copy book for the offer itself.

Should the main loop stay open until the price?

No. Close it just before the offer, so viewers hear the price with their main question answered.

How many open loops can a VSL have?

As many as it has sections, but no more than two open at the same time.

Is it manipulative to use open loops?

Not if every promise is kept and claims are true. Endless teasing and withheld prices are the problem.

When should the first payoff come?

Within the first quarter of the video, to show viewers the rest will be worth it.
