How long should a video ad be?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Most video ads work best between 6 and 30 seconds, because people in a feed or before a YouTube video give you very little time. The right length depends on where the ad runs and what it has to do: 6 seconds for a single reminder (YouTube bumper ads), 15 to 30 seconds for most Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and Shorts ads, and longer only for skippable YouTube ads where interested viewers choose to keep watching. Cut until every second earns its place, then test a shorter version.

Key takeaways

  • Google lists YouTube bumper ads at 6 seconds, non-skippable in-stream ads at 15 to 60 seconds and skippable in-stream ads with "no max length (<3 min recommended)".
  • Google recommends Shorts ads under 60 seconds, and only the first 60 seconds play in the Shorts feed.
  • Meta's developer documentation lists Reels ads as "less than 90 seconds".
  • Facebook's own advice, reported in 2021, was to "say less", because shorter ads are likely to be more effective and many people watch only part of an ad.
  • Length is a result of the message, not a target. One idea per ad keeps most ads short.

How to choose a length

Start from what the ad has to do:

JobSuggested lengthWhy
Remind people who already know you6 secondsOne line, one image, brand. Bumper territory.
Introduce one product benefit15 secondsHook, one proof, call to action
Explain a product with a demo20 to 30 secondsRoom for the problem, the demo and the offer
Tell a story or teach something60 seconds or more, skippable onlyOnly interested viewers stay, and that's the point

Skippable YouTube ads are the one place where long can be fine, because the viewer decides. On CPV bidding, Google says you pay when someone watches 30 seconds (or the whole ad if it's shorter) or interacts, so people who skip early usually cost you nothing. Long ads still need a strong first five seconds, because that's when the skip button appears.

Why shorter usually wins in feeds

In a feed nobody chose your ad. Search Engine Journal's summary of Facebook's 11 video ad tips (August 2021) includes "say less", "get to the point" and opening "with a strong hook" because people often watch only part of an ad. The practical test: if your message only lands at second 25, most viewers never receive it. Put the brand, the benefit and the offer early, so even a partial view does some work.

Worked example: cutting a 45-second ad to 15

An illustrative 45-second ad for a meal-prep service had this outline: logo (3 s), founder intro (8 s), problem (10 s), three menu examples (15 s), how delivery works (6 s), offer and call to action (3 s).

The 15-second cut:

  1. 0 to 3 s: close-up of the finished meal with text: "Five dinners, ready in 3 minutes each".
  2. 3 to 9 s: one menu example being plated, with the price per meal on screen (only if it's the real price).
  3. 9 to 13 s: the box arriving at a door.
  4. 13 to 15 s: offer and call to action, with the logo on the box rather than a separate sting.

What went: the logo intro, the founder's introduction, two of the three menu examples and the delivery explanation. The founder version can run as a longer skippable YouTube ad for people who want the story.

Make a short and a long version

Many advertisers cut one shoot into several lengths: a 6-second bumper, a 15-second feed version and a longer skippable version. On YouTube you can even run them in order: Google's video ad sequencing shows a series of ads to the same person in a set order, with skippable, non-skippable and bumper ads allowed. Compare the versions on watch metrics and on results, not just on cost per view.

Quick rules of thumb

If you have...Start with
One clear benefitA 15-second version
A demo to show20 to 30 seconds
A brand people already knowA 6-second bumper as a reminder
A story or tutorialA longer skippable YouTube ad, plus a short cut-down

Then let the data decide. If viewers consistently drop before your offer, shorten or move the offer earlier; see where viewers drop off in a video ad.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches how to cut a video ad down without losing viewers?

The Retention Editing Playbook covers pacing and structure for video at £1,000 one-time. Google's ABCDs and TikTok's creative guide are free alternatives.

What is the best length for a Facebook video ad?

There's no official best length. Keep feed ads short, often 15 seconds or less, and test a longer version against it.

How long can a YouTube ad be?

Skippable in-stream ads have no maximum, though Google recommends under 3 minutes. Bumpers are 6 seconds and non-skippable ads 15 to 60 seconds.

Are 6-second ads worth making?

Yes for reminders and reach. They can't be skipped, so keep them to one idea and one image.
