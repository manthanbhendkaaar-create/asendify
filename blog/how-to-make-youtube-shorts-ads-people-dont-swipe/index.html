How do I make YouTube Shorts ads people don't swipe away?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

To stop people swiping past a YouTube Shorts ad, make it vertical 9:16 and under 60 seconds, open on movement or a person mid-sentence, put the promise on screen in the first couple of seconds, use sound (music, voiceover or both), and make it feel like a Short rather than a TV ad. Viewers can swipe away immediately, so there's no five-second grace period: the first frame has to earn the next one. Google recommends authentic, creator-style assets for this format.

Key takeaways

  • Google: "Vertical videos, with a 9:16 aspect ratio, are best suited for the Shorts format."
  • Google recommends Shorts ads under 60 seconds; only the first 60 seconds play in the Shorts feed.
  • Viewers can skip Shorts ads immediately by swiping, according to Google's ad formats guide.
  • On CPV bidding, a Shorts TrueView view counts at 10 seconds or the end of the ad, or a CTA click, like, comment or share.
  • Google recommends using sound and "authentic creator assets" when you can.

Google also says sound has been "shown to increase conversions by over 20%", without naming a source on that page.

How Shorts ad views are counted

Google's ad formats guide says users can skip Shorts ads immediately by swiping. On CPV bidding, a TrueView view counts when someone watches 10 seconds or to the end, whichever comes first, or clicks the call to action or likes, comments or shares. Only one view is charged per impression. On Target CPM, you pay per impression from when the ad starts playing. Google's view metrics guide adds that for Shorts, an engagement counts at 5 seconds of watching or a CTA click, like, comment or share.

So the useful targets are: stop the swipe in the first second, hold to 5 seconds, then to 10.

Common reasons Shorts ads get swiped

  • Landscape TV ad dropped into a vertical feed with blurred bars
  • Logo intro or slow establishing shot
  • No sound, although Google recommends music, voiceover or both
  • Text in areas covered by the Shorts interface
  • A message that only makes sense at second 15

Shorts ad checklist before you launch

Run through this list for every Shorts ad. Most swipe problems come from one of these.

CheckWhy it matters
Is it 9:16, full screen?Horizontal ads show with blurred bars and look out of place
Is something moving or speaking in the first frame?Viewers can swipe immediately
Is the promise on screen by second 2 or 3?Cold viewers need a reason to stay
Is there sound (music, voice or both)?Google recommends it for Shorts ads
Are captions burned in and readable on a phone?Some viewers still watch muted
Is key text away from the bottom and right edge?The Shorts interface covers those areas
Does it work under 60 seconds?Only the first 60 seconds play in the Shorts feed
Is the call to action clear without relying on the button?The CTA button appears 3 or 10 seconds in, depending on campaign type
Do you have a 40-character headline and 90-character description?Google recommends both for the mobile CTA card

Make it look like a Short. The best-performing Shorts ads often look like content a creator would post: a person talking to the phone, a quick demo, a reaction. Google's advice to "leverage authentic creator assets" points this way, and it also recommends partnering with creators and using Brand Partner Access to boost organic creator Shorts.

Plan for the 5 and 10-second marks. Engagements count at 5 seconds and CPV views at 10 seconds. Structure the ad so there's a small payoff around 5 seconds (the first proof point) and a reason to stay to 10 (the main result still to come).

Watch your ratios. Compare views per impression and clicks per impression across versions. If one version wins on views but loses on clicks, its hook may be attracting curious viewers rather than buyers. For testing, see how to A/B test video ad hooks.

Refresh regularly. Short-form ads can tire quickly. When views per impression start falling as frequency rises, launch a new opening on the same body; see how to tell if a video ad has creative fatigue.
