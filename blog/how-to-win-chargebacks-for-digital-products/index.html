How do I win chargebacks for digital products and online courses?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 15 November 2026 · Last updated 15 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

To win a chargeback on a digital product or online course, respond before the deadline (usually 7 to 21 days) with short, relevant evidence: proof the cardholder made the purchase, logs showing they downloaded or logged in to the content, a screenshot of the terms and refund policy they agreed to at checkout, and any messages with them. Even strong cases are hard to win, so preventing disputes matters as much as fighting them.

Key takeaways

  • Card networks usually give customers 120 days to dispute; you get about 7 to 21 days to respond once a chargeback opens.
  • For digital goods, Stripe says to include an IP address or system log proving the customer downloaded or used the product.
  • Fraud claims make up over half of all disputes, so proof of authorisation (3D Secure, matching IP, past purchases) is central.
  • Keep evidence short and specific to the dispute reason. Card issuers do not read full terms of service.
  • In the UK, a buyer only loses the 14-day right to cancel digital content if they gave express consent to immediate access and acknowledged losing that right.

This page is general information, not legal advice.

How a chargeback works

Stripe's guide to how disputes work sets out the cycle. When a cardholder disputes a payment, the disputed amount plus a dispute fee comes out of your balance straight away. You cannot refund outside the dispute while it is open. You usually have 7 to 21 days to respond, depending on the card network, and the issuer then usually takes 60 to 75 days to decide. The whole process can take two to three months. The decision belongs to the cardholder's bank, not Stripe.

The dispute received fee is not refunded even if you win, unless your contract says otherwise. If you counter a dispute, Stripe adds a separate countered fee, which it returns if you win.

What evidence wins for digital products

Stripe's dispute evidence best practices give clear guidance. For digital goods, include evidence such as an IP address or system log proving the customer downloaded the content or used the service. For returns or refunds, include a clean screenshot of how your terms appear at checkout, with the relevant policy highlighted, not the whole policy.

Dispute reasonBest evidence for a course or download
Fraudulent ("I didn't make this purchase")3D Secure result, purchase IP matching the customer's location, same email used for logins, earlier undisputed purchases

Stripe also notes that fraudulent disputes are over half of all disputes, and that for Visa fraud disputes you need at least one item meeting Visa's "Compelling Evidence" standard. Under Visa CE 3.0, showing earlier undisputed purchases by the same customer with matching details can significantly improve your chances.

How to present it

The same Stripe page is blunt: issuers review thousands of responses and will not dig through long files.

  • Put evidence in date order and group it by type.
  • Write a short, neutral summary at the top: who bought what, when, and why the claim is wrong.
  • Upload only the relevant section of your terms, with the key line boxed or arrowed.
  • Combine all customer messages into one file.
  • Use PDF, JPEG or PNG; keep combined files under 4.5MB and under the page limit.
  • Use 12-point text or larger and avoid colour highlighting.

Even then, Stripe's own figures show how hard it is: its highest win-likelihood score corresponds to about a 60% chance of winning, and the lowest to about 5%.

The UK cancellation rule for digital content

Many course sellers rely on a "no refunds once accessed" policy. In the UK, that only holds if you follow regulation 37 of the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013. A buyer loses the 14-day right to cancel digital content only if supply began after they gave express consent to immediate access and acknowledged that they would lose the right to cancel. In practice, that means an unticked checkbox at checkout with clear wording, plus a confirmation email. A screenshot of that checkbox is strong chargeback evidence.

Worked example

Example: Dev sells a £199 video course through Stripe. A customer disputes the charge 40 days later as "product not received".

Evidence itemWhat Dev includes
Summary"Customer bought Course X on 3 March, logged in 11 times between 3 and 20 March and completed 14 of 22 lessons. The claim that it was not received is not correct."
ReceiptStripe receipt with date and amount
Access logTable of login dates, times and IP addresses, matching the purchase IP's city

MessagesOne email from the customer on 10 March asking about lesson 6

The pack is six pages. Dev responds on day 4 of a 14-day window. He also adds the course name and his support email to his card statement descriptor, so future buyers recognise the charge.

Prevent disputes in the first place

  • Use a clear statement descriptor and send an instant receipt with a support contact.
  • Reply to refund requests quickly; a refund costs less than a dispute fee plus the time.
  • Turn on 3D Secure for higher-risk payments.
  • Record logins, downloads and progress by user, with dates and IPs.
  • For subscriptions, send renewal reminders and make cancelling easy.

FAQ

Can I win a chargeback if the customer used the course?

Often, yes, if you can prove it. Login records, lesson progress and download logs tied to the buyer's account are the core evidence for digital products.

Should I just refund instead?

If the amount is small or the customer has a fair point, refunding before a dispute opens is usually cheaper. Once a dispute opens, you cannot refund outside the process.

Does "no refunds" in my terms stop chargebacks?

No. Customers can still dispute with their bank. Clear terms the buyer actively agreed to are evidence, not a block.

How long do I have to respond?

Usually 7 to 21 days after the chargeback opens, depending on the card network. Respond early; you normally get one chance to submit evidence.

Do PayPal disputes work the same way?

No. Stripe notes that local payment methods such as PayPal and Klarna run their own dispute processes, often with up to 180 days to file.
