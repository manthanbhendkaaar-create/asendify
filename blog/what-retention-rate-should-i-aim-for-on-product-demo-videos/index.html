What engagement rate should I expect on a product demo video?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Around half is a fair starting point for short demos: Wistia reports that product videos under a minute average a 50% engagement rate on its platform, meaning viewers watch about half on average. Longer demos usually score lower as a percentage, so judge them by whether viewers reach the key feature and the call to action, and by beating your own previous demos rather than a single industry number.

Key takeaways

  • Wistia's data: videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate, with 50% for product videos; it recommends 1 to 5 minutes for videos that educate or explain.
  • Amazon's seller guide tells brands to "showcase your product in action" and keep videos short and concise.
  • Google's ABCDs advise introducing the product early and throughout rather than saving it for the end.
  • On YouTube, compare a demo with typical retention for your 10 latest videos of similar length, because a 4-minute demo and a 30-second Short can't share a target.
  • The number that matters most for sales is often "percentage who reached the key moment", not average engagement.

A structure for a 90-second demo

  1. 0 to 5 s: the result. The product solving the problem: the invoice sent, the photo edited, the room tidy.
  2. 5 to 15 s: the problem. One line on the pain your viewer has.
  3. 15 to 60 s: the hero feature. Three steps, each with a label, at real speed but with dead time cut.
  4. 60 to 80 s: two supporting features. Quick, one line each.
  5. 80 to 90 s: the next step. What to click and what happens next.

Opening on the result works like an open loop: viewers have seen the outcome, so they stay to see how it's done.

Example: a bookkeeping app's demo

Example: a bookkeeping app's 4-minute demo has a 28% average engagement and most viewers leave in the first 40 seconds, during a founder introduction and a login screen. The team cuts a 75-second version: it opens on a receipt photo becoming a categorised expense in two taps, then shows bank sync and VAT summary, then "Start free trial". On the product page, the short version averages 49% engagement, close to Wistia's figure for product videos, and more viewers reach the trial button. The full 4-minute version stays on the help centre, now with chapters. Figures are illustrative.

How to measure it

  • Video host analytics (Wistia, Vimeo, YouTube) show engagement and a drop-off graph.
  • Mark the key moment (hero feature, call to action) and check what share of viewers reach it.
  • Track clicks after the video, since a demo's job is the next step.
  • Test one change at a time: a new opening, a shorter cut, captions.

FAQ

Is there a book on opening videos so people keep watching?

Yes. The Retention Editing Playbook covers hooks and open loops with templates. Wistia's free guide and Google's ABCDs are alternatives.

What is a good engagement rate for a product video?

Wistia reports an average of 50% for product videos under a minute. Longer demos usually score lower as a percentage.

How long should a product demo be?

Under 90 seconds for product pages and social; 1 to 5 minutes for a walkthrough, in line with Wistia's guidance.

Should the demo start with the company story?

No. Start with the product solving the problem, as Amazon and Google's advice both suggest.

Why is my demo's engagement low?

Check the first 10 seconds and any login or loading screens. Those are the usual drop points.
