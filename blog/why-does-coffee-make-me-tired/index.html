Why does coffee make me tired?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Coffee does not make you tired directly. Caffeine blocks adenosine, the chemical that builds sleep pressure, and when it wears off that pressure hits you at once. Tolerance, overnight withdrawal and sleep lost to late caffeine can also leave you feeling more tired than before your cup, especially if you drink coffee all day.

Key takeaways

  • Caffeine hides tiredness rather than removing it. Adenosine keeps building while caffeine blocks it.
  • Regular users can feel sleepy as a withdrawal symptom until their next dose.
  • Late caffeine reduces sleep, which makes you more tired the next day, which makes you drink more.
  • Tolerance means your usual coffee may no longer cover the tiredness you actually have.
  • The fix is usually less caffeine, earlier in the day, plus better sleep, not a stronger cup.

This page is general information, not medical advice. If you feel exhausted most days despite enough sleep, see a doctor or GP, as tiredness can have many medical causes.

Reason 1: caffeine hides tiredness, it does not remove it

The Sleep Foundation explains that adenosine is a sleep-promoting chemical produced in the brain while you are awake. Its level rises the longer you stay up, which is what creates sleep pressure. Caffeine works by blocking adenosine receptors, so you feel alert even though the pressure is still building.

When the caffeine wears off, the receptors are free again and all that built-up adenosine can act. That is why the drop in energy a few hours after coffee can feel sudden. You were tired all along; the caffeine was just hiding it.

Reason 2: you are in withdrawal before your first cup

If you drink caffeine every day, your body adapts. The Sleep Foundation notes that sleepiness can be a symptom of caffeine withdrawal, so regular users may feel drowsy in the morning until their next dose.

A review of the research found withdrawal symptoms include drowsiness, tiredness, low energy and low alertness. They start 12 to 24 hours after the last dose, and can appear even in people who usually have only about 100 mg a day (Juliano and Griffiths, 2004). So some of the tiredness you blame on coffee is really the gap between coffees. Our article on whether your morning coffee is just reversing withdrawal explores this.

Reason 3: caffeine is costing you sleep

This is the big one for many people. A 2023 meta-analysis of 24 studies found caffeine reduced total sleep time by about 45 minutes and deep sleep by about 11 minutes (Gardiner et al., 2023). It calculated that a standard coffee with about 107 mg of caffeine should be drunk at least 8.8 hours before bed to avoid losing sleep.

The Sleep Foundation describes the loop: caffeine use can make sleep loss worse, sleep loss causes sleepiness the next day, and people then drink more caffeine to cope. Over time, the daytime sleepiness from ongoing sleep loss can become something caffeine cannot overcome.

Stage of the loopWhat happens
1. Afternoon coffeeYou get through the slump
2. Lighter, shorter sleepCaffeine is still in your system at bedtime
3. Tired morningMore sleep pressure than usual
4. More coffeeYou need extra just to feel normal
5. RepeatTolerance rises and sleep gets worse

Reason 4: tolerance

Regular caffeine use builds tolerance, so the same dose has less effect. If you are short of sleep and tolerant, your normal coffee may simply not be enough to mask your tiredness any more, so it can feel as if coffee "does nothing" or even makes you sleepy. Our guide to resetting your caffeine tolerance shows how to bring your baseline down.

Reason 5: the timing and the rest of your routine

Other things can play a part:

  • Drinking coffee in place of food. Skipping breakfast and running on coffee can leave you flat by late morning.
  • Large, sweet coffee drinks. Big syrup-heavy drinks are more of a dessert than a coffee; some people notice a dip after them.
  • The natural afternoon dip. Many people feel a slump in the early afternoon anyway, and a coffee wearing off at the same time makes it worse. Our guide on avoiding the afternoon caffeine crash has practical fixes.

Example: a week of breaking the loop

Example: Chris goes to bed at midnight and wakes at 7 am. He drinks coffee at 7:30 am, 10 am, 1 pm and 4 pm, about 100 mg each, so around 400 mg a day. He still feels tired and often nods off on the sofa at 9 pm, then lies awake later.

His 4 pm coffee is only 8 hours before bed, short of the 8.8 hour guide for a standard coffee. His total is right at the 400 mg daily figure that the FDA and EFSA give for healthy adults.

His plan:

WeekChangeNew daily total
1Drop the 4 pm coffee; walk outside insteadabout 300 mg
2Make the 1 pm coffee half-cafabout 250 mg
3Delay the first coffee until after breakfastabout 250 mg, better spaced

By week three, his last caffeine is about 11 hours before bed, comfortably past the 8.8 hours. Because he cut down gradually, he avoids the worst withdrawal headaches. If he still feels exhausted after better sleep, that is a reason to see a GP rather than add coffee back.

When tiredness is not about coffee

Persistent tiredness can be a sign of many things, from iron deficiency and thyroid problems to depression or sleep apnoea. If you sleep seven to nine hours and still feel exhausted, or you fall asleep in the day without meaning to, get it checked by a doctor.

FAQ

Why does coffee make me tired right after I drink it?

Caffeine usually takes a while to take effect, so tiredness straight after a cup is often the sleep pressure you already had. If you drink coffee every day, you may also be in mild withdrawal until it kicks in.

Why does coffee make me tired but energy drinks do not?

Energy drinks often contain more caffeine and sugar, so they may mask tiredness more strongly for a while. That does not mean they are better for you; the same sleep and tolerance rules apply.

Can coffee make you tired if you have ADHD?

Some people with ADHD report feeling calmer or sleepier after caffeine. Responses vary, so if you have ADHD or take medication for it, talk to your doctor about caffeine.

Does decaf make you tired?

Decaf has very little caffeine, so it will not mask tiredness much. Switching to it can make existing sleep pressure more noticeable for a few days, especially if it causes mild withdrawal.

How long does it take for caffeine tolerance to go down?

It varies, but withdrawal symptoms usually ease within 2 to 9 days. Many people feel caffeine work more strongly again after a week or two of lower intake.
