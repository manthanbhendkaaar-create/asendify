Which anime are about social anxiety?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

The best-known anime about social anxiety are Bocchi the Rock!, about a painfully shy guitarist who joins a band, and Komi Can't Communicate, about a popular girl with a communication disorder trying to make 100 friends. Both show change in small, awkward steps rather than overnight. ASCEND, a free original anime by Asendify, follows a quiet student building confidence one technique at a time.

Anime can be comforting, but it is not treatment. If social anxiety is affecting your daily life, the NHS says you can refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies in England without seeing a GP. If you are in crisis, call 999 or Samaritans free on 116 123.

Key takeaways

  • MyAnimeList describes Bocchi the Rock!'s lead as "lonely and socially anxious Hitori 'Bocchi' Gotou", who spends her time playing guitar until she is recruited into a band.
  • In Komi Can't Communicate, Shouko Komi has "crippling anxiety and a communication disorder" and aims to "make one hundred friends during her time in high school", per MyAnimeList.
  • Both first seasons are 12 episodes, so they are easy to start.
  • The NHS calls social anxiety disorder "a long-term and overwhelming fear of social situations" that is "more than shyness".
  • The most useful thing to take from these shows is the idea of small, repeated steps, which also underpins proper treatment.

Bocchi the Rock!

Hitori Gotou spends her time alone playing guitar and posting online, too anxious to talk to anyone in person. When drummer Nijika Ijichi recruits her for Kessoku Band, she has to face exactly the situations she fears: meeting new people, performing and even taking a part-time job.

What it gets right: Bocchi's anxiety is played for laughs, but the show is kind to her. She does not become confident overnight. She improves through repeated exposure, with friends who accept her awkwardness. It suits viewers who feel safest behind a skill and want to see someone use that skill as a bridge to people.

Komi Can't Communicate

Shouko Komi looks like the most popular girl at school, admired from a distance. In reality, she can barely speak to anyone. Her classmate Hitohito Tadano notices, and helps her work towards her goal of making 100 friends, one conversation at a time, often through writing first.

What it gets right: It shows that people who seem calm can be struggling inside, and that tiny steps, like writing a note before speaking, count. It suits viewers who freeze in conversation and want a gentle, low-stakes story.

What these shows get right about social anxiety

The NHS lists symptoms such as dreading everyday tasks like starting conversations or phoning, avoiding group settings, fearing embarrassment, and physical signs like sweating, trembling and a racing heart. Both Bocchi and Komi show versions of these.

The shows exaggerate for comedy. Real social anxiety is often quieter and lonelier, and it rarely resolves just because the right friend appears.

How to turn watching into a small step

  1. Pick one scene that felt familiar. Write down what the character feared.
  2. Choose a tiny real-life version. Komi writes before she speaks; you could reply to a group chat instead of staying silent.
  3. Do it once this week. Small, specific and scheduled beats waiting to feel ready.
  4. Notice what actually happened. The NHS suggests keeping a diary; the real outcome is usually milder than the fear.
  5. Repeat and step up slowly. Bocchi moves from practising alone, to the band, to a stage.

Shyness vs social anxiety

The NHS is clear that social anxiety disorder is "more than shyness". Shyness might make you quiet at a party; social anxiety can make you skip the party, the seminar and the phone call, then worry about them for days. Signs it may be more than shyness:

  • You avoid things you want or need to do because of fear of judgement.
  • The worry starts days before a social event and lasts long after.
  • Physical symptoms, such as a racing heart or trembling, are strong enough to notice.
  • It is affecting your studies, work, friendships or confidence.

When to get help

Watching relatable characters can make you feel less alone, but it does not replace support. The NHS describes cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) as often regarded as the leading treatment for social anxiety, and adults in England can refer themselves to NHS Talking Therapies. See a GP if anxiety is affecting your work, studies or relationships.
