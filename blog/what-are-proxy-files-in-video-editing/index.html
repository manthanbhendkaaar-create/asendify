What are proxy files in video editing, and should you use them?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Proxy files are lightweight, lower-resolution copies of your footage that your editing software uses during editing so playback stays smooth, then swaps back to the full-quality originals when you export. They're worth using whenever 4K, high-bitrate or RAW footage makes your timeline stutter, especially on a laptop. Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve and Final Cut Pro can all create proxies in a few clicks.

Key takeaways

  • Adobe describes a proxy as "a low-resolution copy of your media" that's "easier to play back for your system."
  • In Premiere Pro, "the full resolution media is always used" on export, even if you edited with proxies.
  • Final Cut Pro makes proxies in ProRes 422 Proxy or HEVC at 12.5% to 100% of the original frame size.
  • In Final Cut Pro, switch back to original or optimized media before sharing, Apple says.
  • A common DaVinci Resolve setting is quarter-resolution ProRes 422 Proxy, as one university guide recommends.

Why proxies help

Editing software has to decode every frame you play. Modern camera and phone footage, especially 4K and above, is often heavily compressed or very large, and decoding it in real time can overwhelm a laptop. A proxy is a smaller file in an edit-friendly format, so scrubbing, cutting and playing back become smooth. When you export, the software goes back to the original files.

Adobe also notes proxies take up less storage, which helps when working on the road.

Premiere Pro

Adobe's proxy media tutorial shows two ways:

  1. During ingest: turn on Ingest in the Media Browser, open its settings and choose Create Proxies. Adobe Media Encoder builds them in the background.
  2. For media already in the project: right-click in the Project panel and choose Create Proxies….

Add the Toggle Proxies button to the Source monitor using the Button Editor to switch between proxies and full resolution with one click. Exports always use full resolution.

Final Cut Pro

Apple's guide to creating optimized and proxy files explains:

OptionFormatPurpose
Optimized mediaApple ProRes 422Better editing performance and render times, better colour for compositing
Proxy mediaProRes 422 Proxy or HEVC, 12.5%-100% of original sizeFaster editing, much less storage

Tick Create proxy media in the Media Import window, or Control-click clips and choose Transcode Media. Switch between original, optimized and proxy in the viewer's View menu. Apple warns: "If you use proxy media for playback, make sure to switch back to optimized/original media before sharing your project."

DaVinci Resolve

A Moody College of Communication guide on the University of Texas wiki gives these steps:

  1. In Project Settings > Master Settings, set Proxy media resolution to Quarter and Proxy media format to ProRes 422 Proxy.
  2. Set where proxies are saved in Working Folders.
  3. Select clips in the Media Pool, right-click and choose Generate Proxy Media.
  4. In the viewer, choose Prefer Proxies; clips with proxies show a PXY badge.
  5. Choose Disable All Proxies to go back to originals.

Our page on whether free DaVinci Resolve is good enough for YouTube covers the free version more broadly.

Storage and organisation tips

Proxies are extra files, so decide where they live before you start. Keep them in a clearly named folder next to the project or on the same external drive as the footage, so the links don't break if you move the project to another computer. When a project is finished and exported, you can delete the proxies to free space; the originals stay untouched and the software can regenerate proxies later if you reopen the edit. If you work across two machines, copying the proxies instead of the full footage can make a laptop edit practical.

When you don't need proxies

SituationProxies needed?
1080p footage on a recent computerUsually not
4K phone footage on a laptopOften helpful

Quick edits on footage that already plays smoothlyNo
Final colour grade and export checksUse originals

Proxies speed up editing, but they don't change your export settings, which should be based on your originals.

Do proxies lower the quality of my final video?

No, as long as you export from the originals. Premiere Pro always uses full resolution on export; in Final Cut, switch back to original media before sharing.

What resolution should proxies be?

Quarter or half resolution is common; Final Cut offers 12.5% to 100%.

What format should proxies use?

ProRes 422 Proxy is a common choice; Final Cut also offers HEVC.

Can I delete proxy files after the project?

Yes. In Final Cut, deleting generated files doesn't touch your originals, so you can regenerate them later.

Do I need proxies for 1080p?

Usually not on a recent computer, unless playback stutters.
