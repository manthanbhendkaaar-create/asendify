What is YouTube's "Jump ahead" feature, and what does it mean for your retention?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

"Jump ahead" is a YouTube Premium feature that appears when a viewer double-taps to skip forward: a button offers to take them to the point most viewers typically skip to, found using AI and viewing data. It launched for US Premium members on Android in 2024 and later reached other devices, including TVs. For creators, it is a public signal of the part people skip. If your intro or sponsor segment is what viewers jump past, the fix is in the edit, not in fighting the feature.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube announced Jump ahead for Premium members on 27 June 2024, saying it is "powered by a combination of AI and viewership data" (YouTube blog).
  • During testing, 9to5Google described it as taking you "to where most viewers typically skip ahead to", and noted it was not available on every video.
  • In July 2025, Android Authority reported it rolling out on TVs, with a dot on the progress bar marking the most common skip point.
  • It only exists because viewers already skip that part. It tells you which section your audience finds least worth watching.
  • The editing response is to shorten or move the skipped section, not to hide it.

How Jump ahead works

On a phone, double-tapping the right side of the player skips forward by a few seconds. For eligible Premium viewers on eligible videos, a "Jump ahead" button briefly appears. Tapping it moves the video to the spot many other viewers jumped to. YouTube's blog does not explain the method in detail beyond "AI and viewership data".

Because it depends on many viewers skipping the same way, small channels and new videos are less likely to see it. But the behaviour behind it, viewers skipping, happens on every channel.

Why creators should care

Jump ahead does not create skipping. It makes existing skipping faster. In your own analytics, the same pattern appears as a dip. YouTube's key moments help page says dips "mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video", and that the intro figure shows how many viewers were still watching after the first 30 seconds.

So the parts most likely to be jumped are the ones viewers already find slow:

  • Long intros that repeat the title before delivering anything.
  • Channel updates and housekeeping.
  • Sponsor reads.
  • Recaps of what the viewer just saw.
  • Set-up that comes before the promised payoff.

How to edit so viewers do not want to jump

Start where the value starts

If viewers skip your first 40 seconds, that is 40 seconds they did not want. Open on the moment the title promised, and add context after you have their attention. Our page on why viewers leave in the first 30 seconds covers openings that get to the point.

Make sponsor reads shorter and relevant

Sponsors are a business reality. You can reduce skipping by keeping the read short, tying it to the video's topic and placing it after a strong moment, so the viewer has a reason to stay for what comes next.

Cut the recaps

If the viewer just watched it, they do not need it again. Replace "so, as I said" with the next new point.

Use chapters honestly

Chapters let viewers navigate. A clear chapter list can make people jump to what they want rather than leave the video. YouTube's 2022 player update made chapters and the Most replayed graph more visible on many devices, according to TechCrunch.

Example: reading a skip and fixing it

A 12-minute review video has this pattern in YouTube Studio:

TimeWhat happensWhat the graph shows
0:00-0:45Greeting, channel news, "before we start"Steep drop, then a flat dip
0:45-1:30Sponsor readDip, some viewers skip ahead
1:30First look at the productLine flattens
6:00Side-by-side testSpike, viewers rewatch

If Premium viewers see Jump ahead on this video, it will likely take them to about 1:30. The re-edit:

  1. Open on the product reveal from 1:30, with one line on why the viewer should care.
  2. Move the sponsor read to after the first test result, around 4:00, and cut it from 45 to 25 seconds.
  3. Delete the channel news or move it to a community post.
  4. Tease the 6:00 side-by-side test in the first 20 seconds.

The video loses about a minute of dead time and the first real value now arrives in seconds, not minutes. You cannot see Jump ahead data directly, but you can compare the new upload's 30-second intro figure against your typical retention.

What you cannot do

  • You cannot switch Jump ahead off for your videos; YouTube has not offered creators that setting.
  • You cannot see a Jump ahead report in YouTube Studio. Use dips, key moments and typical retention instead.
  • You should not add fake "don't skip" cliffhangers that do not pay off. Viewers learn and skip anyway.

FAQ

Is there a book that teaches named retention editing techniques, with a 30-day plan?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops, re-hooks and pattern interrupts, with a Bonus Pack that includes a 30-day plan. YouTube Studio's key moments report is a free alternative.

Who can use Jump ahead?

YouTube launched it for Premium members. Reports describe it on Android first, then iOS and TVs.

Does Jump ahead lower my watch time?

It can shorten sessions for viewers who would have skipped anyway. YouTube has not published data on its effect.

Can I turn Jump ahead off?

YouTube has not given creators a setting to disable it.

Is Jump ahead the same as the Most replayed graph?

No. Most replayed shows parts viewers rewatch; Jump ahead uses skipping behaviour to move viewers forward.

Will Jump ahead skip my sponsor?

It takes viewers to where most people skip to, which may be past a sponsor read. A shorter, relevant read placed after a strong moment helps.
