What are key moments for audience retention in YouTube Studio?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 22 November 2026 · Last updated 22 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Key moments for audience retention are four labels YouTube Studio puts on a video's retention graph: Intro (how many viewers are still watching after the first 30 seconds), Top moments (stretches where almost nobody left), Spikes (parts people rewatched or shared) and Dips (parts where viewers skipped or stopped watching). You'll find them in a video's analytics under the audience retention report, on videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube's help page defines Intro as the share of your audience "still watching after the first 30 seconds".
  • "Dips mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping at that specific part of your video."
  • "Spikes are moments in your video that were rewatched or shared," though a spike can also mean something was unclear and people replayed it.
  • Key moments need a video of at least 60 seconds with at least 100 views, and retention data usually takes 1 to 2 days to process.
  • The labels tell you where to look; you still have to watch those seconds and work out why.

The four key moments

YouTube's guide to measuring key moments for audience retention describes them like this:

MomentWhat YouTube measuresWhat it usually meansWhat to do
Intro% still watching after 30 secondsDid the opening match the title and thumbnail?Start on the promise, cut slow set-up
Top momentsSections where almost no one dropped offContent that held peopleDo more of it, and earlier
SpikesRewatched or shared partsSomething valuable, or something confusingCheck which; clip the good ones
DipsParts viewers skipped or abandonedBoredom, a broken promise or an obvious skip pointTighten, move or cut

YouTube suggests placing compelling content earlier, because audience size normally shrinks across a video.

Where to find them

In YouTube Studio, open Content, choose the video and open its analytics. On the Overview or Engagement tab, find the audience retention report; Google's help page says you can select See more to compare with similar-length videos. YouTube's newer help pages call the analytics area Insights, so the label you see may differ. The report works at video level only, and some views may need Advanced mode.

How to read each one properly

Intro. If the intro number is low, your first 30 seconds aren't delivering what the packaging promised. Compare the title and thumbnail with what's on screen at 0:00. See why viewers leave a YouTube video in the first 30 seconds.

Dips. Watch from 10 seconds before the dip to 10 seconds after. Common causes: a long tangent, a repeated point, a sponsor read or a moment where the video seems finished. A dip right after a chapter marker may be people jumping ahead rather than leaving.

Spikes. Ask whether people rewound because the moment was great or because it was confusing. Fast-spoken numbers, small on-screen text and jargon often create spikes for the wrong reason.

Top moments. These are your evidence of what works for this audience. If a top moment arrives late, consider leading with a version of it next time.

Worked example

Illustrative readings for a 12-minute tutorial:

MomentTimeReadingLikely causeChange
Intro0:00 to 0:3058% still watching25-second channel intro before the topicCut the intro, open on the finished result
Dip4:10Sharp dropA 70-second story unrelated to the tutorialTrim to 10 seconds or cut
Spike6:45RewatchedSettings menu shown too fastSlow down, add a zoom and text label
Top moment8:00 to 9:30Almost no drop-offThe main before-and-after revealTease it at 0:15

Each change addresses a specific timestamp, which is why key moments are more useful than the average view duration alone.

Limits of key moments

YouTube's labels flag a handful of moments per video. They don't list every drop, they don't explain causes, and they don't appear on short or low-view videos. You also have to open each video, find the moments and compare with past uploads yourself. For a timestamp-by-timestamp method, see how to find the exact timestamps where viewers drop off.

A simple routine for using key moments

Checking key moments after every upload turns them into a habit rather than an occasional look.

WhenWhat to checkWhat to note
2 to 3 days after uploadIntro percentage vs similar videosDid the opening match the title and thumbnail?
Same checkBiggest dipTimestamp and what happens there
Same checkSpikesGood rewatch or confusion?
Before scripting the next videoLast 5 videos' notesPatterns, such as dips at sponsor reads or long intros

Look for patterns across videos. One dip tells you about one video. The same kind of dip in five videos, say every time you start a long story, tells you about your style. That's where the biggest improvements come from.

Fix forwards. You usually can't re-edit a published video without re-uploading, so the main value is in the next video. Keep a short list of "things that cause dips for us" and check each new script against it.

Use spikes for Shorts and clips. Moments people rewatch or share are good candidates for Shorts, clips or ads; see how to turn a YouTube video into an ad.

Compare like with like. Use the See more comparison with similar-length videos rather than judging a 25-minute video's intro against a 6-minute one's.

FAQ

Is there a tool that explains each drop-off and how to fix it?

Yes. Retention Doctor sends each upload's drop-off timestamps with a fix, at £145 a month. YouTube Studio's key moments are the free alternative.

Why don't I see key moments on my video?

YouTube needs the video to be at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views, and data can take 1 to 2 days.

Is a spike always good?

No. It can mean people rewatched something useful, or that a moment was confusing.

What is a good intro percentage?

There's no single target. Compare with your own videos of similar length using the See more comparison.
