Open loops vs curiosity gaps: what is the difference for editors?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A curiosity gap is the psychological feeling of wanting to know something you don't yet know. An open loop is the editing device that creates that gap on purpose and holds it open until a planned payoff. For editors, the gap is the "why it works"; the loop is the "how you build it" on the timeline.

Key takeaways

  • The curiosity gap comes from Loewenstein's information-gap theory: curiosity "occurs when an individual becomes aware of a gap in his or her knowledge" (Golman and Loewenstein, Carnegie Mellon).
  • An open loop has three parts: open, hold, close. It's a structure you can mark on a timeline.
  • Curiosity helps memory too: a 2009 Psychological Science study linked higher curiosity to better recall of surprising answers 1-2 weeks later.
  • Every open loop relies on a curiosity gap, but not every curiosity gap is an open loop. A thumbnail can create a gap without any loop inside the video.
  • YouTube's retention report shows whether a loop held viewers: "dips mean that viewers are abandoning or skipping".

How editors build an open loop from a curiosity gap

  1. Find the gap. What does the viewer not know but want to? It must matter to them. Golman and Loewenstein note that curiosity grows when people care about the missing information.
  2. State it clearly. "One of these three paints peeled in a week." Specific gaps beat vague ones.
  3. Hold it with progress. Show clues: the first paint passes, the second looks fine.
  4. Close it on time. Reveal the answer before the call to action or the outro, not after.
  5. Optionally open a new loop. Just before closing, raise the next question to carry viewers into the next section.

When gaps go wrong

  • Too vague. "You won't believe this" creates a gap nobody cares about.
  • Too many at once. Five open questions in the first minute confuse viewers.
  • Never closed. The viewer feels cheated and may not trust the next video.
  • Closed too early. If the answer comes at 0:30, there's no reason to stay for 9 minutes.

What the evidence does and doesn't say

Curiosity is well studied, but the popular idea that people always remember unfinished things better (the Zeigarnik effect) is weaker than often claimed. The practical lesson is that the gap has to matter to the viewer; a loop about something trivial holds nobody.

Writing open loops in the script

Loops are easier to place in the script than to find in the edit. When scripting, add a margin note for each loop: "OPEN: which paint peeled?" at the hook and "CLOSE" at the reveal. Before filming, check three things.

CheckQuestion
RelevanceWould the target viewer genuinely want this answer?
DistanceIs the close far enough away to hold attention, but not so far that viewers give up?
PayoffIs the answer as good as the question promised?

If the payoff is weaker than the question, change the question. A modest but honest gap ("Which of these is worth the money?") holds better over time than an inflated one, because viewers learn whether your loops pay off. Editors can then focus on timing rather than rescuing a loop that the footage can't deliver.

FAQ

Is there a guide that teaches open loops and curiosity gaps for editors?

Yes. Asendify's Retention Editing Playbook covers open loops with templates for £1,720 one-time. For theory, Golman and Loewenstein's paper on information gaps is free.

Is an open loop the same as a cliffhanger?

A cliffhanger is an open loop closed in the next episode or video. Most loops close inside the same video.

Is a curiosity gap thumbnail clickbait?

Not if the video delivers what the gap promises.

How many open loops should a video have?

One main loop plus a few small ones is common. Too many confuse viewers.

Do open loops work in short-form?

Yes. In a Short, one loop opened in the first second and closed near the end is usually enough.

Can an open loop span several videos?

Yes, as a series cliffhanger, but close the main loop of each video inside that video. Viewers who only watch one episode should still get a complete payoff, or they may feel tricked.
