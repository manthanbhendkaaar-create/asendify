What are the UK speeding penalties, and how can points affect owning a fast car?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

The minimum penalty for speeding in the UK is a £100 fine and 3 penalty points, unless you are offered a speed awareness course. Points stay on your record for 4 years, and building up 12 or more within 3 years can lead to a driving ban. Drivers within 2 years of passing their test have their licence cancelled at 6 points. For anyone planning to own a fast car, points matter beyond the fine: insurers ask about convictions, and a ban can leave an expensive car unused.

This page is general information and planning estimates, not legal or financial advice.

Key takeaways

  • Minimum penalty: £100 fine and 3 points, or a speed awareness course if offered and eligible.
  • You can only be offered a speed awareness course if you have not attended one in the past 3 years.
  • Speeding endorsements stay on your licence for 4 years.
  • 12 or more points within 3 years can mean disqualification.
  • New drivers: 6 or more points within 2 years of passing means your licence is revoked and you must retake both tests.

The penalties

GOV.UK's page on speeding penalties says the minimum penalty is a £100 fine and 3 penalty points, and that you could be disqualified if you build up 12 or more points within 3 years. If you get a Fixed Penalty Notice and plead guilty, you pay £100 and receive 3 points, unless you are offered a speed awareness course. You may be offered a course if the police decide it is appropriate and you have not been on one in the past 3 years. Your licence keeps a code for 4 years. If you plead not guilty, or the offence is more serious, the case goes to court, where penalties can be higher.

GOV.UK's guide to penalty points (endorsements) says endorsements stay on your record for 4 or 11 years depending on the offence, and repeats the 12-points-in-3-years rule.

For new drivers, your licence is cancelled if you get 6 or more points within 2 years of passing your test. Points from a provisional licence carry over. You then have to apply for a new provisional licence and pass both the theory and practical tests again.

Practical ways to avoid points

  1. Use the car's speed limiter in towns and roadworks.
  2. Know the limits on unfamiliar roads; average speed cameras cover long stretches.
  3. Book a track day if you want to use the car's performance legally.
  4. Check your licence record online so you know where you stand.
  5. Answer insurer questions accurately; see do I need to tell DVLA and my insurer about modifications for how misrepresentation is treated.

Young and newly qualified drivers

If you are planning a fast car early in your driving life, the 6-point rule is a real risk. Insurance is also usually far higher for young and new drivers. Our page on which supercar you can afford and the planner for how much you need to earn help put those costs in context.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a course is always available. Not if you had one in the past 3 years, or if the police decide it is not appropriate.
  • Forgetting new driver rules. 6 points within 2 years ends the licence.
  • Not telling the insurer. Insurers usually ask about convictions; check your policy.
  • Thinking points vanish after 3 years. The code stays for 4.

Check your own record

You can view your driving licence record online on GOV.UK to see current penalty points and when they will be removed. Doing this before renewing insurance helps you answer the insurer's questions accurately.

Planning point for buyers

If you already have points, factor that into your budget before buying a fast car. Get insurance quotes with your current record, not the record you hope to have, and plan for the possibility that premiums stay higher until the points are spent.
