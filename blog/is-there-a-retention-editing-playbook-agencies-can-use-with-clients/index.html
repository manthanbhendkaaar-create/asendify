Key takeaways

  • A playbook gives an agency one method across editors, so results don't depend on who edits.
  • Platform guidance agrees on the basics. Google's ABCDs: "Hook and sustain attention with an immersive story". TikTok: hook "in the first 6 seconds".
  • Client reporting works best on numbers clients can open themselves, like YouTube's retention report, which shows "what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds".
  • Be honest with clients: a method improves the odds; it doesn't guarantee a result.

Where a playbook fits in agency work

Agencies usually have good editors and weak systems. One editor has a feel for hooks; another is great at sound. A playbook turns that feel into rules the whole team can follow and the client can understand. It helps in four places:

  1. Pitching. You can explain how you edit for retention, step by step, instead of showing a showreel and hoping.
  2. Briefing. The client fills in a hook line and first frame, so revision rounds drop.
  3. Editing. Every editor runs the same passes and checklist.
  4. Reporting. You report the same early-retention numbers every month, with the same language.

How to roll it out with clients

  1. Read it as a team. One chapter a week in a short team meeting.
  2. Pick three rules to start. For example: product on screen by frame 1, one open loop per ad, phone QA before export.
  3. Add them to the brief template. Clients see the rules in every brief.
  4. Explain the "why" to clients. When a client wants their logo first, show the platform guidance and offer to test both versions.
  5. Report monthly. Same metrics, same format, best and worst three videos.
  6. Update your rules. Keep what works for each client; drop what doesn't.

Mistakes agencies make

  • Selling the playbook's ideas as guaranteed results. Clients remember promises.
  • Applying every rule to every client. A law firm and a sneaker brand need different hooks.
  • No baseline. Record numbers before you change anything.
  • Keeping the method secret from clients. Explaining it builds trust and speeds approvals.

Talking to clients about retention

Clients often judge videos on taste: "I like the logo at the start", "Can we add our values slide?" A retention method gives you a calmer way to discuss these requests. Explain the principle, show the data and offer a test.

Client requestResponse
Logo first"Platforms advise showing the product early. Let's test logo-first against product-first."
Longer intro about the company"We'll move it after the hook so viewers stay long enough to see it."
More features"One clear benefit holds attention better; we can cover others in separate cuts."

Then report the results honestly, including when the client's version wins. Clients trust agencies that follow the evidence, and that trust makes later approvals much faster.
