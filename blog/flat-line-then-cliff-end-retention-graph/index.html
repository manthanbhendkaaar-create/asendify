What does a flat line then a cliff at the end of a retention graph mean?

By the Asendify editorial team, led by Manthan Bhendkar · 6 min read · Published 25 November 2026 · Last updated 25 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A flat line then a cliff means viewers stayed through the content and left when something told them it was over: a summary, a music change, a sign-off or an end screen. That is common and often fine. It matters if you want a click after the content, such as a next video or a sign-up.

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help says a flat line means viewers are watching that part from start to finish, so a flat run before the end is a good sign.
  • Wistia says "we often see a noticeable drop-off" in the last 2% of a video because summaries and music changes signal the valuable content is over, and suggests a short logo at the end so calls to action show as soon as the content ends.
  • YouTube Help on end screens says end screens can be added to the last 5 to 20 seconds of a video, so they sit right in the cliff.
  • Decide whether the cliff costs you anything. If there is no action you want after the content, it may cost nothing.

What the shape says

A long flat line is the best news a retention graph can give. YouTube Help says it means viewers are watching that part of the video from start to finish. When that line then falls steeply near the end, viewers have decided the video is done. This is usually a sign of a satisfied audience rather than a failure of the middle.

Wistia's guide to audience retention explains why the end drops: closing summaries, a change of music and the visual cues of an ending tell viewers the valuable part is over. It adds that this matters more if the video has a call to action, because the call arrives just as viewers are leaving.

Does the cliff cost you anything?

It depends on what you want viewers to do next.

  • Nothing. A tutorial that solves a problem may be done when the problem is solved. The cliff is natural.
  • Watch another video. Then the cliff is lost watch time. Move the next-video prompt earlier or give viewers a reason to continue.
  • Click, sign up or buy. Then the cliff may cost you. The action should arrive while viewers are still watching, not after the sign-off.
  • Check a different part of the graph. If the flat line is high and the cliff is in the last few seconds, the cost is small.

Steps to edit the end

  1. Find the cliff time and what is on screen. Is it a summary, a goodbye, a logo or a music change?
  2. Decide the one action you want. If there is none, you may not need to change anything.
  3. Move the action before the cliff. Say it while the last useful idea is still on screen, not after a summary.
  4. Cut the recap. Wistia advises against recapping, since viewers can rewatch anything they want to hear again.
  5. Keep the music steady. Do not signal an ending with a sting or a fade before the last useful sentence.
  6. Plan the end screen with the timing in mind. Per YouTube Help on end screens, end screens can be added to the last 5 to 20 seconds, and a video must be at least 25 seconds long to have one. Place your best next video there, and say its name aloud.
  7. Use a short logo or a plain end card. Wistia suggests a short logo at the end so a call to action appears as soon as the content ends.
  8. Compare the next upload. Look at the last 30 seconds only, not the whole graph.

Example: a 6-minute how-to video

Example: a how-to video holds a flat line from minute 1 to 5:20, then drops from 88% of those viewers to 40% between 5:20 and 5:50, where the creator summarises the three steps and says goodbye over a music outro. What not to do

Do not try to remove the cliff completely. People leave when they are finished, and forcing them to stay with a long outro makes viewers feel held back. Do not stretch the video to hide the drop. Do not bury a call to action behind a long thank-you. And avoid judging the video by the cliff alone: the flat run before it is where the value lies.

If the end screen gets few clicks, check whether it appears when viewers are still there. A well-timed end screen in a cliff zone can lose much of its audience before it appears.
