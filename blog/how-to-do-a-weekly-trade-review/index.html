How do you do a weekly trade review, and what should it include?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 4 min read · Published 23 November 2026 · Last updated 23 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

A weekly trade review is a short session, ideally at the weekend away from live prices, where you go through every trade you took and ask two questions: did I follow my rules, and what did each setup actually earn or lose? Check rule-following before profit, compare your average loss with the risk you planned, look for one repeated mistake, and choose one change for next week. CMC Markets suggests reviewing "away from the excitement and gyrations of the market", and CME Group says to focus on the reasons behind results more than the net profit or loss.

This page is education only, not financial advice. Trading is risky: ESMA reported that 74-89% of retail accounts typically lose money on CFDs.

Key takeaways

  • CMC Markets recommends regular reviews of past trades away from live markets, notes on winners as well as losers, and looking for a mistake that keeps recurring.
  • CME Group says to record "why you made the trade", and to analyse strategy-level P&L, maximum loss per trade, trade duration and the effect of news over time.
  • Judge the process first: a rule-following loss is a good trade; a rule-breaking win is a warning.
  • A study of persistent Brazilian day traders found 97% lost money, which is a reason to measure honestly rather than trust how a week "felt".
  • One change per week is enough. Changing everything at once makes it impossible to see what helped.

A 30-minute review routine

  1. Export or open your journal for the week. Make sure every trade has entry, stop, exit, size and reason.
  2. Mark each trade "followed" or "broke rules". Be honest; CME Group calls honesty and self-awareness key.
  3. Total the results in R, not just money, so weeks with different sizes compare fairly.
  4. Group by setup and see which earned their keep.
  5. Find the costliest mistake (moved stop, early exit, revenge trade, oversized position) and add up what it cost.
  6. Look at a chart screenshot for your best and worst trade.
  7. Write one change for next week and one thing to keep doing.

Mistakes to avoid in the review itself

  • Reviewing straight after a big loss, when you're angry.
  • Only looking at losing trades.
  • Changing your strategy after one bad week.
  • Judging by P&L alone, ignoring whether you followed your rules.
  • Skipping the review when the week went well.

FAQ

Is there a trading journal that works out the cost of each mistake?

Yes. Asendify's Trader OS, a Notion template, shows the money each mistake costs alongside R-multiple and win rate. A spreadsheet is a free alternative.

How often should I review my trades?

Weekly suits most traders, with a quick daily note. CMC Markets suggests weekends, away from live prices.

What is the most important thing to check?

Whether you followed your rules. Results from rule-breaking trades tell you nothing about your method.

How many trades before I judge a strategy?

There's no fixed number, but a handful is too few. Many traders wait for at least 30-50 trades.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is general education. Trading carries a high risk of losing money.
