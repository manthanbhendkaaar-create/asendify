Key takeaways

  • YouTube Help says the Device type report "tells you what percent of your watch time comes from each device", covering "mobile, computer, TV and tablet".
  • In February 2025 YouTube said it was "surfacing device type data more prominently across both desktop and mobile" in the Audience tab, as reported by Search Engine Journal.
  • YouTube's analytics guide describes advanced mode for an expanded report where you can "compare performance and export data".
  • Knowing your main screen helps you choose text size, framing, pacing and length.
  • Device share tells you where people watch; your retention graph tells you where they leave. Use both.

How viewing tends to differ by screen

ScreenTypical situationRetention risksEditing response
MobileShort sessions, one hand, easy to swipeSlow intros, small textGet to the point fast; large captions
ComputerOften multitasking in another tabAttention drifts to other windowsVisual changes and clear audio cues
TVSit-back viewing, often longer sessionsLean-forward elements (tiny text) don't landBigger graphics; fewer on-screen paragraphs
TabletRelaxed, often eveningSimilar to mobile, larger screenMiddle ground

These are common patterns described by creators and editors, not guarantees; your own data decides.

How to check your device mix

  1. Open YouTube Studio Analytics and go to the Audience tab.
  2. Find the Device type card to see each device's share of watch time.
  3. Use advanced mode to look at the same data over a longer period or compare it across videos.
  4. Note your top one or two devices.
  5. Open the audience retention graph for your recent videos and look for where viewers leave, keeping your main screen in mind.

Yes. Asendify's Retention Doctor shows the exact second with a fix, £145 a month, with a free check of your latest video. YouTube Studio's retention graph is the free alternative.

Where is the Device type report in YouTube Studio?

In Analytics, on the Audience tab, as a Device type card showing each device's share of watch time.

Do TV viewers watch longer?

Many creators report longer sessions on TV, but it varies by channel; check your own data rather than assuming.

Should I make separate edits for mobile and TV?

Usually not. Edit for your main screen and make sure text and framing work on the others.

Can I see retention for each device separately?

Use advanced mode to explore what YouTube provides for your channel, and compare videos where one device dominates.
