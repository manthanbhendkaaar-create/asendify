How do I stop people re-uploading my YouTube videos?

By Manthan Bhendkar · 5 min read · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Disclosure: Asendify publishes this page and makes some of the options mentioned here; ours are named as ours. Every option is described from its own official page, and prices and features can change, so check before you buy.

Use YouTube's copyright removal request form to take down a copy, and the Copyright Match Tool to find other copies automatically. Content ID, which blocks or monetises copies as they are uploaded, is designed for rights holders with the most complex needs, such as labels and studios, so most creators never get it. Review each match before acting, because a match is not proof of infringement.

Key takeaways

  • The removal request form "is available for any copyright owner to use" and is the fastest route for most owners.
  • The Copyright Match Tool "can automatically identify videos that are copies or potential copies of other videos on YouTube".
  • Content ID is "designed for copyright owners with the most complex copyright management needs" and requires evidence of exclusive rights.
  • From a match, you can archive it, request removal, or contact the channel with a pre-written email.
  • Mashups, compilations, game footage, trailers and non-exclusive material typically don't qualify for Content ID.

YouTube's three tools compared

YouTube's overview of its copyright management tools sets out three levels:

ToolWho it's forWhat it doesEffort
Copyright removal request formAny copyright ownerRequest removal of a specific videoLow
Copyright Match ToolOwners who need ongoing detection; access based on need, resources and copyright knowledgeFinds copies or potential copies; you choose what to doMedium
Content IDOwners with the most complex needs, e.g. labels and studiosScans every upload; block, monetise or track automaticallyHigh

The overview says the Match Tool uses the same matching technology as Content ID but "is easier to manage and requires fewer resources".

Using the Copyright Match Tool

YouTube's page on using the Copyright Match Tool explains what it shows and what you can do:

  • Matches: videos that are matches or potential matches of yours, including potential matches from the "Prevent Copies" feature, which covers public videos only.
  • Archive: removes a match from your list without affecting the other video.
  • Request removal: opens the copyright removal request form.
  • Contact channel: sends the uploader a pre-written email.

It also lists limits. A match "doesn't mean it's infringing on your copyright", so you must consider fair use or similar exceptions. Uploads aren't scanned if you weren't the first to upload the video, or if it is protected by Content ID. If someone used only part of your song or audio, the tool won't surface it.

Why most creators don't get Content ID

YouTube's Content ID eligibility page says owners must give evidence of content for which they control exclusive rights, and sign an agreement limiting reference files to material they exclusively own. Content that typically doesn't qualify includes mashups, compilations and remixes; video game footage, software visuals and trailers; unlicensed and non-exclusively licensed material; and recordings of performances such as concerts or speeches. For creators who don't qualify, the page points to the removal webform and the Copyright Match Tool.

Worked example: a cooking channel's stolen video

Example: a cooking creator finds their most popular recipe video re-uploaded in full by another channel.

  1. Check the match. The copy is the whole video with no commentary; there is no plausible fair-use argument.
  2. Choose the action. For a full copy, the creator requests removal through the form. For a channel that used a 10-second clip with commentary, they would archive the match instead.
  3. Consider a scheduled removal. YouTube's removal requests page says owners can schedule a request to take effect in 7 days, giving the uploader a chance to delete it and avoid a strike, a fair choice for small or first-time copiers.
  4. Keep records. The creator notes the date, URL and outcome in a spreadsheet.

Time spent: about 15 minutes per case. The stolen copy is removed once YouTube validates the request.

Making copies less attractive

You cannot stop every copy, but you can make copies less useful to the people who make them:

  • Build your name into the video. Saying your channel name early and showing it on screen means a copy still points viewers back to you.
  • Keep project files and originals. Dated source files are the simplest evidence that you made the video first.
  • Check the Match Tool regularly. A weekly look catches copies before they gather views.
  • Watch your best performers. Your most-viewed videos are the most likely to be copied, so check them first.
  • Reply calmly. Many small copiers delete when contacted; save formal requests for those who don't.

These steps don't replace YouTube's tools, but they reduce how much a copy can take from you.

Before you file a removal request

  • Make sure you own the rights. Collaborations, commissioned music and client work can complicate ownership.
  • Consider exceptions. Short clips used for commentary may be fair use; YouTube asks you to think about this before filing.
  • Be accurate. YouTube says false information is prohibited and misuse can lead to account termination or legal consequences.
  • Choose the gentlest effective action. Contacting the channel or scheduling the removal often resolves small cases.
  • Check the territory. If you only hold rights in some countries, say so; Content ID owners must specify territories where ownership is not worldwide, and the same care applies to any request.

Can I get Content ID for my channel?

Content ID is for owners with complex needs who can show exclusive rights. Most creators use the removal form and the Copyright Match Tool instead.

Does a Copyright Match Tool match prove infringement?

No. YouTube says a match doesn't mean it's infringing; you must review it and consider exceptions such as fair use.

What is a scheduled removal?

A removal request set to take effect in 7 days, which lets the uploader delete the copy themselves and avoid a strike.

Will the tool find partial copies of my music?

YouTube says that if someone used only a portion of your song or audio, the tool won't surface it.
